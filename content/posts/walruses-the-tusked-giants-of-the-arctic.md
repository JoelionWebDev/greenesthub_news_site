Far away in the icy Arctic, where the sea is cold and the beaches are covered in snow, lives a big, whiskered animal with two long white tusks. That is the walrus! Walruses are some of the biggest animals in the Arctic. They rest together in big noisy groups, swim in the freezing sea, and use their tusks like hooks to climb out of the water. Let us meet these gentle giants of the far north.

## Meet the Walrus

A walrus is a very large sea animal. Grown-up walruses are much bigger than a grown-up person, and they weigh as much as a small car. Their bodies are covered in thick, wrinkled skin that is brown or pinkish in colour. When a walrus has been swimming in cold water, its skin looks pale. When it warms up in the sun, its skin turns pinker, because blood flows close to the surface to warm it up.

Walruses have a round head, small eyes, and a wide flat nose covered in stiff whiskers. Those whiskers are very important. They help the walrus feel its way along the sea floor in dark, murky water, a bit like fingers feeling around in the dark. A walrus also has two long tusks sticking down from its mouth. The tusks are teeth that keep growing all through the walrus's life, and they can be very long.

Both boy and girl walruses have tusks, but the tusks of the males are usually longer and thicker. Walruses use their tusks for many things: to climb out of the water, to break through thin ice, to defend themselves, and to show other walruses how strong they are.

## Life in the Freezing Sea

Walruses live in the cold Arctic seas around the North Pole. They spend much of their time in the water, swimming and looking for food. Their bodies are made for cold water. They have a thick layer of fat under their skin, called blubber, which keeps them warm like a thick coat. Their skin is tough, and their flippers help them swim and steer.

A walrus can dive deep under the water and stay down for a long time while it looks for food. It uses its whiskers to feel along the sea floor for tasty treats hidden in the sand and mud. When it finds something good, it sucks it up with its strong mouth. Walruses do not have sharp teeth for chewing like we do. They suck the soft parts out of shellfish and swallow them whole.

Walruses mostly eat shellfish, especially clams, which live buried in the sea floor. They also eat snails, crabs, shrimps, and small fish when they can find them. They are not fussy eaters, but clams are their favourite food. A walrus can eat a great many clams in a single day, because it needs lots of energy to stay warm in the cold sea.

## Climbing Out with Tusks

One of the most amazing things about walruses is how they climb out of the water. The sea ice and rocky beaches can be slippery, and a walrus is very heavy. So it digs its tusks into the ice or the ground and pulls itself up, like a climber using hooks. That is why walruses are sometimes called the tusked giants: their tusks are their climbing tools as well as their teeth.

When walruses are not in the water, they rest on beaches, rocky shores, and floating ice. They pile up together in big groups, lying so close that they touch each other. This helps them stay warm, and it helps them stay safe, because there are many eyes watching for danger.

## Big Noisy Groups

Walruses are social animals, which means they like being together. In summer, many hundreds of walruses can gather on one beach. They lie side by side, snoring, grunting, and snorting. It is a very noisy place! Young walruses play together, climbing over the grown-ups and splashing in the shallow water.

## Baby Walruses

Baby walruses are called calves. A mother walrus usually has one calf at a time, and she looks after it very carefully. The calf is born on land or on ice, and it is already quite big, with soft fur and tiny tusks that are only just starting to grow.

The mother feeds her calf with her milk, which is very rich and helps the calf grow fast. She keeps it close to her at all times, even when she goes into the water to feed. The calf can swim from a young age, and it holds on close to its mother while she swims.

Calves stay with their mothers for a long time, learning how to find food, how to swim, and how to climb out of the water. The mother teaches her calf by example, and the calf copies everything she does. When the calf is big and strong enough, it starts to spend more time with other young walruses, playing and learning together.

## Staying Safe in the Arctic

Life in the Arctic is not easy. The sea is freezing, the weather is harsh, and there are hunters about. Polar bears and killer whales sometimes hunt walruses, especially young ones. Walruses stay safe by living in big groups, where many eyes can watch for danger, and by using their tusks to defend themselves if they need to.

## Fun Facts About Walruses

- Walruses have two long tusks that are actually teeth, and they keep growing all their lives.
- They use their tusks like hooks to climb out of the sea onto ice and beaches.
- Their whiskers help them feel for clams buried in the sea floor.
- Walruses rest together in big noisy groups, lying close to keep warm.
- Baby walruses are called calves, and they stay with their mothers for a long time.
- Their favourite food is clams, which they suck out of their shells.

The walrus is a gentle giant of the Arctic, with its whiskers, its blubber, and its amazing tusks. It swims in freezing seas, rests in big noisy groups, and looks after its babies with great care. Next time you think of the Arctic, picture a beach full of walruses, snoring in the sun, with their tusks gleaming white against the ice.
