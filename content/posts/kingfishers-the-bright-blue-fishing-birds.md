# Kingfishers: The Bright Blue Fishing Birds

Imagine sitting quietly by a river when suddenly a flash of bright blue zooms past you, like a tiny flying jewel. The bird is small, but its colours are so bright they almost glow: a shiny blue back, an orange belly, and a long, sharp beak. It lands on a branch above the water, watches carefully, and then dives in with a splash. This is a kingfisher, one of the most colourful fishing birds in the world.



## A Bird That Looks Like a Jewel

Kingfishers are small birds, but their colours are amazing. The common kingfisher, which lives in Europe, Asia, and parts of Africa, has a bright blue back and wings, and a warm orange chest. Its head is blue with a touch of green, and its beak is long, straight, and sharp. When the sun shines on its feathers, the blue seems to sparkle.

There are many different kinds of kingfishers, and they come in different colours. Some are blue and orange, some are red and white, and some are black and white. The largest kind, called the giant kingfisher, lives in Africa and is much bigger than the common kingfisher. The smallest kinds are tiny, not much bigger than a sparrow.

Kingfishers have short tails and short legs, and they often sit very still on a branch, watching the water below. They look a bit plump, with a big head and a long beak, but they are very fast and clever when they hunt.

## The Perfect Fishing Bird

Kingfishers are expert fishermen. They hunt by sitting on a branch or a rock above the water, watching carefully for fish swimming below. Their eyes are very sharp, and they can see a fish even when the water is a little bit murky. When they spot a fish, they dive in headfirst, like a tiny arrow, and grab the fish with their beak.

The dive is very fast. The kingfisher tucks in its wings, points its beak down, and plunges into the water with a splash. It catches the fish in its beak, then flies back to its perch. Sometimes it hits the fish against the branch a few times to stun it, then turns the fish around so it goes down headfirst, and swallows it whole.

Kingfishers have special eyes that help them see underwater. They have a clear third eyelid that closes over their eye when they dive, protecting it from the water like a pair of goggles. They also have to correct for the way light bends in water, so that the fish is not quite where it looks like it is. It is a clever trick, and kingfishers are very good at it.


## What Do Kingfishers Eat?

Kingfishers mostly eat small fish. They love minnows, sticklebacks, and other small fish that swim near the surface of the water. They also eat tadpoles, small frogs, and water insects. Some kinds of kingfishers, especially those that live far from water, eat lizards, insects, and small crabs.

A kingfisher eats a lot of fish every day. It needs plenty of food to keep its energy up, because diving and flying use a lot of energy. Young kingfishers learn to fish by watching their parents, and they practise diving until they get good at it.

After a kingfisher swallows a fish, it coughs up a small pellet of fish bones and scales. This is normal, and it helps the bird get rid of the parts of the fish it cannot digest.

## Family Life in a Riverbank Tunnel

Kingfishers are caring parents. The female lays several white eggs in the tunnel nest, and both parents take turns keeping the eggs warm. When the chicks hatch, they are blind and naked, with no feathers. Their parents bring them fish many times a day, and the chicks grow very quickly.

The nest tunnel can get quite messy, because the chicks' droppings and fish remains build up inside. But kingfishers have a clever way of keeping it clean: the chicks turn around and drop their waste near the entrance of the tunnel, so it does not pile up inside.

When the young kingfishers are old enough, they leave the nest and learn to fish for themselves. At first, they are not very good at it, and they sometimes miss their fish. But with practice, they become expert fishermen, just like their parents.

## Protecting the Kingfishers

Kingfishers need clean rivers and healthy fish to survive. When rivers get polluted, the fish die or disappear, and kingfishers have nothing to eat. When riverbanks are damaged or built over, kingfishers lose the places where they dig their nests. Cold winters can also be hard for kingfishers, because the water freezes and they cannot catch fish.

People are working to protect kingfishers by keeping rivers clean and protecting riverbanks. In many places, people plant trees and reeds along rivers, which gives kingfishers places to perch and nest. By looking after our rivers, we help kingfishers and all the other animals that live there.

## Fun Facts About Kingfishers

- A kingfisher can dive into the water and catch a fish in a matter of seconds.
- Kingfishers have a clear third eyelid that protects their eyes when they dive, like goggles.
- They turn the fish around so it goes down headfirst when they swallow it.
- Kingfishers do not build nests in trees; they dig tunnels into riverbanks.
- Their bright blue feathers sparkle in the sunlight.
- Kingfishers make a sharp, high-pitched whistle when they fly.
- After eating a fish, a kingfisher coughs up a small pellet of bones and scales.

Kingfishers are small, bright, and brilliant at fishing. With their jewel-blue feathers, their sharp eyes, and their super-fast dives, they are one of the most exciting birds to spot by a river. Next time you are near a river or a lake, keep your eyes open for a flash of blue, and listen for a sharp whistle. You might just see a kingfisher at work.
