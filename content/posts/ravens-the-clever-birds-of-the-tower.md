High above the River Thames in London stands a very old castle called the Tower of London. Kings and queens lived there long ago, and today thousands of people visit it every year. But the Tower has some very special residents that are not people at all. They are big black birds called ravens — and they have lived at the Tower for hundreds of years.

## Meet the Raven

A raven is a large black bird, bigger than a crow and much bigger than a pigeon. It has a heavy beak, shaggy feathers around its neck, and a deep, croaking voice that sounds like a gruff "cronk, cronk." When a raven flies, its wings make a soft whooshing sound, and its tail is shaped like a wedge.

Ravens live in many parts of the world — in Europe, Asia and North America. They like wild places like mountains, forests and cliffs, but they are happy in towns too. In the wild, a pair of ravens often stays together for life, and they look after their home territory together.

## The Tower of London

The Tower of London is almost a thousand years old. It was built by William the Conqueror, a king who came from France long ago. Over the years it has been a palace, a prison, a place where treasure is kept and a zoo. Today the Crown Jewels — the king's crowns and jewels — are kept there, and the Tower is looked after by guards called Yeoman Warders, who are also known as Beefeaters.

Ravens have lived at the Tower for a very long time. Nobody knows exactly when the first ravens arrived, but stories say they have been there for hundreds of years. Some people say the ravens came because the Tower was once a place where wild animals were kept, and the birds found food there. Whatever the reason, the ravens stayed, and they became part of the Tower's story.

## The Legend of the Ravens

There is a famous old legend about the Tower ravens. It says that if the ravens ever leave the Tower, the Tower will fall down and the kingdom will fall with it. Nobody knows who first told this story, but it is said that a king long ago ordered that the ravens must always be kept at the Tower to protect the kingdom.

Because of this legend, the ravens are very well looked after. A small group of ravens always lives at the Tower, and they are cared for every single day. Even today, when people visit the Tower, one of the first things they look for is the ravens hopping around on the grass.

## The Ravenmaster

The ravens at the Tower have their very own keeper. He is called the Ravenmaster, and he is one of the Yeoman Warders. His job is to look after the ravens every day, from morning to night. He feeds them, checks that they are healthy and makes sure they are happy.

The Ravenmaster gives each raven a name. Over the years the Tower ravens have had names like Jubilee, Harris, Gripp, Rocky, Erin and Poppy. Some ravens have become famous. One raven called Merlina was known for being very clever and flying far away from the Tower, and another called Munin liked to sit on visitors' shoulders!

The ravens eat a healthy diet. The Ravenmaster gives them meat, mice, eggs, cheese and special biscuits made for birds. He also gives them vitamins to keep them strong. The ravens live in a cosy enclosure at night, and during the day they are free to hop around the Tower grounds — although their wings are trimmed a little so they cannot fly too far away.

## A Raven's Day

What does a raven do all day at the Tower? In the morning, the Ravenmaster lets them out and feeds them breakfast. Then the ravens spend the day exploring. They hop across the grass, sit on the old stone walls and watch the visitors go by. Sometimes they play with sticks or pick up shiny objects that catch their eye.

In the evening, the Ravenmaster calls the ravens back to their enclosure and gives them supper. Then they settle down to sleep. Ravens are early risers, so they are often up before the first visitors arrive in the morning.

## Clever Birds, Clever Tricks

Ravens are some of the cleverest birds in the world. Scientists have watched ravens solve puzzles, plan ahead and even trick other animals. A raven will sometimes pretend to hide food in one place while secretly hiding it somewhere else, so that other birds will not steal it. That is a very clever trick!

Ravens can also use tools. They have been seen using sticks to reach food that is hard to get. They can pull a string to bring food closer, and they can work out puzzles that need several steps. Young ravens learn by watching older ravens, and they practise their skills by playing.

Ravens have good memories too. They remember places where they have found food, and they remember people. The Tower ravens know the Ravenmaster very well, and they come to him when he calls. They can also tell the difference between people they know and people they do not know.

## Fun Facts About Ravens
- A raven is the largest member of the crow family, and one of the largest birds that sings — though its song is more of a croak!
- Ravens can copy sounds, including human speech, car alarms and other birds' calls.
- A group of ravens is called an unkindness or a conspiracy — strange names for such clever birds.
- Ravens mate for life, and a pair will defend their home together year after year.
- In the wild, ravens can live for many years, and ravens looked after by people have lived even longer.

Next time you hear a deep croak in the sky, look up. It might be a raven, one of the cleverest birds on Earth, flying past with a secret smile and a story hundreds of years old.
