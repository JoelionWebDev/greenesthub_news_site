Imagine an animal so tall that it can peek into the upstairs window of a house. That animal is the giraffe. Giraffes are the tallest animals that live on land, and they are easy to spot because of their very long necks and their beautiful spotted coats. Giraffes live in Africa, where they wander across grassy plains and open woodlands. They are calm and gentle animals, and they spend most of their day eating leaves from tall trees. When you see a giraffe, it is hard not to smile. It looks like it was drawn by someone with a wonderful imagination.

## How tall is a giraffe?

A giraffe is taller than any other land animal. An adult giraffe can stand much taller than a grown person, and its head can reach high into the trees. Most of its height comes from its long legs and its long neck. A giraffe's neck alone is very long, and it helps the giraffe reach leaves that no other animal can reach. Even a baby giraffe is tall. When a baby giraffe is born, it is already taller than most people. Giraffes grow quickly, and they keep growing for several years. Their great height helps them see far across the grasslands. From up high, a giraffe can spot danger coming from far away, which helps keep it safe.

## The long neck

A giraffe's neck is its most famous feature. It is long and strong, and it holds up a heavy head. Inside the neck there are bones, just like in our necks. A giraffe has the same number of neck bones as a human, but each bone is much, much longer. Giraffes use their necks to reach leaves high in the trees, especially from acacia trees, which are one of their favourite foods. Male giraffes also use their necks in a special way. They swing their necks at each other in contests to show who is stronger. This is called necking. It looks strange, but it is how male giraffes settle their arguments. A giraffe's neck is also useful for grooming. Giraffes can twist their necks to clean hard-to-reach spots.

## Spots like fingerprints

Every giraffe has a coat covered in spots, and no two giraffes have exactly the same pattern. A giraffe's spots are like a person's fingerprints. Each one is different. The spots can be orange, brown, or dark, with lighter skin between them. The pattern helps the giraffe hide among the trees and shadows. When a giraffe stands still under a tree, its spots break up its shape, making it harder to see. Baby giraffes are born with spots too. As a giraffe grows older, its spots may get darker. The spots make every giraffe special.

## What do giraffes eat?

Giraffes are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They mostly eat leaves, buds, and fruits from trees. Their favourite food is the leaves of the acacia tree. Acacia trees have thorns, but giraffes do not mind. Their tongues are long and tough, and they can wrap around leaves and pull them off the branches without getting hurt. A giraffe's tongue can be very long, and it is often dark in colour. Giraffes eat for many hours every day because leaves do not give them much energy. They need to eat a lot to stay big and strong. Giraffes also eat grass and sometimes fruit that has fallen to the ground. They get most of the water they need from the leaves they eat, so they do not need to drink water every day.

## A giraffe's day

A giraffe spends most of its day eating. It walks slowly across the grasslands, stopping at tree after tree to munch on leaves. Giraffes often eat while standing still, reaching up with their long necks. When they want to eat grass on the ground, they have to spread their front legs wide apart and bend down, because their necks are so long. It looks funny, but it works. Giraffes do not sleep much. They often sleep standing up, and they only rest for short naps. When a giraffe does lie down to sleep, it curls its neck around and rests its head on its body. Giraffes live in loose groups called herds. The herd moves together, and the giraffes look out for each other.

## Baby giraffes

Baby giraffes are called calves. A calf is born while its mother is standing up, so it has a long drop to the ground on its first day of life. That sounds scary, but the calf is fine. Within a short time, the calf tries to stand on its long, wobbly legs. Soon it is walking, and before long it is running. A mother giraffe takes great care of her calf. She feeds it milk and stays close to protect it. The calf stays with its mother for a long time, learning where to find food and how to stay safe. Calves love to play and run around with other young giraffes. They practise running and kicking, which helps them grow strong. A young giraffe grows fast, and before long it is almost as tall as its mother.

## Giraffes and their home

Giraffes live in the grasslands and open woodlands of Africa. They need wide open spaces with plenty of trees to eat from. Giraffes are important to their home because they help shape the trees. When they eat leaves from the tops of trees, they help the trees grow in new ways. Giraffes also spread seeds when they eat fruit, helping new plants grow. But giraffes need our help. Their homes are shrinking because people are building farms and towns on the grasslands. Some giraffes are also in danger from hunters. People are working hard to protect giraffes by keeping their homes safe and watching over them. When we protect giraffes, we protect the whole grassland and all the other animals that live there too.

## Fun facts about giraffes

- Giraffes are the tallest land animals in the world, and their long necks help them reach leaves high in trees.
- No two giraffes have the same spot pattern, just like no two people have the same fingerprints.
- A giraffe's tongue is very long and tough, and it can wrap around leaves on thorny trees.
- Giraffes sleep very little, and they often sleep standing up, taking only short naps.
- Male giraffes swing their necks at each other in contests called necking to show who is stronger.
