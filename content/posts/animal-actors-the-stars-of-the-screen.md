# Animal Actors: The Stars of the Screen

Lights, camera, action! A dog runs across the field, a horse gallops through a dusty town, and a clever cat opens a door with its paw. On screen, it looks like magic. But behind every animal star there is a patient trainer, a lot of tasty treats, and many hours of practice.

Animals have been acting in films and television for more than a hundred years. Dogs, cats, horses, dolphins and even bears have become famous stars, loved by millions of people around the world. Let us step behind the scenes and meet the animal actors and the people who train them.

## The First Animal Stars

When films were first made, long before television or computers, animal actors were already stars. One of the most famous early animal actors was Rin Tin Tin, a German shepherd dog found as a puppy during the First World War. He was rescued by an American soldier and brought to Hollywood, where he became one of the biggest stars of silent films in the 1920s. People went to the cinema just to see him.

Another famous early star was a horse called Trigger, the loyal companion of the cowboy Roy Rogers in films and television in the 1940s and 1950s. Trigger could do all kinds of tricks, and children everywhere wanted a horse just like him.

Then came Lassie, the beautiful collie dog who starred in films and a long-running television show. Lassie was famous for rescuing people in danger, and the words "Lassie, come home" became known all over the world. The part of Lassie was played by a line of talented collies, all trained to act in front of the camera.

## Famous Animal Actors You Might Know

Over the years, many animals have become stars. Flipper, the clever dolphin, starred in a television show in the 1960s and made people fall in love with dolphins. The dolphins who played Flipper were trained to jump, spin and even "talk" in dolphin sounds, and the show taught many people to care about the sea.

Cats have been stars too. A cat called Orangey starred in the film Breakfast at Tiffany's and won awards for animal acting. More recently, cats, dogs, horses and many other animals appear in films about adventures, farms, and magical worlds.

## How Do Animals Learn to Act?

Animal actors do not learn by reading a script. They learn by training, step by step, with rewards. A trainer teaches a dog to sit, stay, lie down, run to a mark on the ground, or bark on command. Each time the animal does the right thing, it gets a treat, a toy, or lots of praise. The animal learns that doing the trick means something good will happen.

Training takes patience. A trainer might teach one small trick at a time, and repeat it many times over weeks or months. The trick is broken into tiny steps. For example, to teach a dog to open a door, the trainer first rewards the dog for touching the door, then for pushing it, and then for pushing it open. Bit by bit, the dog learns the whole trick.

Animal trainers use gentle methods. They never hurt the animals or frighten them, because a scared animal will not learn well and will not want to work. The best trainers are kind and calm, and the animals trust them. Many animal actors love their trainers and are excited to work with them.

On a film set, the trainer stands just out of sight of the camera, holding treats and giving quiet signals. The animal watches the trainer, not the camera. If the animal does the trick right, the scene is kept. If not, they try again. Sometimes a scene has to be filmed many times before it is perfect.

## A Day on the Film Set

Life on a film set is very different from life at home. An animal actor arrives with its trainer, who checks that it is healthy and happy. The animal is given time to explore the set and sniff around, so it feels comfortable. Then the trainer practices the tricks needed for the day's scenes.

Between scenes, the animal rests in a quiet, comfortable place with water, shade and a cosy bed. Animal actors do not work all day. They have short working times, with lots of breaks for rest and play. Trainers and vets are always nearby to make sure the animal is safe and well.

Some scenes need several animals to play one part. For example, the part of Lassie was played by several different collies over the years, and a film might use one dog for running scenes, another for close-up shots, and another for tricky stunts. This is because each dog has its own special skills.

## Keeping Animal Actors Safe

Today, there are strict rules to keep animals safe on film sets. In many countries, animal welfare groups watch over filming to make sure the animals are treated well. A vet is usually on set, and animals must have proper food, water, rest and care.

Some scenes that might be dangerous for a real animal are now made with computer effects instead. A horse charging through a battle or a dog leaping from a great height might be a computer-made animal, not a real one. This helps keep real animals safe while still making exciting films.

## Fun Facts About Animal Actors

- Rin Tin Tin was a German shepherd found as a puppy during the First World War, and he became a huge film star in the 1920s.
- Lassie was played by several different collie dogs over the years.
- Trigger was the famous horse of the cowboy Roy Rogers.
- Flipper the dolphin starred in a television show in the 1960s.
- A cat called Orangey won awards for acting in films.
- On a film set, trainers stand just out of sight of the camera and give quiet signals.

Animal actors remind us that animals are clever, talented and full of personality. The next time you watch a film with a dog, a horse or a dolphin, remember the patient trainer just off camera and the star with four legs, doing its best work for a tasty treat.
