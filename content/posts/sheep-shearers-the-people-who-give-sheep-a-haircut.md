Have you ever seen a sheep with a big, fluffy coat and wondered what happens to all that wool? Once a year, sheep get a haircut, and the people who give it are called sheep shearers.

Sheep grow wool all year round, just like your hair grows. If the wool is not cut off, it can become too thick and heavy, especially in warm weather. Shearing keeps sheep cool, clean, and comfortable, and the wool can be used to make jumpers, blankets, hats, and scarves.

## What Does a Sheep Shearer Do?

A sheep shearer carefully removes the wool from a sheep, usually in spring, before the weather gets hot. They use special tools called shears, which can be powered by electricity or worked by hand. The shears cut the wool close to the skin, but they do not hurt the sheep.

Shearing is a skilled job that takes practice. The shearer guides the sheep gently into position, often sitting it on its back in a special way, so the sheep stays calm and still. Then they move the shears in smooth, careful strokes, peeling the wool away in one big piece called a fleece.

A good shearer works quickly but gently. They watch the sheep closely, talk to it in a calm voice, and take care not to nick its skin. When the job is done, the sheep looks much slimmer and feels much lighter. It can run, eat, and play more easily without its heavy coat.

## A Day in the Life of a Sheep Shearer

Many sheep shearers start work early in the morning on a farm. They arrive at the shearing shed, a special barn where sheep are brought in, one at a time, to be shorn. The shed is busy and noisy, with sheep, shearers, and helpers all working together.

The shearer puts on strong work clothes and boots, picks up their shears, and gets ready. A helper, called a rousie, brings each sheep to the shearer and takes the fleece away afterwards. The rousie rolls up the fleece neatly and puts it in a pile, ready to be sorted.

Shearers often work in a team, shearing one sheep after another. They take short breaks to rest their arms and backs, because shearing is hard physical work.

## How Shearers Care for Sheep

Sheep shearers care about sheep, and keeping them calm is part of the job. They handle sheep gently, moving slowly and speaking softly. Sheep are calm animals, but they can get nervous if they are rushed or scared, so a good shearer stays patient.

Shearing also helps farmers check on the health of their sheep. When the wool comes off, the shearer can see the sheep's skin clearly. They might spot a cut, a sore, or a patch of skin that needs attention. They can then tell the farmer, who can help the sheep get better.

Shearing helps sheep stay healthy in other ways, too. A thick woolly coat can hide dirt and damp, and it can make a sheep too hot in summer. Without its heavy fleece, a sheep can stay cool and dry.

## What Happens to the Wool?

After shearing, the wool does not go to waste. The fleece is rolled up, sorted, and packed into large bags. Then it is sent to a mill, where it is washed to remove dirt and grease. This grease is called lanolin, and it helps to keep the sheep's wool waterproof.

At the mill, the clean wool is combed, spun into yarn, and dyed in bright colours. The yarn is then used to knit jumpers, scarves, hats, and blankets, or to weave carpets and cloth. Wool is warm, soft, and strong, and it has been used to make clothes for a very long time.

## What Makes a Good Sheep Shearer?

A good sheep shearer is strong, fit, and patient. Shearing is hard work, and shearers use their arms, legs, and back all day long. They need to be fit and to look after their bodies, with good food, water, and rest.

They also need to be calm and kind. Sheep can sense when a person is nervous or rushed, so a shearer learns to move gently and breathe slowly. They need to be good at learning, too, because shearing has special moves and patterns that take time to master.

Many shearers learn from older, experienced shearers, watching them work and practising on calm, friendly sheep. They might start by helping in the shed, learning how to roll fleeces and bring sheep in, before they learn to shear themselves.

## Fun Facts About Sheep Shearers and Sheep

- Sheep are usually shorn once a year, often in spring, before the warm weather arrives.
- The wool from one sheep is called a fleece, and it often comes off in one big piece.
- Wool is warm in winter and cool in summer, which is why it makes such good clothes.
- Sheep have very good memories and can recognise other sheep and familiar people.
- A shearer's shears are kept sharp and clean, so the wool comes off smoothly and the sheep stays comfortable.

## How You Can Learn More

You do not need to shear a sheep to learn about sheep shearers. You can start by visiting a farm, a country fair, or an agricultural show, where you might see shearing in action. Watch how gently the shearer handles the sheep, and how the fleece comes off in one piece.

If you see sheep in a field, look at their coats. Are they thick and fluffy, or have they just been shorn and look slim and neat? You can also feel wool for yourself. Touch a woollen jumper or blanket, and think about the sheep that grew that wool.

You can read books about farms and sheep, or try a simple craft with wool, such as finger knitting. Every time you learn about a sheep and the people who care for it, you are sharing in the world of the sheep shearer — a world of skill, kindness, and a very important haircut.
