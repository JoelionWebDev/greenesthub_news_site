Guide horses are small, clever horses trained to help people who cannot see. They are calm, brave and they can work for many more years than a dog. Most people have heard of guide dogs, but did you know that tiny horses can do the same job? These little horses are called miniature horses, and they are trained to lead their owners safely through the world.

## A Horse That Helps You See

A guide horse does the same job as a guide dog. It wears a special harness with a handle that its owner holds. The horse walks ahead, guiding its owner around obstacles, stopping at kerbs and crossings, and finding the way to shops, bus stops and other places. It is a very clever and responsible job.

Guide horses are miniature horses, which means they are much smaller than ordinary horses. An adult miniature horse is about as tall as a large dog, and it can fit through doorways and into lifts. They are small enough to live in a house, travel in a car and go into shops and restaurants with their owners, just like a guide dog.

One of the best things about guide horses is that they live a long time. A guide dog usually works for about eight years before it retires. A guide horse, on the other hand, can work for twenty years or more. This means that a person who cannot see can have the same guide horse as a friend and helper for many, many years.

## Why a Horse?

You might wonder why anyone would choose a horse instead of a dog. There are several good reasons. First, horses live much longer than dogs, so the partnership can last longer. Second, horses have excellent eyesight, and they can see very well in the dark. Their eyes are on the sides of their heads, which gives them a wide view of the world around them.

Horses are also very calm animals. They do not get excited easily, and they are not distracted by other animals or by food on the ground. They are steady and reliable, which is exactly what a guide animal needs to be.

Some people cannot have a guide dog because they are allergic to dogs, or because their religion does not allow them to keep a dog in the house. For these people, a guide horse is a wonderful alternative. Horses do not cause the same allergies as dogs, and they are accepted in many homes where dogs are not.

## Training a Guide Horse

Training a guide horse takes a long time, usually about a year or more. The horse must learn to walk at a steady pace, to stop at kerbs and crossings, to avoid obstacles, and to find its way around a town. It must also learn to stay calm in busy places, such as shopping centres, train stations and airports.

Guide horses are trained using kind and patient methods. They are rewarded with treats and praise when they do something right, and they are never punished. They learn to trust their trainers, and their trainers learn to trust them.

The horses wear special shoes to protect their hooves when they walk on pavements and roads. They also wear a harness with a handle, just like a guide dog. When they are working, they wear a special jacket that lets people know they are a guide horse and should not be distracted.

## A Day in the Life of a Guide Horse

A guide horse's working day is much like a guide dog's. In the morning, the horse is groomed, its hooves are checked, and its harness is put on. Then it walks with its owner to the bus stop, to the shops, or to work. It walks at a steady pace, stopping at every kerb and crossing, and steering its owner around bins, roadworks and other obstacles.

During the day, the horse rests when its owner rests, lying down or standing quietly. It eats hay and drinks water, and it enjoys a scratch behind the ears. At the end of the day, it walks home again, ready for its evening meal and a good rest. Guide horses are hard workers, but they also get plenty of time off to play, roll in the grass and simply be a horse.

Because horses are herd animals, they love company. A guide horse that lives with its owner becomes part of the family, sharing the home and the daily routine. Some guide horses even have a companion horse or a friendly dog to keep them company when they are not working.

## Living with a Guide Horse

Guide horses live in their owner's home, just like a pet. They sleep in a special area, they eat hay and horse food, and they need regular exercise and play. They also need visits from the vet and from a farrier, who is a person who looks after horses' hooves.

Owners of guide horses say that their horses become their best friends. The horses are loyal, gentle and loving, and they form a strong bond with their owners. They know when their owner is happy or sad, and they offer comfort and company, just like any other pet.

Guide horses are still quite rare, and there are only a small number of them working in the world. But the people who have them say they are wonderful helpers and friends, and they hope that more people will learn about guide horses in the future.

## A Little Horse with a Big Job

The guide horse shows us that animals can help people in surprising ways. Who would have thought that a tiny horse could guide a person through a busy city, keeping them safe and helping them live independently? It is a reminder that horses are not just for riding or racing — they can also be clever, caring helpers.

So next time you see a small horse, remember the guide horse, the tiny horse with a big job. It is a horse that leads the way, one careful step at a time, helping its owner see the world through its eyes.
