Cluck, cluck, cluck. If you walk past a farmyard in the morning, you may hear a cheerful hen clucking as she scratches in the dirt. There is a good chance that hen is a Rhode Island Red. These friendly brown hens are some of the most popular chickens in the whole world, and farmers love them for their lovely feathers, their tasty eggs, and their calm, happy ways.

Rhode Island Red chickens come from Rhode Island, a small state in the United States of America. Farmers there wanted a chicken that was hardy, healthy, and good at laying eggs, so they bred the Rhode Island Red. It worked so well that the chicken became famous all over the world. Today you can find Rhode Island Reds on farms in many countries, including right here in Nigeria.

## Feathers Like Autumn Leaves

The first thing you notice about a Rhode Island Red is its colour. Its feathers are a rich, warm reddish-brown, like autumn leaves or a shiny chestnut. In the sunlight, the feathers glow with a deep, beautiful red. The hens are a little lighter in colour, while the roosters are darker and glossier, with long, shining tail feathers.

Rhode Island Reds have a single upright comb on top of their head. The comb is the red, floppy bit that sits like a crown. Their wattles, the red flaps under their beak, are bright red too. Their beak is yellow, and their legs are yellow as well. Together, the red feathers, red comb, and yellow legs make a very handsome chicken.

Rhode Island Reds are medium to large chickens with a strong, sturdy body. They are not delicate birds. They are built to be tough and healthy, able to live happily in many kinds of weather. Their feathers are thick and smooth, and they keep them clean by dust-bathing, which means rolling in dry dust to shake off dirt and tiny pests.

## Champions of Egg Laying

Rhode Island Red hens are famous for laying lots of eggs. A healthy hen can lay eggs most days of the week, and her eggs are large and brown. Farmers who keep Rhode Island Reds know they can count on a basket full of fresh eggs almost every morning. That is why these hens are favourites on small farms and in backyards.

The hens start laying eggs when they are young, and they keep laying steadily for a long time. Their eggs are delicious and full of goodness, perfect for breakfast, baking, and cooking. Many families say there is nothing better than a fresh egg from your own hens, still warm from the nest.

Rhode Island Red roosters are also important on the farm. They wake the farm up in the morning with their loud crowing, cock-a-doodle-doo. They watch over the hens, warn them when danger is near, and find tasty treats for them to eat. A good rooster is a brave and caring leader for his flock.

## Friendly and Full of Character

Rhode Island Reds are known for being friendly and calm. They are not shy chickens that run away when people come near. Many Rhode Island Red hens will come to greet you, pecking gently at your shoes and clucking softly. Children who keep them often find that the hens follow them around the yard like little feathered friends.

These chickens are also curious and clever. They love to scratch in the soil to find seeds, bugs, and worms. They will explore every corner of the farmyard, and they always seem to know where the best treats are. If you shake a bag of grain, they will come running from all directions, wings flapping and feet pattering.

Rhode Island Reds are hardy birds, which means they can handle hot days and cold days without fuss. They are not fussy eaters, and they are good at finding their own food when they are allowed to roam free. Farmers love them because they are easy to keep and rarely cause trouble.

## Life on the Farm

A happy Rhode Island Red needs a safe home. On a farm, chickens live in a coop, which is a small house that keeps them safe from rain and from animals that might want to catch them. Inside the coop there are perches for sleeping and nesting boxes lined with straw, where the hens lay their eggs.

During the day, the chickens go outside to scratch, peck, and dust-bathe in the yard. They love to roam in the sunshine, and they enjoy a patch of grass to explore. At night, they return to the coop and roost on their perches, tucked up together for safety and warmth.

Chickens are social animals, and they like to live in a flock. In a flock, there is a pecking order, which is the chickens' way of deciding who goes first at the food and who gets the best perch. It might sound silly, but it keeps the flock calm and organised. Rhode Island Reds fit into a flock very well.

## Looking After Rhode Island Reds

Looking after Rhode Island Red chickens is not hard, but they need care every day. They need fresh food, like grains and greens, and clean water to drink. They need their coop cleaned regularly, and they need to be kept safe from animals that might harm them. A farmer who cares for them well will have happy, healthy hens.

Children can help look after chickens too. You can collect the eggs in the morning, fill the water bowls, and scatter grain for the hens. Always wash your hands after handling chickens, and be gentle with them. Chickens are living animals, and they deserve kind hands and soft voices.

Rhode Island Red chickens show us that farm animals can be beautiful, useful, and friendly all at once. They give us fresh eggs, they keep the farmyard cheerful with their clucking, and they make wonderful friends. Next time you see a rich brown hen scratching in the yard, say hello. It might just be a Rhode Island Red, the friendly brown hen from America.
