Have you ever seen a flash of orange fur in a garden, a field or a park, and wondered what it was? It might have been a fox. Foxes are beautiful wild animals with pointed ears, a bushy tail and a clever, curious face. They live in forests and fields, but many foxes also live near towns and cities, close to people. They are mostly active at dawn and dusk, when the light is soft and the world is quiet. Foxes are shy of people, but they are very clever, and they have learned how to live alongside us. Learning about foxes helps us understand how wild animals and people can share the same world.

## Meet the fox

A fox looks a little like a small dog, but it has its own special style. The most famous fox is the red fox, with its rusty orange coat, white chest and black legs. Its tail is big and bushy, with a white tip, and foxes use it for balance and for warmth. When a fox curls up to sleep, it wraps its tail around itself like a blanket. Foxes have sharp ears that can hear tiny sounds, such as a mouse moving under leaves or snow. They have a very good sense of smell too, which helps them find food. Foxes come in other colours as well, including grey, silver and white, depending on where they live.

## A clever hunter

Foxes are clever hunters. They eat small animals such as mice, rabbits and birds, as well as fruits, berries, insects and eggs. A fox hunts mostly by listening. It can hear a small animal moving under the grass, then pounce with a quick jump to catch it. Foxes are also very good at remembering where they have hidden food. If a fox finds more food than it can eat, it will bury some of it in the ground and come back for it later. This clever habit helps foxes survive when food is hard to find, especially in cold weather. Foxes hunt alone, not in packs, and each fox knows its own patch of land very well.

## The fox family

Foxes live in family groups. A mother fox, called a vixen, gives birth to her babies, called cubs, in a safe underground home called a den. The den may be a hole in the ground, a hollow under tree roots, or a burrow left behind by another animal. The cubs are born blind and helpless, and they stay in the den while their mother looks after them. The father fox brings food to the family. As the cubs grow, they start to play outside the den, tumbling over each other, chasing leaves and practising their pouncing skills. Play is how young foxes learn to hunt and to stay safe. By the time they are grown, they are ready to find their own home and start a family of their own.

## Foxes in towns and cities

Many foxes now live near people, in towns, villages and even big cities. They have learned that gardens, parks and quiet streets can be good places to find food and shelter. A fox might visit a garden at night to look for fallen fruit or small animals, or it might make a den under a shed or in a quiet corner. Foxes are usually shy and will run away if they see a person. They are most active at night, so people often only see them in the early morning or late evening. Living near people has its dangers for foxes, such as busy roads, but it also shows how clever and adaptable they are. Foxes remind us that wild nature is closer than we think.

## Foxes in stories and tales

Foxes appear in stories from all over the world, and in most of them the fox is the clever one. In many tales, the fox outsmarts bigger, stronger animals by thinking quickly and using tricks. Sometimes the fox is a hero, sometimes it is a trickster, but it is almost always smart. These stories show how much people have admired the fox's quick mind for a very long time. In some places, people also see the fox as a symbol of cleverness and quick thinking. When we hear a fox story, we are reminded that being clever and thinking carefully can be just as important as being big and strong. That is a good lesson for children and grown-ups alike.

## How to be a good neighbour to foxes

Foxes are wild animals, and the best way to help them is to give them space. We should never try to feed a fox by hand or keep one as a pet. A wild fox needs to find its own food and stay wild. If you want to help foxes, you can leave a wild corner in your garden with bushes and long grass where small animals can live. Keep your rubbish bins closed so foxes do not get into trouble looking for food. If you see a fox, watch it quietly from a distance and enjoy the moment. If a fox looks hurt or sick, tell a grown-up, who can call a wildlife helper. By being kind and careful neighbours, we can share our towns and countryside with foxes safely.

## What foxes teach us

Foxes teach us to be clever, careful and curious. They show us that we do not need to be the biggest or the loudest to do well in life. A fox uses its ears, its nose and its brain to find food and stay safe. It learns the land around it, remembers where things are, and adapts when things change. Foxes also teach us about family. Mother and father foxes work together to feed and protect their cubs, and the cubs learn by playing and exploring. If we watch foxes with respect and leave them wild and free, these beautiful animals will keep sharing our fields, forests and gardens for a long time to come.

## Fun facts about foxes

- A fox's big, bushy tail helps it balance and keeps it warm when it curls up to sleep.
- Foxes can hear tiny sounds, like a mouse moving under leaves, with their sharp ears.
- A mother fox is called a vixen, and her babies are called cubs.
- Foxes bury extra food in the ground and remember where to find it later.
- Many foxes live near towns and cities, and are most active at dawn and dusk.
