Imagine finding a tiny, prickly animal curled up in your garden. It is a hedgehog, and it looks hurt, or cold, or lost. What would you do? Hedgehog rescuers know exactly what to do. They are kind people who look after injured, sick, or orphaned hedgehogs, help them get better, and then release them back into the wild where they belong.

Hedgehogs are small, shy animals with a very special coat. Instead of fur all over, they have thousands of sharp spines on their backs. When a hedgehog feels scared, it rolls into a tight ball, and those spines protect it from danger. But spines cannot protect a hedgehog from everything. Cars, garden tools, litter, and cold weather can all hurt these little animals. That is when rescuers step in to help.

## Meet the Hedgehog

Hedgehogs are nocturnal, which means they are mostly awake at night and sleep during the day. At night, they snuffle around gardens, parks, and fields, looking for food. They eat beetles, worms, slugs, and other small creatures. They have a very good sense of smell, which helps them find food in the dark.

A hedgehog is about the size of a small melon, with a pointed nose, bright eyes, and little legs that scurry along quickly when it wants to move. Baby hedgehogs are called hoglets. They are born with soft spines that harden as they grow older. Mother hedgehogs look after their hoglets in a nest made of leaves and grass, hidden away in a quiet, safe place.

In autumn, hedgehogs eat a lot to build up fat for winter. Then they hibernate, which means they sleep for a long time through the cold months, tucked away in a nest of leaves. In spring, they wake up hungry and ready to explore again. Hedgehogs are wonderful garden helpers, because they eat many of the bugs that damage plants.

## What Do Hedgehog Rescuers Do?

Hedgehog rescuers work in rescue centres, wildlife hospitals, and sometimes in their own homes. When someone finds a hedgehog that looks hurt or in trouble, they can call a rescuer for help. The rescuer will come and collect the animal, or ask the finder to bring it to the centre in a safe box with a towel and some water.

At the rescue centre, the hedgehog is checked carefully. Is it hurt? Is it too thin? Is it cold? Is it a baby that has lost its mother? The rescuers weigh the hedgehog, look at its spines and skin, and check for cuts or broken bones. If the hedgehog needs medicine, a vet will help. Vets are animal doctors, and they work closely with rescuers to treat sick animals.

Sick or injured hedgehogs are kept warm and quiet while they recover. They are fed special food and given fresh water. Rescuers clean their bedding, watch them closely, and make notes about how they are doing. Some hedgehogs stay for just a few days. Others need weeks of care before they are strong enough to go home.

## Helping Hedgehogs Get Home

The goal of every hedgehog rescuer is to release the animal back into the wild. That is where a hedgehog belongs. When a hedgehog is healthy, eating well, and a good weight, the rescuers choose a safe place to set it free. That place is usually near where the hedgehog was found, in a garden or park with plenty of shelter and food.

Release day is a happy day. The rescuer opens the box, and the hedgehog sniffs the air, waddles out, and disappears into the grass. It is a wonderful feeling to know that a little animal you helped is now free and healthy again. Rescuers do not keep hedgehogs as pets. Wild animals belong in the wild, and the rescuers' job is to help them get back there.

Sometimes, a hedgehog cannot go back to the wild because it is too old or too badly hurt. In that case, it might live in a safe garden with a rescuer who looks after it for the rest of its life. But this is rare. Most rescued hedgehogs do return to the wild, and that is the best outcome of all.

## How Can Children Help Hedgehogs?

Children can help hedgehogs too, and you do not need to be a rescuer to do it. The most important thing is to be gentle and careful. If you see a hedgehog in the daytime, it might be in trouble, because hedgehogs usually sleep during the day. Tell an adult straight away, and do not pick the hedgehog up yourself. An adult can call a rescue centre for advice.

You can also help by making your garden safe for hedgehogs. Leave a pile of leaves in a quiet corner, where a hedgehog can build a nest. Make a small hole in a fence, so hedgehogs can move from garden to garden. Put a shallow dish of water outside on warm nights. Never give a hedgehog milk, because it can make them poorly. If you want to leave food, plain cat biscuits or special hedgehog food is best, and only as a treat.

Be careful with garden tools and litter too. Check long grass before you cut it, and keep netting and rubbish out of the way, so hedgehogs do not get tangled or hurt. Small actions like these make a big difference to a small animal.

## Little Animals, Big Hearts

Hedgehog rescuers show us that even the smallest animals deserve care and kindness. A hedgehog may be prickly on the outside, but it needs gentle hands and a warm heart to help it when it is in trouble. Rescuers give their time, often for free, because they love wildlife and want to protect it.

Next time you are in a garden at dusk, listen carefully. You might hear a soft snuffling sound in the leaves. That could be a hedgehog, out looking for dinner. Leave it alone, watch it quietly from a distance, and smile. Thanks to hedgehog rescuers, and to people like you who care, that little hedgehog has a better chance of living a long, happy, wild life.
