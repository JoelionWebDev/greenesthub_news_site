Tapirs are gentle, shy animals that look a little bit like a pig, a little bit like a small elephant, and a little bit like something from long ago. They have a short, bendy nose like a tiny trunk, a round body, and four toes on their front feet and three on their back feet, which help them walk on soft, muddy ground. Tapirs love water, and they are very good swimmers. They spend their days quietly munching on leaves and fruit in the forest, and as they wander they help to plant new trees.

## What is a tapir?

A tapir is a mammal, and it is related to horses and rhinos, even though it does not look much like either of them. Tapirs have a short, flexible nose that hangs down over their mouth, a bit like a tiny elephant's trunk. They use this nose to grab leaves, pull down branches, and sniff out tasty fruit. A tapir's body is large and round, with short legs and a small, stubby tail. Its skin is thick and covered in short hair, and it is usually brown, grey, or black. Tapirs have good hearing and a very good sense of smell, but their eyesight is not very strong. They are calm, quiet animals, and they like to live alone or in a small family group.

## Where do tapirs live?

Tapirs live in two parts of the world. Some live in the rainforests of Central and South America, and others live in the forests of Southeast Asia. They love warm, wet places with lots of trees, rivers, and ponds. A tapir needs water nearby, because it loves to swim and bathe, and water helps it stay cool and safe. Tapirs live in thick forests where they can hide among the trees and bushes. They make paths through the forest as they walk to the same feeding places and watering holes again and again. Tapirs are mostly active at night or in the early morning and evening, when the forest is cool and quiet.

## What do tapirs eat?

Tapirs are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They love to eat leaves, twigs, buds, shoots, and fruit that has fallen to the forest floor. They use their bendy nose to pull leaves from branches and to pick up fruit from the ground. Tapirs also eat water plants and soft grasses that grow near rivers and ponds. They have strong teeth for chewing tough leaves, and they can eat a lot in one night. Their favourite foods are young, tender leaves and sweet, ripe fruit.

## The gardeners of the forest

Tapirs are called the gardeners of the forest because they help new trees grow. When a tapir eats fruit, it swallows the seeds inside the fruit along with the soft, juicy part. The seeds travel through the tapir's body and come out in its droppings, far away from the tree where the fruit grew. The droppings act like natural fertiliser, helping the seeds to grow into new trees. In this way, a tapir can plant trees all over the forest, just by having its dinner. Some trees depend on tapirs to spread their seeds, and without tapirs those trees would have a much harder time growing in new places. A tapir's long walks through the forest help to keep the forest healthy and full of life.

## A day in a tapir's life

A tapir's day starts in the cool of the evening or early morning. It wakes up from its rest in the thick bushes and starts to wander slowly through the forest, sniffing the air for food. It nibbles on leaves, picks up fallen fruit, and stops to drink from a stream. Tapirs love water, and they often take a swim to cool down and wash off. They can stay underwater for a short time, and they use their bendy nose like a snorkel to breathe while they swim. If a tapir feels scared, it may run into the water and hide there, or crash through the bushes to get away. Tapirs are shy, and they would much rather hide than fight. At the end of the night, a tapir finds a quiet, shady spot to rest until the next evening.

## Baby tapirs

Baby tapirs are called calves, and they are some of the cutest babies in the forest. When a tapir calf is born, it is brown with white spots and stripes all over its body. These spots and stripes help it hide among the dappled sunlight and shadows on the forest floor, just like the spots on a fawn. The mother tapir looks after her calf, feeding it milk and keeping it close to her as they wander through the forest. The calf stays with its mother for a long time, learning which leaves are good to eat and where the best watering holes are. As the calf grows, its spots and stripes slowly fade away, and it turns the plain brown or grey colour of a grown-up tapir. By the time it is old enough to live on its own, it is ready to be a gardener of the forest too.

## Fun facts about tapirs

- A tapir's nose is short and bendy, like a tiny trunk, and it uses it to grab leaves and fruit.
- Tapirs are excellent swimmers, and they can use their nose like a snorkel while they swim underwater.
- Baby tapirs are born with white spots and stripes that help them hide in the forest.
- Tapirs help new trees grow by spreading seeds in their droppings as they wander through the forest.
- Tapirs have four toes on their front feet and three toes on their back feet, which help them walk on soft, muddy ground.

Tapirs are quiet, gentle animals who do their important work without making a fuss. They munch on leaves, swim in rivers, and plant new trees with every meal, keeping the forest healthy for all the animals that live there. Next time you think of the rainforest, picture a tapir wading through a cool stream at dusk, with its little trunk sniffing the air and its round body moving slowly through the trees.
