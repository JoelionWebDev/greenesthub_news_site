Have you ever wondered what horses wear on their feet? Horses wear shoes, just like people do, but their shoes are made of metal and nailed carefully onto their hooves. The person who makes these shoes and looks after horses' feet is called a farrier. A farrier is a bit like a shoemaker and a foot doctor for horses, all in one. Horses are big, strong animals, and they spend their lives standing and walking on their feet, so their hooves need a lot of care. Farriers keep horses healthy, comfortable, and ready for work and play.

## Who is a farrier?

A farrier is a skilled person who trims horses' hooves and fits horseshoes. It is a very old job. For hundreds of years, people have used horses for riding, farming, and travel, and farriers have been there to look after their feet. A farrier needs to learn a lot. They must understand how a horse's foot works, how to shape a piece of metal into a shoe, and how to work safely with a big animal. Farriers spend much of the day bending over, lifting heavy horse legs, and working with hot metal. But they love horses, and they are proud of the skill it takes to keep a horse's feet in perfect shape. A good farrier is a horse's best friend when it comes to happy, healthy feet.

## What is a horse's hoof?

A horse's hoof is a bit like a big, strong toenail. It is made of a tough material called keratin, the same stuff that makes up our fingernails. The hoof grows all the time, just like our nails do, so it needs to be trimmed regularly. Inside the hoof is a soft part called the frog, which helps the horse grip the ground and feel where it is stepping. A horse has one hoof on each foot, and it stands on the tips of its toes. That is why hooves are so important. If a hoof grows too long, cracks, or gets sore, the horse can limp and feel pain. The farrier checks each hoof carefully, looking for cracks, bruises, or signs of trouble, and fixes what needs fixing. A healthy hoof is smooth, strong, and the right shape for the horse.

## Trimming the hooves

One of the farrier's most important jobs is trimming the hooves. First, the farrier lifts the horse's leg and holds the hoof gently but firmly. The horse has learned to trust the farrier and stand still. Then the farrier uses a tool called a hoof knife to clean out dirt and stones from the bottom of the hoof. Next, the farrier uses large clippers called nippers to cut away the extra growth, and a file called a rasp to smooth the edges. It is a bit like giving the horse a pedicure. The farrier must be careful to cut just the right amount. After trimming, the hoof is neat, balanced, and ready for a shoe, or ready to go barefoot if the horse does not need one.

## Making and fitting horseshoes

Many horses wear horseshoes to protect their hooves. A horseshoe is a curved piece of metal that fits around the edge of the hoof. The farrier makes each shoe to fit the horse, because every hoof is a little bit different. The farrier heats the metal in a hot fire called a forge until it glows red and soft. Then the farrier hammers it on an anvil into the shape of a horseshoe. Once the shoe is cool, the farrier holds it against the hoof to check the fit. Then the shoe is nailed onto the hoof with special nails. This does not hurt the horse, because the nails go into the tough outer part of the hoof, where there are no nerves. The farrier bends the ends of the nails over so the shoe stays firmly in place. A well-fitted shoe protects the hoof and helps the horse walk comfortably on hard or rocky ground.


## Working with horses

Working with horses is not always easy. Horses are big and strong, and they can be nervous when a stranger lifts their leg. That is why a farrier must be calm, patient, and kind. The farrier talks softly to the horse, moves slowly, and lets the horse get used to being touched. Most horses learn to trust their farrier and stand quietly during a visit. A good farrier gets to know each horse's character and works in the way that suits that horse best. The farrier also teaches young horses to stand still for hoof care, so that when they grow up, they are happy to have their feet looked after. The bond between a farrier and a horse is built on trust, and it grows stronger with every visit.

## Fun facts about farriers

- A horseshoe is made of metal, and the farrier shapes it by heating it in a hot fire and hammering it on an anvil.
- A horse's hoof is made of the same tough material as our fingernails, and it grows all the time.
- Nailing on a horseshoe does not hurt the horse, because the nails go into the hard outer part of the hoof.
- Farriers use a tool called a rasp, which is like a big file, to smooth the edges of the hoof.
- Some horses do not wear shoes at all. They go barefoot, and the farrier just trims their hooves.
- Farriers have looked after horses' feet for hundreds of years.
- A farrier's job is sometimes summed up in an old saying: "No hoof, no horse," which means a horse is only as healthy as its feet.

Farriers may not be as famous as cowboys or jockeys, but horses could not do their work without them. Every time a horse gallops across a field, jumps a fence, or carries a rider down a trail, its hooves are doing a big job, and the farrier is the person who keeps those hooves strong. Next time you see a horse, look down at its feet. Those shiny shoes were made by hand, fitted with care, and they are a reminder of the quiet, skilled people who look after horses one hoof at a time.
