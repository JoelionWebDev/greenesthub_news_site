# Beluga Whales: The White Whales of the Arctic

Imagine a whale as white as snow, swimming through icy blue water under the Arctic sky. It has a round head, a smiling mouth, and a soft, squishy forehead that wobbles when it moves. It makes lots of squeaks, chirps, and whistles, as if it is chatting with its friends. This is a beluga whale, one of the friendliest and noisiest whales in the sea.



## The White Whale With a Smile

Beluga whales are easy to recognise because they are white. Grown-up belugas are pure white or creamy white, which helps them hide in the icy Arctic water, where there is snow and ice everywhere. Baby belugas, called calves, are born dark grey or brown, and they slowly turn white as they grow older. By the time they are grown up, they are as white as snow.

Belugas are medium-sized whales. They are much smaller than a blue whale, but they are still bigger than a person, and they are quite heavy. They have a round head, a short beak, and a smiling mouth that makes them look happy all the time. They do not have a dorsal fin on their back, like many other whales do. Instead, they have a small ridge, which helps them swim under the ice without getting hurt.

One of the most special things about a beluga is its forehead, called a melon. The melon is a soft, fatty lump on top of the beluga's head, and it is very squishy. The beluga can change the shape of its melon, and it uses it to make sounds and to find its way around. Belugas are the only whales that can move their heads from side to side, because they have a bendy neck. Most whales have stiff necks, but belugas can turn their heads and look around, which makes them look very curious.

## The Canaries of the Sea

Beluga whales are famous for being noisy. They make all sorts of sounds: whistles, clicks, chirps, squeaks, and even sounds that sound like a bird singing. Because of this, sailors long ago called them the canaries of the sea, after the little yellow songbirds.

Belugas use their sounds to talk to each other and to find their way. They use echolocation, which means they send out clicks and listen for the echoes that bounce back. The echoes tell them where things are, even in dark or murky water. It is a bit like shouting in a cave and listening to the echo to work out how big the cave is.

Belugas are very social animals. They live in groups called pods, and they stay close to their family and friends. They play together, chase each other, and sometimes blow bubbles in the water just for fun. Mother belugas are very caring, and they stay close to their calves for a long time, teaching them how to find food and how to stay safe.

## Life in the Icy Arctic

Beluga whales live in some of the coldest waters on Earth. They swim in the Arctic Ocean, around the coasts of Alaska, Canada, Greenland, and Russia. In summer, they swim into shallow bays and river mouths, where the water is a little warmer and there is plenty of food. In winter, they move to areas where the sea ice is not too thick, so they can still come up to breathe.

Belugas need to breathe air, so they come to the surface often. In winter, they find cracks and holes in the ice to breathe through.

Belugas have a thick layer of blubber, which is fat under their skin, that keeps them warm in the cold water. Their white colour also helps them hide from predators, like polar bears and killer whales, because they blend in with the ice and snow.

## What Do Beluga Whales Eat?

Beluga whales eat fish, squid, shrimp, crabs, and other small sea animals. They hunt in shallow water, and they use their echolocation to find food in the dark or murky water. They are good hunters, and they can catch fish that are hiding in the sand or under rocks.

Belugas do not have teeth for chewing. They have small, pointed teeth that they use to grab their food, and then they swallow it whole. They eat a lot of different kinds of fish, including salmon, cod, and herring, and they also eat small animals that live on the sea floor.

Baby belugas drink their mother's milk for a long time. They stay close to their mothers, learning where to find food and how to use echolocation. When they are old enough, they start to hunt for themselves, and they join the pod to swim and play with other young belugas.


## Protecting the Beluga Whales

Beluga whales face some problems. Their Arctic home is changing because the sea ice is melting, and the water is getting warmer. This makes it harder for belugas to find food and safe places to live. Pollution in the Arctic waters is also a problem, and noise from ships can make it hard for belugas to hear each other and find their way.

Long ago, beluga whales were hunted for their meat and oil, but hunting is now controlled in most places. Today, people are working to protect belugas by studying them, keeping their waters clean, and reducing the noise from ships. By protecting the Arctic, we protect belugas and all the other animals that live there.

## Fun Facts About Beluga Whales

- Beluga whales are the only whales that can turn their heads from side to side.
- Their soft, squishy forehead is called a melon, and it helps them make sounds.
- Baby belugas are born dark grey and slowly turn white as they grow up.
- Belugas are called the canaries of the sea because they make so many sounds.
- They use echolocation to find food and to navigate in dark water.
- Belugas do not have a dorsal fin on their backs, only a small ridge.
- Their thick blubber keeps them warm in icy Arctic water.

Beluga whales are some of the most charming animals in the Arctic. With their snowy white skin, their smiling faces, and their chatty songs, they bring life and sound to the icy seas. Next time you think of the Arctic, picture a white whale swimming under the ice, whistling to its friends, and smiling at the world.
