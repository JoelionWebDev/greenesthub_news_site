Therapy dogs visit hospitals, schools and care homes to bring comfort and smiles. A gentle dog can help people feel calmer and happier. You may have heard of guide dogs that help people who cannot see, but therapy dogs have a different job. Their job is to make people feel better, just by being there, wagging their tails and offering a warm, furry friend to cuddle.

## What Is a Therapy Dog?

A therapy dog is a pet dog that has been trained to visit people who need comfort and company. These dogs visit hospitals, schools, care homes for older people, and places where children are learning to read. They do not do tricks or rescue work. Their most important job is to be calm, gentle and friendly, and to let people stroke them, talk to them and feel better.

Therapy dogs come in all shapes and sizes. Some are big, fluffy dogs like golden retrievers, and some are small dogs that can sit on a person's lap. What matters most is not how they look, but how they behave. A good therapy dog is calm, patient and happy to meet new people.

Many different breeds can become therapy dogs, but the most common are golden retrievers, labradors, poodles and spaniels. These breeds are known for being gentle and friendly, and they enjoy being around people. But any dog with the right nature can become a therapy dog, no matter what breed it is.

## Visiting Hospitals and Care Homes

One of the most important places therapy dogs visit is the hospital. When people are ill, they often feel worried and lonely. A visit from a therapy dog can make a big difference. The dog will lie quietly beside the person's bed, let them stroke its soft fur, and wag its tail happily. Many people say that stroking a dog helps them feel calmer and less worried.

Therapy dogs also visit care homes, where older people live. Some older people miss having a pet of their own, and a visit from a therapy dog brings back happy memories. The dog may sit on their lap, rest its head on their knee, or simply be there as a friendly face. For people who do not get many visitors, a therapy dog's visit can be the highlight of their week.

In some hospitals, therapy dogs visit children who are ill. The children love to play with the dog, brush its fur, and read stories to it. The dog listens patiently, without judging, which helps children feel brave and happy, even when they are not feeling well.

## Helping Children Learn to Read

Therapy dogs also help children in schools. Some children find reading difficult, and they feel nervous about reading out loud in front of other people. But reading to a dog is different. A dog does not laugh or correct you. It just listens quietly, wagging its tail. This helps children feel more confident, and they often become better readers because they practise more.

In these reading programmes, a therapy dog and its owner visit a school, and children take turns reading a story to the dog. The children love it, and teachers say that it really helps. The dog is a kind, patient listener, and the children look forward to their reading time with their furry friend.

Therapy dogs also visit children who are feeling sad or worried. Just being with a dog can help a child feel calmer. Dogs are good at sensing how people feel, and they often know when someone needs a cuddle or a quiet friend to sit with.

## How Does a Dog Become a Therapy Dog?

Not every dog can become a therapy dog. First, the dog must have a calm and friendly nature. It must like meeting new people, it must not be scared of strange noises or places, and it must be happy to be stroked by people it does not know. It must also be well-behaved and able to follow basic commands, such as sit, stay and come.

The dog and its owner go through special training together. They learn how to behave in hospitals and schools, how to stay calm in busy places, and how to be gentle with people who are ill or frail. They are tested to make sure they are suitable, and only dogs that pass the test can become therapy dogs.

Therapy dogs are not working all the time. They are pets first, and they live at home with their owners like any other dog. They only do therapy visits for a few hours a week, so they have plenty of time to rest, play and enjoy being a normal dog.

## Why Dogs Make Us Feel Better

Scientists have found that stroking a dog can help people feel calmer and happier. It can lower the heart rate, reduce stress, and make people smile. This is partly because dogs are warm, soft and friendly, and partly because they love us without asking for anything in return.

Dogs also help people talk and connect with each other. When a therapy dog visits a care home, people start chatting about the dog, sharing stories about pets they had when they were younger, and smiling together. The dog brings people together in a way that nothing else can.

## A Dog with a Special Job

Therapy dogs remind us that animals can help people in many ways, not just by working or guarding, but simply by being there. A wagging tail, a warm body to cuddle and a pair of kind eyes can make a difficult day a little bit brighter.

So next time you see a dog wearing a special jacket or bandana that says "therapy dog", remember that it is not just a pet. It is a dog with a special job, bringing comfort and smiles to people who need it most. And that is one of the most wonderful jobs any dog can have.
