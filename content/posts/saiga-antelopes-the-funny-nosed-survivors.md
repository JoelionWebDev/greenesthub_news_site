Far away in Central Asia, where the grasslands stretch as far as the eye can see, lives a strange and wonderful animal called the saiga antelope. It looks a little like a deer, a little like a goat, and a little like something from a storybook. Its most famous feature is its nose, which is big, swollen and hangs down over its mouth like a short trunk. Across the grassy steppes of Central Asia roams an antelope with a strange swollen nose and a story of survival stretching back thousands of years.

## A Nose with a Clever Job

The saiga's nose may look funny, but it has a very clever job to do. The grasslands where saigas live are dusty in the summer and freezing cold in the winter. The big, soft nose works like a filter. In summer, it cleans the dust out of the air before it reaches the saiga's lungs. In winter, it warms up the icy air before the saiga breathes it in, so the cold air does not hurt its chest.

Inside the nose there are special chambers and hairs that trap dust and warm the air. It is a bit like having a built-in scarf and air filter all in one. When a saiga runs, its nose wobbles and bounces, which makes it look even funnier. But without that amazing nose, the saiga could not live in the harsh land it calls home.

Male saigas also have long, pale horns that stand up straight from their heads. The horns have rings around them and point upwards like little spears. Female saigas do not have horns. In the past, people hunted saigas for their horns, which made the animals very rare. Today, saigas are protected, and hunting them is against the law in most places.

## The Great Travellers of the Grasslands

Saigas are great travellers. They live in huge herds that can number thousands of animals, and they move across the grasslands with the seasons. In the summer they travel to find fresh grass and water, and in the winter they move to places where the snow is not too deep to dig through for food.

When a herd of saigas runs, it is a wonderful sight. Hundreds of pale, sandy-coloured animals move together like a wave across the grass, their long legs carrying them swiftly over the ground. Saigas can run fast, and they can keep running for a long time, which helps them escape from wolves and other hunters. Their sandy coats blend in with the dry grass, so they are hard to spot from far away.

Saigas eat grasses, leaves and small plants that grow on the steppe. They are picky eaters in a way, choosing the plants that are easiest to digest and give them the most energy. In the dry season, when the grass is thin, they travel even farther to find enough to eat.

## Babies Born on the Open Grass

In the spring, female saigas give birth to their babies, called calves, out on the open grassland. The calves are born with pale, speckled coats that help them hide in the dry grass. For the first few days, a calf stays very still and quiet, lying flat in the grass while its mother goes off to feed. If danger comes, the mother runs back to protect it.

Saiga mothers are very caring. They visit their calves several times a day to feed them with rich milk, and they call to them with soft sounds. The calves grow quickly, and within a few weeks they are running with the herd, learning which plants to eat and how to keep up with the others. By the time they are a few months old, they are strong enough to travel long distances with the herd.

Saiga herds are some of the biggest gatherings of hoofed animals in the world. When thousands of saigas gather in one place, the ground seems to move. It is a sight that people travel from far away to see.

## Survivors from the Ice Age

The saiga is an ancient animal. Its kind has lived on Earth for thousands and thousands of years, and long ago saigas roamed across a much bigger area, from Europe all the way to North America, during the time when woolly mammoths still walked the Earth. The mammoths are gone now, but the saiga is still here, which makes it a true survivor.

But surviving has not been easy. Saigas have faced hard times. Their numbers fell very low because of hunting and because their grassland home was broken up by fences and farms. Then, in some years, sickness swept through the herds and many saigas died. People around the world were worried that this ancient animal might disappear forever.

The good news is that saigas are now making a comeback. Countries in Central Asia are protecting them, and rangers guard the herds from hunters. Scientists count the saigas every year to see how they are doing, and the numbers have been growing again. It is a hopeful story, and it shows what can happen when people work together to save an animal.

## Why Saigas Matter

Saigas are important to the grasslands they live in. As they travel and graze, they help keep the grass healthy, and their droppings feed the soil. They are also food for wolves and eagles, so they are part of the great chain of life on the steppe. Without saigas, the grasslands would be a lonelier and poorer place.

The saiga also teaches us something important: that an animal does not need to be big or famous to be special. With its funny nose, its speedy legs and its ancient history, the saiga is one of the most remarkable animals on Earth, and it deserves a safe home on the grasslands for thousands of years to come.

Next time you think of antelopes, remember the saiga, the funny-nosed survivor of the steppe, running free across the golden grass under a wide Central Asian sky.
