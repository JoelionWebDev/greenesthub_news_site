# Tuataras: The Living Fossils of New Zealand

If you ever meet a tuatara, you might think you have stepped back in time. It looks like a small dragon, with spiky scales along its back, strong legs, and a long tail. But the tuatara is not a dragon, and it is not a lizard either. It is a living fossil, an animal whose family has been on Earth for a very, very long time.

## A Very, Very Old Animal

Tuataras live in New Zealand, a country of islands far away in the Pacific Ocean. Long ago, when dinosaurs still walked the Earth, the tuatara's relatives lived in many parts of the world. Over time, most of them disappeared. But the tuatara survived in New Zealand, and it still lives there today.

That is why people call it a living fossil. Its body looks much like the bodies of its ancient relatives. While other animals changed and grew into new shapes over millions of years, the tuatara stayed almost the same. It is like a page from a very old storybook that is still being read today.

The tuatara is the only animal of its kind left in the whole world. There are no other animals quite like it. That makes it very special, and very precious.

## What Does a Tuatara Look Like?

A tuatara is about the size of a small lizard, but it is not a lizard. Its skin is rough and scaly, and its colours can be green, brown, or grey. It can change its colour a little, just like some lizards do, to match the place where it is sitting.

Along its back and tail runs a row of spiky scales. These spikes are bigger on male tuataras, and they stand up like a tiny dragon's crest. Its head is wide, its eyes are dark, and it has strong legs with sharp claws for digging and climbing.

One of the strangest things about the tuatara is that it has a third eye. Yes, you read that right! On top of its head, hidden under its skin, there is a small eye. It does not see pictures the way our eyes do. Scientists think it helps the tuatara tell day from night and know when the seasons change. It is a little like having a tiny clock on top of your head!

## Life in a Burrow

Tuataras live on small islands off the coast of New Zealand. They dig burrows in the ground, or they move into burrows left by seabirds. A burrow is a cosy underground home that keeps the tuatara safe and cool.

Tuataras like the cool weather. They are most active at night, when the air is cool and quiet. During the day, they rest in their burrows or bask in the sun for a little while. They do not like hot weather at all, and they can even be active on cold nights when other reptiles would be too sleepy to move.

## What Do Tuataras Eat?

Tuataras are hunters. They eat insects, spiders, snails, and other small animals. They also eat small birds and the eggs of seabirds when they can find them. They have strong jaws and sharp teeth that help them catch and eat their food.

Their teeth are unusual. A tuatara has two rows of teeth on its upper jaw and one row on its lower jaw. When it bites down, the lower teeth fit between the upper teeth, like the teeth of a saw. This helps it hold on to wriggly food.

## Growing Up Slowly

Tuataras live for a very long time. They can live for many decades, and some may live to be more than a hundred years old! That is longer than most people live. A tuatara grows very slowly, and it takes many years before it is fully grown.

When a mother tuatara is ready to lay eggs, she digs a hole in the ground and lays her eggs in it. Then she covers them with soil and leaves them to hatch on their own. The eggs take a long time to hatch, sometimes more than a year! The baby tuataras that come out are tiny, but they are ready to start life on their own.

The temperature of the ground decides whether the babies are boys or girls. If the ground is warmer, more boys hatch. If it is cooler, more girls hatch. That is a very special trick, and it shows how closely the tuatara is linked to the land where it lives.

## Why Tuataras Are in Danger

Long ago, tuataras lived all over New Zealand. But then new animals arrived on the islands, brought by people. Rats, cats, dogs, and other animals began to hunt tuataras and eat their eggs. The tuatara had never met animals like these before, and it did not know how to stay safe from them.

Because of this, tuataras disappeared from the main islands of New Zealand. They survived only on small islands where the new animals had not reached. That is why they are so rare today.

People in New Zealand are working hard to protect tuataras. They keep the small islands safe and free of rats and other hunters. They also breed tuataras in special places and move them to new safe islands, so they have more homes. Scientists study them to learn how to keep them safe for the future.

When you think of New Zealand, you might think of kiwis, the little birds that cannot fly, or of sheep and green hills. But do not forget the tuatara, the small dragon-like animal that has survived for millions of years. It is a reminder that some animals have been here far longer than we have, and that we should take care of them.

## Fun Facts to Remember

- Tuataras are living fossils. Their family has been on Earth since the time of the dinosaurs.
- They are not lizards. They belong to their very own group of animals.
- They have a third eye on top of their head.
- They can live for more than a hundred years.
- They are most active at night and like cool weather.
- They live only in New Zealand.
- Their eggs can take more than a year to hatch.

The tuatara is a small animal with a very big story. It has seen the world change for millions of years, and it is still here. That is something worth protecting!
