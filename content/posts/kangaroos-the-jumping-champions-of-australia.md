## What Is a Kangaroo?

A kangaroo is a very special animal that lives in Australia. You will know a kangaroo at once by its long back legs, its big feet, its strong tail, and the way it hops instead of walking! Kangaroos are marsupials, which means that mother kangaroos carry their tiny babies in a pouch on their belly, just like a pocket.

There are many kinds of kangaroos, and they come in different sizes. The biggest kind is the red kangaroo, which lives in the dry middle of Australia. Red kangaroos have reddish fur, and the males can be very tall when they stand up straight — taller than many grown-ups! Smaller kangaroos are called wallabies, and they look like small kangaroos with the same big feet and long tail.

A kangaroo has a small head, big ears that can turn to listen in all directions, and strong arms with little hands. Its back legs are much bigger than its front legs. A kangaroo's tail is long and thick, and it helps the kangaroo balance when it hops.

## Where Do Kangaroos Live?

Kangaroos live in Australia, and you can find them almost everywhere on that big island continent. They live in grasslands, open woodlands, deserts, and even near the edges of towns and farms. The wide open spaces of Australia, called the outback, are home to many kangaroos.

Red kangaroos love the dry, hot middle of Australia, while grey kangaroos live in greener parts with more trees, and wallabies like thick bushes where they can hide.

Kangaroos are very good at living in hot, dry places. They rest in the shade during the hottest part of the day and come out to eat in the cool morning and evening.

## How Do Kangaroos Hop?

Hopping is the kangaroo's superpower! A kangaroo moves by jumping on its two big back feet, with its tail swinging to help it balance. Its legs work like springs — when a kangaroo lands, its leg muscles and tendons stretch, and then they spring back and push it forward into the next jump. That is why hopping uses so little energy for a kangaroo.

Kangaroos can hop very fast when they need to, faster than a person can run. They can also jump very high and very far in one great leap. A big kangaroo can jump over a fence with ease!

Young joeys learn to hop by practising near their mothers. At first, their hops are small and wobbly, but soon they are bouncing along happily, just like the grown-ups.

## What Do Kangaroos Eat?

Kangaroos are plant eaters. They mostly eat grass, leaves, and shrubs. They like fresh green grass best, but they can also eat dry, tough grass when the weather is hot and there is not much fresh food around. Kangaroos also eat the leaves of bushes and small trees.

Kangaroos chew their food, swallow it, and then bring it back up to chew it again — a bit like cows do! This helps them digest the tough grass of the outback.

Kangaroos do not need to drink water every day like many animals do. They get much of the water they need from the plants they eat. But when there is a waterhole nearby, they will happily have a drink. Kangaroos can go for a long time without water, which is very useful in the dry Australian bush.

## A Kangaroo's Family Life

Kangaroos live in groups called mobs. A mob is a group of kangaroos that live, eat, and rest together. Mobs can be small, with just a few kangaroos, or they can be big, with many kangaroos all hopping around together. Living in a group helps kangaroos stay safe, because there are many eyes watching for danger.

A mother kangaroo's pouch is one of the most amazing things in nature. When a baby kangaroo, called a joey, is born, it is tiny — no bigger than a jellybean! It crawls into its mother's pouch, where it drinks milk and grows safe and warm. The joey stays in the pouch for many months, growing bigger and stronger every day.

As the joey grows, it starts to peek out of the pouch and look at the world. Then it begins to hop out for short trips, always staying close to its mother. If it gets scared, it dives back into the pouch in a flash! When the joey is too big to fit in the pouch anymore, it still stays close to its mother, following her around and learning where to find food and how to stay safe.

## Kangaroos and People

Kangaroos are a famous symbol of Australia — you can see them on money, sports teams, and aeroplanes! People from all over the world visit Australia hoping to see kangaroos hopping across the grasslands.

Kangaroos are wild animals, so people should always watch them from a safe distance. A kangaroo can give a strong kick with its big back feet if it feels scared.

Kangaroos are doing well in Australia, and there are many of them. But they still need their grasslands and bushland to stay healthy. When people protect the wild places where kangaroos live, the kangaroos can keep hopping, eating, and raising their joeys, just as they have done for a very long time. If you ever visit Australia, keep your eyes open — you might see a kangaroo family grazing in the early morning light!
