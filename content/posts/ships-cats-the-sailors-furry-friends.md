Imagine life on a ship hundreds of years ago. The sea stretches in every direction, the ship rocks and rolls, and the sailors will not see land for weeks or even months. It is hard, lonely work. But there is one small, furry friend on board who makes life better for everyone: the ship's cat.

For hundreds of years, cats have sailed on ships all over the world. They were not just pets — they were working animals, companions, and good-luck charms. Sailors loved their cats, gave them names, and told stories about them. Some ship's cats even became famous.

## Why Did Ships Need Cats?

Ships in the old days carried a lot of food: grain, biscuits, dried meat, and cheese to feed the crew on long voyages. But where there is food, there are mice and rats. Mice and rats would sneak on board in port, hide in the cargo, and eat the sailors' food. They would also chew through ropes, sails, and wooden boxes, and they could spread sickness.

That is where the cat came in. Cats are natural hunters, and they love to catch mice and rats. A good ship's cat could keep the food stores safe and protect the ship from damage. Without cats, a ship's food could be ruined, and the crew could go hungry on a long journey. So cats were not a luxury — they were an important part of the crew.

Cats were also good at catching mice in places where humans could not reach. They could squeeze into small spaces, climb into the cargo hold, and hunt in the dark. A cat could do a job that no sailor could do as well.

## A Cat's Life at Sea

Life at sea was not easy for a cat, but cats are clever and adaptable animals. They learned to walk on a rocking deck without falling over, to sleep in a cosy corner of the captain's cabin, and to find the warmest spot near the ship's stove. They learned the sounds of the ship — the creaking wood, the flapping sails, the waves against the hull — and they were not frightened by storms.

Ship's cats had their own little jobs and routines. In the morning, they might patrol the deck, checking for mice. In the afternoon, they might curl up in the sun for a nap. In the evening, they might sit with a sailor, purring softly as the sailor told stories. Sailors often shared their food with their cats, giving them bits of fish, meat, or milk.

Some cats even learned to climb the rigging — the ropes and ladders that hold up the sails. Sailors would watch in amazement as a cat climbed high up the mast, looked out at the sea, and then climbed down again.

## Friends in Lonely Times

Sailing in the old days could be very lonely. Sailors were away from their families for months at a time, and life on board could be hard and boring. A cat brought comfort and joy. Stroking a purring cat could make a sailor feel calm and happy. Playing with a kitten could make the whole crew laugh.

Cats also gave sailors something to care for. When you are far from home, having a small animal that depends on you can make you feel less alone. Sailors would talk to their cats, tell them their worries, and share their hopes of returning home. The cats, in their own way, listened — or at least, they purred and stayed close, which was just as good.

Many sailors believed that cats brought good luck. A cat on board was thought to protect the ship from bad weather and keep the crew safe. Some sailors even believed that cats could sense a storm coming before humans could, because cats would become restless or hide when the weather was about to change.

## Famous Ship's Cats

Some ship's cats became so famous that their stories are still told today. One of the most famous was a cat named Unsinkable Sam. Sam was a black and white cat who survived not one, not two, but three ships sinking during the Second World War. Each time his ship went down, Sam was rescued, floating on a piece of wood or found by another ship. After his third rescue, sailors called him Unsinkable Sam, and he became a legend. He lived the rest of his life on land, in a sailor's home, where he was loved and cared for.

Another famous ship's cat was Trim, a black and white cat who sailed with the explorer Matthew Flinders in the early eighteen hundreds. Trim sailed around Australia with Flinders, and Flinders loved him so much that he wrote a whole story about him. Trim was brave, clever, and loyal, and he became a hero to sailors and cat lovers alike. Today, there is a statue of Trim in Sydney, Australia, where people can remember him.

There was also a cat named Peebles, who sailed on a British warship during the Second World War. Peebles was known for being very clever — he even learned to shake hands with visitors to the ship.

## Cats on Ships Today

You might think that ship's cats are a thing of the past, now that ships have better ways to store food and keep out mice. And it is true that most modern navy ships do not keep cats anymore. But cats still appear on some ships, and they are still loved by sailors. In some navies, cats are kept as mascots, and in other places they live on fishing boats, where they still catch mice and keep the crew company.

## Why We Still Love Ship's Cats

Ship's cats remind us that animals and humans have always worked together, even in the most surprising places. On a ship in the middle of the ocean, a small cat could make a big difference — protecting food, catching mice, and lifting the spirits of the crew.

They also remind us that animals can be brave. A cat that lives on a ship faces storms, high waves, and long journeys, and it does it all with a calm, steady heart.

So the next time you see a cat, imagine it on the deck of a tall ship, with the wind in its whiskers and the sea all around. That cat is following in the pawsteps of hundreds of years of ship's cats — the sailors' furry friends.
