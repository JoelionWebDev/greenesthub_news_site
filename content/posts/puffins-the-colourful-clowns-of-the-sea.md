# Puffins: The Colourful Clowns of the Sea

If you ever see a puffin, you might think it is wearing a clown costume. It has a big, bright beak in shades of red, orange, and yellow, a white face, a black cap on its head, and orange feet. It waddles along on its short legs like a little sailor in a hurry. But do not be fooled by its funny looks: the puffin is also a champion swimmer and a brilliant flyer.

Puffins are seabirds that live in the cold northern seas, around the coasts of places like Iceland, Scotland, Norway, Canada, and Alaska. They spend most of their lives out at sea, and they only come to land to raise their babies on rocky cliffs and islands. Let us meet these colourful clowns of the sea.

## A Beak Like a Rainbow

The puffin's beak is its most famous feature. In the summer, when puffins come to land to breed, their beaks become bright and colourful, with stripes of red, orange, and yellow. Their feet turn bright orange too. In the winter, when they are out at sea, their beaks fade and become smaller and duller in colour. Then, when spring comes, the bright colours grow back again.

The puffin's beak is not just for looking pretty. It is a very useful tool. Puffins use their beak to catch fish, and they can carry lots of fish at once. They have a special spiky tongue that holds the fish in place, so they can keep catching more fish without dropping the ones they already have. Some puffins have been seen carrying many small fish in their beak at the same time, lined up like a row of tiny silver sticks.

There are three kinds of puffins in the world: the Atlantic puffin, the horned puffin, and the tufted puffin, which has long golden feathers on its head.

## Flying Underwater

Puffins are not just birds of the air; they are also birds of the sea. When a puffin dives into the water, it uses its wings to "fly" underwater, just as it flies in the air. Its wings beat fast, and it steers with its feet. Underwater, a puffin is quick and graceful, chasing small fish and catching them in its beak.

It eats small fish, like sand eels and sprats, and sometimes it eats tiny sea creatures too. When it catches fish for its baby, it carries them back to the nest in its beak.

In the air, puffins are fast flyers, but they are not the most graceful. They beat their wings very quickly, many times every second, and they look a bit clumsy when they take off and land. Sometimes they crash-land on the cliffs, tumbling over in a heap of feathers. But once they are in the air, they can fly fast over the waves, and they can travel long distances across the sea.

## Life on the Cliffs

In the spring and summer, puffins come to land to raise their families. They return to the same rocky islands and cliffs every year, often to the same burrow they used before. A burrow is a hole in the ground, a bit like a rabbit's burrow, where the puffin lays its egg. Some puffins dig their own burrows with their claws and beak, and others use cracks in the rocks.

Puffins usually mate for life, which means the same two puffins stay together year after year. They greet each other by rubbing their beaks together, which looks a bit like they are kissing. The female puffin lays one egg, and both parents take turns sitting on it to keep it warm until it hatches.

When the baby puffin, called a puffling, hatches, it is covered in soft, dark fluff. Its parents bring it fish every day, and it grows quickly. The puffling stays in the burrow for several weeks, safe from gulls and other hunters. When it is ready, it leaves the burrow at night and heads out to sea on its own. It will not come back to land for a few years, until it is old enough to raise a family of its own.

## The Long Winter at Sea

After the breeding season is over, puffins leave the cliffs and fly out to sea. They spend the winter months far from land, floating on the waves and diving for food. During this time, they lose their bright beak colours, and they look much plainer.

Puffins are tough birds. They can survive storms, cold winds, and huge waves in the open ocean. They rest on the water, tucking their head under their wing, and they ride the waves like little boats. When spring comes again, they fly back to the same cliffs and islands to start the whole cycle over again.

## Protecting the Puffins

Puffins are loved by people all over the world, but they face some problems. The fish they eat are becoming harder to find in some places, because the seas are getting warmer and because too many fish are being caught. Oil spills and plastic in the sea are also dangerous for puffins and other seabirds. On some islands, rats and other animals eat puffin eggs and babies.

People are working hard to protect puffins. On many islands, people have removed rats and other animals that hunt puffins. They also protect the cliffs where puffins breed, so that the birds can nest safely. Scientists count puffins every year to check how many there are and how healthy they are. By keeping the seas clean and full of fish, we can help puffins thrive for many years to come.

## Fun Facts About Puffins

- Puffins are sometimes called "sea parrots" because of their colourful beaks.
- A puffin can carry many small fish in its beak at once.
- Puffins "fly" underwater using their wings, just like they fly in the air.
- They beat their wings very fast when they fly.
- Puffins usually mate for life and return to the same burrow every year.
- A baby puffin is called a puffling.
- Puffins spend the winter far out at sea, away from land.

Puffins may look like clowns, with their bright beaks and funny waddles, but they are also brave, clever, and hard-working birds. They fly through the air, swim under the sea, and raise their babies on wild, rocky cliffs. Next time you see a picture of a puffin, remember: this little clown is also a champion of the sea.
