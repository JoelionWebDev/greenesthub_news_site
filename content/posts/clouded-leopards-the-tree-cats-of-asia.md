Deep in the rainforests of Asia, high up in the trees, lives one of the most beautiful and mysterious wild cats in the world. It is called the clouded leopard, and it is named for the big, cloud-shaped spots that cover its golden coat. It is not as famous as the lion or the tiger, but many people who study wild cats think it is one of the most amazing climbers of them all. High in the rainforests of Asia lives a cat with cloud-shaped spots and the longest canine teeth of any wild cat for its size.

## A Coat Like a Cloudy Sky

The first thing you notice about a clouded leopard is its coat. It is a soft golden-brown colour, covered with large dark spots that look like clouds drifting across the sky. Each clouded leopard has its own pattern of spots, just like each person has their own fingerprints. The spots help the cat hide among the leaves and shadows of the forest, so its prey cannot see it coming.

Clouded leopards are medium-sized cats, bigger than a house cat but smaller than a leopard. They have short legs, big paws and a very long tail that is almost as long as their whole body. The tail helps them balance when they walk along thin branches high in the trees, just like a tightrope walker uses a long pole to stay steady.

But the most surprising thing about the clouded leopard is its teeth. For its size, it has the longest canine teeth of any wild cat. Its fangs are so long that people sometimes call it the modern sabre-toothed tiger. Those long teeth help it catch and hold on to its prey.

## The Greatest Climbers

Clouded leopards are among the best climbers in the whole cat family. They can climb down a tree trunk headfirst, which is something very few cats can do. They can hang upside down from a branch by their back feet, and they can leap from tree to tree with great ease. Their ankles are so flexible that they can turn their back feet right around, which helps them grip the bark as they climb down.

They spend most of their lives in the trees, resting on branches during the day and hunting at night. When a clouded leopard wants to sleep, it may curl up on a branch and wrap its long tail around itself like a blanket. It looks very comfortable up there, high above the forest floor.

Because they are so good at climbing, clouded leopards can hunt animals that live in the trees, such as monkeys and birds, as well as animals on the ground, such as deer and wild pigs. They are quiet and patient hunters. They move slowly through the branches, then pounce with a sudden burst of speed.

## A Shy and Secret Cat

Clouded leopards are very shy animals, and they are rarely seen by people. They live in the thick rainforests of countries such as Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Nepal, where the trees grow close together and the forest is dark and green. Even scientists who study them find them hard to follow, because they move silently and hide so well.

For a long time, people knew very little about how clouded leopards live. Scientists have learned more in recent years by using cameras hidden in the forest that take pictures when an animal walks past. These camera traps have shown clouded leopards walking along forest paths at night, climbing trees and carrying food to their cubs.

Young clouded leopards stay with their mother for many months, learning how to climb and hunt. The mother teaches them by example, and the cubs practise their climbing skills on low branches before they are brave enough to go high. When they are old enough, they leave to find their own part of the forest.

## The Senses of a Hunter

Clouded leopards have wonderful senses that help them hunt in the dark forest. Their eyes are large and sharp, and they can see very well in the dim light under the trees. Their ears can turn to catch the smallest sounds, such as a bird moving in the leaves or a small animal stepping on a dry twig. They also have a very good sense of smell, which helps them find prey and avoid danger.

Their whiskers are long and sensitive, and they help the cat feel its way through thick branches in the dark. When a clouded leopard is hunting, it uses all of its senses together — eyes, ears, nose and whiskers — to build a picture of the forest around it. This is why it is such a successful hunter, even though it is smaller than many other wild cats.

## Why the Clouded Leopard Needs Our Help

Clouded leopards need large areas of rainforest to live in, because each cat needs plenty of space to hunt and roam. But rainforests in Asia are being cut down for farming and building, and the forest is getting smaller every year. When the forest shrinks, clouded leopards lose their homes and their food.

People are working hard to protect these beautiful cats. Parks and reserves keep parts of the forest safe, and scientists study the cats to learn how best to help them. Some clouded leopards also live in zoos, where people can see them up close and learn about them, and where young cubs are born to help keep the species going.

## A Cat Worth Knowing

The clouded leopard reminds us that there are still wonderful animals in the world that most people have never heard of. It is not the biggest cat, or the loudest, or the most famous, but it is one of the most graceful. With its cloudy coat, its long tail and its amazing climbing skills, it is perfectly made for life in the trees.

Next time you think of wild cats, remember the clouded leopard, the quiet tree cat of Asia, moving like a shadow through the rainforest canopy. It is a gentle reminder that the wild world is full of surprises, waiting for curious young explorers like you to discover them.
