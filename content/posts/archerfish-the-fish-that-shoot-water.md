# Archerfish: The Fish That Shoot Water

Imagine a fish that can shoot like an archer. The archerfish does just that! It swims just under the surface of the water, looks up at an insect sitting on a leaf, and then, with a quick spit, it shoots a jet of water at the insect. The insect falls into the water, and the archerfish gobbles it up. It is one of the cleverest hunters in the water world.

## A Fish With a Superpower

The archerfish is a small fish, about the size of your hand. It lives in the warm waters of Asia and Australia, in rivers, lakes, and mangrove forests. It has a silvery body with dark spots, and a mouth that points up, perfect for shooting water at things above.

How does it shoot? The archerfish has a special groove in the roof of its mouth. It presses its tongue against this groove to make a little tube, like a tiny water pistol. Then it squeezes its gills shut and pushes water through the tube. The water comes out in a fast, strong jet that can hit an insect sitting on a leaf above the water.

The archerfish can shoot water quite far, much farther than you might think for such a small fish. It can hit an insect that is sitting high above the water, on a branch or a leaf. And it is very good at it! It can hit its target again and again, with amazing aim.

## Learning to Aim

Archerfish are not born perfect shots. Young archerfish have to practise. When they are small, they try to shoot at things above the water, and they often miss. But as they grow, they get better and better. They watch other archerfish and learn from them, just like you might learn a new game by watching your friends play.

Scientists have found that archerfish are very clever. They can learn to hit moving targets, and they can even judge how far away a target is. They look at the insect, think about where it is, and then shoot. It is a bit like playing darts, but underwater!

## What Do Archerfish Eat?

Archerfish eat insects, spiders, and other small animals that live on plants above the water. They love to eat flies, ants, and other bugs that sit on leaves and branches. When an insect falls into the water, the archerfish is ready to catch it.

But archerfish do not only eat insects from above. They also eat small animals that live in the water, like tiny shrimp and other small creatures. They are not fussy eaters. They will eat whatever they can find.

When an archerfish shoots an insect down, it has to be quick. Other fish might try to steal the insect before it can get to it. So the archerfish swims fast to catch its prize. It is a race, and the archerfish is usually the winner!

## Life in the Mangroves

Archerfish live in many places, but they love mangrove forests. Mangroves are trees that grow in salty water along the coast. Their roots stick out of the water, and their branches hang low over the surface. This is a perfect place for archerfish, because there are lots of insects living on the branches and leaves.

Archerfish also live in rivers and lakes. They like warm, shallow water where they can see up to the plants above. They often swim in small groups, and they work together to find food. When one fish finds an insect, the others watch and wait for their turn.

## How Do Archerfish See?

You might wonder how a fish can see an insect above the water. After all, water and air bend light in different ways, which can make things look like they are in a different place than they really are. But the archerfish has learned to deal with this.

The archerfish has big eyes that can see above the water. It has learned how light bends when it moves from air into water, and it can work out where the insect really is. It is like a little scientist, doing sums in its head every time it takes a shot!

Scientists have studied archerfish for many years, and they are still amazed by how clever they are. The archerfish can even tell the difference between different kinds of insects, and it knows which ones are good to eat. It is a smart little fish with a big brain for its size.

## Why Archerfish Are Special

Archerfish are special because they use tools. Well, not tools like a hammer or a spoon, but they use water as a tool to catch food. Very few animals can do this. It is a clever trick that helps them find food in places where other fish cannot.

They are also special because they show us how clever fish can be. For a long time, people thought fish were not very smart. But the archerfish proves that fish can learn, practise, and solve problems, just like other animals.

Archerfish need clean water and healthy mangrove forests to live in. But in some places, mangrove forests are being cut down to make room for farms and buildings. When the mangroves disappear, the archerfish lose their homes and their food.

Pollution is also a problem. When dirty water flows into rivers and lakes, it can make the fish sick. Archerfish need clean water to see their targets and to stay healthy.

People are working to protect mangrove forests and keep rivers clean. By looking after these places, we help archerfish and many other animals that live there. Every animal, from the smallest fish to the biggest whale, needs a healthy home.

## Fun Facts to Remember

- The archerfish shoots jets of water to knock insects off leaves and into the water.
- It has a special groove in its mouth that works like a tiny water pistol.
- Young archerfish have to practise to become good shots.
- It can hit insects sitting high above the water, and it can even judge how far away they are.
- It lives in warm rivers, lakes, and mangrove forests in Asia and Australia.
- It is one of the few animals that uses water as a tool to catch food.
- It can also jump out of the water to catch insects that are sitting low.

Next time you are near water, look up at the leaves above. You might just spot a clever little fish taking aim!
