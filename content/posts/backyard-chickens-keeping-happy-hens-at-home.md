Cluck, cluck! Have you ever heard a hen chatting in a garden? Backyard chickens are hens that live in a garden or a small yard, cared for by a family. They scratch in the soil, peck at seeds, lay fresh eggs, and make funny clucking sounds all day long. Keeping chickens at home is fun, and it teaches children where food comes from and how to care for animals.

Long ago, many families kept chickens in their gardens. Today, lots of families are keeping them again, because chickens are friendly, useful, and full of character. Each hen has its own personality. Some are bold and curious, some are shy and quiet, and some love to follow people around the garden, hoping for a tasty treat.

## Meet the Happy Hen

A hen is a female chicken, and she is a wonderful garden friend. Hens have soft feathers, a red comb on top of their heads, and bright, watchful eyes. They come in many colours, from white and brown to black and speckled. Some hens have fluffy feathers on their feet, and some have feathers that shine in the sun like silk.

Hens love to scratch the ground with their feet, looking for seeds, bugs, and tasty bits to eat. They peck at the soil, turn over leaves, and dig little holes to dust-bathe in. A dust bath is when a hen lies down in dry soil and fluffs it through her feathers. It helps keep her feathers clean and free of tiny pests.

Hens are also chatty birds. They cluck softly when they are happy, and they make a louder sound when they lay an egg. They call to each other when they find food, and they warn each other when they see something strange.

## A Cosy Home for Hens

Hens need a safe and cosy home, called a coop. A coop is a small house where hens sleep at night and lay their eggs. Inside the coop, there are nesting boxes filled with soft straw, where a hen can lay her egg in peace. There are also perches, which are bars where hens sit and sleep, tucked up high and safe.

During the day, hens need space to roam. Many families give their hens a run, which is a fenced area in the garden where hens can scratch, peck, and enjoy the sunshine. Hens love to explore, so a bigger run makes them happier. They need shade on hot days, shelter from rain, and fresh water to drink all day long.

Keeping hens safe is very important. At night, the coop must be closed, so that foxes and other animals cannot get in. During the day, a fence helps keep hens safe, and it also keeps them from wandering into places they should not go, like a vegetable garden full of tasty young plants. A safe hen is a happy hen.

## What Hens Eat

Hens are not fussy eaters, but they need good food to stay healthy and lay eggs. Most hens eat special chicken feed, which gives them all the food they need. They also love to eat greens, like lettuce and cabbage, and tasty treats like corn, grains, and fruit. Children can help by giving hens small treats, but treats should only be a small part of what a hen eats.

Hens also need grit, which is made of tiny stones. Hens do not have teeth, so they swallow small stones that help them grind up their food inside their bodies. Hens also need a source of calcium, which helps them make strong eggshells.

Fresh, clean water is just as important as food. Hens drink water many times a day, especially on hot days. Their water should be changed often, so it stays clean and fresh. A hen that has clean water and good food will be healthy, active, and full of energy.

## The Magic of Fresh Eggs

One of the best things about keeping hens is collecting fresh eggs. A hen usually lays an egg in the morning, in her nesting box. Children love to lift the lid of the nesting box and find a warm, smooth egg waiting there. It feels like finding treasure. The eggs may be white, brown, or even a soft blue-green colour, depending on the kind of hen.

Fresh eggs are delicious, and they can be used to make many tasty foods, like scrambled eggs, omelettes, and cakes. Collecting eggs also teaches children where food comes from. Instead of only seeing eggs in a shop, they see the hen that laid the egg, and they learn that food comes from living animals that need care.

Hens do not lay an egg every single day, and that is normal. A hen lays eggs when she is healthy, happy, and has enough daylight. In winter, when the days are shorter, hens may lay fewer eggs.

## Caring for Hens Every Day

Keeping hens is a job that needs doing every day. Hens need fresh food and water in the morning, and their coop needs to be checked and cleaned often. Children can help by filling the water bowl, scattering feed, and collecting eggs. They can also help by watching the hens and telling a grown-up if a hen looks sad, tired, or unwell.

Hens need gentle handling. When you pick up a hen, you should hold her carefully, with both hands, and keep her close to your body. Move slowly and speak softly, so she does not get scared. A hen that is handled gently will learn to trust you, and she may even sit on your lap while you stroke her soft feathers.

Hens also need a visit from the vet sometimes, just like other pets. The vet can check that the hens are healthy and give advice on how to keep them well. A healthy hen is active, bright-eyed, and full of clucks.

Backyard hens bring joy, fresh eggs, and lots of laughter to a home. They teach children to care for animals, to be patient, and to enjoy the simple things in life, like the sound of clucking in the morning. If you ever visit a family with hens, watch them closely.
