Have you ever seen a chicken that looks like a fluffy cloud? That is a Silkie chicken. Silkies are one of the most unusual and loveliest chickens in the world. Their feathers are soft and silky, like fur, and they come in colours like white, black, buff and grey. They have fluffy feet, a puffy crest on their heads, and even black skin and bones, which surprises everyone who sees them for the first time. Children love Silkies because they are gentle, friendly and look like little pom-poms with beaks.

## Feathers Like Silk

Most chickens have feathers that are smooth and flat, with a strong central shaft that holds each feather in shape. Silkie feathers are different. They have no strong shaft, so the soft parts of the feather hang loose and fluffy, like strands of silk or fur. That is how they got their name. When you pick up a Silkie, it feels like holding a soft toy, not a bird at all.

Because their feathers are so loose and fluffy, Silkies cannot fly well. Their wings do not work like other chickens' wings, so they stay on the ground. They also get wet more easily in the rain, because their feathers do not keep the water out the way normal feathers do. That means Silkie owners need to make sure their birds have a dry, cosy shelter.

Silkies come in many colours. The most famous is white, which makes them look like little snowballs. But you can also find black, blue, grey, buff and reddish-brown Silkies. No matter what colour they are, they all have the same soft, fluffy look.

## A Chicken with Surprises

Silkies have some surprises hiding under all that fluff. First, they have black skin, black bones and dark meat. This might sound strange, but it is completely natural for them, and in some parts of Asia, Silkie chickens are kept as a special traditional food, believed to be good for health. Most people who keep Silkies as pets just enjoy them for their looks and their sweet nature.

Second, Silkies have five toes on each foot, while most chickens have only four. If you look closely at a Silkie's fluffy feet, you can count the extra toe. It is one of the easiest ways to tell a real Silkie from other fluffy chickens.

Third, Silkies have a crest of feathers on top of their heads, like a little pom-pom hat. This crest can grow so big that it flops over their eyes, and the chicken has to peep out from underneath it. Some owners gently trim the crest so their Silkies can see better.

## The Friendliest Chickens Around

Silkies are famous for their gentle, calm nature. They are one of the friendliest breeds of chicken, and they love being held and stroked. Many children keep Silkies as pets because they are so tame. A Silkie will often sit happily in your lap and fall asleep while you stroke its soft feathers.

Silkies are also wonderful mothers. A Silkie hen loves to sit on eggs and hatch them, and she will look after her chicks with great care. In fact, Silkie hens are so good at being mothers that farmers sometimes use them to hatch the eggs of other kinds of birds, like ducks and quails. The Silkie hen does not mind at all — she just loves being a mum.

Because they are so calm, Silkies are also popular at petting zoos and school farms, where children can meet them up close. If you ever get the chance to hold a Silkie, you will understand why everyone falls in love with them.

## Where Did Silkies Come From?

Silkies have a long history. They come from China, where they have been kept for hundreds of years. The famous traveller Marco Polo wrote about fluffy chickens with fur-like feathers when he visited China long ago, and many people believe he was writing about Silkies. From China, Silkies travelled along trade routes to other parts of Asia and then to Europe and the rest of the world.

In China, Silkies were kept not just as pets but as special birds, and their unusual black skin made them famous. Today, Silkies are kept all over the world, mostly as pets and show birds. People enter their Silkies in poultry shows, where judges look at their fluffiness, their colour and their shape.

## Looking After a Silkie

Keeping Silkies is a joy, but they need a little extra care. Because they cannot fly, they need a safe run on the ground, protected from animals that might hurt them. Their coop should be dry and warm, with soft bedding to sleep on. In wet weather, they need to stay under cover, so their fluffy feathers do not get soaked.

Silkies eat the same food as other chickens: a mix of grains, pellets, and greens, with fresh water every day. They also love treats like corn, lettuce and mealworms. Like all chickens, they love to scratch in the dirt and take dust baths, rolling around in dry soil to keep their feathers clean. It is very funny to watch a Silkie taking a dust bath — it looks like a little cloud rolling in the dirt!

Silkie hens lay small eggs, fewer than some other chickens. They lay cream-coloured eggs, and they are not the best layers. But nobody keeps Silkies for their eggs. People keep them because they are beautiful, gentle and full of character.
