High in the mountains of Asia, yak herders live and work with shaggy yaks, the strong animals that carry loads and give milk, wool and warmth. In places like Tibet, Nepal and Mongolia, where the air is thin and the winters are very cold, the yak is one of the most important animals in people's lives. Without the yak, life in the high mountains would be almost impossible.

## Life at the Top of the World

Yak herders live in some of the highest places on Earth. Their homes are in the mountains of the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau, where the land is rocky, the winds are strong and the winters are long and freezing. Many herders live in tents made from yak hair, which are warm and strong, and they move with their herds from place to place, following the grass as the seasons change.

In summer, the herders take their yaks up to high mountain pastures, where the grass is fresh and green. In winter, they bring them down to lower valleys, where it is a little warmer and there is shelter from the snow. This way of life, moving with the animals, is called nomadic herding, and families have lived this way for hundreds of years.

Children in herder families learn to look after yaks from a young age. They help to milk the yaks, to watch over the young calves, and to lead the herd to new pastures. It is a hard life, but it is also a life full of fresh air, wide open spaces and a close bond with animals.

## The Amazing Yak

Yaks are wonderful animals, perfectly made for life in the high mountains. They have long, shaggy hair that hangs down like a skirt, keeping them warm in the freezing cold. Their bodies are strong and sturdy, and they can carry heavy loads up steep mountain paths without tiring.

Yaks have large lungs and big hearts, which help them breathe in the thin mountain air where there is less oxygen. They can live at heights where most other animals would struggle to breathe. Their wide hooves help them walk on rocky ground and through snow.

Yaks give their herders many useful things. Their milk is rich and creamy, and it is used to make butter, cheese and a special drink called yak butter tea. Their wool is soft and warm, and it is made into clothes, blankets and tents. Their dung, when dried, is used as fuel for cooking fires, because there are few trees in the high mountains to burn as wood.

## Working Together

Herders and yaks work together as a team. The yaks carry loads of food, tents and belongings when the family moves to a new pasture. They are strong and steady, and they can walk for hours along mountain paths, carrying heavy packs on their backs.

The herders look after the yaks in return. They protect them from wolves and other dangers, they make sure they have enough grass to eat, and they help them when they are ill or injured. The bond between a herder and their yaks is strong, and it is built on years of living and working together.

Yaks are also used in festivals and celebrations. In some places, there are yak races, where herders ride their yaks and race each other, cheered on by the crowd. Yaks are also decorated with colourful cloths and ribbons for special occasions, showing how much they are valued.

## The Herders' Daily Life

A yak herder's day starts early. They wake up before sunrise, milk the yaks, and make breakfast. Then they take the herd out to graze, walking with them across the mountain pastures. They watch over the animals all day, making sure they do not wander too far or get into danger.

In the evening, the herders bring the yaks back to camp, milk them again, and make supper. They may sit around the fire, drinking yak butter tea and telling stories, while the yaks rest nearby. It is a simple life, but it is a life that is close to nature and full of meaning.

Herders also make cheese and butter from yak milk, which they sell or trade for other things they need, such as rice, tea and clothes. Yak wool is spun into yarn and woven into clothes, blankets and ropes. Nothing from the yak is wasted.

## Why Yak Herders Matter

Yak herders are keepers of an old and important way of life. They know how to live in the high mountains, how to look after animals in a harsh land, and how to use everything the yak gives them wisely. Their knowledge has been passed down from parents to children for many generations.

Today, life is changing for yak herders. Some young people are moving to towns and cities, and fewer families are living the nomadic life. But many herders are still proud of their way of life, and they are working to keep it alive, sharing their culture with visitors and teaching others about the yak.

## Friends in the High Mountains

The story of the yak herder and the yak is a story of friendship and teamwork in one of the toughest places on Earth. Together, they survive the cold, the wind and the thin air, and they build a life that is simple, strong and full of heart.

So next time you think of the mountains, remember the shaggy yaks and the people who live with them, high above the clouds. They are a reminder that with the right animal friend, people can live and thrive almost anywhere in the world.
