Far to the north, where the ground is covered in snow for most of the year and the wind is icy cold, lives a small, fluffy animal with a very clever trick. The Arctic fox can change the colour of its coat with the seasons. In winter, it turns white as snow. In summer, it turns brown and grey like the rocks and plants of the tundra. It is a master of disguise, and one of the toughest little animals on Earth.

Arctic foxes live in the Arctic, the cold land around the North Pole. It is one of the coldest places on our planet, but these little foxes are perfectly built for it. With their thick fur, furry feet and clever ways of finding food, they survive where many other animals could not. Let us find out how they do it.

## A Coat That Changes Colour

The Arctic fox's most famous trick is its changing coat. In the winter, when snow covers everything, the fox's fur turns bright white. This helps it hide from hungry animals that might want to eat it, and it also helps the fox sneak up on its own prey without being seen. A white fox on white snow is almost invisible.

When spring comes and the snow melts, the fox's coat changes again. It turns brown, grey or even a little blue-grey, to match the rocks, earth and plants of the summer tundra. This seasonal change is called moulting, when the old fur falls out and new fur grows in. The fox sheds its thick winter coat and grows a thinner summer coat.

## Built for the Cold

The Arctic fox is a small animal, about the size of a pet cat, but it is built like a champion of the cold. Its fur is thick and soft, covering its whole body — even the bottoms of its feet. The furry feet work like snow boots, keeping the fox's paws warm and helping it walk on the snow without slipping.

## What Do Arctic Foxes Eat?

Finding food in the Arctic is not easy. In the winter, snow covers the ground, and many animals leave or hide away. But the Arctic fox is not fussy. It will eat almost anything it can find, and that is one of the secrets of its survival.

Its favourite food is the lemming, a small mouse-like animal that lives under the snow. Arctic foxes also eat voles, birds, eggs, fish, and berries when they can find them. In the summer, they eat insects and plants too. They are clever hunters and patient watchers, and they use their sharp hearing to find food hidden under the snow.

Arctic foxes are also scavengers, which means they eat food left behind by other animals. When a polar bear catches a seal and leaves some of it behind, the Arctic fox may come along and eat what is left. In this way, nothing goes to waste, and the fox gets a good meal.

## Clever Hunters in the Snow

Watching an Arctic fox hunt is one of the most delightful sights in the animal kingdom. The fox listens carefully, tilting its head from side to side, using its excellent hearing to find a lemming moving under the snow. When it hears the tiny animal, the fox leaps high into the air and dives headfirst into the snow, paws first, to catch its prey. It looks like a fox doing a somersault in the snow!

This special hunting jump works because the fox can hear the lemming's tiny movements through the snow. Its ears are sharp, and its brain can work out exactly where the lemming is hiding. The fox's leap is so well aimed that it often catches its prey on the first try.

## Family Life in a Den

Arctic foxes live in family groups, and they raise their pups in dens. A den is a burrow dug into the ground, often on a hillside or in a sandy bank. Some dens are very old and have been used by fox families for many years. A good den has many entrances and rooms, like a little underground house.

In the spring, the mother fox gives birth to her pups in the den. The pups are tiny, dark and fluffy when they are born, and they stay safe inside the den while they are small. Both parents help to look after them. The father brings food to the mother, and both parents guard the den from danger.

When the pups are a few weeks old, they come out of the den to play. They tumble and chase each other in the sunshine, practising the skills they will need as grown-ups. They learn to hunt by playing, and they learn to dig by copying their parents. By the autumn, the young foxes are ready to leave home and find their own territories.

## Fun Facts About Arctic Foxes

- Arctic foxes change their coat colour with the seasons — white in winter, brown in summer.
- Their furry feet work like snow boots, keeping their paws warm and helping them grip the snow.
- An Arctic fox can hear a lemming moving under the snow and leap high to catch it.
- They curl up in a ball and wrap their bushy tail around themselves like a blanket to stay warm.
- Arctic foxes store extra food by burying it and can find it again under deep snow.
- Some Arctic foxes have a blue-grey coat all year round.
- They can survive in temperatures far below freezing.

## Surviving in a Changing World

Arctic foxes are tough little survivors, but their icy home is changing. As the world warms up, the snow melts earlier, and the seasons are changing. This can make it harder for the white winter coat to match the snowy ground. The foxes also face competition from red foxes, which are bigger and are moving further north as the Arctic warms.

The Arctic fox teaches us an important lesson: even a small animal can be incredibly tough when it is perfectly made for its home. With its fluffy coat, furry feet and clever hunting, the Arctic fox is a true survivor of the snow — and one of the most charming animals in the frozen north.
