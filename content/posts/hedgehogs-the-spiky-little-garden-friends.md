Hedgehogs are small, round, and very cute animals with a prickly coat of spines. They have a pointed little nose, bright black eyes, and tiny ears, and they waddle around gardens and woods at night, snuffling and sniffing as they look for food. Hedgehogs are shy and gentle, and when they feel scared they curl up into a tight, spiky ball so nothing can hurt them. They are one of the best friends a garden can have, because they eat slugs, beetles, and other small creatures that munch on plants.

## What is a hedgehog?

A hedgehog is a small mammal, about the size of a large orange or a small melon. Its back and sides are covered in thousands of short, sharp spines, which are really hairs that have grown hard and pointy. Its face, belly, and legs are covered in soft brown fur. Hedgehogs have a long, pointy nose that twitches as they sniff the ground, and they have small, strong legs for walking and digging. When a hedgehog feels scared, it pulls its head and legs in and rolls into a ball. The spines stick out in every direction, and most animals decide to leave it alone. Hedgehogs are not related to porcupines, even though they both have spines.

## Where do hedgehogs live?

Hedgehogs live in Europe, Asia, and Africa, and they love places with lots of cover, like gardens, parks, hedges, woods, and fields. They make their homes in piles of leaves, under bushes, in log piles, and in quiet corners of gardens. A hedgehog's home is called a nest, and it is made of dry leaves and grass. They like gardens because gardens have lots of food and lots of good hiding places. In the colder months, many hedgehogs sleep for a long time in a deep sleep called hibernation, curled up in their nest of leaves until the warm weather comes back again.

## What do hedgehogs eat?

Hedgehogs eat lots of small creatures that live on the ground. Their favourite foods are beetles, caterpillars, worms, slugs, and snails. They also eat other insects, and sometimes they eat fallen fruit and berries. A hedgehog uses its wonderful nose to sniff out food in the dark. It snuffles along the ground, pushing its nose into leaves and soil to find tasty insects hiding underneath. Hedgehogs have strong teeth for crunching up beetles and snails, and they can eat a lot in one night. Gardeners love hedgehogs because they eat slugs and snails that would otherwise munch on flowers and vegetables. A hedgehog is like a tiny night-time gardener, helping to keep the garden healthy while it has its dinner.

## A night in a hedgehog's life

Hedgehogs are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. During the day, a hedgehog sleeps curled up in its cosy nest of leaves, hidden away from the world. When the sun goes down, it wakes up and starts to explore. It waddles through the garden, sniffing and snuffling, stopping to munch on a beetle or a worm. Hedgehogs can walk a long way in one night, visiting many gardens as they look for food. If a hedgehog meets a dog or a fox, it curls into a spiky ball and waits until the danger goes away.

## How hedgehogs stay safe

A hedgehog's spines are its best way of staying safe, but it has other clever tricks too. Its brown and cream colours help it blend in with dry leaves and soil, so it is hard to spot in the dark. It moves quietly, and its excellent sense of smell helps it find food and sense danger before it gets too close. When a hedgehog curls into a ball, it pulls its skin tight over its head and legs, and the spines point outwards in every direction. Hedgehogs also have a funny habit called self-anointing. When they find a strong new smell, they lick it and spread the frothy spit onto their spines.

## Baby hedgehogs

Baby hedgehogs are called hoglets, and they are born in the summer in a warm nest of leaves. A mother hedgehog usually has a small family of hoglets, and they are born with their eyes closed and their tiny spines hidden under soft skin. At first the spines are soft and white, but they soon grow hard and dark. The mother looks after her hoglets, feeding them milk and keeping them warm in the nest. After a few weeks, the hoglets open their eyes and start to explore. They follow their mother on short trips outside the nest, learning how to find food and how to curl into a ball. By the time they are old enough to live on their own, they are small copies of their mother, with a full coat of spines and a very good nose.

## Fun facts about hedgehogs

- A hedgehog has thousands of spines on its back, and each spine is a hair that has grown hard and pointy.
- Hedgehogs are nocturnal, which means they sleep during the day and wake up at night to look for food.
- When a hedgehog feels scared, it rolls into a tight, spiky ball that most animals will not touch.
- Hedgehogs have a wonderful sense of smell, and they snuffle along the ground to find beetles, worms, and slugs.
- In cold weather, many hedgehogs sleep for a long time in a deep sleep called hibernation.

Hedgehogs are small, shy, and very helpful garden friends. They ask for very little, just a quiet garden, a pile of leaves, and plenty of insects to eat, and in return they keep the garden tidy and full of life. If you ever spot a little spiky ball waddling through the garden at night, watch it quietly from a distance and let it carry on with its important work. The hedgehog is a reminder that even the smallest animals can be brave, clever, and full of charm.
