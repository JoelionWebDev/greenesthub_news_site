Picture a golden cat with black spots, very long legs, and ears so big they look like they belong to a much larger animal. Now picture it standing in tall grass, listening carefully, and then leaping high into the air like a dancer. That is a serval, one of the most graceful hunters in Africa.

Servals live in the grasslands and wetlands of Africa. They are wild cats, but they are not as big as lions or leopards. They are medium-sized cats with a special talent: they can hear and catch animals hiding deep in the grass. Let us find out how they do it.

## Meet the Serval

A serval has a beautiful golden coat covered in black spots and stripes. No two servals have exactly the same pattern of spots, just like no two people have exactly the same fingerprints. The spots help the serval blend in with the dappled light and shadows of the tall grass.

The most striking thing about a serval is its legs. Servals have the longest legs of any cat compared to the size of their body. Their long legs help them see over tall grass and leap high into the air. Their neck is long too, which helps them look around like a meerkat on lookout duty.

Servals also have very large ears. Their ears can turn and swivel to pick up the smallest sounds. A serval can hear a tiny mouse moving in the grass from far away, even when it cannot see it. That super hearing is the secret to how servals hunt.

## Where Do Servals Live?

Servals live in many parts of Africa, south of the Sahara Desert. They love grasslands, especially places near water, such as wetlands, reed beds, and the edges of rivers and lakes. These wet, grassy places are full of the small animals that servals love to eat.

A serval needs tall grass to hide in and plenty of open space to hunt. It usually lives alone and has its own home area, which it marks with scent. Servals are shy and careful animals. They are most active in the early morning and in the evening, when the light is soft and many small animals are out and about.

During the heat of the day, a serval may rest in the shade of tall grass or bushes. When the sun goes down, it wakes up and starts its evening hunt.

## What Do Servals Eat?

Servals mostly eat small animals. Their favourite foods are rodents such as rats and mice, but they also catch birds, frogs, lizards, and insects. They are not fussy eaters, and they will hunt whatever small animals are living in the grass around them.

A serval hunts in a clever way. First, it stands very still in the grass, listening with its big ears. When it hears a small animal moving underground or in the grass, it fixes its position with great care. Then, it leaps straight up into the air and comes down with its front paws on the animal, pinning it to the ground. It is a bit like playing a game of leapfrog, except the serval always knows where to land.

Servals are very good at this. They catch their prey more often than many other wild cats do. Their long legs help them jump high, and their excellent hearing helps them know exactly where to jump.

## The Amazing Leap

The serval's leap is one of the most amazing sights in the animal world. A serval can jump high into the air from a standing start, using its powerful back legs. As it leaps, it tucks its front paws together and comes down right on top of its prey.

Sometimes, a serval will leap up to catch a bird that is flying low over the grass. It can snatch a bird right out of the air with its paws. That takes wonderful timing and great eyesight.

Young servals learn to leap by playing. They pounce on leaves, chase each other through the grass, and practise jumping on anything that moves. Play is not just fun for serval kittens. It is how they learn the skills they need to survive.

## Serval Kittens

Serval mothers give birth to kittens in a safe, hidden place, such as a thick patch of grass or an old burrow. The kittens are small and helpless at first, with their eyes closed. Their mother feeds them and keeps them warm and safe.

As the kittens grow, they start to explore the grass around their den. They play together, wrestling and chasing, and their mother brings them food. She also teaches them how to hunt by bringing them small animals to practise on. Slowly, the kittens learn to listen, stalk, and pounce, just like their mother.

After many months, the young servals are ready to leave their mother and find their own home area. They are graceful, clever hunters by then, ready to live on their own in the grasslands.

## Staying Safe in the Grasslands

Life in the grasslands is not always easy for a serval. Bigger animals such as leopards and hyenas can be dangerous, and eagles may try to catch young servals. The serval stays safe by hiding in tall grass, using its spotted coat as camouflage, and being quick and alert.

People can also be a danger to servals. When grasslands are turned into farms or towns, servals lose the places they need to live and hunt. Wetlands are sometimes drained, which takes away the serval's favourite hunting grounds.

The good news is that servals are protected in many parts of Africa, and there are parks and reserves where they can live safely. Scientists study servals to learn more about them and to help protect the grasslands they call home.

## Why Servals Matter

Servals matter because they help keep the grasslands healthy. By hunting rodents, they stop those small animals from eating too much grass and crops. They are also a sign of a healthy wetland. When servals are doing well, it usually means the grasslands and wetlands have enough food, water, and space for wildlife.

Servals remind us that you do not have to be the biggest animal to be a great hunter. With long legs, big ears, and a lot of patience, a serval can catch dinner hiding deep in the grass. Next time you see tall grass swaying in the wind, imagine a spotted serval standing still inside it, listening carefully, and getting ready to leap.
