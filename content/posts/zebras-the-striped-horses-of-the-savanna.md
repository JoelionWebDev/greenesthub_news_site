## What Is a Zebra?

A zebra is an animal that looks like a horse wearing black and white stripes! Zebras are cousins of horses and donkeys, but they are wild animals that live free on the grasslands of Africa.

A zebra's stripes are its most famous feature. Every zebra's stripe pattern is different — no two zebras have exactly the same stripes, just like no two people have the same fingerprints.

Their ears are big and pointy, and they can turn them in different directions to listen for sounds. Zebras have strong legs and hard hooves, which help them run fast across the grasslands. A zebra's eyes are on the sides of its head, so it can see danger coming from many directions at once.

## Where Do Zebras Live?

Zebras live in Africa, south of the great Sahara Desert. They make their homes on the savanna — wide, open grasslands with a few trees here and there. Zebras also live in grassy plains, light woodlands, and even on rocky hillsides in some places.

The savanna is a perfect home for zebras because it has lots of grass to eat and plenty of open space to run in. Zebras like to live where they can see far across the land, so they can spot lions and other hunters before the hunters get too close. When the dry season comes and the grass gets thin in one place, zebras move to a new place where the grass is fresh and green.

Some zebras travel very long distances every year, following the rains and the fresh grass.

## What Do Zebras Eat?

Zebras are plant eaters. They mostly eat grass, and they eat a lot of it! A zebra spends most of its day eating, moving slowly across the grassland and munching as it goes. Zebras also eat leaves, twigs, and bark when grass is hard to find, especially in the dry season.

A zebra's teeth are strong and flat, perfect for grinding tough grass. Zebras need to eat for many hours every day because grass does not give them much energy, so they must eat a lot of it to stay strong and healthy. They also need to drink water every day, so they stay near rivers and waterholes when they can.

Baby zebras drink their mother's milk when they are very young, then start nibbling grass alongside their mothers.

## A Zebra's Family Life

Zebras live in groups called herds. A herd is like a big family that stays together, eats together, and looks after each other.

Zebras take very good care of each other. Mother zebras stay close to their foals and protect them from danger. When the herd rests, some zebras keep watch while others sleep standing up. Zebras can even sleep standing up — their legs lock in place so they do not fall over! That way, they are ready to run away in a flash if a lion appears.

Zebras are also friendly animals. They stand side by side and nibble each other's backs, like giving a friend a nice scratch! Zebras also nuzzle and nicker softly to their friends, a bit like horses do.

A young foal can stand up and walk very soon after it is born, so it is ready to run with the herd if danger comes. For the first days of its life, a foal stays very close to its mother, learning her smell and her special stripe pattern so it can always find her in the crowd.

## Zebras and Their Enemies

Life on the savanna is not always easy for zebras. Lions, hyenas, and other hunters try to catch them. But zebras are not helpless — they have clever ways to stay safe.

Their stripes are their first secret weapon. When a whole herd of zebras stands close together, their black and white stripes blend into one big confusing pattern. A lion looking at the herd finds it hard to pick out one single zebra!

Zebras are also fast runners. They can run very fast, and they zigzag from side to side to make it hard for a hunter to catch them. If a lion does get close, a zebra can fight back with a powerful kick from its strong back legs. A zebra's kick is so strong that it can scare off even a lion!

When one zebra spots danger, it barks loudly to warn the whole herd, and the foals stay in the middle of the group where it is safest.

## Zebras and People

People have loved zebras for a very long time, and many travel from far away to see them on safari in Africa.

But zebras need our help. Some kinds of zebras are in danger because their grasslands are getting smaller as people build farms and towns. In some places, zebras are also hunted, which is against the law. The good news is that many people are working to protect zebras and their homes. Big wild parks and reserves keep zebras safe, and rangers watch over them day and night.
