If you think rabbits are soft, wait until you meet an Angora rabbit. Angoras grow long, silky fur that is softer than almost anything you can touch. They look like little balls of fluff with ears, and when they hop around, their fur bounces like a cloud. People have kept Angora rabbits for hundreds of years, both as pets and for their amazing wool. But these fluffy friends need a lot of care, so they are best for families who love grooming.

## A Rabbit That Looks Like a Pom-Pom

The Angora rabbit is easy to spot. Its fur grows very long, all over its body, including on its ears and face. Some Angoras look so fluffy that you can hardly see their eyes! Their fur comes in many colours, including white, black, grey, brown and cream. The most famous Angora is the English Angora, which is covered in long, soft wool from nose to tail. There are also French Angoras, German Angoras and Satin Angoras, each with slightly different fur.

Under all that fluff is a small, gentle rabbit. Angoras are usually calm and friendly, and they love to be stroked — as long as you are gentle. They are a little bigger than some pet rabbits, but they are still small enough to sit happily in your lap. When you hold an Angora, it feels like holding a warm, soft pillow that breathes.

## Wool Softer Than Sheep's Wool

Angora wool is famous around the world. It is much softer and lighter than sheep's wool, and it is also warmer. People spin it into yarn and knit it into jumpers, scarves, hats and gloves. A jumper made from Angora wool feels like wearing a cloud.

Long ago, Angora rabbits came from Turkey, where the city of Ankara was once called Angora. The rabbits were kept there for their wool, and traders carried them to other countries. In France, they became very popular, and French ladies loved to wear Angora wool. From France, Angora rabbits spread all over Europe and then to the rest of the world.

Angora wool is collected by gently brushing or trimming the rabbit's fur every few months. The rabbit is not hurt at all — in fact, it needs its fur trimmed, or the fur would get tangled and matted. A well-cared-for Angora enjoys being groomed, because it feels like a nice massage.

## Brushing, Brushing, Brushing

If you want to keep an Angora rabbit, you need to know one thing: grooming is a big job. An Angora's long fur tangles easily, so it needs to be brushed several times a week, sometimes every day. If the fur is not brushed, it forms knots called mats, which can pull on the rabbit's skin and make it uncomfortable. Mats can also hide problems, like sore skin or parasites, so regular brushing keeps the rabbit healthy too.

Brushing an Angora takes patience. You use a special brush with soft pins, and you work gently through the fur, a little at a time. Many owners say that grooming time becomes a special bonding time. The rabbit learns to trust you, and you learn to read its moods. It is a quiet, peaceful activity that both of you can enjoy.

Angoras also need their fur trimmed every few months, especially in warm weather. A rabbit in a full woolly coat can get too hot in summer, so a haircut helps it stay cool and comfortable.

## A Gentle, Quiet Friend

Angora rabbits are known for being calm and sweet-natured. They are not as bouncy and hyper as some rabbits. They like to sit and watch the world, and they enjoy gentle strokes. They are good pets for older children who can be calm and patient with them, and who will help with the brushing.

Like all rabbits, Angoras need space to hop and explore. They should have a large hutch with a run, or a safe room indoors where they can move around. They love to chew, so they need hay to munch on all day, and safe wooden toys to gnaw. Hay is the most important part of a rabbit's diet — it keeps their teeth healthy and their tummies working properly. They also enjoy fresh vegetables like carrots, leafy greens and herbs, but only in small amounts.

Angoras are clean animals. They groom themselves, like cats, and they can be litter trained, just like a cat. They like a clean home, so their hutch should be cleaned regularly, with fresh bedding every day.

## Baby Angoras

Baby rabbits are called kits. A mother Angora, called a doe, makes a nest of soft fur and hay for her babies. The kits are born tiny, blind and naked, without any fur at all. But they grow fast. Within a few weeks, they have a coat of soft baby fur, and soon their long Angora wool starts to grow.

Young Angoras are playful and curious. They hop around, explore everything, and practise binkying — the happy jump and twist that rabbits do when they are excited. Watching a fluffy baby Angora binky is one of the cutest things in the world.
