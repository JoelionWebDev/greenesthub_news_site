Baa! Have you ever worn a warm, soft jumper on a cold day? There is a good chance that jumper was made from the wool of a merino sheep. Merino sheep have the softest, finest wool of any sheep in the world. It is so soft and warm that people have loved it for hundreds of years.

Merino sheep first came from Spain, a country in Europe, a very long time ago. Spanish farmers worked hard to breed sheep with the finest wool they could, and they kept their merino sheep a secret for many years. Only later did merino sheep travel to other countries, including Australia, where huge flocks now live on farms under the wide, sunny sky.

## Wool Like a Soft Cloud

The most famous thing about a merino sheep is its wool. Merino wool is very fine, which means each strand is thin and soft. When you touch it, it feels like a soft cloud, not scratchy at all. That is why merino wool is used to make jumpers, scarves, hats, and blankets that feel cosy against the skin.

Merino wool is also wonderfully warm. It traps air between its curly fibres, and that air keeps the heat in. That is why a merino jumper keeps you warm on a cold day, and why merino wool is perfect for winter clothes.

A merino sheep's fleece, which is the name for its coat of wool, is thick and curly. The curls are called crimp, and they look like tiny waves all over the fleece. When the fleece is ready, the farmer shears it off with special clippers, and the sheep feels light and cool afterwards. The wool then grows back, ready for the next shearing.

## Wrinkles and a Woolly Face

Merino sheep are easy to recognise once you know what to look for. They are usually white, though some merinos are black or brown. Their wool is so thick that it often covers their face, with just their eyes and nose peeking out. They look like fluffy clouds with faces, and it is hard not to smile at them.

Many merino sheep have wrinkles in their skin, especially around their neck. These wrinkles help them grow even more wool, because there is more skin for the fleece to grow on. The wrinkles are a special feature of merino sheep, and they make each sheep look a little different from the next.

Merino sheep have gentle, dark eyes and a calm expression. They are medium-sized sheep with strong legs for walking across hills and fields. Rams, the male sheep, often have large, curly horns that twist around their ears like a spiral. Ewes, the female sheep, usually have no horns at all. Both rams and ewes have the same soft, thick fleece.

## Gentle Animals of the Flock

Merino sheep are gentle, calm animals. They are happiest when they are together in a flock, grazing on grass and resting in the shade. Sheep are herd animals, which means they feel safe when they are with other sheep. A merino sheep on its own will feel lonely and worried, so farmers always keep them in groups.

Merino sheep are also smarter than many people think. They can recognise the faces of other sheep and of the people who look after them. They remember where the good grass grows and where the water is.

Sheep talk to each other with their bleats. A mother sheep, called a ewe, knows her lamb's bleat, and the lamb knows its mother's voice. Even in a big flock with hundreds of sheep, a ewe can find her own lamb just by listening. It is a lovely example of how much sheep care for their families.

## Lambs in Springtime

Springtime on a merino farm is a joyful season, because that is when the lambs are born. Baby lambs are small, wobbly, and covered in soft, curly wool. They stay close to their mothers, drinking milk and learning to walk. Within a short time, they are running and jumping around the field, full of energy and fun.

The bond between a ewe and her lamb is very strong. The ewe licks her lamb clean when it is born, and she keeps it safe from danger. She calls to it with a soft bleat, and the lamb answers.

## Looking After Merino Sheep

Merino sheep need good care from their farmers. They need large fields of fresh grass to eat, clean water to drink, and shelter from the rain and the hot sun. Farmers check their sheep every day, making sure they are healthy and happy.

Shearing is an important part of caring for merino sheep. Once a year, or sometimes more often in warm places, a shearer carefully clips the wool off the sheep. Shearing keeps the sheep cool and comfortable, and it stops the fleece from becoming too heavy or dirty.

## From Sheep to Jumper

Have you ever wondered how a sheep's wool becomes a jumper? After shearing, the wool is cleaned, combed, and spun into yarn. Then the yarn is woven or knitted into cloth, and the cloth is made into clothes. It is a long journey from the farm to your wardrobe, and it all starts with a merino sheep in a sunny field.

Merino wool is loved all over the world. It is used for school jumpers, sports clothes, blankets, and even socks. People choose merino wool because it is soft, warm, and lasts a long time.

Merino sheep remind us that farm animals give us wonderful gifts. They give us soft wool for warm clothes, and they fill the fields with their gentle bleating.
