Imagine an animal so big that it weighs more than ten grown men put together, yet so gentle that it spends its summers munching berries and catching fish. That animal is the brown bear. Brown bears live in forests and mountains across North America, Europe and Asia, and they are some of the most amazing animals on Earth. They are strong, clever, and they have one of the most surprising habits in the animal kingdom: they sleep for months at a time!

## Meet the Brown Bear

Brown bears are one of the largest land animals that eat both plants and meat. Their fur is not always brown — it can be golden, dark brown, or even almost black. Some brown bears have fur with pale tips that look frosty in the sunlight. That is why some people call the brown bears of North America "grizzly bears" — their fur looks grizzled, or grey-tipped.

A big male brown bear can stand taller than a grown man when it rises up on its back legs. Its shoulders have a big hump of muscle that helps it dig and push things around. Brown bears have huge paws with long, curved claws. These claws are perfect for digging up roots and turning over rocks to find insects hiding underneath.

Even though they look big and slow, brown bears can run very fast for a short distance, and they are very good swimmers.

## A Bear's Dinner Table

Brown bears are omnivores, which means they eat almost everything. In spring, they eat fresh green plants, roots and insects. In summer, they enjoy berries, and they love to catch fish in rivers and streams. In autumn, they eat nuts and more berries to build up fat for the winter.

One of the most famous sights in nature is a brown bear standing in a river, waiting to catch a salmon jumping upstream. The bear watches carefully, then splashes its paw into the water and scoops the fish out. Salmon are very important food for bears in places like Alaska, because the fish are full of goodness that helps the bear grow strong.

Bears also eat honey when they can find it, and they will happily munch on grasses and flowers. A brown bear spends most of its waking hours looking for food, because it needs to eat a lot. In the months before winter, a bear eats almost all day long.

## The Long Winter Sleep

Here is the most amazing thing about brown bears: they hibernate. Hibernation is a very deep sleep that lasts through the whole winter. In late autumn, when the weather turns cold and food becomes hard to find, the brown bear digs a den. The den might be a hollow under a fallen tree, a cave, or a hole dug into a hillside. The bear lines it with leaves and grass to make it cosy.

Then the bear curls up and goes to sleep — for months! Its heart beats much more slowly, and its body temperature drops a little. The bear lives off the fat it stored during the summer and autumn. Imagine sleeping from November until March without waking up once!

Mother bears even give birth during this long winter sleep. The tiny cubs are born in the dark, warm den in the middle of winter. When they are born, they are very small — about the size of a small toy — and they have almost no fur. They snuggle close to their mother and drink her milk while she keeps sleeping. By spring, when the family finally leaves the den, the cubs are bigger, fluffy, and ready to explore the world.

## Growing Up a Bear Cub

Bear cubs stay with their mother for a long time, often for two or three years. During that time, the mother teaches them everything they need to know: which berries are good to eat, how to catch fish, where to find safe places to sleep, and how to stay away from danger. Cubs are playful little animals. They wrestle with each other, climb trees, and splash in streams.

## Where Do Brown Bears Live?

Long ago, brown bears lived in forests all across Europe, Asia and North America. Today, their homes have become smaller because people have built towns, roads and farms in many of the forests where bears used to live. But brown bears still live in many wild places. In North America, they live in Alaska, Canada, and parts of the western United States. In Europe, they live in countries like Romania, Sweden and Finland. In Asia, they live in Russia and other northern countries.

Brown bears need large wild spaces with plenty of food, clean water, and quiet places to make their dens. They usually live alone, except for mothers with cubs. Each bear has its own home area, and bears try not to bother each other. A bear's home area can be very large, because one bear needs a lot of food to survive.

## Bears and People

Brown bears are wild animals, and they are not pets. They are usually shy and try to stay away from people. If a bear hears people coming, it will usually walk away quietly. Most of the time, bears want to be left alone to eat berries and fish in peace.

People can help brown bears by keeping their forests safe and clean. When people go camping in bear country, they keep their food stored safely so bears do not learn to come looking for human food. Wild bears should stay wild, eating their natural food in their natural homes.

## Why Brown Bears Are Special

Brown bears are special for many reasons. They are one of the biggest animals in the forest, yet they are also some of the best sleepers. They can eat almost anything, swim in cold rivers, climb trees, and raise their cubs in the dark of a winter den. They help the forest too — when they eat berries and walk around, they drop seeds in their droppings, and new plants grow from those seeds. When they carry fish from the river into the forest, the leftover fish help feed the trees and plants.

So the next time you think about the forest, picture a great brown bear standing in a sparkling river, or a mother bear and her cubs snuggled together in a warm winter den. Brown bears remind us that nature is full of surprises — and that sometimes, the biggest, strongest animals are also the biggest sleepers of all.
