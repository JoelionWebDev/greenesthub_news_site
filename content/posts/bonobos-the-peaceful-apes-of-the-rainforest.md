Deep in the rainforest of the Congo, in the heart of Africa, there lives a very special ape. It has dark fur, a friendly face, and a gentle way of moving through the trees. Its name is the bonobo, and it is famous for being one of the most peaceful animals in the whole animal kingdom. While many animals fight over food or space, bonobos have a different way of solving problems: they share, they play, and they look after one another.

Bonobos live high in the rainforest, where the trees are tall and the leaves are thick and green. They spend their days swinging through the branches, resting in the shade, and looking for sweet fruit to eat. They are wonderful climbers, and they also walk on the forest floor. Bonobos are clever, curious, and full of life, and they love being together.

## Apes Who Love Peace

Bonobos are known all over the world for their peaceful nature. When two bonobos want the same tasty fruit, they usually do not fight about it. Instead, they share it. Sharing food is one of the most important things in bonobo life, and it helps the whole group stay happy and calm.

Bonobos also settle disagreements in a gentle way. Instead of shouting or chasing each other, they play, groom each other, and spend time close together. Grooming means picking through each other's fur, and bonobos do it for a long time, just like friends brushing each other's hair. It helps them relax, and it keeps their friendships strong.

Because bonobos are so peaceful, scientists love to watch them and learn from them. Bonobos show us that even in the wild, animals can choose kindness, and that sharing and being gentle can make a whole community stronger.

## Life in the Congo Rainforest

Bonobos live only in one place in the whole world: the rainforests of the Congo, in central Africa. They live south of a great river called the Congo River, and they never cross to the other side. Their home is warm and wet, with rain falling often and trees growing tall all year round. The forest gives them everything they need: food, shelter, and safe places to sleep.

At night, bonobos build nests high in the trees. They bend branches and leaves together to make a soft, springy bed, just like making a nest out of pillows. Each bonobo makes its own nest, and mothers share a nest with their babies. In the morning, they wake up, stretch, and climb down to start a brand new day.

## What Bonobos Eat

Bonobos love fruit more than anything else. They eat wild figs, berries, and many other sweet fruits that grow in the rainforest. When fruit is ripe, bonobos climb high into the trees to pick it, and they eat it slowly, enjoying every bite. Fruit gives them energy to climb, play, and swing through the forest all day long.

But fruit is not the only food on the menu. Bonobos also eat leaves, flowers, seeds, and shoots from trees and plants. Sometimes they eat small creatures like insects and worms too. They are not fussy eaters, and they try lots of different foods that grow in their forest home.

Bonobos are very good at finding food. They remember where the best fruit trees are, and they visit them again and again when the fruit is ripe. Older bonobos teach the younger ones which fruits are safe to eat, so the knowledge of the forest is passed down from one generation to the next.

## Families That Stick Together

Bonobos live in big family groups, and the group is the heart of bonobo life. A group has mothers, babies, young bonobos, and older bonobos, all living, eating, and travelling together. No bonobo is ever left alone for long, and being together keeps them safe and happy.

In bonobo groups, the mothers and older females are the leaders. They guide the group to food, settle disagreements, and look after the babies. The females work together, share food with each other, and help each other raise their babies.

Baby bonobos stay close to their mothers for a long time. A mother carries her baby everywhere, holding it close to her chest as she climbs and walks through the forest. She feeds it, grooms it, and teaches it how to find food and climb trees, and the baby learns by watching and copying her.

## Playtime in the Trees

Bonobos love to play, and they play more than almost any other ape. Young bonobos chase each other through the trees, wrestle gently on the branches, and swing from vine to vine. They laugh and squeal with delight as they tumble and roll, and even grown-up bonobos join in games with the little ones.

Play is not just fun for bonobos; it is also how they learn. When young bonobos play, they practise climbing, balancing, and swinging. They learn how strong a branch is, how far they can jump, and how to move safely through the trees. Play also helps them make friends and learn how to get along with others.

Bonobos also love to swing and hang from branches. They use their long arms to reach from branch to branch, and they can move through the trees quickly and gracefully. Sometimes a bonobo will hang upside down just for fun, swinging back and forth like a child on a swing.

## Protecting the Peaceful Apes

Sadly, bonobos are in danger. Their rainforest home is being cut down, and there are fewer and fewer places for them to live. Bonobos need big, healthy forests with plenty of fruit trees, and when the forest disappears, they have nowhere to go.

Good people are helping bonobos in many ways. Some protect the rainforest so bonobos have a safe home. Others look after bonobos that need help and teach people about these gentle apes.

You can help bonobos too, even from far away. Learning about them, telling your friends about them, and caring about rainforests all make a difference. The bonobo is a gentle, peaceful ape that shows the world how to live together in kindness. If we look after the rainforest, bonobos will keep swinging through the trees and playing together for many years to come.
