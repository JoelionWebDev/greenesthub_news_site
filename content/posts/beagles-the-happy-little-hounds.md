# Beagles: The Happy Little Hounds

Imagine a small dog with a shiny coat of white, black and tan, long floppy ears, big brown eyes and a tail that never stops wagging. It is sniffing the ground with great excitement, following a scent that only it can smell. This is a Beagle, one of the happiest and friendliest dogs in the world. Beagles are merry little hounds that love people, love food and love to explore with their amazing noses.

Let us meet these cheerful dogs and discover what makes them so special.

## Where Do Beagles Come From?

Beagles come from England, where they have lived for hundreds of years. Long ago, people in England loved to go hunting on foot, following dogs that chased rabbits and hares through fields and woods. They needed a small dog that could follow a scent, run all day and make a loud sound so the hunters could follow it. The Beagle was the perfect dog for the job.

The word "beagle" may come from an old French word meaning "loud mouth", because Beagles are famous for their voices. They bay, which is a loud, musical howl that carries across fields. Hunters could hear their Beagles even when the dogs were far away in the woods.

Over time, Beagles became popular far beyond England. They travelled to America and many other countries, where people fell in love with their cheerful nature. Today, Beagles are among the most popular family pets in the world, and they also work in important jobs using their super-powered noses.

## What Do Beagles Look Like?

Beagles are small to medium-sized dogs with a sturdy, compact body. They have short, smooth coats that are easy to look after. Their most famous coat pattern is white with black and tan patches.

Their ears are long, soft and floppy, hanging down beside their faces. Their eyes are big, brown and full of warmth, and they have a sweet, gentle expression that makes everyone smile. Their tails are short and strong, and they almost always have a white tip. Hunters liked the white tip because it helped them spot their Beagle in tall grass. Most of all, a Beagle's tail is almost always wagging.

Beagles have strong legs and a deep chest, built for running and following scents for hours. They may be small, but they are tough and full of energy.

## A Nose Like a Superpower

A Beagle's greatest gift is its nose. Dogs already have a much better sense of smell than humans, but Beagles are among the best sniffers of all. They have a huge number of scent receptors in their noses, which help them pick up smells that we could never notice. When a Beagle puts its nose to the ground, it can follow a trail that is hours old.

This amazing nose is why Beagles work in important jobs today. At airports in many countries, Beagles work as detection dogs. They sniff luggage and packages to find fruit, plants and food that should not be brought into the country. These things might carry pests or diseases that could harm farms and forests. Beagles are perfect for the job because they are friendly, they do not scare travellers, and they love to work for a food reward.

Beagles are also used to find lost people, search for hidden things and help scientists study wildlife. Wherever a great nose is needed, a Beagle is ready to help.

## Happy, Friendly and Full of Fun

Beagles are famous for their happy nature. They are cheerful, gentle and loving, and they make friends easily. They love people, including children, and they are usually happy to meet strangers. A Beagle will often greet a visitor with a wagging tail and a hopeful look, as if to say, "Will you be my friend?"

Beagles love company. They were bred to live and work in packs, groups of dogs that hunt together, so they do not like being left alone for a long time. They are happiest when they are with their family, or with another dog to play with.

They are also very playful. Beagles love games, especially games that involve sniffing and searching. Hide a treat in the garden and a Beagle will find it in no time. They love walks, runs and adventures, and they are always ready for fun.

## Beagles Love Food

If there is one thing Beagles love almost as much as sniffing, it is food. Beagles will eat almost anything, and they are famous for stealing food when no one is looking. A Beagle can sniff out a biscuit in a closed bag or beg with those big brown eyes until someone gives in.

Owners have to be careful with a Beagle's food. Because they love eating so much, Beagles can easily become overweight if they are given too many treats. A healthy Beagle needs the right amount of food and plenty of exercise, however cute its begging face may be.

## Looking After a Beagle

Beagles are quite easy to look after. Their short coats need only a quick brush once a week, though they do shed fur. They need a bath now and then, and their long floppy ears should be checked and cleaned to keep them healthy.

Exercise is very important for a Beagle. They need long walks every day, with plenty of time to sniff and explore. A Beagle that does not get enough exercise can become bored and naughty. They also need a secure garden, because a Beagle that catches an interesting scent might try to follow it over a fence or under a gate.

Training a Beagle takes patience. Beagles are clever, but they can be stubborn, especially when they smell something wonderful. Short, fun training sessions with food rewards work best. A Beagle that is trained kindly becomes a well-behaved and happy companion.

## Fun Facts About Beagles

- Beagles come from England, where they hunted rabbits and hares.
- They have one of the best noses of any dog.
- Beagles work at airports, sniffing luggage for forbidden food and plants.
- Their tails almost always have a white tip.
- Beagles have three voices: a bark, a bay and a howl.
- They were bred to live and work in packs, so they love company.
- Beagles love food and can be very good at begging.
- The cartoon dog Snoopy is a Beagle.

Beagles are small dogs with big hearts and even bigger noses. They are cheerful, friendly and full of fun.
