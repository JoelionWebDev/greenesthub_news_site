Far out at sea, on rocky islands and lonely shores, stand tall towers with bright lights at the top. These are lighthouses, and their job is to warn ships about dangerous rocks and guide them safely home. Long ago, the people who looked after lighthouses, called lighthouse keepers, lived in these lonely towers for weeks or months at a time, far away from other people. And they were rarely alone. In lonely lighthouses far from shore, cats kept the keepers company, guarded the food from rats, and became beloved members of the lighthouse family.

## A Lonely Life at Sea

Life in a lighthouse was not easy. The keepers lived on small, rocky islands, or on tall towers surrounded by the sea. In winter, storms could cut them off from the mainland for weeks, and the wind and waves would crash against the tower day and night. The keepers had to keep the light burning all night, every night, no matter how bad the weather was. If the light went out, ships could crash into the rocks, and people could die.

It was a lonely life. The keepers spent most of their time on their own, or with just one or two other keepers for company. They missed their families, their friends and the busy towns on the mainland. They had books to read, and chores to do, but the long, quiet nights could feel very lonely indeed. That is why so many lighthouse keepers were glad to have a cat for company.

A cat was the perfect lighthouse companion. Cats are independent, they do not need much looking after, and they are happy to sit quietly with a person, purring softly. For a lonely keeper, the warm weight of a cat on his lap, or the soft sound of purring in the night, was a great comfort.

## The Cats with an Important Job

Lighthouse cats were not just pets. They had a very important job to do. Lighthouses stored food for the keepers, and they also stored oil for the lamps, rope, tools and other supplies. Rats and mice loved to sneak into lighthouses, especially when food was delivered by boat. The rats would eat the food, chew through ropes, and make a mess of everything.

The cats were the lighthouse's pest controllers. They hunted the rats and mice, and kept the lighthouse clean and safe. A good lighthouse cat could catch many rats in a single night, and the keepers were very grateful. Some lighthouse cats became famous for their hunting skills, and the keepers would brag about how many rats their cat had caught.

The cats also protected the food stores from mice that could carry sickness. In a lonely lighthouse, far from a doctor, keeping the food clean was very important. The cats were like little guardians, watching over the lighthouse and keeping it safe from tiny invaders.

## Cats That Loved the Sea

Lighthouse cats lived a very unusual life. They lived in tall towers, with spiral staircases winding up to the top. The cats learned to climb the stairs, and they loved to sit on the windowsills, watching the waves and the seabirds. Some cats even learned to sit beside the great lamp at the top of the tower, where they could see for miles across the sea.

The cats were used to the sound of the waves, the cry of the gulls, and the beam of the light sweeping across the water at night. They were not afraid of storms, and they seemed to enjoy the wild, windy life. When supply boats arrived, the cats would come out to greet the visitors, rubbing against their legs and asking for a fuss.

Some lighthouse cats became so famous that people wrote about them in newspapers and books. Visitors to lighthouses would ask to meet the cat, and the keepers would proudly introduce their furry friend. The cats were a part of the lighthouse family, and they were loved by everyone who knew them.

## A Friend in the Loneliest Places

The friendship between lighthouse keepers and their cats was very special. The keepers talked to their cats, played with them, and shared their meals with them. In the long winter nights, when the wind howled around the tower and the waves crashed on the rocks, the cat was often the keeper's only friend.

When a keeper's time at the lighthouse came to an end, he would often take his cat with him, or pass the cat on to the next keeper, so the cat could carry on its important work. Some cats spent their whole lives in lighthouses, and they were buried on the island when they died, with a little marker to remember them by.

Today, most lighthouses are automatic, and they do not need keepers any more. The lights turn on and off by themselves, and no one lives in the towers. But the story of the lighthouse cats is still remembered, and it is a lovely reminder of how animals and people can be friends in even the loneliest places on Earth.

## Remembering the Lighthouse Cats

Lighthouse cats show us that animals do not just live with people in houses and farms. They have shared our lives in some of the wildest, loneliest places in the world, from tall towers in the middle of the sea to small islands battered by storms. They kept us company, they did important work, and they made hard lives a little bit easier.

Next time you see a lighthouse, shining its beam across the dark sea, think of the cats who once lived there, the cats who guarded the light, curled up on a windowsill, purring softly as the waves crashed below.
