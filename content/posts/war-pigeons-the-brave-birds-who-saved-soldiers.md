Long before there were mobile phones, radios or the internet, armies had a very special way of sending messages: they used pigeons. These were not ordinary pigeons. They were brave, clever birds called homing pigeons, and they carried secret messages across battlefields, flying through smoke, storms and danger to reach the soldiers who needed them. Long before radios and phones, brave pigeons carried secret messages across battlefields, flying through danger to bring help to soldiers in trouble.

## How a Pigeon Finds Its Way Home

Homing pigeons have an amazing talent: no matter how far away you take them, they can find their way back home. Scientists think pigeons use the sun, the Earth's magnetic field, and even smells in the air to navigate, like a built-in compass and map. They can fly for hundreds of miles, and they always seem to know which way to go.

Long ago, people noticed this talent and began to use pigeons to carry messages. A soldier would write a tiny message on a thin piece of paper, roll it up, and put it in a little tube tied to the pigeon's leg. Then he would let the pigeon go, and the bird would fly straight home, carrying the message to the people waiting there. It was a simple idea, and it worked beautifully.

Pigeons were trained from a young age. Trainers would take them a short distance from home, let them go, and watch them fly back. Each time, the trainers would take them a little bit farther away, until the pigeons could find their way home from very far away. The best pigeons became famous, and their owners were very proud of them.

## Pigeons in the First World War

Pigeons were used in many wars, but they were most famous during the First World War, which took place more than a hundred years ago. In those days, radios were new and unreliable, and telephone wires were often cut by enemy fire. Pigeons were often the only way for soldiers to send messages for help.

One of the most famous war pigeons was a bird named Cher Ami, which means "Dear Friend" in French. During a battle in France, a group of American soldiers was trapped behind enemy lines, and they were being attacked by their own side by mistake. They had no way to call for help, so they sent their last pigeon, Cher Ami, with a message asking the guns to stop.

Cher Ami flew through heavy gunfire and was badly hurt, but he kept flying. He reached his home loft with the message tied to his leg, and the soldiers were saved. Cher Ami became a hero, and he was given a medal for his bravery. Today, his story is still told in schools and museums, and his body is kept in a museum in the United States, so people can remember what he did.

## Pigeons in the Second World War

Pigeons were heroes again in the Second World War, which took place about twenty years later. This time, pigeons were used by soldiers, sailors and airmen all over the world. They carried messages from sinking ships, from crashed aeroplanes, and from soldiers who were lost behind enemy lines.

One famous pigeon was named G.I. Joe. During a battle in Italy, American soldiers were about to bomb a town, not knowing that their own British friends had already taken it over. A pigeon named G.I. Joe flew twenty miles in just twenty minutes with a message to stop the bombing, and he saved the lives of many soldiers. G.I. Joe was given a medal too, and he became one of the most decorated pigeons of the war.

In Britain, pigeons were given a special medal called the Dickin Medal, which is like the Victoria Cross for animals. Many pigeons received this medal for their brave work, and their stories are still remembered today. Some pigeons even had tiny parachutes, so they could be dropped from aeroplanes into places where soldiers needed them.

## Why Pigeons Were So Special

Pigeons were special because they were fast, reliable and brave. A pigeon could fly much faster than a soldier could run, and it could fly over rivers, forests and enemy lines without stopping. Pigeons were also hard to catch, because they flew high and fast, and they were small targets. Even when they were hurt, many pigeons kept flying until they reached home.

Soldiers loved their pigeons, and they looked after them carefully. The pigeons lived in special lofts, where they were fed good food and kept warm and dry. Soldiers talked to their pigeons, gave them names, and trusted them with their lives. The bond between soldiers and their pigeons was very strong, and many soldiers said that their pigeons were their best friends.

Today, we do not need pigeons to carry messages, because we have phones, radios and computers. But the story of the war pigeons reminds us of how clever animals can be, and how much they have helped people in difficult times.

## Remembering the Brave Birds

War pigeons are remembered in many ways. There are statues of pigeons in some cities, and there are books, films and stories about their adventures. In some places, people still keep homing pigeons and race them for fun, and the birds still amaze everyone with their ability to find their way home.

The story of the war pigeons teaches us something important: that even a small, ordinary-looking bird can be a hero. Pigeons do not look brave or strong, but when people needed them most, they flew through danger and saved lives. They are a wonderful example of the friendship between animals and humans, and of the amazing things that animals can do.

Next time you see a pigeon in the park, remember the war pigeons, the brave birds who saved soldiers, and the little heroes who carried messages through the skies when people needed them most.
