Imagine a herd so big that it stretches as far as your eyes can see. Thousands and thousands of animals move together across the golden plains of Africa, their hooves thudding like distant thunder. These are wildebeest, the great travellers of Africa. Every year, they set off on one of the longest journeys in the animal world, walking for many miles in search of fresh grass and water.

A wildebeest looks a little bit like a cow, a horse, and an antelope all mixed together. It has a big head, curved horns, a shaggy mane, and a beard under its chin. Its body is strong and sturdy, built for long walks across the hot plains. Let us follow the herd and discover why these animals never stay in one place for long.

## What Is a Wildebeest?

A wildebeest is a kind of antelope that lives in Africa. It has a dark grey-brown coat, a long face, and horns that curve outwards and then upwards, like a pair of handlebars. Both male and female wildebeest have horns. Its mane is dark and shaggy, and it has a tuft of hair under its chin that looks like a beard. Its legs are long and slim, perfect for walking and running.

Wildebeest are sometimes called gnus, which comes from the sound of their call. They make a funny grunting noise that sounds a bit like "gnu, gnu." When a whole herd is calling at once, the plains are filled with grunts, snorts, and the sound of many hooves on the ground.

## Life on the Plains

Wildebeest live on the open grasslands of Africa, where the grass stretches out in every direction. They eat grass, and they need a lot of it. A wildebeest spends much of the day grazing, pulling up mouthfuls of grass with its wide mouth. It also needs to drink water often, so it stays near rivers and waterholes when it can.

Wildebeest are not alone on the plains. They share the grass with zebras, gazelles, and many other grazing animals. Zebras often travel with them, eating the tall, tough grass first, which leaves the shorter, softer grass for the wildebeest. It is a clever team effort, and everyone gets a good meal. Living in a big herd also helps keep the wildebeest safe, because there are many eyes watching for danger.

## The Great Migration

The most famous thing about wildebeest is their great journey, called the Great Migration. Each year, huge herds of wildebeest travel in a big circle across the plains, following the rains. When the rain falls in one place, the grass grows green and fresh there. When that grass is eaten or dries up, the herds move on to where the rain has fallen next.

The journey is long and hard. The wildebeest must cross wide rivers, where the water can be deep and fast. They must walk under the hot sun for many days. Some animals get tired, and some do not make it to the end. But the herd keeps moving, because the fresh grass at the end of the journey is worth it. When the rains return, the grass grows again, and the circle begins once more. This amazing journey has been happening for a very long time.

## Crossing the Rivers

One of the most exciting and dangerous parts of the journey is crossing a river. The herd gathers at the riverbank, and the wildebeest stand close together, looking at the water. Then, one brave wildebeest jumps in, and soon thousands follow, splashing and swimming across. The river can be full of surprises, and the wildebeest must be strong swimmers to reach the other side.

On the far bank, they climb up the steep mud and shake the water from their coats. Then they carry on walking, heading for the green grass ahead. It is a wonderful sight, and many people travel from far away just to watch the great river crossing. The wildebeest do not know they are famous. They are simply following the food and the water, as their ancestors did before them.

## Baby Wildebeest

Baby wildebeest are called calves. They are born during a special time of year, when the grass is green and there is plenty of food for their mothers. A calf can stand up and walk just a few minutes after it is born, which is very important. A baby that can walk can stay with the herd, and staying with the herd keeps it safe.

Calves have light brown coats that help them hide in the tall grass. They stay close to their mothers and drink their mother's milk. As they grow, they begin to nibble grass, just like the grown-ups. Within a few months, a young wildebeest can run fast and keep up with the herd on its long journey.

## Staying Safe Together

Life on the plains is not always easy. Wildebeest have many predators, such as lions, hyenas, and crocodiles. A lion might hide in the tall grass, waiting for a wildebeest to wander too far from the herd. This is why wildebeest stay together in such big groups. When there are thousands of animals, it is harder for a hunter to catch one.

Wildebeest also watch out for each other. If one animal sees danger, it may snort or stamp its foot to warn the others. Then the whole herd starts to run. A running herd is a powerful sight. Their hooves thunder across the plain, and dust rises into the air. Wildebeest can run fast when they need to, and they can keep running for a long time.

## Why the Journey Matters

The Great Migration is not just a journey for the wildebeest. It helps the whole land. As the herds move and graze, they trim the grass and drop dung that feeds the soil. This helps new grass grow. The herds also feed many other animals, from lions to tiny birds that pick insects from their backs. The wildebeest are a very important part of life on the African plains.

Today, the wildebeest still make their great journey, but they need safe paths to travel on. When people protect the grasslands and the rivers, the herds can keep moving, just as they always have. The wildebeest remind us that sometimes the longest journey is the one that keeps life going. So the next time you hear about the Great Migration, picture the great herds moving across the plains, on their endless search for green grass and fresh water.
