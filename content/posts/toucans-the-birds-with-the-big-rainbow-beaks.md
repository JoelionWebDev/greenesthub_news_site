# Toucans: The Birds With the Big Rainbow Beaks

Imagine a bird with a beak so big and so colourful that it looks like it was painted by an artist. The beak is bright red, orange, yellow, green, or blue, and it is almost as long as the bird's whole body. This is a toucan, one of the most eye-catching birds in the world. Toucans live in the rainforests of Central and South America, where they hop from branch to branch, munching fruit and showing off their amazing beaks.

Let us fly into the rainforest and meet these rainbow-beaked birds.

## A Beak That Is Bigger Than It Looks

The toucan's beak is its most famous feature. It can be very long, sometimes about a third of the length of the whole bird, and it is covered in bright colours. The toco toucan, the biggest kind of toucan, has a huge orange beak with a black tip. Other toucans have beaks that are green, red, yellow, and blue, all mixed together like a rainbow.

You might think that such a big beak would be very heavy, and that the toucan would find it hard to fly. But the toucan's beak is not solid. Inside, it is full of tiny holes, a bit like a sponge, which makes it very light. Even though the beak looks big and heavy, it weighs very little.

Toucans use their beaks to reach fruit on branches that are too thin for them to stand on. They stretch out their long beak, pluck the fruit, and then toss it into the air and catch it in their throat. It looks like a clever trick, and it is fun to watch. They also use their beaks to peel fruit and to reach into holes in trees.

## Life in the Rainforest

Toucans live in the tropical rainforests of Central and South America, in countries like Brazil, Costa Rica, Peru, and Colombia. They spend most of their time high up in the trees, in the canopy, where the branches are thick with leaves and fruit. They hop from branch to branch and make short flights between trees.

Toucans are noisy birds. They make croaking, clicking, and barking sounds that echo through the forest. Some toucans make a sound like a frog croaking, and others make a loud, yapping call. These sounds help toucans talk to each other and warn each other about danger.

There are many different kinds of toucans. The toco toucan is the biggest, with a white throat and a bright orange beak. The keel-billed toucan has a beak that is green, orange, and blue, like a rainbow. The aracari is a smaller toucan with a colourful beak and a yellow belly.

## What Do Toucans Eat?

Toucans mostly eat fruit. They love figs, berries, and other soft fruits that grow in the rainforest. They use their long beaks to reach fruit on thin branches, and they swallow the fruit whole. The seeds inside the fruit pass through the toucan and come out in its droppings, which helps new trees grow in the forest. In this way, toucans help the rainforest stay healthy and full of trees.

Toucans also eat insects, small lizards, and eggs from other birds' nests. They are not picky eaters, and they will eat whatever they can find. But fruit is their favourite food, and they spend most of their day searching for ripe fruit in the trees.

## Family Life in a Tree Hole

Toucans nest in holes in trees. They do not dig the holes themselves; they use holes made by woodpeckers or holes that form naturally in old trees. The female toucan lays a few white eggs inside the hole, and both parents take turns sitting on the eggs to keep them warm.

When baby toucans hatch, they are tiny, naked, and helpless, with no feathers at all. Their parents bring them fruit and other food, and they grow quickly. As they grow, their feathers come in, and their beaks grow bigger and more colourful.

Toucans are social birds, and they often live in small groups. They like to groom each other, using their beaks to clean each other's feathers. Sometimes several toucans squeeze into the same tree hole to sleep at night, folding their long tails up over their backs to fit inside. It is a tight squeeze, but they seem to like being close together.

## Flying With a Big Beak

Toucans are not the best flyers in the rainforest. Their wings are short and rounded, and they flap quickly and then glide for a short distance. They fly in a bouncy, up-and-down way, which looks a bit funny. But they do not need to fly far, because everything they need is in the trees around them.

## Protecting the Toucans

Toucans are beautiful birds, but they face some problems. Their rainforest homes are being cut down for farming and building, which means toucans are losing the trees they need for food and nesting. Some toucans are also caught and sold as pets, which is not good for them, because they belong in the wild, flying free in the forest.

People are working to protect toucans and their rainforest homes. National parks and reserves have been set up in Central and South America to keep the forests safe. Scientists study toucans to learn more about them, and people are taught that toucans are happier in the wild than in a cage. By protecting the rainforest, we protect toucans and all the other amazing animals that live there.

## Fun Facts About Toucans

- A toucan's beak can be about a third of the length of its whole body.
- The beak is very light inside, full of tiny holes like a sponge.
- Toucans toss fruit into the air and catch it in their throat.
- The toco toucan is the biggest kind of toucan.
- Toucans nest in holes in trees, often made by woodpeckers.
- Several toucans sometimes squeeze into one tree hole to sleep.
- Toucans help the rainforest by spreading seeds when they eat fruit.
- Their feet have two toes pointing forwards and two pointing backwards.

Toucans are some of the most colourful and cheerful birds in the world. With their huge rainbow beaks, their noisy calls, and their love of fruit, they bring colour and life to the rainforest. Next time you see a picture of a toucan, remember: that big beak is light as a feather, and it is one of nature's most amazing designs.
