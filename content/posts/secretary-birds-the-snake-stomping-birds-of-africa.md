Have you ever seen a bird that looks like a secretary from long ago, with pens tucked behind its ears? The secretary bird is one of the strangest and most wonderful birds in Africa. It has very long legs, like a crane, but it is actually a bird of prey, related to eagles and hawks. Instead of flying after its food, it spends most of the day walking across the grasslands, hunting on foot. And when it finds a snake, watch out — its kick is one of the fastest and strongest in the bird world.

## A Bird That Looks Like It Is Going to Work

The secretary bird gets its name from its looks. Long ago, office workers called secretaries used to tuck quill pens behind their ears. The secretary bird has long black feathers sticking out from the back of its head, and they look just like pens tucked behind an ear. It also has a grey body, black feathers on its wings and legs, and a face with orange-yellow skin around its eyes. It looks smart, serious and ready for business.

Secretary birds are tall. Standing on their long legs, they can reach about as high as a young child. Their legs are long and strong, covered in black feathers at the top and scaly skin below. Those legs are not just for walking — they are the bird's greatest weapon.

## The Hunter That Walks

Most birds of prey hunt from the sky. Eagles soar high, spot their food, and dive down fast. Secretary birds do things differently. They are brilliant walkers. Every day, a secretary bird can walk for many hours across the open grasslands of Africa, covering a huge distance on foot. It steps slowly and carefully, watching the grass for movement.

When it spots something small moving in the grass, it freezes, then pounces. It eats insects, lizards, small birds, mice and other small animals. It is not a picky eater. Anything it can catch and swallow is on the menu. Walking all day like this uses a lot of energy, but the grasslands are full of food if you know where to look.

## The Famous Snake Stomp

The most amazing thing about secretary birds is how they hunt snakes. Snakes are dangerous food, and many animals stay far away from them. But the secretary bird is a snake-hunting champion. When it finds a snake, it does not grab it with its beak straight away. That would be too risky. Instead, it uses its feet.

The secretary bird stamps on the snake again and again with powerful kicks. Its legs move so fast that it is hard to follow them with your eyes. Each kick is strong enough to hurt or even kill the snake. The bird is careful to keep its body away from the snake's head, so it cannot be bitten. Once the snake stops moving, the bird picks it up with its beak and swallows it whole, head first.

Scientists have studied these kicks and found that they are incredibly quick and powerful. The secretary bird's legs are built for this job, with thick bones and strong muscles. It is one of the few animals that can hunt even poisonous snakes safely, thanks to its speed and clever way of fighting from a distance.

## A Home in the Grasslands

Secretary birds live in the wide-open grasslands and savannas of Africa, south of the Sahara Desert. They like places with short grass where they can see their food and walk easily. They do not live in thick forests, because there is not enough room to walk and hunt there.

Unlike many birds, secretary birds spend most of their time on the ground. They can fly, and they fly well, but they prefer to walk. At night, they sleep in trees, safe from animals that hunt on the ground. In the morning, they come down and start walking again.

Secretary birds often live in pairs, and a pair stays together for a long time. They share a home area, which they defend from other secretary birds. When they want to talk to each other, they make croaking sounds. They are not songbirds, so do not expect a sweet tune!

## Building a Nest High Up

When it is time to raise a family, secretary birds build a large nest at the top of a flat-topped acacia tree or a thorny bush. The nest is made of sticks and is lined with softer material. It is big and strong, because the birds will use it again and again.

The female usually lays two eggs. Both parents take turns sitting on the eggs to keep them warm. After the chicks hatch, both parents bring them food. The chicks are covered in soft grey fluff at first. They grow quickly, and after a few months they are ready to leave the nest and start hunting on their own. But learning to stomp snakes takes practice, and young birds watch their parents carefully before trying it themselves.

## Why Secretary Birds Matter

Secretary birds help farmers and people who live near the grasslands. By eating snakes, mice and insects, they keep the numbers of these animals in balance. In places where there are many poisonous snakes, a secretary bird is a welcome neighbour.

Sadly, secretary birds are becoming harder to find in some places. Their grassland homes are being changed into farms and towns, and there is less open space for them to hunt. Some birds are also hurt by poisons meant for other animals. People who work to protect wild animals are trying to keep the grasslands safe so that secretary birds can keep walking, stomping and hunting for many years to come.

## Fun Facts to Remember

Here are some fun facts about secretary birds to share with your friends. A secretary bird can walk for many hours in a single day without getting tired. Its kick is so fast that scientists needed special cameras to study it properly. Even though it spends most of its time on the ground, it is a true bird of prey, just like an eagle. And its fancy head feathers really do look like pens tucked behind its ears, which is how it got its funny name.

Next time you see a picture of a secretary bird, look closely at those long legs and that serious face. This is a bird that works hard every day, walking the grasslands and keeping snakes in check — all while looking like it is dressed for the office.
