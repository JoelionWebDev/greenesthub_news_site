Picture a wide, open plain under a big blue sky. The grass stretches as far as you can see, and far away a herd of cattle is grazing. Riding towards them is a person on a horse, wearing a wide hat, boots, and a big smile. This is a cowboy, and the horse is his best friend and partner. Cowboys are the people who look after cattle on large farms called ranches, mostly in the western parts of the United States and other countries with wide open land. They spend their days riding horses across the range, guiding cattle, mending fences, and working under the sun and the stars. It is a life full of fresh air, hard work, and a very special bond between a rider and a horse.

## Who is a cowboy?

A cowboy is a person who works with cattle on a ranch. Long ago, in the days of the Wild West, cowboys rode across huge open plains to drive herds of cattle from one place to another. The cattle had to be moved to new grass, to water, or to market, and the cowboys guided them on horseback. Today, cowboys still do this work, although they sometimes use trucks and other machines too. A cowboy's main job is to look after the cattle: to make sure they have food and water, to keep them safe, and to bring them together when needed. A cowboy needs to be a good rider, a good judge of animals, and a hard worker. The days are long, the weather can be hot or cold, and the work is never finished. Cowgirls do this work too, and they are just as skilled and brave as the cowboys.

## The cowboy's horse

A cowboy's horse is much more than a way to travel. It is a partner, a friend, and a workmate. Cowboy horses are usually strong and steady, and they are trained to move quickly, turn sharply, and stay calm around cattle. A good cowboy horse knows what to do almost before the rider asks. When a cow tries to run away from the herd, the horse can dash after it and turn it back. When the cowboy needs to rope a calf, the horse stands still and keeps the rope tight. Cowboys and their horses spend so much time together that they learn to understand each other very well. The cowboy learns how the horse moves and what it is thinking, and the horse learns to trust the cowboy completely.

## The cowboy's gear

A cowboy has special gear for life on the range. The wide-brimmed hat, called a Stetson, keeps the sun off the cowboy's face and the rain off his head. The cowboy's boots have a high heel that helps the foot stay in the stirrup. A bandana around the neck can be pulled up over the face to keep out dust when the cattle are on the move. The saddle is the cowboy's seat, and it is strong and comfortable, with a horn at the front for holding a rope. The cowboy also carries a lasso, which is a long rope with a loop at the end, used to catch cattle. Every piece of gear has a job, and a good cowboy knows how to use it all.

## A cowboy's day

A cowboy's day starts before the sun comes up. First, the cowboy feeds and saddles the horse, checking that the saddle is comfortable and the horse is ready to work. Then the cowboy rides out onto the range to check the cattle. The cowboy looks for any animals that are sick or hurt, makes sure they have water, and counts the herd. Sometimes the cowboy rides for many hours, moving cattle from one field to another. Other days are spent mending fences, fixing gates, or branding calves so the ranch knows which cattle belong to it. In the evening, the cowboy rides back to the ranch, feeds the horse, and gives it a good rub down. Then it is time for supper, stories around the fire, and a good night's rest under the stars.

## Working with cattle

Cattle are big animals, and moving them takes patience and skill. A cowboy does not shout or rush the cattle. Instead, the cowboy rides slowly and calmly, guiding the herd with gentle movements. The horse helps by walking around the edge of the herd and turning back any animal that tries to wander off. When a calf gets lost, the cowboy and horse search the range until they find it and bring it back to its mother. Cowboys know their cattle well. They can tell which ones are strong, which ones need extra care, and which ones like to cause trouble. It is a job that needs a quiet voice, a steady hand, and a lot of patience.


## Fun facts about cowboys

- A cowboy's wide hat is called a Stetson, and it protects the rider from sun, rain, and dust.
- Cowboys use a lasso, a long rope with a loop, to catch cattle from horseback.
- A cowboy's boots have high heels to help the foot stay safely in the stirrup.
- The saddle horn at the front of the saddle is used to hold the rope when a cowboy catches a calf.
- Cowgirls do the same ranch work as cowboys, and they are just as skilled and brave.
- Rodeos began as a way for cowboys to show off the real skills they used every day on the ranch.
- Many cowboys say their horse is their best friend, and they spend more time with their horse than with anyone else.

The life of a cowboy is a life of wide skies, open land, and loyal horses. It is hard work, but it is also a life of freedom and friendship. Cowboys remind us that when a person and a horse trust each other, they can do amazing things together. Next time you see a cowboy on a horse, think of the long rides across the range, the quiet mornings with the cattle, and the wonderful partnership between a rider and the horse that carries him home.
