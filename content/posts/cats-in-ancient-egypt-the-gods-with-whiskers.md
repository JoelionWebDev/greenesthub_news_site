# Cats in Ancient Egypt: The Gods With Whiskers

Imagine living in a place where cats are not just pets, but gods. Where harming a cat is against the law, where cats wear golden collars and jewellery, and where people build grand statues in their honour. That place was ancient Egypt, thousands of years ago, along the great River Nile.

Cats have lived alongside people for a very long time, but nowhere were they loved and honoured as much as in ancient Egypt. To the Egyptians, cats were beautiful, mysterious and powerful. They believed cats were connected to the gods, and they treated them with great care and respect. Let us travel back in time and meet the cats of ancient Egypt.

## How Cats Came to Egypt

Long ago, before cats became pets, they were wild animals. As people in Egypt began to farm and store grain, mice and rats came to steal the food. Wild cats came to hunt the mice and rats, and people were glad to have them around. The cats kept the grain safe, and in return, they got plenty of food to eat.

Over time, the wild cats became tamer. They moved closer to people's homes, and people began to feed them and care for them. The cats became part of the family, and people loved their soft fur, their graceful movements and their clever hunting skills. This was the beginning of the friendship between people and cats that continues today.

The Egyptians were so grateful to cats for protecting their food that they began to see them as special gifts from the gods. A cat that kept the mice away was not just a useful animal, it was a blessing.

## Bastet: The Cat Goddess

The most famous cat in ancient Egypt was not a real cat at all. She was a goddess called Bastet. Bastet was shown as a woman with the head of a cat, or sometimes as a cat itself. She was the goddess of home, family, music, dance and joy, and she protected people from illness and bad spirits.

People believed that Bastet watched over their homes and kept them safe. They prayed to her for good health and happiness, and they gave her offerings of food and small statues. Beautiful statues of Bastet were made from bronze, gold and stone, and many of them survive to this day. You can see them in museums all over the world.

Bastet was often shown holding a special musical instrument called a sistrum, which made a rattling sound, and wearing a golden collar. She was gentle and kind, but she could also be fierce when protecting the people she loved, just like a mother cat protecting her kittens.

## Cats in Egyptian Homes

Cats were beloved pets in Egyptian homes. They were given names, just like pets today, and they were treated as part of the family. Egyptian art shows cats sitting under chairs, lying on laps, and playing with children. Some cats even wore collars made of gold and beads, with bells and ornaments.

Cats were so important that when a family cat died, the whole family mourned. People shaved off their eyebrows as a sign of sadness, and the cat was given a proper burial. Some cats were mummified, which means their bodies were carefully preserved with bandages and oils, just like the bodies of important people. Many mummified cats have been found in ancient Egyptian tombs, sometimes buried with their owners.

Egyptians believed that cats would continue to be with them in the afterlife, the life they believed came after death. So a beloved cat might be buried alongside its owner, to keep them company on their journey.

## Protected by Law

Cats were so highly valued in ancient Egypt that harming one was a serious matter. It was against the law to kill a cat, even by accident, and the punishment could be very severe. Stories from the ancient world tell of people who were in great trouble for harming a cat, even when it was not on purpose.

Cats were so precious that Egyptians did not want them to leave Egypt. They tried to keep cats from being taken to other countries, because they believed cats were a treasure that belonged to Egypt.

## Cats in Egyptian Art

The Egyptians loved to draw and carve cats. You can see cats in paintings on the walls of tombs and temples, in statues, and in jewellery. Cats were shown sitting gracefully, hunting birds in the marshes, or playing with their kittens. Some of the most beautiful Egyptian art shows cats with elegant bodies and bright, watchful eyes.

One famous painting shows a cat sitting under a chair, while a family eats dinner. Another shows a cat helping its owner hunt birds in the reeds along the Nile. The artists paid close attention to the way cats moved and looked, and their pictures are so lifelike that we can still enjoy them thousands of years later.

## Cats Today

Cats are no longer worshipped as gods, but they are still some of the most loved animals in the world. Millions of people keep cats as pets, and they enjoy the same things the Egyptians enjoyed: a cat's soft purr, its graceful movements, and its clever, independent spirit.

## Fun Facts About Cats in Ancient Egypt

- The cat goddess Bastet was shown as a woman with the head of a cat.
- Cats protected stored grain by hunting mice and rats.
- It was against the law to kill a cat, even by accident.
- When a family cat died, people shaved off their eyebrows in mourning.
- Some cats were mummified and buried with their owners.
- Cats wore collars of gold and beads, sometimes with bells.

The cats of ancient Egypt were hunters, protectors, pets and gods, all at the same time. They kept the grain safe, they guarded the home, and they won the hearts of a whole civilisation. Next time you see a cat curl up in a sunny spot, remember: thousands of years ago, a cat just like it was honoured as a god.
