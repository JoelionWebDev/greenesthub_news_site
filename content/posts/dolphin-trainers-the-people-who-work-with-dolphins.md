Dolphins are some of the cleverest animals in the sea. They can learn new things quickly, they love to play, and they seem to smile all the time. The people who work closely with them are called dolphin trainers, and their job is one of the most fascinating in the world. A dolphin trainer spends their day in and around the water, feeding dolphins, playing with them, teaching them, and checking that they are healthy and happy.

## What Is a Dolphin Trainer?

A dolphin trainer is a person who looks after dolphins that live in aquariums and marine parks. Some dolphins in these places were born there, and some were rescued after being injured or stranded and could not safely return to the wild. Trainers help care for them every day.

Trainers do many different jobs. They prepare food, usually fish, and feed the dolphins several times a day. They clean the pools and check the water to make sure it is clean and at the right temperature. They watch the dolphins carefully for signs of illness, just as a doctor watches a patient. And they spend time playing and training with the dolphins, which keeps the animals active and their minds busy.

Dolphins are very social animals. In the wild, they live in groups called pods, and they chat to each other with clicks and whistles. In an aquarium, the trainer becomes part of the dolphin's social world. A good trainer knows each dolphin by name, knows what food it likes best, knows which games it enjoys, and can tell when it is feeling playful, tired, or unwell.

## How Do Trainers Train Dolphins?

Training a dolphin is not like training a dog to sit. Dolphins cannot be forced to do anything. Instead, trainers use a kind and clever method called positive reinforcement. That means that when a dolphin does something right, it gets a reward, usually a tasty fish or a favourite toy. The dolphin soon learns that doing the right thing leads to something nice, and it wants to do it again.

Trainers use a special tool called a whistle. When a dolphin does the right thing at exactly the right moment, the trainer blows the whistle. The dolphin learns that the whistle sound means "Yes, that was right, and a reward is coming." This helps the trainer communicate clearly with the dolphin, even under water.

Trainers break every new behaviour into small, easy steps. For example, if they want a dolphin to jump through the air, they start by rewarding it for swimming fast near the surface. Then they reward it for lifting its body a little out of the water. Step by step, the dolphin learns the whole jump. It can take many weeks or even months to teach a new behaviour, and trainers celebrate every small success along the way.

## A Day in the Life of a Dolphin Trainer

A dolphin trainer's day usually starts early in the morning. The first job is to check on the dolphins. The trainer looks at each one carefully. Is it swimming normally? Is it eating well? Does it look bright and cheerful? Trainers keep careful notes about each dolphin, so they can spot any small change straight away.

Next comes feeding time. Dolphins eat a lot of fish, and trainers weigh the fish carefully to make sure each dolphin gets just the right amount. Some dolphins like one kind of fish best, and trainers learn each dolphin's favourites. Feeding is also a good time to give the dolphins their vitamins, hidden inside a fish, just like hiding medicine in a tasty treat.

After feeding, the trainer spends time training and playing. This is when the dolphins practise the behaviours they have learned, learn new ones, and play games. Trainers might toss a ball for a dolphin to fetch, or play a game where the dolphin follows hand signals. Play is not just fun. It is exercise for the body and a workout for the brain, and it keeps the dolphins healthy and happy.

Later in the day, the trainer helps clean the pools, checks the water, and talks to visitors. Many trainers love telling visitors about dolphins and teaching them how to protect the oceans. At the end of the day, there is a final feed, a final health check, and the dolphins settle down to rest. Dolphins sleep in a very special way: one half of their brain sleeps while the other half stays awake, so they can keep swimming and come up to breathe.

## Fun Facts

- Dolphins are mammals, just like us, so they breathe air and must come to the surface to breathe.
- A dolphin can recognise itself in a mirror, which is a sign of how clever it is.
- Dolphins use clicks and whistles to talk to each other, and each dolphin has its own special whistle, a bit like a name.
- Trainers use a whistle to tell a dolphin it has done the right thing at exactly the right moment.
- Dolphins sleep with one half of their brain awake so they can keep swimming and breathing.

## The Friendship Between Trainer and Dolphin

The best part of a dolphin trainer's job is the friendship they build with the dolphins. Over months and years, a trainer and a dolphin get to know each other very well. The dolphin learns to trust the trainer, and the trainer learns to read the dolphin's moods. Sometimes a dolphin will swim up to its trainer just to say hello, or bring a toy to start a game. These moments show how deep the bond can be.

Dolphin trainers remind us that animals are not just creatures to look at. They are clever, feeling beings that can learn, play, and form friendships. By caring for dolphins with patience and kindness, trainers help these wonderful sea animals live happy lives, and they inspire all of us to care for the oceans and the animals that live in them.
