## Meet the Armadillo

Imagine an animal wearing a suit of armour, with a long nose, big ears, and strong claws for digging. That is an armadillo! Armadillos live in the Americas, from the southern United States all the way down to South America. They are quiet, shy animals that spend most of their time sniffing the ground and digging for food.

The word "armadillo" comes from Spanish, and it means "little armoured one." That is a perfect name, because an armadillo's most famous feature is its shell. The shell is made of hard plates, a bit like a suit of armour, and it covers the armadillo's back, head, and tail. Underneath the shell, the armadillo has soft skin and fur, just like other mammals.

There are many kinds of armadillos, and they come in different sizes. Some are small, about the size of a rabbit. Others are much bigger, as big as a medium dog. But they all share the same armour and the same love of digging.

## A Suit of Armour

An armadillo's shell is not like a turtle's shell. A turtle's shell is one solid piece, but an armadillo's shell is made of many bony plates, joined together by flexible skin. This lets the armadillo bend and move more easily than a turtle can.

In the middle of the shell, there are bands that work a bit like the folds of a concertina, letting the armadillo curl and stretch. The shell is covered with a thin layer of skin and tiny hairs. It is tough, but it is not unbreakable. It is there to protect the armadillo from thorns, bites, and scratches as it pushes through bushes and digs in the dirt.

Not all armadillos can roll into a ball, though. The kind that can is famous for it, but other kinds have different tricks for staying safe, like running away fast or jumping straight up into the air to surprise a hunter.

## Champion Diggers

Armadillos are brilliant diggers. Their front feet have long, strong, curved claws that work like shovels. They can dig a burrow quickly, sometimes in just a few minutes, when they need to escape danger or find a place to sleep.

Armadillos dig burrows to live in, and they dig holes to find food. They sniff the ground with their long noses, listening and smelling for insects hiding under the soil. When they find a good spot, they dig with their claws, tossing dirt behind them like a little furry bulldozer.

An armadillo's burrow is a cosy underground home. It has a tunnel leading down to a sleeping chamber, lined with dry leaves and grass. The burrow stays cool on hot days and warm on cool nights, and it keeps the armadillo safe from hunters.

Armadillos often have more than one burrow, and they move between them. Old armadillo burrows are useful to other animals too. Rabbits, snakes, and even small owls sometimes move into an empty armadillo burrow, because it is already dug and ready to live in.

## What Do Armadillos Eat?

Armadillos mostly eat insects. Their favourite foods are ants, termites, beetles, and grubs. They use their long, sticky tongues to lick up ants and termites, a bit like an anteater does.

Armadillos also eat earthworms, snails, and sometimes fruit, berries, and roots. They are not fussy eaters, and they will try many different foods that they find while digging.

An armadillo's sense of smell is excellent. It can smell an insect hiding deep under the ground, even when it cannot see it.

## Baby Armadillos

Baby armadillos are called pups, and they are born in the safety of the burrow. When they are born, their shells are soft and pink, a bit like soft leather. As they grow, their shells get harder and stronger, until they are proper armour.

Mother armadillos feed their pups with milk, just like other mammals. The pups stay in the burrow with their mother for the first few weeks, and then they start to follow her out at night, learning how to dig and find food.

One of the most amazing facts about armadillos is that some kinds almost always have four identical babies at a time.

Young armadillos grow quickly, and by the time they are a few months old, they are ready to dig their own burrows and live on their own. But they always stay near the places where their mothers taught them to find food.

## Staying Safe

Armadillos are shy and gentle animals, and they do not want to fight anyone. When they sense danger, their first plan is usually to run. Even though they look clumsy, armadillos can run quite fast, and they can jump straight up into the air when they are startled. That jump can surprise a hunter and give the armadillo time to run away.

If running does not work, an armadillo can dig. It can dig a hole and bury itself in seconds, leaving only its hard shell showing. Or it can run into its burrow and hide deep underground, where most hunters cannot follow.

## The Little Armoured One

Armadillos may be shy and quiet, but they are full of surprises. They wear armour, dig like champions, eat with a sticky tongue, and some of them can even roll into a ball. They are the little armoured ones of the Americas, and they do a big job keeping insect numbers down and turning over the soil.

Next time you see a picture of an armadillo, look closely at its shell, its claws, and its long nose. Every part of it is made for a life of digging, sniffing, and staying safe, and that makes it one of nature's cleverest little engineers.
