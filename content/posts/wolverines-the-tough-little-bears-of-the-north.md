High in the cold forests and snowy mountains of the far north lives an animal that looks like a small bear, but is really a member of the weasel family. It is called the wolverine, and it has one of the biggest reputations of any animal its size. Wolverines are not very big, but they are so strong, brave and stubborn that even bears and wolves sometimes step aside and let them eat first. In the cold forests of the far north lives a small animal with a huge reputation, a tough little hunter that can chase bigger animals away from their food.

## A Small Bear That Is Not a Bear

The first thing you notice about a wolverine is that it looks like a small, shaggy bear. It has a broad head, small round ears, short legs and a thick coat of dark brown fur with a pale stripe along each side. But the wolverine is not a bear at all. It belongs to the same family as weasels, otters and badgers, and it is the biggest land animal in that whole family.

A grown wolverine is about the size of a medium dog, but it is much heavier and stronger than it looks. Its body is packed with muscle, and its paws are big and wide, like snowshoes, which help it walk on top of deep snow without sinking. Its claws are long and curved, perfect for digging and climbing. Wolverines can climb trees, swim across cold rivers and dig through hard, frozen ground. There is very little in the northern forest that can stop a wolverine.

## The Strongest Jaws in the North

Wolverines are famous for their amazing strength. Their jaws are so powerful that they can crush bones and bite through frozen meat that other animals cannot even chew. This is very useful in winter, when food is hard to find and the only meals around may be animals that died long ago and froze solid in the snow.

A wolverine's teeth are sharp and strong, and its neck and shoulders are full of muscle. It can drag a whole animal much heavier than itself across the snow to a safe place to eat. People who study wolverines say that one wolverine can pull a dead deer, which may weigh far more than the wolverine itself, up a steep snowy hill. That is like a child dragging a whole grown-up up a hill, and it shows just how much power is packed into this furry little animal.

## A Traveller That Never Stays Still

Wolverines are great travellers. A single wolverine may roam over a huge area of forest and mountain, walking many miles every day in search of food. In the summer it may climb high into the mountains, and in the winter it may travel down into the valleys. Some wolverines have been known to walk farther in a few days than most people walk in a whole month.

They do not build a fixed home. Instead, they sleep wherever they stop, curled up in a hollow log, under a rock, or in a snow cave they dig themselves. A mother wolverine digs a special den deep in the snow to keep her babies warm and safe. The den may have long tunnels, like a little snow house, where the babies stay hidden while their mother goes out to find food.

Wolverines live alone for most of their lives. Each wolverine has its own large territory, and it marks the trees and rocks with its scent to tell other wolverines to keep away. They only meet other wolverines when it is time to raise a family, and then they go back to their lonely wandering ways.

## A Hunter and a Cleaner of the Forest

Wolverines eat many different things. They hunt small animals such as mice, squirrels and hares, and they also eat birds' eggs, berries and roots when they can find them. But they are best known as scavengers, which means they eat animals that are already dead. This may sound a bit yucky, but it is a very important job. By eating dead animals, wolverines help keep the forest clean and stop sickness from spreading.

Their amazing sense of smell helps them find food buried deep under the snow. A wolverine can smell a dead animal from far away, even when it is covered by a thick blanket of snow. Then it digs down through the snow and ice to reach its meal. In the cold north, where food can be very hard to find in winter, this skill can mean the difference between life and death.

## Why Wolverines Need Wild Places

Wolverines need huge areas of wild, quiet land to live in, because each one needs so much space to find enough food. They live in the cold north of Canada, Alaska, Russia and northern Europe, in places where the winters are long and snowy. They do not like busy places with lots of people, roads and towns, because roads and buildings break up the wild land they need to travel across.

In some places, wolverines have become rare because their wild homes have been broken up by roads and towns. People are working to protect the forests and mountains where they live, and to build safe paths for animals to cross under or over busy roads. In some countries, wolverines are protected by law, so no one is allowed to hunt them.

## A Little Animal with a Big Heart

The wolverine reminds us that you do not have to be the biggest animal to be one of the toughest. With its strong jaws, its wide snowshoe paws and its never-give-up spirit, the wolverine survives in some of the coldest, hardest places on Earth. It is a true survivor of the north, and a wonderful example of how clever and brave wild animals can be.

Next time you hear the name wolverine, do not think of a superhero from a film. Think of the real wolverine, the tough little bear of the north, padding silently through the snow, searching for its next meal in a world of ice and pine trees.
