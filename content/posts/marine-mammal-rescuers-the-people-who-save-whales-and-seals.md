Sometimes, animals of the sea get into trouble. A whale may become stranded on a beach, a seal pup may be found alone and hungry, or a dolphin may be caught in a fishing net. When this happens, special heroes rush to help — they are called marine mammal rescuers!

Marine mammal rescuers are brave, caring people who save whales, dolphins, seals, and other sea animals. They work day and night, in all kinds of weather, to give these animals a second chance at life in the wild sea. Let us meet these ocean heroes and find out how they save lives.

## Who Are Marine Mammal Rescuers?

Marine mammal rescuers are people who are trained to help sea animals in trouble. They work for rescue centres, aquariums, and wildlife groups, and they are often volunteers who give their time to help animals. They are vets, animal carers, and ordinary people who love the sea and its creatures.

When someone finds a stranded whale or an injured seal, they call the rescue team. The rescuers hurry to the scene with special equipment — stretchers, buckets, towels, and medicine — ready to help. They work as a team, and they know exactly what to do to keep the animal safe and comfortable.

Rescuers need to be brave, strong, and patient. Working with a stranded whale is hard work, and it can be dangerous too. But rescuers love what they do, and they are always ready to help an animal in need.

## Saving a Stranded Whale

One of the hardest jobs for a marine mammal rescuer is helping a stranded whale. Sometimes, a whale swims into shallow water and gets stuck on the beach. This is very dangerous for the whale, because it is too heavy to move on land, and it can quickly become too hot and dry in the sun.

When rescuers arrive, they work quickly to keep the whale cool and wet. They pour buckets of seawater over the whale's skin, cover it with wet towels, and dig little trenches around it to let the water in. They also keep people away, so that the whale is not frightened or disturbed.

If the tide comes in and the water is deep enough, the rescuers may be able to guide the whale back out to sea. They use boats, ropes, and gentle hands to help the whale swim away. It is a wonderful moment when a rescued whale swims off into the deep water, free at last!

## Looking After Seal Pups

Seal pups are another animal that rescuers often help. Sometimes, a young seal is found alone on a beach, cold, hungry, and far from its mother. The pup may be sick or injured, and it needs help to survive.

Rescuers gently pick up the seal pup and take it to a rescue centre, where it is given warm shelter, food, and medicine. The carers feed the pup special milk and fish, and they look after it until it is strong and healthy enough to go back to the sea.

At the rescue centre, the seal pups live in pools of clean seawater, where they can swim and play. The carers are careful not to let the pups become too used to people, because they need to be wild animals when they go back to the sea. When a pup is ready, it is taken to a quiet beach and set free, where it swims away into the waves. It is a happy day for everyone!

## Helping Dolphins and Other Sea Animals

Marine mammal rescuers also help dolphins, porpoises, and other sea animals. Sometimes, a dolphin becomes tangled in a fishing net or caught in a rope, and it needs help to get free. Rescuers work carefully to cut the animal free without hurting it, and then they check that it is healthy before letting it swim away.

Rescuers also help sea animals that have swallowed plastic or other rubbish from the sea. This is very sad, because plastic in the ocean can hurt sea animals badly. Rescuers treat the sick animal, and they also work to teach people about keeping the seas clean, so that fewer animals get hurt.


## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about marine mammal rescuers:

- Rescuers pour buckets of seawater over stranded whales to keep them cool and wet.
- Seal pups are looked after at rescue centres until they are strong enough to return to the sea.
- Rescuers help dolphins that are tangled in fishing nets or ropes.
- A rescued animal that swims back into the wild is called a release, and it is a happy day!
- Rescuers teach people to keep the seas clean, so that fewer sea animals get hurt.
- Many rescuers are volunteers who give their time to help sea animals.
- Rescue centres have pools of clean seawater where seal pups can swim and play.
- Whales, dolphins, and seals are all marine mammals, which means they breathe air like we do.

## Why Marine Mammal Rescuers Are Special

Marine mammal rescuers are special because they are heroes of the ocean. They rush to help whales, dolphins, and seals in trouble, and they give these wonderful animals a second chance at life. Their work is brave, caring, and very important, and it helps to keep our seas full of life.

They also teach us an important lesson: that we must look after our oceans and keep them clean, so that sea animals can live safely and happily. If we all help to keep plastic and rubbish out of the sea, fewer animals will need rescuing in the first place.

So next time you visit the beach, look out to sea and think of the whales, dolphins, and seals swimming in the waves — and of the brave rescuers who are always ready to help them. They are true ocean heroes, and the sea is a better place because of them.
