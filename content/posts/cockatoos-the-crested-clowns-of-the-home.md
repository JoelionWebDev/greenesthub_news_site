Have you ever seen a bird that can dance, laugh, wave and show off a fancy feather crown whenever it gets excited? That is a cockatoo! Cockatoos are playful parrots with a tall crest of feathers on their heads that pops up like a crown. They are known as the clowns of the parrot world because they love to make people laugh with their silly tricks.

## Meet the Cockatoo

Cockatoos come from Australia, Indonesia and nearby islands in the South Pacific. In the wild, they live in big, noisy flocks and fly across forests and open fields in search of food. They are strong flyers and very good climbers, using their beaks and feet to scramble up branches.

There are many kinds of cockatoos. The sulphur-crested cockatoo is mostly white with a bright yellow crest, like a splash of sunshine on its head. The umbrella cockatoo is all white, with a big crest that fans out like an umbrella when it is excited. The galah, also called the rose-breasted cockatoo, is soft grey and pink, and is one of the most common parrots in Australia. The black palm cockatoo is dark grey with bright red cheeks and a huge beak.

A cockatoo is a medium to large parrot. It has a strong, curved beak, a short tail, and strong feet with toes that can grip things tightly. Its most famous feature is its crest. When a cockatoo is calm, the crest lies flat. When it is excited, surprised or showing off, the crest pops straight up. Watching a cockatoo raise its crest is like watching someone put on a party hat!

## The Silliest Birds Around

Cockatoos are famous for their sense of humour. They love to dance, bob their heads up and down, spin in circles and flap their wings to music. Some cockatoos even seem to keep time with a beat, rocking from side to side as if they are at a party. 

Cockatoos are also very clever. They can learn to open locks, unscrew things and solve simple puzzles. If a cockatoo gets bored, it will find its own fun — sometimes by taking apart something it should not! That is why they need plenty of toys and attention every day.

Many cockatoos can learn to talk. They might learn words like "hello," "goodbye" and their own name, and some learn to whistle tunes. A cockatoo might copy the sound of a doorbell, a phone ringing, or even your laughter.

## Cuddly Companions

Cockatoos are some of the most loving parrots you can meet. They adore being close to their people, and many of them love to cuddle, snuggle under your chin, and have their heads scratched. A happy cockatoo will close its eyes, fluff up its feathers and lean into your hand like a cat asking for a pat.

In the wild, cockatoos spend their days with their flock, grooming each other and staying close. A pet cockatoo sees its human family as its flock, so it wants to be with you as much as possible. This means cockatoos need a lot of time and attention. A lonely cockatoo can become sad and bored, so families who keep one need to be ready to spend lots of time playing and talking with it every day.

## What Do Cockatoos Eat?

In the wild, cockatoos eat seeds, nuts, fruits, berries, roots and leafy greens. They use their strong beaks to crack open seeds and nuts, and they are very good at finding food on the ground and in the trees.

A pet cockatoo needs a healthy, varied diet. Good foods include special parrot pellets, fresh vegetables like broccoli, carrots and sweet potatoes, and fruits like apples, pears and berries. Seeds and nuts can be part of the diet, but only in small amounts, because too many can make a cockatoo unhealthy. Fresh water should always be available, and food and water bowls need to be cleaned every day. Grown-ups should always check that a new food is safe before giving it to a cockatoo. Foods like chocolate, avocado, and salty or sugary snacks are not safe for parrots and should never be offered.

## A Cockatoo's Home and Care

Cockatoos need a large, strong cage with room to stretch their wings, climb and play. The cage bars need to be sturdy, because cockatoos are strong chewers and can bend weak bars. Inside, they need perches of different sizes, and lots of toys — wooden toys to chew, ropes to climb, bells to ring and puzzles to solve. Toys should be changed often, so the cockatoo always has something new to explore.

A cockatoo should also spend time outside its cage every day in a safe room with a grown-up watching. Flying, climbing on a play stand and sitting on a family member's arm are all wonderful fun. Before letting a cockatoo out, make sure windows and doors are closed, and that nothing dangerous is within reach.

Cockatoos can live for a long time — many live for decades — so bringing one home is a big, long promise. That is why the whole family should be part of the decision, and why grown-ups should always be in charge of the bird's care. A pet cockatoo needs gentle handling every day, and children should always handle one with an adult nearby. Cockatoos also need regular visits to a bird vet, called an avian vet, to stay healthy.

## Fun Facts About Cockatoos

- A cockatoo can raise and lower its crest to show how it feels — up means excited, happy or surprised.
- Cockatoos are excellent dancers. Some have been seen bobbing their heads and swaying in time to music.
- The palm cockatoo uses sticks as tools, tapping them against tree trunks to make a drumming sound.
- Galahs are so common in Australia that huge pink and grey flocks can fill the sky at sunset.
- A cockatoo's beak is strong enough to crack open a hard walnut with ease.

Cockatoos are clowns, dancers, cuddlers and clever friends all rolled into one feathery package. If you ever meet one, get ready to laugh — it will probably make you giggle before you even say hello!
