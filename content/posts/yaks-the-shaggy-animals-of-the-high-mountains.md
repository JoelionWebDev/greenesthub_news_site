High up in the cold mountains, where the air is thin and the snow is deep, lives a big, shaggy animal with long hair and curved horns. It is the yak. Yaks are strong, tough animals that can live in places where most other farm animals would be too cold. They give people milk, wool, and transport, and they are a very important part of mountain life. Let us climb up and meet the yak.

## What Is a Yak?

A yak is a large mammal related to cattle and buffalo. It has a big, heavy body, short legs, and a long, shaggy coat that hangs down almost to the ground. The coat is usually dark brown or black, and it is very thick and warm. Under the long outer hair, yaks have a soft, woolly undercoat that keeps them warm in freezing weather. They also have curved horns, a humped back, and a bushy tail.

Yaks are built for the cold. Their thick coat protects them from wind and snow, and their big lungs help them to breathe in the thin mountain air. They have strong legs and wide hooves that help them to walk on rocky, snowy ground. Yaks are calm and gentle animals, but they are also very strong. They can carry heavy loads up steep mountain paths. Yaks are smart, too, and they know their owners and their home. They are not fast runners, but they are steady and sure-footed, which is perfect for mountain life.

## Where Do Yaks Live?

Yaks live in the high mountains of Central Asia, especially in the Himalayas, which are the highest mountains in the world. They live in places such as Tibet, Nepal, and parts of China and Mongolia. These areas are cold for much of the year, with snow, wind, and thin air. Yaks are perfectly suited to this harsh home, because their thick coats and strong bodies keep them warm and safe.

Yaks live on mountain pastures, which are grassy areas high up in the mountains. In summer, they graze on green grass and herbs. In winter, they move to lower pastures where there is less snow and more food. Yak herders, people who look after yaks, move with their herds, following the seasons. Yaks live in herds, and they stay together for warmth and safety. They rest in shelters or in the open, huddled together to keep warm. A yak's home is a tough, beautiful place, full of mountains, valleys, and wide open skies.

## What Do Yaks Eat?

Yaks are herbivores, which means they eat plants. Their main food is grass, and they also eat herbs, leaves, and mosses that grow in the mountains. In summer, when the grass is green and plentiful, yaks eat a lot and grow strong. In winter, when the ground is covered in snow, food is harder to find. Yaks use their horns and hooves to dig through snow to find grass underneath. They are very good at finding food, even in the coldest months.

Yaks also need to drink water, and they often drink from mountain streams and rivers. In winter, they may eat snow for water. Baby yaks, called calves, drink their mother's milk when they are young. As they grow, they start to eat grass and plants, just like adult yaks. Yaks chew their food slowly, and they often chew the cud, which means they bring food back up to chew it again. This helps them to digest tough mountain plants and to get as much goodness as possible from their food.

## Baby Yaks

Baby yaks are called calves. When a calf is born, it is small and covered in soft, fluffy hair. It stays close to its mother, who keeps it warm with her body and feeds it with milk. The mother yak is very protective and will keep her calf safe from cold, wind, and danger. Calves can stand and walk soon after birth, which is important in the mountains, where they need to move with the herd.

Calves are playful and curious. They like to run, jump, and explore the mountain pastures near their mothers. They play with other calves and learn how to graze and how to move on rocky ground. As they grow, their coats become thicker and shaggier, and their horns start to grow. They stay with their mothers for many months, learning and growing. When they are older, young yaks can help to carry loads and work with herders, just like adult yaks. Growing up in the mountains makes them strong and tough.

## Fun Facts About Yaks

Yaks have many fun facts. One is that their long, shaggy hair hangs down like a skirt, and it keeps their legs warm in the snow. Their hair is so long that it almost touches the ground. Yaks also have a special kind of wool, called yak wool, which is very soft and warm. People use yak wool to make clothes, blankets, and ropes. Yak wool is one of the warmest wools in the world.

Another fun fact is that yaks can grunt, which is why they are sometimes called grunting oxen. They make a low, grunting sound, especially when they are happy or when they are calling to each other. Yaks are also very good at climbing. They can walk up steep, rocky paths that would be difficult for other animals. Their wide hooves grip the ground well, and their strong legs help them to carry heavy loads. Yaks are truly mountain champions.

## How Yaks Help People

Yaks help people in the high mountains in many ways. They give milk, which people drink and use to make butter, cheese, and yoghurt. Yak butter is very important in mountain life, and it is used in food and in tea. Yaks also give wool, which is used to make warm clothes, tents, and blankets. Their dung is used as fuel for fires and as fertiliser for crops, because wood is hard to find in the high mountains.

Yaks also help with transport. They carry loads up mountain paths, bringing food, clothes, and other goods to villages that are hard to reach. They are strong and steady, and they can walk for a long time without tiring. Looking after yaks teaches herders to be patient and kind. A yak needs food, water, shelter, and care every day. When we treat yaks well, they give us milk, wool, and help. They are true friends of the mountain people.
