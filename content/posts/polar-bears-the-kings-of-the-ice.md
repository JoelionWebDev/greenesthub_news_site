## Meet the Polar Bear

Far away in the cold, white world of the Arctic lives a huge, beautiful bear with thick white fur. That is the polar bear! Polar bears are the largest land hunters on Earth, and they are perfectly made for life on the ice and snow.

An adult male polar bear can weigh as much as a small car, and it can stand taller than a grown-up person when it rises up on its back legs. Female polar bears are smaller, but they are still very big and strong. Under all that white fur, a polar bear's skin is actually black, which helps it soak up warmth from the sun.

Polar bears live in the Arctic, around the North Pole, in places like Canada, Russia, Norway, Greenland, and Alaska. They spend most of their life on the sea ice, which is frozen ocean water. The sea ice is their home, their hunting ground, and their highway.

## Made for the Cold

Polar bears are built for the cold in amazing ways. They have two layers of fur: a thick, fluffy layer close to their skin, and a longer layer on top. Their fur looks white, but each hair is actually clear and hollow, and it reflects light so the bear looks white against the snow.

Under their fur, polar bears have a thick layer of fat, called blubber, which keeps them warm like a blanket and gives them energy when food is hard to find. Their small ears and short tail help them lose less heat, and their large, furry paws work like snowshoes, spreading their weight so they do not sink into the snow.

A polar bear's paws are also great for swimming. They have partly webbed toes, which help them paddle through icy water. Polar bears are excellent swimmers, and they can swim for many hours at a time. They are sometimes called sea bears, because they spend so much time in the water.

## Hunting on the Ice

Polar bears are hunters, and their favourite food is seals. Seals live in the Arctic ocean, and they come up to the surface to breathe through holes in the ice. A polar bear will wait quietly by a seal's breathing hole, sometimes for hours, until a seal pops up for air. Then it pounces!

Polar bears also hunt seals that are resting on the ice. They move slowly and quietly, using snow and ice to hide, and then they rush forward with a burst of speed. A polar bear is a carnivore, which means it eats meat, and seals give it the fat and energy it needs to survive in the cold.

A polar bear's sense of smell is amazing. It can smell a seal from far away, even under thick snow and ice. Scientists think a polar bear can smell a seal that is many kilometres away. That is like smelling your dinner cooking from the other side of town!

## A Polar Bear Family

A mother polar bear usually has two cubs at a time, and sometimes one or three. The cubs are born in winter, in a den that the mother digs in the snow. When they are born, they are tiny, about the size of a small puppy, and their eyes are closed.

The mother stays in the den with her cubs all winter, keeping them warm with her body and feeding them with her rich milk. She does not eat during this time, and she lives off the fat she stored up before winter. That is a long time to go without food, but a mother polar bear is very strong and brave.

In spring, the mother and her cubs come out of the den. The cubs are bigger now, and they are ready to explore the icy world. They stay with their mother for about two years, learning how to hunt, how to swim, and how to stay safe on the ice. They learn by watching her and by playing in the snow.

## Playing in the Snow

Polar bear cubs love to play! They wrestle with each other, slide down snowy slopes, and splash in icy water. Playing is fun, but it is also how cubs learn to be strong and clever hunters. They practise pouncing, chasing, and climbing, just like kittens play at home.

Adult polar bears also enjoy playing sometimes. They may roll in the snow to clean their fur, or splash in the water on a warm summer day.

## Life on the Sea Ice

The sea ice is the most important thing in a polar bear's life. It is where they hunt, where they travel, and where they rest. In winter, the sea ice grows bigger, and polar bears can walk far out over the frozen ocean to find seals. In summer, some of the ice melts, and polar bears may spend time on land, waiting for the ice to return.

The Arctic is a cold and harsh place, but polar bears have lived there for thousands of years. They are a symbol of the Arctic, and they are one of the most loved animals in the world.

## Polar Bears Need Our Help

Sadly, polar bears are in danger because the Arctic is getting warmer. As the world warms up, the sea ice melts earlier in spring and freezes later in autumn. This means polar bears have less time to hunt seals, and they have to go longer without food.

When polar bears cannot find enough food, they become thinner and weaker, and mothers have a harder time looking after their cubs. People are working hard to protect polar bears by protecting the Arctic and by trying to slow down the warming of our planet.

You can help polar bears by learning about them, telling your friends about them, and looking after our planet. Small things, like saving energy and caring for nature, can help the Arctic stay cold and icy for polar bears.

Next time you see a picture of a polar bear walking across the white ice, remember how strong, clever, and beautiful it is. With its thick white fur, its amazing sense of smell, and its love of the cold, the polar bear is truly the king of the ice. What a wonderful Arctic superstar!
