The sea is full of amazing animals, from tiny fish that glow in the dark to giant whales that sing beautiful songs. But who studies all these creatures and learns how they live? Marine biologists do. A marine biologist is a scientist who studies animals and plants that live in the sea. They want to know how sea animals eat, where they travel, how they have babies, and how they stay safe in the big, wide ocean. Their work helps us understand the sea better, and it helps us protect the animals that live there. Without marine biologists, we would know very little about the wonderful world under the waves.

## What is a marine biologist?

A marine biologist is a scientist who studies life in the sea. The word marine means "of the sea," and biology is the study of living things. So a marine biologist studies living things in the sea. Some marine biologists study big animals like whales, dolphins, and sharks. Others study small animals like crabs, starfish, and tiny fish that live on coral reefs. Some study sea plants, like seaweed and the tiny green plants that float in the water. A marine biologist might work in one place for many years, learning all about one animal, or they might travel all over the world to study many different sea creatures. What they all share is a love of the sea and a wish to understand the animals that call it home.

## Where do marine biologists work?

Marine biologists work in many different places. Some work on boats, sailing out into the ocean to watch whales and dolphins. Some work on beaches and rocky shores, looking at the animals that live in tide pools when the sea goes out. Some work in aquariums, where they look after sea animals and help visitors learn about them. Others work in laboratories, where they look at tiny sea creatures through microscopes and test samples of sea water. Some marine biologists even dive deep under the sea wearing special suits and breathing tanks, so they can see fish, corals, and other sea life up close. A few work in very cold places near the poles, studying penguins, seals, and the animals that live under the ice.

## What do marine biologists study?

Marine biologists study many things about sea animals. They study what animals eat and how they find their food. They study how animals move, like how a whale swims thousands of miles across the ocean, or how a tiny turtle finds its way back to the beach where it was born. They study how sea animals talk to each other, because many whales and dolphins make sounds to call their friends and families. They also study the homes of sea animals, like coral reefs, rocky shores, and the deep, dark ocean floor. By learning these things, marine biologists can tell us what sea animals need to live happy and healthy lives.

## How do marine biologists study animals?

Marine biologists use lots of clever tools to study sea animals. They use cameras that can take pictures deep under the water, so they can see animals they cannot reach. They use boats with special sound machines that can hear whales singing far away. They put small tags on animals, like sharks and turtles, so they can follow where the animals swim. These tags send signals that tell the scientists where the animal has been. Marine biologists also count animals, watch how they behave, and write down everything they see in notebooks. Sometimes they gently catch an animal, measure it, check that it is healthy, and then let it go again. They are always careful to be kind to the animals and to leave them safe in their homes.

## A day in the life of a marine biologist

A marine biologist's day can be very exciting, and no two days are the same. In the morning, a marine biologist might get on a boat and sail out to sea to look for dolphins. They watch the dolphins jump and play, and they take photos to tell one dolphin from another. At lunch, they might eat on the boat while they write down what they saw. In the afternoon, they might go back to the laboratory to look at water samples and check for tiny animals that are too small to see with just our eyes. Some days, a marine biologist might walk along a beach at low tide, turning over rocks to find crabs, snails, and small fish hiding underneath. Other days, they might talk to school children about the sea, or write a report about what they have learned. It is a job full of fresh air, adventure, and discovery.

## Why marine biologists matter

Marine biologists do very important work for our planet. The sea is home to more animals than anywhere else on Earth, and many of them need our help. Some sea animals are in danger because their homes are being hurt, or because the sea is getting warmer and dirtier. Marine biologists find out what is wrong and tell people how to help. They help to make safe places in the sea, called marine reserves, where animals can live without being disturbed. They help fishermen catch fish in a way that leaves enough fish in the sea for the future. When we listen to marine biologists, we can protect the sea for the animals and for ourselves.

## Fun facts about marine biologists

- Marine biologists study the sea, which covers most of our planet.
- Some marine biologists dive under the sea to watch animals in their own homes.
- Marine biologists use sound machines to listen to whales singing under the water.
- Some marine biologists study animals so small you need a microscope to see them.
- Marine biologists help to protect sea animals and keep the ocean clean and safe.

Marine biologists are like detectives of the sea, finding answers about the amazing animals that live under the waves. They dive, sail, watch, and listen, all so that we can understand whales, dolphins, sharks, corals, and tiny fish a little better. The next time you visit the beach or watch a film about the ocean, remember the marine biologists who work hard to learn about sea animals and keep them safe. Maybe one day, you will join them on a boat, looking out at the blue sea and waiting for a whale to leap out of the water.
