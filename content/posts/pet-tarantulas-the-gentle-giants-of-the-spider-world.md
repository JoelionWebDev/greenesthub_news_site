# Pet Tarantulas: The Gentle Giants of the Spider World

Eight legs, a furry body and a name that sounds a little bit scary — tarantulas might seem like the last animal you would want as a pet. But look closer, and you will find a calm, quiet and truly fascinating creature. Many tarantulas kept as pets are gentle and slow-moving, and watching one build its home, hunt its dinner or shed its old skin is like watching a tiny nature show in your own room.

## Meet the Tarantula

Tarantulas are spiders — but they are very different from the little spiders you might find in a corner of your house. They are much bigger, with thick, hairy legs and a body that can be as wide as your palm. Their "fur" is not really fur at all. It is made of tiny hairs that help them feel vibrations and sense the world around them.

Tarantulas live in many warm parts of the world, including South America, Africa, Asia and Australia. In the wild, some live in burrows underground, while others live in trees or hide under rocks and logs. There are hundreds of kinds of tarantulas, and they come in many colours — brown, black, blue, red, orange and even purple.

One of the most popular pet tarantulas is the Chilean rose tarantula, a calm, dusty-pink spider that is often recommended for people who are new to keeping spiders. Another favourite is the Mexican red-knee tarantula, which has bright orange-red stripes on its knees, like it is wearing colourful socks. The curly hair tarantula is covered in long, wavy hairs that make it look extra fluffy.

## Gentle Giants

Tarantulas get the nickname "gentle giants" because, even though they are the biggest spiders in the world, many pet species are calm and slow. They do not jump around, they do not make noise, and they spend most of their time sitting quietly in their home.

Tarantulas do have fangs and venom, which they use to catch their food. For the tarantulas commonly kept as pets, a bite is very rare, and it is usually compared to a bee sting. Still, tarantulas are wild animals, and they should be treated with respect. They are best watched, not handled, and any handling should only be done by an adult who knows how to do it safely — or not at all.

## Growing a New Skin

One of the most amazing things about tarantulas is that they moult. A tarantula's body is covered by a hard outer shell, called an exoskeleton, which does not grow with the spider. So when a tarantula grows too big for its old shell, it sheds it and climbs out in a brand-new, bigger one!

Before a moult, a tarantula may stop eating, become quiet and lie on its back. This can look worrying, but lying on its back is normal — it is how the spider pushes its way out of the old skin. After moulting, the tarantula is soft and pale, and it needs peace and quiet while its new skin hardens. The old skin that is left behind looks like a perfect, empty copy of the spider — legs, fangs and all!

## What Do Tarantulas Eat?

Tarantulas are hunters. In the wild, they eat insects, and the biggest ones may occasionally catch small creatures like frogs or mice. A pet tarantula eats insects such as crickets, cockroaches and mealworms, which can be bought from a pet shop. The insects should be smaller than the spider, and they should be offered once or twice a week.

A tarantula does not chew its food like you do. It uses its fangs to give its prey a bite, and then it uses special juices to turn the inside of its prey into a kind of soup, which it drinks. Tarantulas also need a shallow dish of fresh, clean water. Some tarantulas drink from it, and it also helps keep the air in their home a little bit damp, which tarantulas like. The water dish should be shallow so the spider cannot fall in, and it should be cleaned and refilled often.

## A Tarantula's Home and Care

A pet tarantula needs a home that is safe and cosy. Its tank, called a terrarium or vivarium, should have a secure lid, because tarantulas are good climbers and can escape through a loose lid. The bottom of the tank should be covered with a soft layer of soil, coconut fibre or peat moss, deep enough for a burrowing tarantula to dig in. Tarantulas like a hiding place, such as a piece of bark, a half-buried pot or a small cave, where they can rest in the dark. A few fake plants can make the tank look like a little jungle. The tank should be kept in a warm room, away from bright sunlight and cold draughts. Grown-ups should check the temperature and humidity to make sure the spider is comfortable. Tarantulas should not be handled often. They are delicate — a fall from even a small height can hurt them badly, because their bodies are soft under that hard shell. The best way to enjoy a tarantula is to watch it through the glass of its tank. If a tarantula seems sick or has trouble moulting, a vet who knows about spiders and other small animals can help.

## Fun Facts About Tarantulas

- Tarantulas do not use webs to catch food like many other spiders. Most are hunters that creep up on their prey.
- A tarantula's feet have tiny claws and sticky hairs that help it climb smooth glass.
- Some tarantulas can go for weeks without eating, especially before they moult.
- The Goliath birdeater is the biggest tarantula in the world, with legs that can span wider than a dinner plate.
- A tarantula's shed skin is called an exuvia, and it looks like a perfect empty spider.

Tarantulas may look scary at first, but they are calm, curious and full of surprises. They remind us that even the creatures we fear can be gentle — and that the spider world is far more wonderful than most people think!
