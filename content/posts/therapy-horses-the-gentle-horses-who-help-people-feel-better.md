Horses are big, strong animals. They can run fast, jump high, and carry people on their backs. But some horses have a very special job that has nothing to do with racing or jumping. They are therapy horses, and their job is to help people feel better.

A therapy horse is a horse that has been specially trained to work with people who have difficulties. Some of these people have trouble walking or moving. Some find it hard to talk or make friends. Some feel worried, sad, or scared a lot of the time. Spending time with a calm, kind horse can help them in ways that surprise even doctors and teachers.

Therapy with horses is sometimes called equine therapy, because "equine" is a special word that means "to do with horses". It has been used for many years, and it helps people of all ages.

## What Makes a Good Therapy Horse?

Not every horse can be a therapy horse. The best therapy horses are calm, patient, and gentle. They do not get frightened easily by sudden noises or movements. They are happy to stand still while someone brushes them, and they walk slowly and steadily when someone is riding them.

Therapy horses are often older horses that have had lots of experience with people. Many of them are ponies or small horses, because their size feels less scary to children.

Before a horse becomes a therapy horse, it goes through special training. Trainers teach the horse to stay calm around wheelchairs, crutches, and noisy groups of children. They teach it to walk very slowly and to stop the moment someone asks.

## How Horses Help People Move

One of the most important ways therapy horses help is by helping people move their bodies. When a person rides a horse, the horse's walking movement gently moves the rider's body from side to side, forwards and backwards. This movement is very similar to the movement of walking. For a child who finds walking difficult, riding a horse can help their muscles grow stronger and their balance get better.

Riding also helps people sit up straight and use their tummy muscles. A rider must hold the reins, sit tall, and look ahead, which is good exercise without feeling like exercise at all.

## How Horses Help People Feel

Horses are very good at sensing how people feel. They are herd animals, which means they naturally live in groups, and they are experts at reading body language. If a person is nervous, a horse can often tell.

For children who find it hard to talk about their feelings, a horse can be a safe friend. A child might find it easier to whisper a secret into a horse's ear than to tell a grown-up. Horses do not judge, they do not laugh, and they do not interrupt.

Spending time with a horse can also help people feel more confident. Learning to ride, to groom, and to lead a horse gives people a sense of achievement. A child who thought "I can't do anything" might discover "I can ride a horse!"

## A Day at a Therapy Riding Centre

Therapy riding usually happens at a special place called a riding centre. When a person arrives, they are met by friendly helpers and, of course, by the horses.

First, the person might help groom their horse. They brush its coat until it shines and comb its mane. Grooming is a lovely way to get to know a horse, because it is quiet and gentle.

Then it is time to ride. Helpers walk beside the horse, and sometimes someone leads the horse from the front. The rider might play games on horseback, like reaching for coloured rings or steering the horse around cones. These games are fun, but they also help the rider practise balance and coordination.

After the ride, there is time to say thank you to the horse, with a pat, a carrot, or a gentle hug. Horses love carrots and apples, and a treat after a good ride is a lovely way to end the session.

## Horses Helping in Other Ways

Therapy horses do not only work at riding centres. Some visit schools, hospitals, and care homes. A miniature horse, which is a tiny horse about the size of a big dog, might visit children in a hospital, walking gently down the corridor to say hello. For a child who has been in hospital for a long time, a visit from a small, friendly horse can bring a huge smile.

Some therapy horses work with soldiers and other adults who have been through frightening experiences. Being with a horse, caring for it, and learning to trust it can help people feel safe again.

Therapy horses also help people learn to communicate. A child who does not speak much might start giving the horse commands like "walk on" or "whoa". A shy child might start talking to the helpers about their horse. The horse becomes a reason to talk, to make friends, and to join in.

## Caring for Therapy Horses

Therapy horses work hard, so they need lots of care. They need good food, clean water, comfortable stables, and regular visits from the vet, who is the animal doctor, and the farrier, who looks after their hooves. They also need rest days and time to graze in a field with their horse friends.

If you ever visit a riding centre, remember to be calm and gentle around the horses. Speak softly, move slowly, and always ask before you touch a horse. Horses appreciate kindness, and they give it back many times over.

## Fun Facts About Therapy Horses

- Therapy horses are chosen because they are calm, patient and gentle.
- Riding a horse moves the rider's body in a way that is very similar to walking.
- Horses are experts at reading body language and sensing how people feel.
- Some miniature therapy horses visit children in hospitals.
- Grooming a horse, brushing its coat and combing its mane, is part of therapy too.
- Horses do not judge people, which makes them wonderful listeners.
- Therapy horses need rest days, field time and lots of carrots.

Therapy horses show us that animals can heal in ways that medicine alone cannot. With their calm hearts, their gentle steps, and their kind eyes, they help people feel stronger, braver, and happier, one ride at a time.
