Imagine touching the softest thing in the whole world. Softer than a teddy bear, softer than a cloud, softer than your cosiest blanket. That is a chinchilla! Chinchillas are small, gentle animals with giant ears, big bright eyes, and the softest fur of any animal on Earth. Let us hop up into the mountains and meet them.

## Where Do Chinchillas Come From?

Chinchillas come from the Andes mountains in South America, where they live in countries like Chile, Peru, Bolivia, and Argentina. Up in the mountains, the air is cold and dry, and the land is rocky. Chinchillas are perfect for this home. Their thick fur keeps them warm in the cold mountain air, and their strong back legs help them hop and climb over rocks like little mountain acrobats.

Long ago, chinchillas lived in large numbers in the mountains. But people hunted them for their soft fur, and their numbers fell. Today, wild chinchillas are rare and protected by law, which means it is against the rules to hunt them. Most chinchillas today are pets, and they live in homes all over the world.

## What Does a Chinchilla Look Like?

A chinchilla is a small animal, about the size of a rabbit, with a round body, a bushy tail, and very large ears. Its eyes are big, dark, and bright, and its nose twitches when it sniffs the air. Chinchillas have small front paws that they use like hands to hold food, and strong back legs that help them jump high.

The most amazing thing about a chinchilla is its fur. Chinchilla fur is the softest fur in the world. Each tiny spot on a chinchilla's skin grows many hairs, far more than most animals grow. That makes their coat thick, soft, and fluffy. Most pet chinchillas are grey, but they can also be white, black, brown, or beige.

## The Dust Bath

Here is one of the funniest things about chinchillas: they do not take baths in water! Instead, they take baths in dust. In the wild, chinchillas roll around in fine volcanic dust, and pet chinchillas do the same thing in a bowl of special chinchilla dust.

A dust bath is a happy, wriggly show. The chinchilla jumps into the dust, rolls over and over, kicks its legs, and shakes its fur. The dust soaks up the oil in its fur and keeps it clean, soft, and fluffy. A chinchilla that has just had a dust bath looks very pleased with itself — and a little bit dusty!

Chinchillas should never get wet in water, because their thick fur takes a very long time to dry, and they can catch a chill. That is why the dust bath is so important. Pet chinchillas usually have a dust bath a few times a week, and they love it.

## What Do Chinchillas Eat?

Chinchillas are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite foods are hay, grass, and special chinchilla pellets made from plants. They also enjoy small treats like dried rosehips, oats, and herbs. In the wild, chinchillas eat grasses, leaves, seeds, and bark.

Chinchillas have teeth that never stop growing, so they need to gnaw on things to keep their teeth short and healthy. They love to chew on wooden toys, apple wood sticks, and hay. A chinchilla's home always needs safe things to chew on, and fresh hay and clean water every day.

Chinchillas are careful eaters. They sit up on their back legs, hold their food in their little front paws, and nibble it slowly. Watching a chinchilla eat is one of the cutest sights in the world.

## Chinchillas Love to Jump and Play

Chinchillas are full of energy, and they love to jump, climb, and explore. In the wild, they hop from rock to rock in the mountains, and they can jump very high. Pet chinchillas need a big home with shelves and ledges to climb on, tunnels to run through, and toys to play with.

Chinchillas are most active in the evening and at night. They are crepuscular, which means they are awake at dawn and dusk. While you are getting ready for bed, your chinchilla might be bouncing around its home, popcorn jumping — that is when a chinchilla jumps straight up in the air with joy — and racing from shelf to shelf.

## Baby Chinchillas Are Called Kits

A baby chinchilla is called a kit. Kits are born with their eyes open and a full coat of soft fur, which is different from many baby animals that are born blind and naked. A kit can hop around within a day or two of being born, and it stays close to its mother for milk and warmth.

Mother chinchillas are gentle and caring. They nurse their kits, groom them, and keep them safe. Kits grow quickly, and they learn to eat hay and pellets when they are still very young.

## How to Care for a Pet Chinchilla

Pet chinchillas need gentle, patient care. They need a large home with room to jump and climb, fresh hay and pellets every day, clean water, and a dust bath a few times a week. Their home needs to stay cool, because chinchillas come from cold mountains and they do not like heat.

Chinchillas are shy animals, and they need time to trust people. If you are calm and gentle, a chinchilla may come to your hand, sniff your fingers, and even climb onto your shoulder. Never grab a chinchilla by its tail, and always be soft and slow around it.

## Fun Facts About Chinchillas

Here are some fun facts about chinchillas. A chinchilla's fur is so thick that fleas cannot live in it. Chinchillas can jump high, and they can squeeze through small gaps. They have excellent hearing, and their big ears help them hear danger from far away. And chinchillas can live for a long time — many pet chinchillas live into their teens.

## Why We Love Chinchillas

Chinchillas remind us that the softest things can also be the bravest. These little mountain animals survive in cold, rocky places, and they do it with a smile, a bounce, and the softest fur in the world. They are gentle, funny, and full of energy, and they make wonderful friends.

The next time you see a picture of a chinchilla, look at its big ears and its soft, grey fur, and imagine it rolling happily in a dust bath. You might just fall in love with chinchillas too — and who could blame you?
