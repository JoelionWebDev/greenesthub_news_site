Imagine an animal with a very long nose, a very long tongue, a big bushy tail, and no teeth at all. That is the giant anteater! It lives in the grasslands and forests of South America, and it spends its days sniffing out ants and termites to eat. It may look strange, but it is a gentle, quiet animal that minds its own business. Let us find out how this amazing insect eater lives.

## A Very Unusual Animal

The giant anteater is a big animal, about as long as a grown-up is tall when you count its tail. Its body is covered in long, shaggy fur that is grey and brown, with a bold black stripe edged in white running across its shoulders. Its tail is huge and bushy, like a giant feather duster, and it can be as long as the rest of its body.

Its head is long and narrow, with a tube-shaped snout at the end. It has small ears, small eyes, and a very good sense of smell. In fact, its sense of smell is one of the best in the animal world. A giant anteater can smell an ant nest from far away, even when the nest is hidden under the ground.

## The Amazing Tongue

The giant anteater's tongue is its most amazing tool. It is long and sticky, and it can stretch out far from its mouth. When the anteater finds an ant nest, it pokes its tongue into the nest and the ants stick to it. Then it pulls its tongue back in and swallows the ants whole. It does this again and again, very fast, like a little machine.

The tongue is attached deep inside the anteater's body, which is why it can stretch so far. It is covered in sticky spit that helps the ants and termites stick to it. The anteater does not need teeth at all, because it does not chew its food. It just slurps up the insects and swallows them.

A giant anteater eats many thousands of ants and termites every day. That sounds like a lot, but ants are tiny, so the anteater needs to eat a great many of them to feel full. It visits many ant nests in a single day, spending only a short time at each one. This is clever, because it leaves enough ants behind in each nest for the nest to grow back. The anteater can then come back another day and eat again.

## Digging for Dinner

Ants and termites live in nests that can be very hard. Some termite nests are like little hills made of hard earth, and they can be taller than a child. The giant anteater uses its strong front legs and its long, curved claws to rip open the nest. Its claws are very sharp and very strong, like little digging tools.

Once the nest is open, the anteater pokes its long nose inside and starts licking up the insects. Its tongue flicks in and out so fast that it is hard to follow with your eyes. If the ants start to bite, the anteater simply moves on to another nest. It does not stay too long in one place, and it does not destroy the whole nest.

## Home on the Grasslands

Giant anteaters live in the grasslands, savannas, and open forests of South America. They like places with lots of open space and plenty of ant nests. They are most active in the early morning and late afternoon, when it is not too hot. In the middle of the day, they may rest in the shade of a tree or a bush.

## Baby Anteaters

A mother giant anteater usually has one baby at a time. The baby is small and soft, and it rides on its mother's back for many months. It holds on tight with its little claws while its mother walks around looking for food. The baby's fur looks a lot like its mother's, with the same bold stripe, which helps it blend in and stay safe.

The mother looks after her baby for a long time. She feeds it with her milk and teaches it how to find ants and termites. The baby watches its mother dig and lick, and slowly it learns to do the same. When it is big and strong enough, it leaves its mother and starts its own life on the grasslands.

Baby anteaters are very cute, with their long noses and their striped fur. They stay close to their mother because the grasslands can be a dangerous place, with large cats and other hunters about. Riding on her back keeps them safe and warm.

## Staying Safe

The giant anteater may be gentle, but it is not defenceless. Its long claws are very sharp, and it can use them to protect itself if a hunter comes too close. When it feels scared, it may rear up on its back legs and spread its front legs wide, showing its claws. Most hunters decide to leave it alone.

Sadly, giant anteaters are in danger in some places. Their grassland homes are being turned into farms and towns, which leaves them with less space to live and fewer ant nests to eat from. Cars on roads can also hurt them. People who care about wildlife are working hard to protect the grasslands so that giant anteaters can keep living there. Keeping wild places safe helps not only anteaters, but all the other animals that live there too.

## Fun Facts About Giant Anteaters

- A giant anteater has no teeth at all, but it does not need them.
- Its tongue is longer than your arm and is covered in sticky spit.
- It can eat many thousands of ants and termites in a single day.
- Its sense of smell is one of the best in the animal world.
- It only spends a short time at each ant nest, so the nest can grow back.
- Giant anteaters live in the grasslands and open forests of South America.

The giant anteater is a gentle giant with a very special way of life. It does not roar or chase other animals. It simply walks across the grasslands, sniffing, digging, and slurping up tiny insects with its amazing tongue. Next time you see a picture of one, look at that long nose, that bushy tail, and those clever claws, and remember how this quiet animal makes its living one ant at a time.
