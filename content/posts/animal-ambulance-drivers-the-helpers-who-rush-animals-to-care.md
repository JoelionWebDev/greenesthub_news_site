When a person is hurt, an ambulance comes to help. Did you know that animals have ambulances too? Animal ambulance drivers are kind, brave helpers who rush to rescue animals that are hurt, sick, or lost. Their vans are like small animal hospitals on wheels, and their job is to get every animal to safety as quickly and gently as possible.

Animal ambulances work in towns, cities, and the countryside. They help all kinds of animals: dogs and cats, birds, rabbits, foxes, deer, and even farm animals. Sometimes they help a pet that has been hurt in an accident, or a wild animal that has fallen or been hit by a car. Every call is different, and every animal needs careful help.

The driver is often the first person to arrive. They wear gloves, carry soft blankets, and bring boxes, baskets, and stretchers made just for animals. They speak in a calm voice and move slowly, because a frightened animal does not understand that help has arrived. It only knows that it is scared and hurting, and it needs someone gentle.

## A Call for Help

It all starts with a phone call. Someone might see a dog limping by the side of the road, or a bird lying on the pavement with a sore wing. They call the animal rescue service and explain where the animal is and what is wrong. The driver writes down the details, grabs the right equipment, and sets off.

Driving an animal ambulance takes skill. The driver must drive safely but quickly, watching the road and thinking about the animal in the back. The van is fitted with soft bedding, warm blankets, and cages in different sizes. There is also a first aid kit, clean water, and food for animals that are hungry or thirsty.

When the driver arrives, the first job is to look and listen. Is the animal awake? Is it breathing normally? Can it move? The driver checks from a safe distance first, because even a gentle animal can bite or scratch when it is scared and in pain. Then the driver moves closer, slowly and kindly, and talks softly to the animal.

Sometimes the animal is easy to help. A driver might lift a small bird into a warm box, or wrap a cold kitten in a blanket. Sometimes the rescue is harder. A deer might be tangled in a fence, or a dog might be hiding under a car. The driver stays patient, works carefully, and never rushes in a way that could hurt the animal more.

## The Journey to the Vet

Once the animal is safe in the van, the driver heads to a vet or a wildlife hospital. A vet is an animal doctor, and the vet will check the animal properly and give it medicine if it needs it. On the journey, the driver keeps the van warm and quiet. Loud music and bumpy roads would frighten the animal, so the driver goes smoothly and steadily.

Drivers also keep notes about the animal. Where was it found? What happened to it? Was it wearing a collar? These notes help the vet, and they can also help find the animal's owner. If a pet has a tag or a tiny chip under its skin, the vet can read it and call the owner with the happy news that their pet has been found.

Some animals need help right away in the van. A driver might give a thirsty animal a little water, or keep a cold animal warm with a blanket. Drivers learn basic animal first aid, so they know how to stop bleeding gently, how to keep a broken leg still, and how to hold an animal safely. They do not try to do the vet's job, but they do everything they can to keep the animal comfortable until it reaches the vet.

Animal ambulance drivers also rescue animals from tricky places. They might climb a ladder to reach a cat stuck in a tree, or wade into shallow water to help a duck with a hurt wing. They work with firefighters, police officers, and vets when a rescue needs more than one pair of hands. Teamwork is a big part of the job.

## More Than Just Driving

An animal ambulance driver does much more than drive. They clean the van after every rescue, wash the blankets, and check that all the equipment is ready for the next call. They fill in reports, answer phone calls, and sometimes teach children and grown-ups how to help animals safely.

One of the most important lessons they teach is this: if you find a hurt animal, do not pick it up straight away. Stay calm, keep a safe distance, and call an adult or an animal rescue service. Wild animals can bite, and a hurt pet may be frightened. The best way to help is to get the right people there quickly.

Drivers also help animals that are lost. A dog that has run away from home might be scared and hungry. The driver brings it to a shelter, where it gets food, water, and a warm bed. Staff at the shelter check for a tag or a chip, and they try to find the owner. If no owner comes forward, the shelter looks for a new loving home for the animal.

## Why Animal Ambulance Drivers Matter

Animal ambulance drivers show us that every animal matters. A tiny bird, a lost dog, or a frightened deer all deserve kindness and care. Without these drivers, many hurt animals would have no one to help them. With them, animals get a second chance.

Their work is not always easy. They work in rain and sunshine, in the day and sometimes at night. They see animals that are hurt, and that can be sad. But they also see animals get better, go home, and return to the wild, and that makes all the hard work worthwhile.

You can help too. If you see an animal that looks hurt, tell an adult straight away. Learn the number for animal rescue in your area, and keep your pets safe with a collar and a tag. Be kind to animals every day, and remember that small acts of care make a big difference.

Animal ambulance drivers are helpers on wheels, racing to rescue, driving with care, and bringing hope to animals in need. The next time you see a van with an animal rescue sign, give the driver a wave. They might be on their way to save a life.
