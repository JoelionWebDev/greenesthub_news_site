When you think of a kangaroo, you probably picture a big animal hopping across the grasslands of Australia. But there is a kangaroo that does something very different: it climbs trees! It is called the tree kangaroo, and it lives high in the rainforests of Australia and the island of New Guinea. It is a gentle, quiet animal with soft fur, a long tail, and strong arms made for climbing.

Tree kangaroos are cousins of the kangaroos that hop on the ground, but they have learned to live a life in the trees. They move slowly and carefully from branch to branch, munching leaves as they go. They are shy animals, and they are very good at hiding among the leaves.

## A Kangaroo That Climbs

Tree kangaroos look quite different from their hopping cousins. Their back legs are shorter and stronger, and their front legs are long and powerful, with curved claws for gripping bark. Their feet are wide, with soft pads that help them hold on to branches. These are perfect tools for a life spent climbing.

A tree kangaroo climbs by hugging the trunk of a tree with its front legs and pushing up with its back legs, a little bit at a time. It moves slowly and carefully, checking each branch before it steps. When it wants to move to another tree, it can leap through the air and land on a branch with a soft thud.

Tree kangaroos have thick, soft fur that keeps them warm in the cool, misty rainforest. Their fur can be brown, reddish, or golden, with a paler belly. They have a long, round face, small ears, and a sweet, gentle expression.

## The Wonderful Tail

A tree kangaroo's tail is one of its most useful tools. It is long and thick, and it hangs down behind the animal as it climbs. The tail works like a balancing pole, helping the tree kangaroo stay steady as it moves along branches. When it leaps from one tree to another, the tail helps it balance in the air and land safely.

The tail is also useful when the tree kangaroo rests. It can wrap its tail around a branch to hold on, or let it hang down like a rope. Some tree kangaroos have tails with pale rings or a light tip, which makes them easy to spot among the leaves.

## Life in the Rainforest

Tree kangaroos live in the rainforests of far north Queensland in Australia, and in the mountain forests of New Guinea. These forests are warm and wet, with tall trees, thick vines, and mist that drifts through the leaves in the morning. The trees give tree kangaroos everything they need: food, shelter, and safe places to hide.

Tree kangaroos spend most of their time high in the trees, resting on branches during the day. They are most active in the early morning and in the evening, when the forest is cool and quiet. At these times, they move through the canopy looking for fresh leaves to eat. During the heat of the day, they curl up on a branch and sleep, hidden among the leaves. They are mostly quiet animals who like their own company, and they meet other tree kangaroos when it is time to find a mate or raise a family.

## What Tree Kangaroos Eat

Tree kangaroos are leaf eaters, and leaves are their favourite food. They munch on the soft, young leaves of rainforest trees, picking them carefully with their hands. They also eat fruit, flowers, and shoots when they can find them. Their favourite leaves are tender and green, and they chew them slowly, just like a cow chewing grass.

Eating leaves takes a lot of time, because leaves do not have as much energy as fruit. So tree kangaroos spend many hours each day eating, moving slowly from branch to branch in search of the best leaves. Their strong teeth and jaws are made for grinding leaves.

Sometimes a tree kangaroo will climb down to the forest floor to eat fallen fruit or to find a drink of water. On the ground, it hops in a clumsy, wobbly way, quite different from its smooth climbing in the trees. It does not stay on the ground for long, though. The trees are its true home, and it soon climbs back up to safety.

## Mothers and Joeys

Like all kangaroos, tree kangaroos are marsupials, which means that mothers carry their babies in a pouch. A baby tree kangaroo is called a joey, and when it is born it is tiny and helpless. It crawls into its mother's pouch, where it stays warm and safe, drinking milk and growing bigger every day.

The joey stays in the pouch for a long time, peeking out at the world as it grows. At first, only its little head pops out, looking around at the leaves and branches. Later, it climbs out to explore, but it jumps straight back in if it gets scared. The pouch is the safest place in the world for a baby tree kangaroo.

As the joey grows older, it leaves the pouch for good and starts to climb on its own. Its mother teaches it which leaves are good to eat and which branches are safe to climb. The young tree kangaroo stays near its mother for a while, until it is ready to find its own home among the trees.

## Protecting the Tree Kangaroos

Sadly, tree kangaroos are in danger. Their rainforest homes are being cut down, and there is less and less forest for them to live in. Tree kangaroos need big, healthy forests with tall trees, and without them they cannot survive.

Good people are helping tree kangaroos in many ways. Some protect the rainforest so tree kangaroos have safe homes. Others plant new trees to join forest patches back together, so tree kangaroos can travel and find food.

You can help tree kangaroos by learning about them and caring about rainforests. Every tree that stays standing is a home, a dining room, and a playground for a tree kangaroo. If we look after the forests, these gentle climbers will keep munching leaves and raising their joeys for many years to come.
