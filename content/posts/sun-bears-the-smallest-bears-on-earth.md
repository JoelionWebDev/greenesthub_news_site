The sun bear is the smallest bear in the world, but it has the longest tongue of any bear, and a golden patch on its chest like a rising sun. These little bears live in the rainforests of Southeast Asia, where they climb trees, eat honey and sleep in nests high above the ground. They may be small for a bear, but they are full of surprises.

## The Bear with the Sunny Chest

Sun bears get their name from the beautiful golden patch on their chests. The patch is shaped a bit like a crescent moon or a rising sun, and no two bears have exactly the same one. It stands out against their short, dark fur, which is usually black or very dark brown. When a sun bear stands up, its sunny chest shines in the light.

Sun bears are much smaller than other bears. An adult sun bear is about the size of a large dog, and it weighs far less than a grizzly bear or a polar bear. But what it lacks in size, it makes up for in skill. Sun bears are excellent climbers, and they spend a lot of their time in trees.

Their claws are long, curved and very sharp, perfect for climbing tree trunks and for tearing open dead wood to find insects inside. Their paws are wide, and the soles of their feet are bare, which helps them grip branches as they climb.

## The Longest Tongue of Any Bear

The sun bear's tongue is amazing. It is very long, much longer than the tongues of other bears, and the bear can stick it out a long way to reach into narrow cracks. This long tongue is perfect for licking honey from beehives and for slurping up insects from inside trees.

Sun bears love honey, and they will climb high into a tree to find a wild beehive. They use their strong claws to break open the hive and their long tongues to lick out the sweet honey. They do not mind a few bee stings. Their thick fur protects them, and the honey is worth it.

They also eat a lot of insects, especially termites and beetle larvae, which they find by tearing apart dead logs and tree trunks. They eat fruit too, and they are very fond of figs. When fruit is ripe in the forest, sun bears will climb up to eat their fill, and the seeds they drop help new trees grow.

## A Bear That Lives in the Trees

Sun bears are the most tree-loving of all bears. They climb trees to find food, to rest and to sleep. They even build nests in the branches, bending twigs and leaves into a soft platform where they can sleep safely above the ground. A sleeping sun bear in a tree nest looks very cosy.

Unlike many other bears, sun bears do not hibernate. They live in warm tropical forests where food is available all year round, so they do not need to sleep through a cold winter. They are active during the day, though they often rest during the hottest hours and become busy again in the cooler parts of the day.

Sun bears are shy animals, and they usually live alone. Each bear has its own area of forest, which it roams in search of food. They mark trees with their claws and scent to let other bears know that the area is taken.

## Mother Bears and Their Cubs

Female sun bears usually have one or two cubs at a time. The cubs are born tiny and blind, and they stay with their mother in a safe den for the first few months of their lives. As they grow, they learn to climb by copying their mother, practising on low branches before they dare to go high.

Mother sun bears are very protective of their cubs. They teach them which trees have honey, how to tear open logs to find insects, and how to build a nest for sleeping. The cubs stay with their mother for a year or more, until they are big and skilled enough to live on their own.

Cubs are playful, and they love to wrestle and chase each other around the forest floor. Play helps them learn the skills they will need as adults, such as climbing, balancing and using their claws.

## Why Sun Bears Need Our Help

Sun bears are in trouble. Their rainforest homes are being cut down for farming and palm oil plantations, and the forest is shrinking fast. Many sun bears are also caught and kept in small cages, which is very cruel. In some places, people hunt them for their body parts, which is wrong and must be stopped.

Thankfully, many kind people are working to save the sun bear. Rescue centres in Southeast Asia look after bears that have been saved from cages, and give them a safe forest home where they can climb trees and eat honey again. Forest reserves protect the trees the bears need to live.

## A Little Bear with a Big Heart

The sun bear shows us that you do not need to be big to be special. This small bear is a brilliant climber, a honey lover with an amazing tongue, and a gentle forest gardener that helps new trees grow. Its golden chest patch is like a little sun it carries everywhere it goes.

If you ever see a picture of a sun bear, look for that sunny patch and its long tongue. Then remember the little bear of the rainforest, climbing high in the trees and living its quiet, honey-sweet life. It is one of nature's smallest bears, and one of its brightest.
