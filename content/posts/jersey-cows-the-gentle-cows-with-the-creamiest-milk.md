If you have ever drunk a glass of milk that tasted extra rich and creamy, it may well have come from a Jersey cow. Jersey cows are famous all over the world for their milk, which is thicker, sweeter, and creamier than the milk from most other cows. These gentle animals come from Jersey, a small island in the sea between England and France, and they have become one of the most popular dairy cows on Earth.

Jersey cows are easy to recognise. They are smaller than most cows, with soft, light-brown coats and big, gentle eyes with long eyelashes. They look like the cows from a storybook, and they have a sweet, calm nature to match.

## From a Small Island

Jersey cows come from the island of Jersey, which is one of the Channel Islands, a group of small islands near the coast of France. Long ago, the people of Jersey kept these cows on their small farms, and they bred them carefully for hundreds of years. The cows became smaller than other breeds, because the island farms were small, but their milk stayed rich and creamy.

The people of Jersey were so proud of their cows that they made a law to protect them. For a very long time, no other cows were allowed to come to the island, so the Jersey breed stayed pure and special. Today, Jersey cows live on farms all over the world, and they are loved wherever they go.

## Small Cows, Big Eyes

A Jersey cow is smaller than most dairy cows. Its coat is a warm, light-brown colour, sometimes called fawn, and it is soft to touch. Some Jersey cows have white patches on their faces or bellies, and some have darker brown coats.

The most beautiful thing about a Jersey cow is its face. It has huge, dark eyes with long, soft eyelashes, and a gentle, kind expression. Its ears are small and neat, and its nose is soft and pinkish-brown. Jersey cows have small, curved horns, although many farm cows have their horns removed when they are young so that they do not hurt each other.

## Why Is Jersey Milk So Creamy?

The milk of a Jersey cow is famous for being rich and creamy. It has more cream and butterfat than the milk from most other cows, which is why it tastes so smooth and sweet. When you leave a bottle of Jersey milk to stand, a thick layer of cream rises to the top. That cream can be used to make butter, cheese, and ice cream that taste extra delicious.

## Gentle and Friendly Cows

Jersey cows are known for being gentle, calm, and friendly. They are not nervous like some other breeds, and they enjoy being around people. Many farmers say that Jersey cows are like big, gentle pets, and they will come to the fence to say hello, hoping for a pat or a treat.

Because they are so calm, Jersey cows are easy to look after. They are happy in a herd with other cows, and they enjoy grazing in green fields. They do not like to be alone, so they should always live with other cows. A happy Jersey cow spends much of its day eating grass, chewing the cud, and resting in the shade with its friends.

Cows have a special way of eating called chewing the cud. A cow swallows its grass quickly, then later brings it back up into its mouth and chews it again, slowly and carefully. This helps the cow to digest its food properly. A Jersey cow may spend many hours each day chewing the cud, and it often looks sleepy and content while it does so.

## Calves: Baby Jersey Cows

Baby Jersey cows are called calves, and they are small and adorable. A calf is born after its mother has carried it for about nine months. When it is born, it can stand up within an hour and walk around the field within a day. Calves drink their mother's milk for the first few months of their lives, and they grow quickly.

Calves love to play. They gallop around the field, kick up their heels, and chase each other in games of tag. They are curious little animals that love to explore, sniffing new things and following their mothers around. As they grow older, they start to eat grass alongside their mothers. A female calf will grow up to become a dairy cow like her mother.

## Jersey Cows on the Farm

On a dairy farm, Jersey cows are milked every day, usually twice a day. The farmer brings the cows into a special building called a milking parlour, where the milk is collected. Today, most milking is done by machines, which are gentle and quick. The cows soon learn the routine, and they walk into the parlour on their own.

Jersey cows give less milk than some bigger breeds, but their milk is richer and creamier, so farmers love them. They are also hardy animals that do well in hot weather and in different climates. Because they are smaller, they eat less food than bigger cows, which makes them cheaper to keep.

## Fun Facts About Jersey Cows

- Jersey cows come from the island of Jersey, between England and France.
- Their milk is richer and creamier than the milk from most other cows.
- Jersey milk has a lovely golden colour.
- They are smaller than most dairy cows, with soft, light-brown coats.
- They have huge, dark eyes with long eyelashes.
- Jersey cows are gentle, calm, and friendly, and they love being around people.
- Cows chew the cud, which means they chew their food twice.
- A baby Jersey cow is called a calf, and it can stand within an hour of being born.

Jersey cows are proof that good things come in small packages. They may be smaller than other cows, but they give some of the richest, creamiest milk in the world, and they do it with a gentle, friendly nature that makes everyone smile. The next time you drink a glass of creamy milk, think of the lovely brown cow from the island of Jersey.
