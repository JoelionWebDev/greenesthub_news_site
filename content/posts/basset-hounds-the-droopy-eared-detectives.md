Have you ever seen a dog with ears so long they almost touch the ground? Meet the basset hound, a dog that looks a little bit like a detective in a funny costume. With its long droopy ears, short little legs and sad-looking eyes, this dog has a face that makes almost everyone smile. But do not be fooled by that sleepy look. Underneath those droopy ears is one of the best noses in the whole dog world.

## What Does a Basset Hound Look Like?

A basset hound is easy to spot. It has a long body, very short legs and a big head with long, soft ears that hang down on either side of its face. Its ears are so long that they can drag along the ground when the dog lowers its head to sniff. Its skin is loose and wrinkly, especially around the face, which gives it that famous sad and thoughtful look.

Basset hounds come in many coat colours. Lots of them have a mix of white, black and brown, but some are lemon and white, or red and white.

Even though their legs are short, basset hounds are not tiny dogs. Their bodies are long and heavy, and they are much stronger than they look.

## A Nose Like a Detective

The most amazing thing about a basset hound is its nose. Basset hounds were bred long ago to follow smells on the ground, and they are brilliant at it. Their sense of smell is one of the best of all dog breeds, and only the bloodhound is thought to have a better nose.

Those long ears are not just for show. When a basset hound lowers its head to sniff, its ears sweep along the ground and help stir up smells so the dog can follow them more easily. The loose skin on its face also helps to trap smells close to its nose. It is like the dog carries its own smelling tools everywhere it goes.

A basset hound can follow a smell trail for a very long time without giving up. Once it finds an interesting scent, it will follow it step by step, nose to the ground, tail wagging. This is why people sometimes call basset hounds detectives. They are always on the case, sniffing out clues!

## Where Do Basset Hounds Come From?

Basset hounds come from France. Their name comes from the French word "bas", which means "low". That is a perfect name for a dog that is so low to the ground!

Long ago, hunters in France wanted a dog that could follow the scent of rabbits and hares through thick bushes and forests, but at a slow and steady pace that people could follow on foot. The basset hound was just right. Its short legs meant it moved slowly, and its amazing nose meant it rarely lost the trail.

Later, basset hounds became popular in England and then all around the world. Today most basset hounds are family pets, not hunting dogs, but they still love to follow smells on walks. If you take a basset hound for a walk, be ready for lots of sniffing stops!

## Life at Home with a Basset Hound

Basset hounds make wonderful family pets. They are calm, gentle and patient, and they usually get along well with children. They are not jumpy or wild. Instead, they like to lie down in a comfy spot, enjoy a good snooze and be near their family.

These dogs love company. A basset hound that is left alone for a very long time can get sad and lonely. They are happiest when they are part of the family, joining in with everyday life. They often follow their owners from room to room, just to see what is happening.

Basset hounds also have a funny side. They can be stubborn, which means they sometimes pretend not to hear you when you call them, especially if they have found a really good smell. They are also famous for their deep, loud bark and their long, musical howl. When a basset hound howls, the whole street knows about it!

## Food, Exercise and Care

Basset hounds love food. In fact, food is one of their favourite things in the whole world. This means owners have to be careful not to give them too many treats, because basset hounds can put on weight easily. A heavy basset hound can find it hard to walk and play, so healthy meals and the right amount of food are very important.

Even though they look sleepy, basset hounds need daily walks and playtime. A walk in the park, a game in the garden or a gentle sniffing adventure all help keep them healthy and happy. Because they love smells so much, a walk with a basset hound is never in a hurry. Every tree, every fence and every patch of grass has to be sniffed!

Their long ears need a little extra care. Owners check and clean them gently so they stay healthy. Brushing their short coat is easy, and a quick brush now and then keeps it looking shiny.


## Fun Facts About Basset Hounds

- A basset hound's long ears help it smell better by sweeping scents up from the ground towards its nose.
- The basset hound's name comes from the French word for "low", because the dog is so low to the ground.
- Basset hounds have been used as cartoon characters and in adverts because their droopy faces are so funny and loveable.
- Some basset hounds can howl in a deep, musical voice that sounds almost like singing.
- A basset hound will follow its nose almost anywhere, so owners keep them on a lead in places that are not fenced.

## A Loyal Friend with a Super Nose

The basset hound is a dog full of surprises. It may look slow and sleepy, but it has a detective's nose, a loyal heart and a funny personality that makes families laugh. It does not need to run fast or jump high to be amazing. It just needs a good smell to follow, a comfy place to sleep and a family to love.

If you ever meet a basset hound, watch what happens when it catches an interesting smell. Its whole body wakes up, its nose goes to the ground and off goes the detective, ready to solve the mystery of the smell. That is the magic of the basset hound.
