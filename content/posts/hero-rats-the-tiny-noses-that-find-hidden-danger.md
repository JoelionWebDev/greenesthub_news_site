When you hear the word "rat", what do you think of? Many people think of a small animal that hides in dark corners. But there is a group of rats that are true heroes. They are called Hero Rats, and they use their amazing sense of smell to find hidden danger and save human lives.

These are not ordinary rats. They are African giant pouched rats, also known as Gambian pouched rats. They are much bigger than the rats you might see in a city, about the size of a small cat, and they have big cheek pouches where they can store food, like a hamster. But their real superpower is their nose.

## A Nose That Can Smell Danger

Rats have a wonderful sense of smell. They can smell things that humans cannot smell at all, even when the thing is buried deep underground. This makes them perfect for a very important and dangerous job: finding landmines.

A landmine is a small bomb that is hidden in the ground. Long ago, during wars, soldiers buried landmines in fields and paths to stop enemy soldiers. But after the war ended, the landmines stayed in the ground, hidden and forgotten. They are still dangerous, even many years later. A farmer walking in a field, a child playing, or an animal wandering by could step on one and be badly hurt.

Finding landmines is very hard and very dangerous for people. Metal detectors can help, but they beep for every piece of old metal in the ground, like nails, bottle caps, and broken tools. That means people have to dig up lots of things that are not landmines, and it takes a very long time. But a rat can smell the special scent of the explosive inside a landmine, and it can tell the difference between a landmine and a piece of scrap metal.

## How Hero Rats Are Trained

Hero Rats are trained by a special organisation called APOPO, which works in countries in Africa and Asia. The training starts when the rats are still young. Just like search and rescue dogs, the rats learn that finding things is a fun game with a tasty reward at the end.

At first, the rats learn to sniff out a special scent and scratch at the ground when they find it. When they get it right, they get a reward — a favourite treat like a banana or a peanut. Rats love food, so they are very happy to play this game again and again.

As they grow older, the training gets harder. The rats learn to walk on a long rope across a field, sniffing the ground as they go. When they smell a landmine, they scratch the ground and their handler marks the spot. Then a human team comes later to safely remove the landmine. The rats are light, so they do not set off the landmines when they walk over them. That makes them much safer than a person or a big animal doing the same job.

Training a Hero Rat takes many months, and the rats keep practising throughout their working lives. They work in the morning, when it is cool, and rest in the afternoon. They live in comfortable homes with plenty of food, clean water, and care from their handlers.

## Clearing the Fields, Saving Lives

Once a field has been checked by Hero Rats and the landmines have been removed, the land becomes safe again. Farmers can plant crops, children can walk to school, and families can build homes without fear. This changes lives in a huge way.

In countries like Mozambique, Angola, and Cambodia, Hero Rats have helped clear many fields and paths. They have found landmines that had been hidden in the ground for decades. Each landmine they find is one less danger for the people who live there.

The rats are fast too. A Hero Rat can search a large area much more quickly than a person with a metal detector, so more land can be made safe, more quickly.

## The Famous Rat Called Magawa

One Hero Rat became famous all around the world. His name was Magawa, and he was a true hero. Magawa worked in Cambodia, a country in Asia where many landmines were left behind after a war long ago. Over his working life, Magawa found many landmines and other unexploded bombs hidden in the ground.

Magawa was so good at his job that he received a special medal for bravery — a gold medal from an animal charity in the United Kingdom. It was the first time a rat had ever received such an award. Magawa became a star, and people all over the world learned about Hero Rats because of him.

Magawa retired after a long and happy working life, and he spent his retirement resting, eating his favourite treats, and being cared for by the people who loved him.

Hero Rats have another amazing job too: they can sniff out a sickness called tuberculosis, a serious illness that affects people's lungs. Rats can smell the sickness in a sample very quickly, and when they find one, doctors can test that person again and give them the right medicine. A tiny nose, a clever brain, and a love of treats can help doctors save lives.

## Not All Rats Are Heroes — But These Are

It is true that wild rats can carry diseases and damage food stores, and that is why people are often afraid of them. But Hero Rats are different. They are bred and raised by people, they live in clean homes, they are checked by vets, and they are loved by their handlers. They are not pests — they are partners.

The handlers who work with Hero Rats love their rats very much. They give them names, they play with them, and they care for them like pets.

## Small Animals, Big Heroes

Hero Rats teach us an important lesson: heroes come in all shapes and sizes. You do not have to be big and strong to save a life. Sometimes, all you need is a good nose, a brave heart, and a willingness to help.

The next time you hear someone say something unkind about rats, you can tell them about Magawa and his friends — the fields made safe, the farmers who can plant again, and the children who can walk to school without fear.

Hero Rats may be small, but their work is huge. With their tiny noses and big hearts, they are finding hidden danger and saving lives.
