Have you ever seen a lizard that looks like it is wearing a spotted coat? Meet the leopard gecko! This small, friendly lizard is covered in dark spots, just like a tiny leopard. It has big eyes, a wide smile and soft skin. Many families keep leopard geckos as pets because they are calm, gentle and fun to watch.

Leopard geckos come from dry, rocky places in parts of Asia, including Pakistan, India and Afghanistan. In the wild, they live among rocks and sandy ground, where their spotted skin helps them hide from hungry birds and other animals. A wild leopard gecko is not a pet — it belongs in its home in nature. The leopard geckos people keep as pets are raised by breeders, and they need kind, careful owners.

## What Does a Leopard Gecko Look Like?

A leopard gecko is a small lizard. Grown-ups are usually about as long as a school ruler, from the tip of the nose to the end of the tail. Their bodies are often yellow or pale, with black spots all over. Some leopard geckos have different colours, such as orange, white or pale grey, but spots are what make them special.

Look closely at a leopard gecko's face and you will see something sweet: it looks like it is always smiling! That is just the shape of its mouth, but it makes these little lizards look very friendly. Their eyes are big and bright, with eyelids that can blink — most other geckos cannot blink at all. That blinking is one of the things that makes leopard geckos so special.

Their tails are thick and full. A leopard gecko stores fat in its tail, a bit like a camel stores fat in its hump. The tail is a handy snack store for hard times. If a leopard gecko is frightened, it can drop its tail to escape, and a new one grows back — but it hurts the gecko, so we must never grab a gecko by its tail.

## Night Owls of the Lizard World

Leopard geckos are most active at dusk and during the night. In the daytime, they like to sleep in a cool, dark hiding place, such as under a rock or inside a little cave. When evening comes, they wake up and start to explore. They walk slowly around their home, flicking out their tongues to smell the air and find food.

Their feet are not sticky like many other geckos. Leopard geckos have tiny claws instead of sticky toe pads, so they are good walkers and climbers on rocks, but they cannot climb smooth glass. That is why a pet leopard gecko stays safely inside its home, called a terrarium.

## What Do Leopard Geckos Eat?

Leopard geckos are insect eaters. In the wild, they hunt small insects such as crickets and mealworms. A pet leopard gecko eats the same kind of food, offered by its owner. The insects must be small enough for the gecko to swallow, and they should be dusted with a special powder that gives the gecko the vitamins it needs to stay healthy.

A gecko does not need food every single day. Young geckos eat more often because they are growing, while grown-up geckos may eat every other day. Fresh, clean water should always be in their home, in a shallow dish they can drink from. Like all animals, leopard geckos need fresh food and water to stay strong and happy.

## A Happy Gecko Home

A pet leopard gecko needs a home that feels safe and comfortable. A glass terrarium with a warm side and a cool side is best, so the gecko can choose where to rest. Geckos need warmth to stay healthy, because they are cold-blooded animals — their bodies cannot make their own heat. A gentle heat mat or lamp, set up by an adult, keeps one side of the home warm.

Inside the home, a leopard gecko needs a hiding place on each side, a shallow water dish and a little damp hiding spot to help it shed its skin. Geckos shed their old skin as they grow, and they usually eat the shed skin afterwards. It may sound strange, but it is normal and keeps their home tidy!

The floor of the home should be safe and easy to clean. Paper towels or special reptile mats work well. Sand can be dangerous for young geckos, so it is better to avoid it. An adult should always help set up and clean a gecko's home.

## Gentle Hands, Happy Gecko

Leopard geckos are gentle and can learn to enjoy being held, but only with calm, gentle hands. When you hold a gecko, you must be slow and quiet, and always support its whole body. Never squeeze it, and never grab its tail. Short, calm visits are best, especially at first. If a gecko seems scared, put it back in its home and try again another day.

Children should always have an adult nearby when handling a pet gecko. After holding any pet, it is important to wash your hands with soap and water. That keeps both you and your gecko healthy.

Leopard geckos can live for a long time — many live for well over ten years with good care. That means getting a leopard gecko is a big promise. A family must be ready to care for it for many years, feed it, keep its home clean and take it to a vet who knows about reptiles if it ever gets sick.

## Why Leopard Geckos Matter

Leopard geckos teach us something wonderful: even small animals have clever ways of living. Their spots hide them from danger, their tails store food for hard times, and their blinking eyes help them see in the dark. They remind us that every creature has its own special tricks for staying alive.

They also teach us to be kind and patient. A leopard gecko does not play fetch or cuddle like a dog, but it trusts the people who care for it. Watching it explore, eat and rest helps us slow down and notice the small wonders of nature.

If you ever meet a leopard gecko, look at its spots, its smile and its bright eyes — and remember that this little lizard is a true survivor, a gentle hunter and a wonderful friend to the families who love it.
