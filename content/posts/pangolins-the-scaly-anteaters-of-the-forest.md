Imagine an animal that looks like a pine cone, a lizard, and an armadillo all rolled into one. It has no fur on its back, but it is covered in hard scales. It has a long, sticky tongue, strong claws, and a tail that can curl around a tree branch. When it feels scared, it rolls up into a tight ball, like a football. This amazing animal is the pangolin.

Pangolins are shy, gentle animals that live in Africa and Asia. They are the only mammals in the world that are covered in scales. Their scales are made of the same material as your fingernails. Pangolins are sometimes called scaly anteaters, because ants and termites are their favourite food.

## What Does a Pangolin Look Like?

There are eight different kinds of pangolins in the world. Four live in Africa and four live in Asia. They come in different sizes, but most pangolins are about the size of a small dog or a large cat. Some of the biggest pangolins can be as long as a metre and a half, including their tail.

A pangolin's body is covered in overlapping scales that look like the tiles on a roof. The scales are brown, grey, or yellowish, and they protect the pangolin from predators. Under the scales, the pangolin has soft skin and a little fur on its belly and face.

Their front feet have very strong, curved claws. Pangolins use these claws to dig into ant nests and termite mounds, and to climb trees. Some pangolins spend most of their time on the ground, while others are excellent climbers and spend their lives in trees.

## The Ant-Eating Champion

Pangolins are insect eaters, and they are very good at it. Ants and termites are their favourite food, but they also eat other small insects and larvae. A pangolin can eat tens of thousands of ants or termites in a single day. That is a lot of tiny insects.

Here is how a pangolin eats. First, it uses its strong claws to break open an ant nest or a termite mound. Then it sticks its long, sticky tongue into the nest. The ants and termites stick to the tongue, and the pangolin pulls its tongue back into its mouth and swallows them. It does this again and again, until it has had enough to eat.

A pangolin's tongue is attached deep inside its body, near its ribs, which is why it can be so long. When the pangolin is not using its tongue, it keeps it folded up inside its mouth.

## Rolling Into a Ball

Pangolins are shy and gentle animals. They do not fight, bite, or run very fast. Instead, they have a very clever way to stay safe. When a pangolin feels scared, it rolls up into a tight ball, tucking its head under its tail. Its hard scales cover its whole body, like a suit of armour. A lion, leopard, or hyena cannot easily bite through the scales, and the pangolin can wait inside its ball until the danger goes away.

Some pangolins can also use their tail to hang from tree branches, and they can curl their tail around a branch to hold on tight.

## Where Do Pangolins Live?

Pangolins live in many different places. African pangolins live in forests, grasslands, and savannas south of the Sahara Desert. Asian pangolins live in forests and grasslands in countries like China, India, Vietnam, and the Philippines.

Some pangolins live on the ground and sleep in burrows they dig in the earth, or in hollow trees. Others live in trees and sleep curled up in a hollow trunk or a thick branch. Pangolins are mostly active at night, which is called being nocturnal. During the day, they sleep in a safe, hidden place. At night, they come out to hunt for ants and termites.

Pangolins are solitary animals, which means they like to live alone. They do not live in groups or families, except for mothers with their babies.

## Baby Pangolins

A female pangolin usually has one baby at a time. The baby is called a pup. When a baby pangolin is born, its scales are soft and pale, but they soon harden and turn darker. The baby drinks milk from its mother, just like other mammal babies.

Baby pangolins stay with their mother for several months. They ride on her back or her tail as she moves through the forest, and they learn how to find ants and termites by watching her.

## Pangolins Need Our Help

Sadly, pangolins are in trouble. They are the most trafficked mammals in the world, which means that more pangolins are caught and sold illegally than any other mammal. People hunt pangolins for their meat and for their scales, which are used in some traditional medicines. But scientists have shown that pangolin scales have no special healing powers, and pangolins are much more valuable alive in the wild.

All eight kinds of pangolins are in danger. Some are endangered, which means there are very few left in the wild. It is against the law to hunt or trade pangolins in most countries, and many people are working hard to protect them.

## Fun Facts About Pangolins

- Pangolins are the only mammals in the world covered in scales.
- Their scales are made of keratin, the same material as your fingernails.
- A pangolin's tongue can be longer than its whole body.
- Pangolins have no teeth. They swallow small stones to help grind up their food.
- A pangolin can eat tens of thousands of ants and termites in one day.
- When scared, a pangolin rolls into a tight ball, protected by its scales.
- A baby pangolin is called a pup, and it rides on its mother's back or tail.
- There are eight kinds of pangolins: four in Africa and four in Asia.

Pangolins are gentle, shy, and amazing animals. They help keep forests healthy by eating ants and termites, and they ask for nothing in return except a safe home.
