Imagine a tiny mouse that does not run on four little legs, but instead hops and leaps across the sand on two big back legs, just like a kangaroo. It has huge ears, a long tail, and big, bright eyes. It looks like something from a storybook, but it is a real animal. It is called a jerboa, and it is one of the cutest and most surprising animals in the desert.

Jerboas live in dry, sandy places in Asia and North Africa. They make their homes in deserts and dry grasslands, where the sun is hot, the rain is rare, and the ground is covered in sand. Most animals would find it hard to live there, but jerboas are perfectly suited to desert life. They are small, clever, and very good at jumping.

## Small Body, Big Legs

A jerboa is a small animal. Most jerboas are about as long as your hand, not counting their tail, and their tail can be even longer than their body. They have soft fur, which can be sandy brown, grey, or yellowish, and this helps them blend in with the sand around them. If a hungry animal is looking for a snack, a jerboa that matches the colour of the sand is much harder to spot.

The most amazing thing about a jerboa is its legs. Its front legs are tiny, and it hardly uses them for walking at all. Its back legs are long and strong, with big feet that are perfect for jumping. When a jerboa moves, it hops along on its back legs, a bit like a kangaroo, and it can jump many times its own body length in a single leap. It can also change direction very quickly in mid-air, which helps it escape from animals that want to catch it.

Jerboas also have very long tails. The tail is not just for show. It helps the jerboa balance when it jumps, a bit like a tightrope walker using a long pole. The tail also helps it steer as it leaps through the air. Some jerboas have a little tuft of fur at the end of their tail, which makes them look even cuter.

## Big Ears and Bright Eyes

Jerboas have huge ears, much bigger than you would expect for such a small animal. These ears are not just cute, they are very useful. In the desert, jerboas need to hear very well, because many of the animals that hunt them are quiet and sneaky. Big ears help them pick up the smallest sounds, such as the soft footsteps of a fox or an owl, and that gives them time to jump away to safety.

Their eyes are also big and bright. Jerboas are mostly active at night, when the desert is cooler and safer. Big eyes help them see in the dark, so they can find food and avoid danger even when the moon is the only light. During the hot daytime, they stay safe in their burrows, sleeping away from the sun.

Jerboas have soft, whiskery noses, too. They use their whiskers to feel their way around in the dark and to find seeds and other food hidden in the sand.

## Life in the Desert

The desert is a hard place to live. It is hot during the day, cold at night, and there is very little water. But jerboas have clever ways of coping. They dig burrows in the sand, which are tunnels and rooms underground. A burrow stays cool during the day and warm at night, so it is a perfect home. Jerboas sleep in their burrows during the day, and they come out at night to look for food.

Jerboas do not need to drink much water. They get most of the water they need from the food they eat, such as seeds, plants, and insects. This is very helpful in the desert, where water is hard to find. Some jerboas can go for a long time without drinking at all, which is amazing for such a small animal.

When a jerboa is out looking for food, it moves in short hops and jumps. It stops often to listen and look around, and if it senses danger, it can leap away in a flash.

## What Do Jerboas Eat?

Jerboas are not fussy eaters. They eat seeds, grains, leaves, and roots, and they also enjoy eating insects when they can find them. They use their tiny front paws to pick up food and hold it while they eat, a bit like a person holding a sandwich. They often carry food back to their burrow to eat in safety, or to store for later.

In the desert, food can be hard to find, so jerboas have to be good at searching. They use their sense of smell to sniff out seeds hidden under the sand, and they dig with their front paws to uncover them.

Baby jerboas are born in the burrow, safe and warm. They are tiny and helpless at first, and their mother looks after them carefully until they are old enough to hop out on their own.

## Staying Safe From Danger

Jerboas have many enemies in the desert. Foxes, owls, snakes, and other animals would all love to catch a jerboa for dinner. But jerboas have a lot of clever tricks to stay safe. Their sandy-coloured fur helps them hide, their big ears help them hear danger coming, and their powerful legs help them escape in a flash.

If a jerboa is scared, it can jump high into the air and land a good distance away, then jump again and again until it is safe. It can also dive into its burrow and hide until the danger has gone.

Jerboas are shy animals, and they do not like to be seen. That is why they are mostly active at night, when it is dark and quiet.

## A Tiny Desert Champion

The jerboa is a wonderful example of how animals can adapt to live in hard places. It is small, but it has big legs for jumping, big ears for listening, big eyes for seeing in the dark, and a long tail for balance. It lives in the desert, where few other animals can survive, and it does it with style and grace.

Next time you think of a kangaroo, remember its tiny cousin, the jerboa. It may be small enough to fit in your hand, but it can jump like a champion.
