Deep in the rainforests of Africa lives the largest ape in the world — the gorilla. Gorillas are big, strong, and powerful, but they are also gentle, shy, and loving. They spend their days eating leaves, playing with their babies, and taking naps in the shade. Gorillas are our close relatives in the animal kingdom, and watching them is like looking into a mirror of the forest. Let us meet these gentle giants.

## The Biggest Apes on Earth

Gorillas are the largest of all the apes. A big adult male, called a silverback because of the silvery-grey fur on his back, can be as tall as a grown man and weigh more than two grown men put together. His arms are long and very strong, and his chest is broad and powerful. Female gorillas are smaller than the males, but they are still much bigger and stronger than a human.

Even though they look powerful, gorillas mostly walk on all fours, using their knuckles to support their front legs. This is called knuckle-walking. It looks a little funny, but it is a very good way to move through the forest. Gorillas can also stand up on two legs for a short time, and they can climb trees, though the big adults usually stay on the ground because they are so heavy.

Gorillas have dark black fur, and their faces, hands and feet have no fur at all. They have big, expressive eyes and a broad nose. Each gorilla has a unique nose print, just like each person has unique fingerprints. Scientists can tell gorillas apart by looking at the pattern of their noses!

There are two main kinds of gorillas: western gorillas and eastern gorillas. Western gorillas live in the rainforests of West and Central Africa. Eastern gorillas live further east, in countries like the Democratic Republic of Congo, Rwanda and Uganda. The famous mountain gorillas are a type of eastern gorilla that lives high up in misty mountains.

## What Do Gorillas Eat?

Gorillas are mostly vegetarians. That means they eat plants, not meat. Their favourite foods are leaves, stems, fruits, and sometimes insects like ants and termites. They spend a large part of each day eating — a big gorilla can eat a huge amount of leaves and plants every day!

Gorillas are clever about finding food. They use their strong hands to pull leaves off branches, peel bark from trees, and dig up roots. In some places, they eat a plant called wild celery, and they love to munch on bamboo shoots. When fruit is in season, they enjoy it very much. Baby gorillas learn which plants are good to eat by watching their mothers.

Because they eat so many leaves, gorillas have big tummies. Their stomachs are like big fermentation tanks that slowly break down the tough plant material. This is a bit like how cows digest grass. It takes a long time, so gorillas often sit quietly and rest while their food digests.

## Family Life in the Forest

Gorillas live in family groups called troops. A troop is usually led by one big silverback male. He is the leader and protector of the family. The troop also includes several adult females, their babies, and some younger gorillas.

The silverback is a calm and caring leader. He decides where the group will go each day, where they will eat, and where they will sleep at night. He protects the family from danger, and he settles arguments between the other gorillas. When a silverback stands up and beats his chest, it is not always a sign of anger — it is often a way to show who is in charge, or to warn other animals to stay away.

Mother gorillas are very loving. A baby gorilla stays close to its mother for years. At first, the baby rides on its mother's belly, holding on tightly to her fur. As it grows, it starts to ride on her back. The mother carries her baby everywhere, feeds it, and keeps it safe. Baby gorillas are playful and curious, just like human children. They wrestle with each other, swing from low branches, and explore the forest floor.

Gorillas talk to each other in many ways. They grunt, hoot, bark, and roar. They also use body language — a gentle touch, a playful push, or a long stare. Mothers make soft humming sounds to their babies, and babies make little whimpering sounds when they want attention. Gorillas are very social animals, and they enjoy being together.

## A Gorilla's Day

A gorilla's day is peaceful and simple. In the early morning, the troop wakes up and starts eating. They move slowly through the forest, stopping to eat leaves and fruit along the way. Around midday, they rest. Some gorillas take a nap, while others groom each other — gently picking through each other's fur. Grooming is a friendly, loving thing to do, and it helps keep the gorillas clean and close to each other.

In the afternoon, they eat some more and move to a new spot. In the evening, each gorilla makes a nest to sleep in. They bend branches and leaves together to make a soft, cosy bed on the ground or in a low tree. Baby gorillas sleep in their mother's nest, snuggled up close. Gorillas build a new nest every night — they do not sleep in the same bed twice!

## Gorillas Need Our Protection

Sadly, gorillas are endangered, which means there are not many left in the wild. Their rainforest homes are being cut down for farms, mines and roads. Sometimes gorillas are also harmed by poachers, or they catch diseases from people. Baby gorillas are especially at risk, because they need their mothers for so long.

But there is hope. Many people are working hard to save gorillas. In Rwanda and Uganda, mountain gorillas are protected in national parks, and tourists who visit them help pay for their protection. Rangers patrol the forests to keep gorillas safe. Scientists study gorillas to learn how to help them. Thanks to these efforts, mountain gorilla numbers have started to grow again — a wonderful success story!

You can help gorillas too, by learning about them, telling others why they matter, and supporting groups that protect rainforests. Gorillas are gentle giants who ask for very little: a safe forest, plenty of leaves to eat, and a family to love. If we protect their forests, these amazing apes will be there for future generations of children to wonder at.
