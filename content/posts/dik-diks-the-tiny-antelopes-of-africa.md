In the dry bushlands of eastern and southern Africa, there lives one of the smallest antelopes in the world. It is so small that it could stand on your school desk. It has big, dark eyes, a tiny pointed nose, and a funny name that sounds like a whistle. Meet the dik-dik, a tiny antelope with a big heart and a very clever way of staying safe.

## What Is a Dik-Dik?

A dik-dik is a small antelope, and antelopes are animals related to deer and goats. But dik-diks are much smaller than most antelopes. An adult dik-dik is only about as tall as a small dog, and it is one of the tiniest antelopes on Earth. Its name comes from the sound it makes, a sharp "dik-dik" whistle that it uses to warn others of danger.

Dik-diks have soft, greyish-brown fur that helps them blend in with the dry grass and bushes of their home. Their bellies are lighter in colour, and they have a white ring around each eye, which makes their big, dark eyes stand out. They have a small, pointed snout that can move and wiggle, helping them sniff out food and smell danger.

Male dik-diks have small, straight horns on their heads. Female dik-diks do not have horns. The horns are short and pointed, and the male uses them to defend his territory from other males. But dik-diks are not fighters. They prefer to run and hide rather than fight, and they are very good at it.

Dik-diks live in the bushlands and savannas of eastern and southern Africa. They can be found in countries like Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Somalia, and Namibia. They like dry, open places with plenty of bushes and shrubs to hide in. They do not like thick forests, because they need open space to run and to spot danger coming.

## Life in Pairs

One of the most special things about dik-diks is that they live in pairs. A male and a female dik-dik stay together for life, which is unusual for antelopes. Most antelopes live in large herds, but dik-diks prefer the company of just one partner. Together, the pair defends a small territory, which is the area where they live and find food.

The pair works as a team. While one dik-dik is eating, the other keeps watch for danger. If one spots a predator, it whistles a warning, and both of them run for cover. This teamwork helps them stay safe in a place full of hungry hunters like leopards, eagles, and jackals.

Dik-dik pairs are very loyal to each other. They stay close together, and they often groom each other, licking each other's fur to keep it clean. They also mark their territory together, leaving scent marks on bushes and trees. These scent marks tell other dik-diks that this area is already taken.

When a baby dik-dik is born, the parents take great care of it. The baby is tiny and helpless at first, and it stays hidden in the bushes while its parents go out to find food. The mother comes back to feed it milk, and the father keeps watch nearby. As the baby grows, it starts to follow its parents and learn how to find food and stay safe.

Young dik-diks stay with their parents until they are old enough to find a partner of their own. Then they leave to start a new life in a new territory. This is a big moment for a young dik-dik, but by then it has learned all the skills it needs to survive in the bush.

## The Whistle That Saves Lives

Dik-diks are famous for their whistle. When a dik-dik spots danger, it makes a sharp, high-pitched "dik-dik" sound. This whistle warns its partner and any other dik-diks nearby that a predator is coming. The whistle is so distinctive that it gave the dik-dik its name.

The dik-dik's whistle is not just a warning. It also tells the predator that it has been spotted. When a predator knows it has been seen, it often gives up and goes looking for easier prey. This is a clever trick. By whistling, the dik-dik is saying, "I see you, and I am ready to run." Many predators decide it is not worth the chase.

Dik-diks have other ways of staying safe too. Their fur blends in with the dry grass and bushes, making them hard to spot. They are very quiet animals, moving softly through the undergrowth. When they sense danger, they can freeze and stay perfectly still, becoming almost invisible. If freezing does not work, they run in a zigzag pattern, making it hard for a predator to catch them.

Dik-diks are most active in the early morning and late afternoon, when the air is cooler. During the heat of the day, they rest in the shade of a bush or tree. At night, they also rest, but they stay alert for danger. They sleep lightly, ready to leap up and run at the first sign of trouble.

## What Dik-Diks Eat

Dik-diks are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They feed on leaves, shoots, fruits, and berries. They are very picky eaters, choosing only the softest and most nutritious parts of plants. They use their small, pointed snouts to pick leaves from bushes and to reach into tight spaces.

Dik-diks do not need to drink much water. They get most of the water they need from the plants they eat, especially from juicy leaves and fruits. This is very useful in the dry bushlands where they live, where water can be hard to find. They can go for a long time without drinking, which helps them survive in a hot, dry place.

Dik-diks have a special way of eating. They use their long, flexible tongues to pull leaves into their mouths. Their teeth are designed for nibbling soft plants, not for chewing tough grass. They eat slowly and carefully, always keeping one eye out for danger. A dik-dik never forgets to watch for predators, even when it is enjoying a tasty meal.

If you ever visit a safari park in eastern or southern Africa, keep your eyes open for dik-diks. Look for a tiny antelope with big eyes and a pointed nose, standing quietly in the bushes. The dik-dik may be tiny, but it is full of courage, loyalty, and cleverness.
