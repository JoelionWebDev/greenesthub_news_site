Australia is home to many strange and wonderful animals, and the emu is one of the most famous. The emu is a big bird that cannot fly, but it can run very fast across the open plains of Australia. With its shaggy brown feathers, long neck, and strong legs, the emu looks a little bit like a small ostrich, but it is a very special bird in its own right.

Emus have lived in Australia for a very long time. The Aboriginal peoples of Australia have known emus for thousands of years, and the emu is so important in Australia that it appears on the country's coat of arms, along with the kangaroo.

## How Big Is an Emu?

An emu is the second biggest bird in the world, after the ostrich. A grown-up emu can stand about one and a half to nearly two metres tall. That is about as tall as a grown-up person. Emus can weigh as much as a large dog, and some big emus weigh even more.

Emus are covered in soft, shaggy feathers that look a bit like hair. Their feathers are brown, grey, and black, which helps them blend in with the dry grass and bushes of Australia.

An emu has three toes on each foot, and each toe has a strong claw. Its legs are long and powerful, perfect for running. Like the ostrich, the emu cannot fly because its wings are very small and its body is too heavy. Its wings are hidden under its feathers, so you can hardly see them at all.

## A Runner Built for Speed

Emus are excellent runners. They can run at speeds of about fifty kilometres per hour, which is faster than a car driving through a town. They can also keep running for a long time, covering great distances across the Australian outback.

An emu's legs work like springs. Each step is long and springy, and the bird can change direction quickly if it needs to. Emus also use their speed to escape from danger. Their main enemies are dingoes, which are wild dogs, and eagles, which can attack young emus from the air.

## What Do Emus Eat?

Emus eat a wide range of foods. They eat grasses, leaves, seeds, fruits, flowers, and berries. They also eat insects, caterpillars, and other small creatures when they can find them. This makes them omnivores, which means they eat both plants and animals.

Emus love to eat the fruits and seeds of native Australian plants. As they travel across the land, they help to spread seeds in their droppings, which helps new plants to grow.

## The Emu Dad Does the Hard Work

One of the most amazing things about emus is the way they raise their chicks. In the emu world, it is the father who does most of the work. After the female emu lays her eggs, the male takes over. He sits on the nest and keeps the eggs warm until they hatch.

The male emu sits on the eggs for about eight weeks, which is a very long time. During this time, he hardly eats or drinks at all. He loses a lot of weight, but he stays on the nest to protect the eggs from cold, heat, and predators. He only gets up to turn the eggs, which helps them develop properly.

Emu eggs are very special. They are dark green in colour, about the size of a grapefruit, and have a thick, strong shell. A female emu can lay many eggs in one nest, and one nest can hold a dozen eggs or more.

When the chicks hatch, they are covered in stripy cream and brown feathers. These stripes help them hide in the grass. The father emu stays with the chicks for many months, teaching them where to find food and protecting them from danger. The chicks grow fast, and by the time they are about a year old, they are nearly as big as their parents.

## Where Do Emus Live?

Emus live all over Australia. You can find them in grasslands, open woodlands, and dry scrubland. They do not live in thick forests or in very dry deserts, but they are happy in most other parts of the country. Emus are the only emus in the world, and they are found nowhere else in the wild.

Emus like to roam. They do not stay in one place for long. They walk and run across the land, following the rain and looking for fresh food and water. A single emu can travel many kilometres in a day.

Emus have very good eyesight and hearing, which helps them spot danger from far away. If they see a dingo or a person, they will usually run away.

## Emus and People

Emus have been important to Aboriginal Australians for thousands of years. Their meat and eggs were a source of food, their feathers were used for decoration, and their fat was used as a medicine and for rubbing into skin. Emus also appear in Aboriginal stories and art, where they are often shown as clever and strong animals.

Today, emus are protected by law in Australia. It is against the law to hunt them without a licence. There are many emus living in the wild, and they are not in danger of disappearing.
