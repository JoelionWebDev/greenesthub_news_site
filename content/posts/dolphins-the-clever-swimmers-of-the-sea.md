Dolphins are some of the cleverest animals in the sea. They are fast swimmers, playful jumpers, and wonderful friends to each other. Dolphins are not fish, even though they live in the water. They are mammals, just like us, which means they breathe air and feed their babies milk. Dolphins have smooth grey skin, a curved smile, and a fin on their backs. They live in oceans all over the world, and they love to play. Watching dolphins leap out of the waves is one of the most joyful sights in nature. Let us dive in and learn all about these clever swimmers.

## Dolphins are mammals

Dolphins may live in the sea, but they are mammals, not fish. Fish breathe underwater using gills, but dolphins breathe air through a blowhole on the top of their heads. A dolphin must swim to the surface to take a breath, just as we must come up for air when we swim underwater. Dolphins are warm-blooded, like humans, and they feed their babies milk. A baby dolphin is called a calf, and it stays close to its mother for a long time. Dolphins have lungs, not gills, so they can drown if they cannot reach the surface. That is why dolphins sleep in a special way, with one half of their brain awake so they can remember to swim up and breathe.

## How do dolphins talk?

Dolphins are very chatty animals. They talk to each other using clicks, whistles, and squeaks. Each dolphin has its own special whistle, almost like a name. When dolphins meet, they whistle to say hello and to tell each other who they are. Dolphins also use sound to find their way and to find food. They make clicking sounds that travel through the water and bounce off things. When the sound comes back, the dolphin can tell where things are, how big they are, and how far away they are. This is called echolocation. It works a bit like a bat's way of finding things in the dark. Dolphins can find fish even in dark or cloudy water by listening to the echoes.

## What do dolphins eat?

Dolphins eat fish, squid, and other small sea animals. They are hunters, and they are very good at it. Dolphins often hunt together in groups. They work as a team to chase a school of fish and herd the fish into a tight ball. Then they take turns swimming through the ball and catching fish. This teamwork helps every dolphin get a good meal. Dolphins have many small, sharp teeth, but they do not chew their food. They swallow fish whole, head first. Dolphins need to eat a lot because swimming fast and playing all day uses lots of energy. A dolphin's clever hunting tricks show just how smart these animals are.

## Playful dolphins

Dolphins love to play. They leap out of the water, spin in the air, and splash back down with a big splash. They surf on waves and ride in the wake of boats. Dolphins play with seaweed, bubbles, and each other. Young dolphins chase each other and practise jumping, just like children playing games. Dolphins are curious animals, and they often swim up to boats to say hello. Sometimes they race alongside boats, jumping in and out of the waves. Playing helps dolphins learn, stay strong, and make friends. A dolphin that plays a lot is a happy, healthy dolphin. Their playfulness is one of the things people love most about them.

## Dolphin families

Dolphins live in groups called pods. A pod is like a big family. The dolphins in a pod look after each other, hunt together, and play together. A pod can have just a few dolphins or many dozens. Dolphins in a pod talk to each other all the time. They help sick or hurt dolphins by holding them up so they can breathe at the surface. Mother dolphins take great care of their calves. A calf stays with its mother for years, learning how to hunt, how to talk, and how to stay safe. Dolphins are very social, and they do not like to be alone. Their friendships are strong, and they remember their friends for a long time.

## How do dolphins swim so fast?

Dolphins are built for speed. Their bodies are smooth and shaped like torpedoes, which helps them glide through the water. Their strong tails move up and down, pushing them forward. Dolphins can swim very fast, and they can leap high out of the water. Their flippers help them steer and turn quickly. Dolphins can dive deep under the sea, holding their breath for a long time. Their bodies are clever in many ways. When a dolphin dives deep, its heart slows down to save oxygen. Dolphins are some of the best swimmers in the ocean, and they make swimming look easy and fun. Watching a dolphin swim is like watching a dancer move through the water.

## Dolphins and people

People have loved dolphins for a very long time. Sailors told stories about dolphins guiding their ships and playing beside them. Today, scientists study dolphins to learn how smart they are. Dolphins can learn new tricks, solve puzzles, and remember things. They recognise themselves in mirrors, which is something only a few animals can do. Dolphins need our help to stay safe. The sea must be clean so dolphins can find food and stay healthy. Fishing nets can hurt dolphins, so people are working on safer ways to fish. When we keep the ocean clean and safe, dolphins can keep playing, jumping, and chatting in the waves for many years to come.

## Fun facts about dolphins

- Dolphins are mammals, not fish, and they breathe air through a blowhole on top of their heads.
- Each dolphin has its own special whistle, almost like a name, that other dolphins recognise.
- Dolphins use echolocation, sending out clicks and listening to the echoes to find food and find their way.
- Dolphins sleep with one half of their brain awake so they remember to swim up and breathe.
- Dolphins can recognise themselves in mirrors, which shows just how clever they are.
