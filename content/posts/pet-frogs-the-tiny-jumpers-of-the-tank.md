Pet frogs are small, jumpy animals that make fascinating pets. They have smooth, damp skin, big round eyes, and strong back legs that let them leap high into the air. Some pet frogs live mostly in the water, while others like to sit on damp plants and rocks at the edge of the water. They are quiet animals that do not need to be cuddled or played with, but they are wonderful to watch as they swim, hop, and catch their food with a flick of a long, sticky tongue.

## What is a pet frog?

A pet frog is an amphibian, which is an animal that can live both in water and on land. Frogs start their lives in the water as tadpoles, and then they change into frogs with legs and lungs. Pet frogs come in many kinds, and some of the most popular are African dwarf frogs, which live in the water all the time, and tree frogs, which like to climb on plants and branches. African dwarf frogs are small and brown, with tiny front legs and big back legs for swimming. Tree frogs are often bright green, with sticky pads on their toes that help them grip leaves and glass. All frogs have smooth, moist skin that they breathe through as well as through their lungs, which is why their homes must always be damp.

## From tadpole to frog

One of the most amazing things about frogs is the way they grow up. A frog's life begins as an egg, laid in the water in a soft, jelly-like clump. From the egg hatches a tadpole, a tiny creature with a tail and no legs that swims around and eats tiny bits of food in the water. As the tadpole grows, it slowly begins to change. First, back legs appear, then front legs, and the tail begins to shrink. The tadpole's gills, which it uses to breathe under water, are replaced by lungs, so it can breathe air. At last, the tail disappears altogether, and the tadpole has become a little frog.

## Life in the tank

Pet frogs need a home that is warm, damp, and safe. A frog's tank is called a terrarium if the frog lives mostly on land, or an aquarium if the frog lives mostly in the water. African dwarf frogs live in a tank full of water, just like fish, with plants and hiding places to swim among. Tree frogs need a tall tank with damp soil or moss at the bottom, plants to climb on, and a shallow dish of water to sit in. All frog tanks need a lid, because frogs are good jumpers and climbers and can escape through a small gap. The tank should be kept warm, like a summer's day, and the air inside should be damp, so the frog's skin stays moist.

## What do pet frogs eat?

Pet frogs eat small, live food, and catching it is part of the fun of watching them. In the wild, frogs eat insects, worms, and other tiny creatures that they can catch with their quick, sticky tongues. In a home tank, pet frogs eat small crickets, fruit flies, tiny worms, and frozen foods like bloodworms and brine shrimp. Tree frogs catch insects with a flick of the tongue so fast that it is hard to see. Frogs should be fed every day or every other day, with only as much food as they can eat in a short time. A frog's food must be the right size: if it is too big, the frog cannot swallow it.

## How frogs use their amazing senses

Frogs have wonderful senses that help them live in their watery world. Their big, round eyes sit on top of their heads, so a frog can sit in the water with just its eyes poking out and still see all around. Frogs are very good at spotting movement, which helps them catch insects and spot danger. Their skin is another amazing sense: frogs can drink water through their skin, and they can also breathe through it, which is why their skin must always stay damp. A frog's ears are flat, round patches behind its eyes, and they can hear the sounds of other frogs and the world around them. Frogs also have a very good sense of touch, and they can feel the tiniest ripples in the water when an insect or a fish swims nearby.

## Frogs and their songs

Many frogs make sounds, and each kind of frog has its own song. Male frogs call to attract females, and they make their calls by puffing out a sac of skin under their chin, like blowing up a little balloon. The sound of a frog's call can be a croak, a chirp, a trill, or a deep hum, and in the wild, a whole pond full of frogs can be very noisy on a warm evening. Pet frogs in a home tank are usually much quieter than wild frogs, but some kinds, like African dwarf frogs, make soft buzzing or humming sounds under the water that you can hear if you listen carefully. Listening to a frog's gentle song is one of the quiet joys of keeping these little animals.

## Fun facts about pet frogs

- Frogs start life as tadpoles and change into frogs in a process called metamorphosis.
- A frog can breathe and drink through its skin, which must always stay damp.
- Frogs catch insects with a long, sticky tongue that flicks out very fast.
- African dwarf frogs live in the water all the time and are popular pet frogs.
- Tree frogs have sticky pads on their toes that help them climb leaves and glass.
- Male frogs call to attract females by puffing out a sac of skin under their chin.

Pet frogs are small, quiet, and full of surprises. From tiny tadpoles to leaping, singing adults, they show us one of nature's most amazing changes right in front of our eyes. They ask for very little: a warm, damp home, clean water, and the right food. In return, they give us hours of quiet wonder as they swim, hop, and catch their dinner with a flick of the tongue. If you are patient and gentle, and you love to watch animals rather than cuddle them, a pet frog might be the perfect little friend for you. Just remember to keep its home warm and damp, and your tiny jumper will be happy and healthy for a long time.
