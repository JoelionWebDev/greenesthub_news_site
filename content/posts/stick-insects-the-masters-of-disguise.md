Look closely at that twig. Is it really a twig, or is it an insect? If it has six long legs and it is slowly swaying in the breeze, it might be a stick insect! Stick insects are some of the best hiders in the whole animal world. They look so much like sticks, twigs, and leaves that hungry birds and other animals walk right past them. They are long, thin, and quiet, and they spend their days pretending to be part of a plant. Pet stick insects are calm, gentle, and wonderful to watch.

## What is a stick insect?

A stick insect is an insect, and insects have six legs and three body parts. A stick insect's body is long and thin, just like a twig. Its legs are long and skinny too, and its colour is usually green or brown, just like the plants it lives on. Some stick insects are smooth, and some have little bumps that look like the knots on a branch. A stick insect has a small head, two long feelers, and big eyes that help it spot danger. Some stick insects have wings, and some do not. Stick insects come in many sizes. Some are as short as your finger, and some grow as long as your hand or even longer. They are harmless animals, and they do not bite or sting.

## Where do stick insects live?

Wild stick insects live in warm places all around the world. You can find them in forests, gardens, and bushes in Africa, Asia, Australia, and the Americas. They live on trees and shrubs, where they can hide among the leaves and branches. A stick insect spends most of its life on one plant, eating its leaves and swaying gently like a twig in the wind. During the day, it stays very still. At night, it wakes up and moves around to eat. Pet stick insects live in a tall home called a vivarium. Their home has branches to climb on, leaves to eat, and fresh air to breathe. A stick insect's home should be tall, because stick insects love to climb up high and hang upside down from branches.

## What do stick insects eat?

Stick insects are plant eaters. They love to munch on fresh leaves. In the wild, a stick insect eats the leaves of the trees and shrubs where it lives. It uses its small mouth to nibble tiny bites from the edge of a leaf. Stick insects eat slowly and quietly, one small bite at a time. Pet stick insects eat fresh leaves that a grown-up gives them. The leaves must be clean, fresh, and safe for them to eat. Different kinds of stick insects like different kinds of leaves, so a grown-up should find out which leaves are right for the pet. Fresh water is important too. A stick insect drinks tiny drops of water from leaves.

## A day in a stick insect's life

A stick insect's day is slow and quiet. During the day, it hangs from a branch and stays very still. It sways gently from side to side, just like a twig moving in the breeze. This clever trick helps it blend in with the plant, so hungry animals do not notice it. A stick insect can stay still for a very long time. It does not run away when danger comes near. Instead, it freezes and pretends to be a stick. At night, the stick insect wakes up and starts to eat. It walks slowly along a branch, nibbling leaves as it goes. They shed their skin as they grow, just like other insects.

## Baby stick insects

Baby stick insects hatch from tiny eggs. The mother stick insect drops her eggs onto the ground, where they look like small seeds. The eggs rest among the leaves and soil until they are ready to hatch. When a baby stick insect hatches, it is called a nymph. A nymph looks like a tiny copy of its parent, but it is much smaller. It is long, thin, and already good at hiding. A baby stick insect grows by shedding its skin many times. Each time it sheds, it grows a little bigger. Baby stick insects eat soft, fresh leaves, and they need a warm home with branches to climb. They are very delicate, so they need gentle care.

## Looking after a pet stick insect

Stick insects can be lovely pets, but they need a grown-up to help look after them. A pet stick insect needs a tall, warm home with branches to climb and fresh leaves to eat every day. Its home should be cleaned often, and its leaves should be fresh and clean. Stick insects are very delicate, so you should never squeeze them or pull them off a branch. If you want to hold one, a grown-up should help. Let the stick insect walk onto your hand by itself, and move very slowly and gently. Always wash your hands before and after touching a stick insect or its home. Stick insects need quiet and calm, and they like a home that is not too hot or too cold. They are not toys, and they need time to rest and eat. With kind, patient care, a pet stick insect can be a happy, healthy friend for a long time.

## Fun facts about stick insects

- Stick insects look so much like twigs that hungry animals often walk right past them.
- A stick insect sways gently from side to side, just like a twig moving in the breeze.
- Stick insects stay very still during the day and eat at night.
- A stick insect sheds its skin many times as it grows bigger.
- Baby stick insects are called nymphs, and they look like tiny copies of their parents.
- Some stick insects can grow as long as your hand, and some grow even longer.

Stick insects are quiet, gentle, and very clever. They have one of the best tricks in nature: they can disappear simply by standing still. They teach us to look closely and to be patient. Next time you see a twig on a plant, take a second look. It might just be a stick insect, swaying softly.
