# Pet Snails: The Slow and Peaceful Little Pets

Not every pet runs, jumps or barks. Some pets glide slowly along on a wave of slime, carry their house on their back, and munch on a lettuce leaf as if they have all the time in the world. Meet the pet snail — a quiet, peaceful and surprisingly charming little creature that is perfect for children who love watching nature up close.

## Meet the Snail

Snails are molluscs, which means they belong to the same big family of animals as clams and octopuses. A land snail has a soft, squishy body and a hard, spiral shell that it carries everywhere it goes. When a snail feels scared, it pulls its whole body into its shell, like closing the door of its house. When it feels safe, it pops back out again.

A snail's head has two pairs of feelers, called tentacles. The long top pair have eyes on the ends, like tiny periscopes. The shorter bottom pair are used for smelling and feeling. Snails do not have great eyesight, but they are very good at smelling food — they can sniff out a tasty leaf from quite far away.

One of the most popular pet snails is the giant African land snail. As its name suggests, it is big for a snail, and its shell can grow to be as long as your hand. Giant African land snails are calm, friendly and easy to care for. Smaller garden snails can also be kept as pets, but they need the same care and attention as any other animal.

## Slow and Steady

Snails are famous for being slow — and they are! A snail moves by sliding along on a rippling wave of muscles in its foot, leaving a trail of slime behind it. The slime helps the snail glide smoothly and protects its soft body from sharp things on the ground. It might look yucky, but slime is a snail's clever invention.

Snails are mostly active at night or when it is damp and cloudy, because sunshine can dry them out. During the day, your pet snail may sleep tucked into its shell or buried under a leaf. At night, it wakes up and goes exploring.

## A House on Its Back

A snail's shell is not just a pretty spiral — it is its home, its armour and its bedroom all in one. The shell is made mostly of calcium, the same mineral that makes your bones and teeth strong. As a snail grows, its shell grows with it, adding new rings around the edge like the rings of a tree.

The pattern on a snail's shell is unique, just like your fingerprint. No two snails have exactly the same shell. Some shells are striped, some are spotty, and some are plain brown or golden. The giant African land snail has a beautiful shell with stripes of brown, cream and gold.

## What Do Pet Snails Eat?

Snails are mostly plant-eaters. They love fresh fruits and vegetables, and their favourite foods include lettuce, cucumber, courgette, carrots, sweet potato, apples, bananas and strawberries. Leafy greens like spinach and kale are good for them too. Food should be washed well before it is offered, and any uneaten food should be removed before it goes bad.

Snails also need calcium for their shells, so a piece of cuttlebone in the tank is a must. Grown-ups should check which foods are safe, because some foods, like citrus fruits and salty snacks, are not good for snails. Fresh water is important too. Snails need a shallow dish of water, and their tank should be sprayed with a little water every day to keep it damp. Snails breathe air through a small hole in their body, and they need a moist home to stay healthy.

## A Snail's Home and Care

A pet snail needs a home called a terrarium. It should have a secure lid, because snails are better climbers than you might think and can squeeze through small gaps. The bottom of the terrarium should be covered with damp soil or coconut fibre, deep enough for the snail to burrow into when it wants to rest. Snails love hiding places. A piece of bark, a small flowerpot turned on its side, or a few leaves make perfect snail shelters. A shallow dish of water, some fresh food and a piece of cuttlebone complete the home. The terrarium should be kept in a warm room, away from direct sunlight and cold draughts. A grown-up should check the temperature and spray the tank with water to keep it nice and damp.

Snails are gentle pets, and they can be held — carefully. Never pick a snail up by its shell alone, and never pull it off a surface — this can hurt it badly. Children should always handle a snail with an adult nearby, and handling should be gentle and brief. The best way to enjoy a snail is to watch it explore, eat and glide around its home.

## Fun Facts About Snails

- A snail can have thousands of tiny teeth on its tongue — some snails have more than ten thousand!
- Snails leave a trail of slime wherever they go, and they can even crawl across a sharp blade without getting hurt, thanks to the slime.
- A snail can sleep for a very long time. Some snails sleep for weeks when the weather is too hot or too dry.
- Snails can pull their whole body into their shell and close the door with a special cover when they need to rest.
- The biggest land snail in the world, the giant African land snail, can grow a shell as long as your hand.
- Snails have been on Earth for a very long time — their ancestors were crawling around hundreds of millions of years ago.

Pet snails teach us that slow is not boring — slow is peaceful, careful and full of wonder. If you ever hold a snail in your hand and feel it glide gently across your palm, you will understand why so many children fall in love with these tiny, shelled friends.
