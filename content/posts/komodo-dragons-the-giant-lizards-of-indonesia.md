Long ago, people told stories about dragons: huge, scaly creatures with sharp teeth and a fearsome bite. Most people thought dragons were only in stories. But on a few small islands in Indonesia, there really are giant lizards that look like dragons. They are called Komodo dragons, and they are the biggest lizards in the world.

Komodo dragons live only on a handful of Indonesian islands, including Komodo, Rinca, Flores, and Padar. They have lived there for thousands of years, and they are the top predators on their islands, which means no other animal hunts them. Let us find out what makes these real-life dragons so amazing.

## How Big Is a Komodo Dragon?

A Komodo dragon is a very big lizard. A grown-up male can grow to about three metres long from nose to tail, which is longer than a small car. They can weigh as much as a grown man, and some very big ones weigh even more. Female Komodo dragons are usually a little smaller than males.

Komodo dragons have thick, scaly skin that is grey, brown, or greenish in colour. Their skin is covered in small bony plates, which act like a suit of armour. They have a long, flat head, a strong neck, and a long, muscular tail that helps them balance and move. Their legs are short but powerful, and their feet have long, sharp claws that help them dig and climb.

A Komodo dragon's tongue is long and forked, like a snake's tongue. It flicks its tongue in and out of its mouth to taste the air and pick up smells. This helps it find food from far away.

## A Hunter With a Super Sense of Smell

Komodo dragons have an amazing sense of smell. They can smell a dead animal from many kilometres away. When they flick their forked tongue, they collect tiny scent particles from the air, and a special organ in the roof of their mouth helps them work out where the smell is coming from. This is how they find food on their islands, even when the food is hidden or far away.

Komodo dragons eat many different animals. They hunt deer, wild pigs, goats, and smaller animals like birds and rodents. They also eat dead animals, which is called carrion. Young Komodo dragons eat insects, lizards, and small birds, and they spend a lot of time in trees, where they are safe from bigger dragons.

## The Powerful Bite

Komodo dragons have a very powerful bite. They have about sixty sharp, curved teeth that are like a shark's teeth. Their teeth are serrated, which means they have tiny jagged edges, like a saw. This helps them tear meat easily. When a Komodo dragon loses a tooth, a new one grows to replace it, just like a shark.

For a long time, people thought that Komodo dragons killed their prey with germs in their saliva. Scientists now know that the dragon's bite is dangerous because it is so strong and sharp, and because their saliva contains venom that stops blood from clotting and lowers blood pressure.

## Life on the Islands

Komodo dragons live on hot, dry islands with grasslands, savannas, and forests. They like warm weather, and they spend a lot of time basking in the sun to keep their bodies warm. Like all reptiles, Komodo dragons are cold-blooded, which means they need the sun's heat to warm up their bodies so they can move and hunt.

Komodo dragons are good swimmers and can swim between islands. They are also good climbers when they are young. Baby Komodo dragons climb trees to stay safe from adult dragons, which might eat them if they catch them on the ground.

## Baby Komodo Dragons

Female Komodo dragons lay their eggs in a nest, often in a hole in the ground or in a mound of earth. They lay about twenty to thirty eggs at a time, which is a lot. The eggs take many months to hatch, and the mother guards the nest for a while, but then she leaves, and the babies must look after themselves.

When baby Komodo dragons hatch, they are about the size of a small lizard, with bright colours and patterns that help them hide. They run straight to the trees, where they live for the first few years of their lives.

## Protecting the Dragons

Komodo dragons are endangered, which means there are not many left in the wild, and they need protection. There are only a few thousand Komodo dragons living in the wild, all on the Indonesian islands where they have always lived. Their numbers are small because they live on such small islands, and because their habitat is under threat.

Komodo National Park was set up in Indonesia to protect Komodo dragons and their islands. The park covers several islands, including Komodo and Rinca, and it is a special place where dragons can live safely.

## Fun Facts About Komodo Dragons

- Komodo dragons are the largest lizards in the world.
- They can grow to about three metres long.
- They have about sixty sharp, serrated teeth, and new teeth grow to replace lost ones.
- They can smell dead animals from many kilometres away.
- Their tongue is long and forked, like a snake's tongue.
- Young Komodo dragons live in trees to stay safe from adults.
- They can swim between islands.
- Komodo dragons live only on a few small islands in Indonesia.
- They can eat more than half their own body weight in one meal.

Komodo dragons are not fire-breathing monsters from a storybook, but they are real, amazing, and a little bit scary. They are the kings of their islands, with a super sense of smell, a powerful bite, and a size that no other lizard can match.
