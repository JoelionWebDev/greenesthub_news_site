Have you ever seen a bright white chicken dashing around a farmyard, pecking at the ground and flapping its wings? There is a good chance it was a Leghorn chicken. Leghorns are some of the most famous chickens in the whole world, and farmers love them because they lay lots and lots of eggs. These busy birds are always on the move, always looking for something tasty to eat, and always ready to explore a new corner of the farm. They are cheerful, hard-working, and full of life. Let us meet the Leghorn and find out what makes this wonderful chicken so special.

## A Chicken From Italy

Leghorn chickens come from Italy, a country in Europe famous for pasta, pizza, and beautiful green farms. Long ago, in a part of Italy called Tuscany, farmers kept hard-working white chickens that laid plenty of eggs for their families. The chickens took their name from the city of Livorno, which English speakers called Leghorn. In the 1800s, sailors and farmers brought these chickens across the sea to America and Britain, where people soon discovered just how wonderful they were. Before long, Leghorns were living on farms all over the world, from the sunny fields of Italy to the wide open spaces of Australia and the backyards of many other countries. Today, if you visit almost any egg farm, you are very likely to find Leghorns there.

## Why Farmers Love Leghorns

The biggest reason farmers love Leghorn chickens is their eggs. A healthy Leghorn hen can lay around 280 white eggs in a single year. That is almost one egg nearly every single day! Most of these eggs are white, with smooth, strong shells that protect the tasty egg inside. Farmers like white eggs because they are easy to spot in the nest, and many people enjoy eating them for breakfast, boiled, fried, or scrambled. Leghorns start laying eggs when they are still young, at about five or six months old, and they keep laying for a long time. Because they lay so many eggs while eating only a modest amount of food, Leghorns are one of the best egg-laying chickens a farmer can keep. That is why so many egg farms choose Leghorns above all other chickens.

## Bright White Feathers and Red Combs

Leghorns are easy to spot in a farmyard. Most of them have bright white feathers that shine in the sun, although some Leghorns can be brown, black, or speckled with spots. Their feathers lie close to their bodies, which helps them move quickly and easily through grass and fences. On top of their heads, Leghorns have tall red combs that flop over to one side like a floppy red hat. Roosters, the male chickens, have even bigger combs, and their tails are long and curved, with feathers that shine green and black in the sunlight. Leghorns are light and slim birds, which is part of the reason they can run so fast and even fly short distances when they want to reach a high perch or escape from danger.

## Busy, Brave, and Full of Energy

Leghorns are not lazy chickens. They love to scratch in the dirt, peck at seeds, and chase after tiny insects. They are curious birds that like to explore every corner of the farmyard, and they are very good at finding their own food. Because they are light, Leghorns can flap their wings and fly up onto fences, low branches, and even rooftops if they get the chance. They are also alert and quick, so they are good at spotting danger and running away before trouble arrives. Leghorns do not like to sit still for long, and they are happiest when they have plenty of space to roam, scratch, and search for treats. If you watch a Leghorn for a few minutes, you will see it is always doing something.

## What Do Leghorns Eat?

Like all chickens, Leghorns eat grains such as corn and wheat, and they love leafy greens, vegetables, and fruit. On a farm, they spend much of the day scratching the ground to find seeds, worms, and tiny insects hiding in the soil. Farmers also give them special chicken feed that has everything they need to stay healthy and lay strong eggs. Leghorns need clean, fresh water every day, especially when the weather is hot, and they enjoy a tasty treat now and then, such as watermelon, cooked rice, or pumpkin. A well-fed Leghorn is a happy Leghorn, and a happy Leghorn lays plenty of eggs for the farmer to collect each morning.

## Leghorns Around the World

Because Leghorns are such great egg layers, people have taken them to nearly every country on Earth. You can find Leghorns on big egg farms, on small family farms, and even in backyards where people keep a few hens for fresh eggs. In some places, people keep Leghorns as pets too, because they are fun to watch and they give the family fresh eggs for breakfast. Leghorns do best in warm weather, since they come from sunny Italy, but with a dry, safe coop to sleep in at night, they can live happily in cooler places too. Wherever they live, Leghorns bring the same busy energy and the same gift of fresh eggs.

## Fun Facts About Leghorns

Here are some fun facts about Leghorn chickens. First, Leghorns almost never sit on their eggs to keep them warm, unlike some other chicken breeds that love to brood. That means farmers collect the eggs and keep them safe in another way. Second, Leghorn roosters are known for their loud crowing, which they do early in the morning to wake up the farm. Third, Leghorns are one of the oldest chicken breeds still kept today, and they have been laying eggs for farm families for hundreds of years. And fourth, a Leghorn hen can be quite noisy when she has just laid an egg, clucking proudly as if to tell everyone the good news.

## A Chicken Worth Knowing

Leghorn chickens may look simple in their white feathers, but they are truly amazing birds. They work hard, lay eggs nearly every day, and bring life and energy to any farm they call home. They remind us that even small animals can do a very big job. If you ever visit a farm and see a bright white chicken running past you, say hello. It might just be a Leghorn, the busiest egg layer in the whole chicken world, and one of the hardest workers on the farm.
