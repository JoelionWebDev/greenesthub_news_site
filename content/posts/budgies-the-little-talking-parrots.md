Imagine a tiny bird, no bigger than your hand, that can learn to say "hello" and copy the sounds it hears around the house. That bird is the budgie — also called a budgerigar. Budgies are small, colourful parrots from Australia, and they are one of the most popular pet birds in the world. They are cheerful, clever, and full of personality. Some budgies can learn dozens of words! Let us meet these little talking parrots.

## From the Australian Outback

Wild budgies come from Australia, where they live in large flocks in the dry, grassy plains of the outback. In the wild, budgies are mostly green and yellow, with black markings on their wings and backs. The green colour helps them hide in the grass and bushes from hungry birds of prey.

Wild budgies are nomads, which means they travel around looking for food and water. When it rains, seeds grow and budgies gather in huge flocks — sometimes thousands of birds together! They fly fast and chatter noisily as they swoop across the sky. When the land dries out again, the flock moves on to a new place.

Pet budgies were first brought to Europe in the 1800s, and people were amazed by their bright colours and friendly nature. Over many years, breeders produced budgies in many new colours: blue, white, yellow, violet, and pretty mixtures of all of them. Today, budgies are loved as pets in homes all over the world.

## A Little Bird with a Big Voice

Budgies are small parrots, but they have a big talent: they can learn to copy sounds. In the wild, budgies copy the calls of other budgies in their flock. This helps them stay together and recognise each other. Pet budgies use this same skill to copy human words!

A pet budgie that spends a lot of time with people can learn to say words like "hello," "pretty bird," and even its own name. Some very talented budgies have learned more than a hundred words! Male budgies are often the best talkers, but female budgies can learn words too. To teach a budgie to talk, you need to repeat a word slowly and clearly, many times a day, and be patient. Budgies learn best when they are young and when their owner talks to them gently every day.

Budgies also love to sing and chatter. A happy budgie will sit on its perch and sing a cheerful song of chirps, warbles and whistles.

## What Do Budgies Eat?

In the wild, budgies eat seeds from grasses and plants. Pet budgies also eat seeds, and their owners give them a special seed mix made just for budgies. But seeds alone are not enough for a healthy budgie. They also need fresh vegetables like broccoli, carrots, spinach and sweetcorn, and small pieces of fruit like apple and banana.

Budgies need fresh, clean water every day, and they need a special mineral block or cuttlebone in their cage. Cuttlebone is the soft inner shell of a cuttlefish, and it gives budgies calcium to keep their beaks and bones strong. Budgies nibble on it happily.

One food budgies must never eat is chocolate, and they should not eat avocado either, because these foods are poisonous to birds. A good budgie owner always checks that a new food is safe before offering it.

## Life in a Budgie's Home

A budgie's cage should be as big as possible, with room to stretch its wings, hop between perches, and play with toys. Budgies love toys — bells, swings, ladders, and colourful hanging toys to chew and climb on. They also need different kinds of perches, made of wood, so their feet stay healthy.

Budgies are social birds. In the wild, they live in huge flocks, so they do not like to be alone. A pet budgie that lives on its own needs lots of time and attention from its owner every day. Many people keep two or more budgies together so they can chat, play and keep each other company. Two budgies will groom each other, feed each other, and sit close together on a perch — a sign of true bird friendship!

Budgies also need time outside their cage each day, in a safe room with the windows and doors closed. They love to fly around, explore, and sit on their owner's shoulder or finger. But their wings must never be near a ceiling fan or an open window, so owners always make the room safe first.

## How Budgies Groom and Sleep

Budgies are very clean birds. They spend a lot of time preening their feathers — using their beaks to tidy each feather and keep it in place. They also love to have a bath! Many budgies enjoy splashing in a shallow dish of water, or sitting under a gentle mist of water sprayed by their owner. A wet, happy budgie with its feathers all fluffed up is a funny sight.

At night, budgies sleep on a perch, with their heads tucked back into their feathers and one foot tucked up into their tummy. They like to sleep in a quiet, dark place. Many owners cover the cage with a light cloth at night to help their budgie feel safe and sleep well.

## Why Budgies Make Lovely Pets

Budgies are wonderful pets for children who are calm and gentle. They are small and easy to care for, and they can become very tame. A hand-tamed budgie will step onto your finger, sit on your shoulder, and maybe even give you a little kiss on the nose with its beak!

Budgies can live for many years, and they form a strong bond with their owners. They learn to recognise the people in their family, and they get excited when their favourite person comes into the room. They are clever, funny, and full of character — every budgie has its own personality. Some are bold and adventurous, while others are shy and sweet.

Looking after a budgie teaches children patience and responsibility. Feeding it, cleaning its cage, and talking to it every day builds a real friendship between a child and a bird. And there is nothing quite like coming home to a cheerful little voice saying "hello!" from inside its cage.

So the next time you hear a tiny bird chattering away, it might just be a budgie practising its words. These little talking parrots from the Australian outback have travelled all the way around the world to become our friends — and they have a lot to say!
