Deep in the warm, green rainforests of Southeast Asia lives an animal with long, shaggy, reddish-orange hair and a kind, thoughtful face. It is the orangutan. Orangutans spend almost all of their lives up in the trees, swinging, climbing, and resting in nests made of leaves. They are gentle, clever, and very loving mothers. Let us climb up into the rainforest and meet these wonderful red apes.

## What Is an Orangutan?

An orangutan is a great ape, which means it is closely related to gorillas, chimpanzees, and even humans. The name orangutan comes from words that mean person of the forest, and that is a perfect name, because orangutans live in the forest and are very clever. They have long arms that are much longer than their legs, which helps them to swing from branch to branch. Their hands and feet can grip branches tightly, a bit like our hands can grip a rope.

Orangutans have shaggy, reddish-brown hair that covers their bodies. Adult males are much bigger than females, and they often have large cheek pads on the sides of their faces. Males also have a throat pouch that they can puff up to make loud, deep calls that travel far through the forest. Orangutans are very intelligent. They can learn how to use tools, such as using a stick to get insects out of a hole, or using leaves as a kind of umbrella when it rains. They can also remember where the best fruit trees are.

## Where Do Orangutans Live?

Orangutans live only in the rainforests of two islands in Southeast Asia, Borneo and Sumatra. These islands are warm and wet, with tall trees that form a green roof over the forest, called the canopy. Orangutans spend most of their time in the canopy, high above the ground. They rarely come down to the forest floor, because the trees give them food, shelter, and safety.

The rainforest is a busy, noisy place, full of birds, insects, and other animals. Orangutans move slowly and carefully through the trees, using their long arms to swing and their feet to grip branches. They are not as fast as monkeys, but they are very strong and careful. They often travel alone, because they need a lot of space to find enough food. Mothers and their babies stay together, but adult orangutans usually live on their own. At night, orangutans build a nest in a tree, bending branches and leaves together to make a soft, cosy bed high up in the branches.

## What Do Orangutans Eat?

Orangutans are mostly fruit-eaters, and their favourite food is fruit. They love ripe, sweet fruits, such as figs, durian, and other rainforest fruits. They use their strong teeth and hands to peel fruit and eat the soft flesh inside. When fruit is hard to find, they also eat leaves, bark, flowers, and insects. They are not fussy eaters, but fruit is their favourite treat.

Orangutans are very good at finding food in the rainforest. They remember where the best fruit trees are and when the fruit will be ripe. They travel through the trees, stopping at each tree to eat. They also drink water from rain that collects in tree hollows and from leaves. Young orangutans learn what to eat by watching their mothers. The mother shows her baby which fruits are safe and how to peel them. She also teaches her baby how to find insects and how to use tools to get food. This learning takes a long time, because there are so many different plants in the rainforest.

## Baby Orangutans

Baby orangutans are some of the cutest babies in the animal world. When they are born, they are tiny and helpless, with soft, sparse hair and big, curious eyes. They cling tightly to their mother's fur, holding on as she moves through the trees. The mother carries her baby everywhere, keeping it safe and warm.

Orangutan mothers are very loving and patient. They feed their babies with milk, groom their fur, and play with them. They teach them how to climb, how to build nests, and how to find food. Baby orangutans love to play, swinging on branches, tumbling, and exploring. Play helps them to become strong and clever. Orangutan babies stay with their mothers for a very long time, longer than most other animals. They may stay for many years, learning everything they need to know. When they are finally ready, they leave their mothers and start to live on their own.

## Fun Facts About Orangutans

Orangutans have many wonderful talents. They are excellent climbers, and they can move through the trees with grace and strength. They use their long arms to swing, and their feet are almost like extra hands, with toes that can grip branches. This helps them to hold on tight, even when they are high up in the canopy.

Another fun fact is that orangutans are very clean animals. They groom themselves and each other, picking out dirt and insects from their fur. They also use leaves to wipe their faces and hands. Orangutans are also very good at using tools. They have been seen using sticks to fish for insects, and using leaves as gloves to handle prickly fruit. Orangutans also have their own personalities. Some are shy, some are playful, and some are curious. Adult males make a loud call that can be heard from far away.

## How We Can Help Orangutans

Orangutans need our help, because their rainforest homes are being cut down. Trees are cleared to make farms, especially for palm oil, which is used in many foods and products. When the forest is lost, orangutans lose their homes, their food, and their safe places to live. This is very sad, because orangutans cannot live without the rainforest.

Many people are working to save orangutans and their forests. Conservationists protect rainforest areas and help to plant new trees. Rescue centres care for orphaned baby orangutans and teach them how to live in the wild again. Scientists study orangutans to learn more about them and to find the best ways to protect them. You can help orangutans by learning about them and by making kind choices. Ask adults to look for products that use sustainable palm oil, which means the palm oil was grown without cutting down rainforest. Support wildlife charities that protect orangutans and plant trees. When we protect the rainforest, we protect orangutans and thousands of other animals and plants that live there too.
