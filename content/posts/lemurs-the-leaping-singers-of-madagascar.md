Far away in the Indian Ocean, there is a big island called Madagascar. It is home to animals that live nowhere else on Earth, and the most famous of them all is the lemur. Lemurs are primates, which means they are related to monkeys, apes, and us. They have big, round eyes, soft fur, and long tails, and they can leap through the trees like furry acrobats. Some lemurs even sing loud songs in the morning that can be heard right across the forest.

There are many different kinds of lemurs, from tiny ones no bigger than a mouse to bigger ones about the size of a small dog. Let us travel to Madagascar and meet these amazing animals.

## An Island Full of Lemurs

Madagascar is a very special place. It broke away from Africa a very long time ago, and the animals there grew and changed in their own way, separate from animals anywhere else. That is why lemurs live only in Madagascar and nowhere else in the wild. If you want to see a wild lemur, you have to go to Madagascar.

Lemurs live in the forests of Madagascar, from the rainy forests in the east to the dry forests in the west and south. Some live high in the trees, and some live more on the ground. All of them are very good climbers, with long fingers and toes that grip branches tightly.

The most famous lemur is the ring-tailed lemur. It has a long, bushy tail with black and white rings, like a stripy scarf. It holds its tail up high when it walks, which helps other lemurs spot it in the tall grass. Ring-tailed lemurs spend more time on the ground than most other lemurs, and they love to sunbathe, sitting up with their arms outstretched and their faces towards the sun, as if they are worshipping it.

## The Singing Lemurs

One of the most amazing lemurs is the indri, the biggest lemur of all. Indris do not have long tails like other lemurs; their tails are very short. But they have something even more special: a voice. Every morning, indri families sing together. Their songs are loud and beautiful, and they can be heard from far away across the forest.

Indris sing in pairs and family groups, with the male and female singing different parts of the song, a bit like a duet. People who visit Madagascar often wake up to the sound of indris singing, and it is a sound they never forget.

Other lemurs make sounds too. Ring-tailed lemurs purr, grunt, and make a loud call that sounds like a cat's meow.

## Leaping Through the Trees

Lemurs are brilliant jumpers. Their strong back legs let them leap from tree to tree, sometimes jumping many metres in a single bound. The indri is the best leaper of all: it can jump from one tree trunk to another, holding itself upright like a person standing up, and then push off with its powerful legs to fly through the air to the next tree.

Lemurs use their long tails to help them balance when they jump and climb. A lemur's tail is almost as long as its body, and it acts like a tightrope walker's pole, helping it stay steady on thin branches.

Lemurs have big, forward-facing eyes, which help them see well, especially at night. Some lemurs, like the mouse lemur and the aye-aye, are active at night, when they hunt for insects and fruit in the dark. Their huge eyes collect as much light as possible, so they can see in the dim forest.

## What Do Lemurs Eat?

Most lemurs eat fruit, leaves, flowers, and sometimes insects. Different kinds of lemurs like different foods. Ring-tailed lemurs love fruit, especially the fruit of the tamarind tree. Indris eat mostly leaves, and they are very picky about which leaves they choose. Some lemurs eat bamboo, and some eat the nectar of flowers.

The aye-aye is the strangest lemur of all when it comes to food. It has a very long, thin middle finger, which it uses to tap on tree bark and listen for grubs hiding inside. When it hears a hollow sound, it bites a hole in the bark and uses its long finger to fish out the grub.

Lemurs help the forest in an important way. When they eat fruit, they swallow the seeds, and later the seeds come out in their droppings, far away from the parent tree. This helps new trees grow in different parts of the forest.

## Family Life

Lemurs live in family groups. Ring-tailed lemurs live in big groups, with many females, males, and babies together. The females are in charge of the group, which is unusual among primates. They decide where the group goes and when it is time to eat or rest.

Baby lemurs are born small and helpless, and they cling to their mother's belly for the first few weeks of their lives. As they grow, they ride on their mother's back, holding on tightly with their little hands and feet.

## Protecting Madagascar's Lemurs

Sadly, lemurs are in danger. Their forests are being cut down for farming and wood, which means lemurs are losing their homes. Many kinds of lemurs are now endangered, which means there are not many left in the wild. Some kinds of lemurs have only a small number left, and they need our help to survive.

People in Madagascar and around the world are working hard to protect lemurs. National parks and reserves have been set up to keep the forests safe. Scientists study lemurs to learn how to help them, and local people are taught how to protect the forests.

## Fun Facts About Lemurs

- Lemurs live only on the island of Madagascar.
- There are many different kinds of lemurs, big and small.
- The ring-tailed lemur has a long tail with black and white rings.
- Indris sing loud morning songs that can be heard across the forest.
- Lemurs are amazing leapers and can jump many metres between trees.
- The aye-aye uses a long, thin finger to fish grubs out of trees.
- Baby lemurs ride on their mother's back.

Lemurs are some of the most special animals on Earth. They leap through the trees, sing in the morning, and live on an island like no other. If we protect their forests, these wonderful singers and jumpers will be leaping through the trees of Madagascar for many years to come.
