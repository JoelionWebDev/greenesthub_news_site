When you think of a pig, you probably picture a big farm animal rolling in the mud. But pot-bellied pigs are different. They are small, cute, and clever, and many people keep them as pets in their homes. A pot-bellied pig gets its name from its round tummy, which hangs low and wobbles when it walks. With their short legs, big ears, and friendly snouts, these little pigs look like characters from a storybook.

Pot-bellied pigs come from Vietnam, a country in Asia, where they were first kept as farm animals. Today, they live in homes all over the world, and they are famous for being smart, clean, and full of fun.

## Small Pigs With Big Bellies

A pot-bellied pig is much smaller than a farm pig, but it is not as tiny as people sometimes think. A grown-up pot-bellied pig is about as tall as a medium dog, and it weighs about as much as a grown person. They have short, sturdy legs, a big round belly, and a short, curly tail. Their snouts are flat and strong, and they use them to sniff the ground and dig in the soil.

Pot-bellied pigs come in many colours. Some are black, some are white, some are pink, and some have patches of black and white. Their skin is covered in short, bristly hair. Some pot-bellied pigs have a little tuft of hair on top of their heads, which looks like a tiny mohawk. Each pig looks a little different, and owners soon learn to recognise their own pig by its colour and markings.

## Cleverer Than You Think

Pigs are some of the cleverest animals in the world, and pot-bellied pigs are no exception. Scientists say that pigs are about as smart as a three-year-old child. They can learn their names, follow simple instructions, and solve puzzles to find food. A pot-bellied pig can learn to sit, spin around, fetch a ball, and even walk on a lead, just like a dog.

Pot-bellied pigs have excellent memories. They remember people they like, places they have been, and where their favourite food is hidden. They can also learn to open doors and gates, so owners have to use special locks that pigs cannot work out. Some clever pigs have even learned to open the fridge, which is why owners keep their food safely out of reach.

## What Do Pot-Bellied Pigs Eat?

Pot-bellied pigs love food, and they will eat almost anything you offer them. In the wild, pigs eat roots, leaves, fruit, and vegetables. Pet pot-bellied pigs eat special pig food, along with fresh vegetables and fruit. Their favourite treats are apples, carrots, cucumbers, and leafy greens. They also enjoy a little cooked rice or oats, but treats should only be given in small amounts.

Like many animals, pot-bellied pigs can get fat if they eat too much. Because they love food so much, they will keep eating as long as there is food in front of them. Owners must measure their food carefully and make sure they get plenty of exercise. A healthy pot-bellied pig needs a balanced diet, fresh water, and lots of time to walk around and explore.

## Pigs Love to Be Clean

Many people think pigs are dirty animals, but that is not true at all. Pot-bellied pigs are very clean animals that like to keep their sleeping area tidy. They choose one corner of their home to use as a toilet, and they keep the rest of their space clean. Pet pigs can even be trained to use a litter tray, just like a cat, or to go outside to the toilet, just like a dog.

Pigs do love mud, but not because they are dirty. Pigs roll in mud to keep cool on hot days, because they cannot sweat the way people do. The mud also protects their skin from the sun and from biting insects. A pot-bellied pig that has had a good mud bath will happily shake itself off and trot back home for a nap.

## Playful Pigs and Their Friends

Pot-bellied pigs are friendly, playful animals that love company. They enjoy being stroked, having their bellies rubbed, and spending time with their owners. A happy pot-bellied pig will wag its curly tail, grunt softly, and flop down on its side for a rest. They make many funny noises, including grunts, snorts, and squeals.

Pot-bellied pigs need friends and things to do, or they can become bored. They love to play with balls, dig in the garden, and explore new places. Some owners give their pigs puzzle toys filled with food, which keeps them busy and happy. Pot-bellied pigs also like the company of other animals, and they often get along well with dogs and cats.

## Baby Pot-Bellied Pigs

Baby pot-bellied pigs are called piglets, and they are tiny and adorable. A piglet is born after its mother has carried it for about three months. A mother pig can have a litter of many piglets at once, and they are all born with their eyes open and ready to explore. Piglets drink their mother's milk for the first few weeks of their lives.

Piglets love to play. They chase each other around the pen, wrestle with their brothers and sisters, and snuggle together in a pile to sleep. As they grow, they start to eat solid food alongside their mother's milk. By the time they are a few months old, they are ready to live more on their own, although they still love to be near their family.
