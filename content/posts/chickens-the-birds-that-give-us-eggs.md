Chickens are some of the most useful animals in the world. You can find them on farms, in villages, and even in back gardens. They give us fresh eggs to eat, and they are fun to watch as they peck and scratch in the yard. A chicken may look like a simple farm bird, but it is clever, chatty and full of surprises. Let us walk into the farmyard and meet the chicken.

## What is a chicken?

A chicken is a bird that cannot fly very far. It has wings, feathers, a beak and two scaly legs, but it spends most of its time walking on the ground. A female chicken is called a hen, and a male chicken is called a rooster or a cockerel. A baby chicken is called a chick. Roosters are easy to spot because they are often bigger and brighter than hens, with a tall red comb on top of their heads and a long, shiny tail.

Chickens come in many colours and sizes. Some are white, some are brown, some are black, and some have feathers that shine with green and blue in the sun. Some chickens have fluffy feathers on their feet, and some have feathers that curl up like a fancy dress.

## The rooster's morning song

One of the most famous sounds on a farm is the rooster crowing. A rooster often crows early in the morning, just as the sun comes up. Cock-a-doodle-doo! It is like a farm alarm clock. Roosters also crow during the day, to tell other chickens where they are, or to warn them that something is near.

Hens make sounds too. A hen clucks softly when she is happy and busy, and she makes a louder clucking sound when she has just laid an egg, as if she is telling the whole farm her good news. Hens and roosters talk to each other all day long. They have different sounds for food, for danger and for calling their chicks.

## Pecking, scratching and dust baths

Chickens spend most of their day looking for food. They scratch the ground with their strong feet to uncover seeds, grains and tiny insects. Then they peck quickly with their beaks to pick the food up. Chickens eat seeds, grains, grass, leaves, small insects and worms. They also love kitchen scraps like cooked rice and vegetables. A chicken's beak is hard and pointed, just right for pecking.

One of the funniest things a chicken does is take a dust bath. A chicken finds a dry, dusty patch of ground, sits down, and fluffs the dust all through its feathers with its wings and feet. It looks messy, but it is a very clever habit. The dust helps to keep the feathers clean and gets rid of tiny bugs that might hide in them.

## From egg to chick

Hens lay eggs, and that is one of the reasons chickens are so important to people. A hen does not need a rooster to lay eggs. Most hens lay one egg a day, often in the morning. They like to lay their eggs in a quiet, safe nest, often a box filled with soft straw. The eggs come in different colours. Some are white, some are brown, and some are even a soft blue or green, depending on the type of chicken.

When a hen sits on her eggs to keep them warm, she is called a broody hen. She stays on the nest almost all day and night, turning the eggs gently so they stay warm all over. After about three weeks, the eggs begin to hatch. A tiny chick pecks its way out of the shell, using a small point on its beak called an egg tooth.

## A mother's care

A mother hen is one of the most caring animals on the farm. As soon as her chicks hatch, she keeps them warm under her wings and teaches them how to find food. She clucks softly to call them, and the chicks run to her at once. If danger comes near, she spreads her wings wide to hide her chicks underneath, or she calls them to run to her side.

The chicks stay close to their mother for the first few weeks of their lives. She shows them how to peck at seeds and how to scratch the ground. Little by little, the chicks grow feathers and learn to look after themselves. They grow up fast.

## Chickens in the farmyard

Chickens like to live in a group, called a flock. In a flock, the chickens know each other well. They have a pecking order, which means some chickens are bolder and eat first, while others wait their turn. This is how chickens keep things calm in the group. Chickens also love to roost, which means they sleep up high on a perch, like a branch or a wooden bar, safe from the ground.

## Looking after chickens kindly

Chickens are easy to look after if you know what they need. They need fresh water every day, food like grains and greens, and a clean, dry coop to sleep in. Their coop should be cleaned often, and they need a shady spot to rest in when the sun is hot. Chickens also need a safe place to lay their eggs and a perch to sleep on at night.

If you visit chickens, move slowly and speak softly. Do not chase them, because that frightens them. If you hold a chick, be very gentle, hold it with both hands, and put it back with its mother soon. Chickens that are treated kindly become tame and friendly, and some will even eat from your hand. Like all animals, chickens deserve clean food, fresh water, shelter and kind hands every day.

## Fun facts about chickens

- A rooster's crow is one of the loudest sounds on the farm, and it can be heard from far away.
- Hens can lay eggs in different colours, including white, brown, blue and green.
- A chick uses a tiny point on its beak, called an egg tooth, to peck its way out of its shell.
- Chickens take dust baths to keep their feathers clean and free of tiny bugs.
- A group of chickens living together is called a flock.
