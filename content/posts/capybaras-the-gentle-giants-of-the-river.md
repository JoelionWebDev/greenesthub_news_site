## Meet the Capybara

Imagine a guinea pig, then imagine it growing as big as a large dog. That is a capybara! Capybaras are the biggest rodents in the world. They are about the size of a medium dog, with a sturdy body, short legs, and no tail at all. Their fur is brown and a little rough, and their faces are friendly, with a big nose and small ears.

Capybaras live in South America, in countries like Brazil, Venezuela, and Argentina. They love places where there is water nearby: rivers, lakes, ponds, and marshy grasslands. If there is water and plenty of grass to eat, a capybara is a happy animal.

Even though they are big, capybaras are very gentle. They are not hunters, and they do not fight other animals. Instead, they spend their days grazing, swimming, and relaxing with their family and friends. It is no wonder that many people call them the calmest animals in the world.

## Life by the Water

A capybara is a brilliant swimmer. Its toes are partly webbed, a bit like a duck's feet, which help it paddle through the water. Its eyes, ears, and nose sit high on its head, so it can stay almost completely underwater and still see, hear, and breathe. Only the top of its head pokes out, like a tiny submarine!

Capybaras spend a lot of time in the water. On hot days, they cool off by taking a long swim or lying in a muddy pool. The water also helps keep them safe. If a capybara senses danger, it can slip into the river and hide under the water, holding its breath for a while. Its brown fur blends in with the muddy banks, making it hard to spot.

They are good at holding their breath, and they can even sleep in the water, with just their noses sticking out.

## What Do Capybaras Eat?

Capybaras are herbivores, which means they only eat plants. Their favourite food is grass, and they spend much of the day munching on it. They also eat water plants, leaves, and sometimes fruit that has fallen from trees.

A capybara's teeth never stop growing, a bit like your fingernails. Chewing tough grass all day helps wear the teeth down and keep them the right size. That is one reason capybaras are always nibbling: their teeth need the workout!

Capybaras eat a lot, because grass is not very filling. They need to munch for many hours each day to get enough energy.

## Friends of Everyone

One of the most amazing things about capybaras is how friendly they are. They live in groups, sometimes with just a few family members, and sometimes with many capybaras together. They groom each other, rest side by side, and take care of the young ones together.

Other animals seem to love capybaras too. Birds often sit on a capybara's back, picking off insects and ticks from its fur. The birds get a meal, and the capybara gets a free cleaning service. Turtles and even small monkeys have been seen resting on a capybara's back, as if it were a warm, furry bench.

Capybaras are so calm that they rarely chase other animals away. They just let their visitors sit there while they go on munching grass. It is like they have an open invitation: everyone is welcome to share the riverbank.

## Baby Capybaras

Baby capybaras are called pups, and they are some of the cutest babies in the animal world. They are born with fur and with their eyes open, and they can walk and even swim soon after they are born. That is very different from many other baby animals, which are born helpless and need weeks before they can move around.

A mother capybara usually has several pups at once. The whole group helps to look after them.

## Staying Safe

Capybaras are gentle animals, but life in the wild is not always easy. They share their rivers and grasslands with hunters like jaguars and caimans, a type of large reptile related to crocodiles. Capybaras need to be alert, even when they look relaxed.

Their best safety plan is to stay near water and stay together. In a group, there are many eyes and ears watching for danger. If one capybara barks a warning, the whole group knows to run for the water. In the river, they can hide or swim away quickly.

Capybaras also have good hearing and a good sense of smell, which help them notice danger early. And when they do need to move fast, they can run surprisingly quickly for such a chunky animal, and they can dive into the water in a flash.

## Why Capybaras Matter

Capybaras are an important part of their home. By grazing on grass, they help keep the grasslands healthy, a bit like a gardener trimming a lawn. They are also food for other animals, and their droppings help feed the soil and the plants that grow in it.

Capybaras also help scientists learn about nature. Because they are so calm and easy to watch, researchers can study how they live in groups and care for their young.

Sadly, capybaras face problems when their wetland homes are drained or damaged by people.

## The Gentle Giant of the River

Capybaras remind us that being big does not mean being scary. They are gentle, social, and happiest when they are splashing in the water with their friends and family. They share their riverbanks with birds, turtles, and monkeys, and they ask for nothing in return except grass to eat and water to swim in.

Next time you see a picture of a capybara with a bird on its head, remember: that is not a trick photo. That is just a normal day in the life of one of the friendliest animals on Earth.
