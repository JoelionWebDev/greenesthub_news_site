## What Is an Eagle?

An eagle is a very big bird of prey, which means it is a bird that hunts other animals for food. Eagles are some of the most powerful birds in the whole world. They have strong wings, sharp talons — the claws on their feet — and a hooked beak that is perfect for tearing food.

An eagle's eyes are amazing. They can see much, much better than we can! An eagle flying high in the sky can spot a small fish swimming in a river far below, or a tiny mouse moving in the grass. Eagles have two special parts in their eyes that help them see clearly, while we only have one.

There are many kinds of eagles. The bald eagle is the famous bird of the United States, with a white head and a dark brown body. The golden eagle lives in mountains and open lands in many parts of the world. The harpy eagle lives in the rainforests of South America and is one of the strongest eagles of all. The African fish eagle is famous for its loud, beautiful call. Every kind of eagle is a skilled hunter and a wonderful flyer.

## Where Do Eagles Live?

Eagles live all over the world, except in Antarctica. You can find them in North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, and Australia. They live in many different homes — in mountains, forests, deserts, grasslands, and near rivers, lakes, and the sea.

Eagles like places where they can find food and build a safe nest. Many eagles build their nests high up in tall trees or on the sides of cliffs, where their babies are safe from animals on the ground. Some eagles return to the same nest year after year, adding more sticks and branches each time. Over many years, an eagle's nest can become huge — one of the biggest nests of any bird in the world!

Some eagles stay in the same place all year round. Others travel long distances when the seasons change, flying to warmer places where food is easier to find. Eagles are strong flyers, and they can travel very far in a single day. When they fly, they often ride on warm air currents, called thermals, that lift them up high without them having to flap their wings very much.

## How Do Eagles Hunt?

Eagles are hunters, and they are very good at it. An eagle hunts by soaring high in the sky, looking down at the ground or water below with its sharp eyes. When it spots its prey — a fish, a rabbit, a snake, or a small bird — it folds its wings and dives down fast, like a lightning bolt!

An eagle catches its prey with its strong talons. The talons grab hold of the animal and do not let go. Then the eagle flies back to a tree or a rock to eat its meal, or it carries the food to its nest to share with its family. Some eagles, like the bald eagle, love to eat fish. They swoop down over the water and snatch fish right out of the river with their talons — without even getting wet!

Eagles do not hunt every day. After a big meal, an eagle can rest for a long time. Eagles are also clever — some of them steal food from other birds! A bald eagle might chase an osprey until the osprey drops its fish, and then the eagle catches the fish in mid-air.

## An Eagle's Family Life

Eagles usually live in pairs — a mother and a father who stay together and raise their babies as a team. The mother and father build their nest together, take turns sitting on the eggs, and both bring food to their chicks. Baby eagles are called eaglets.

When eaglets are born, they are covered in soft, fluffy down feathers. They are hungry little birds, and their parents bring them food every day. The parents tear the food into small pieces and feed it to the eaglets, beak to beak. As the eaglets grow, they get their real feathers, and their wings become strong.

Young eagles learn to fly by hopping around the nest and flapping their wings. Then one brave day, they take their first flight! At first, they are a bit wobbly, but soon they are soaring through the sky, just like their parents. Young eagles stay with their parents for a while, learning how to hunt and find food on their own. Then they fly off to find their own home and start a family of their own.

## Eagles and People

For thousands of years, people have admired eagles. The eagle is a symbol of strength, courage, and freedom in many countries around the world. The bald eagle is the national bird of the United States, and eagles appear on flags, coins, and badges in many places. In old stories and legends, eagles are often the kings of the birds.

But eagles need our help. Long ago, some kinds of eagles became very rare because people hunted them and because harmful chemicals got into their food. The bald eagle nearly disappeared from the United States, but people worked hard to protect it — they stopped using the harmful chemicals and guarded the eagles' nests. Today, bald eagles are doing much better, and their story shows that we can save animals when we work together!

If you ever see an eagle flying in the sky, watch how it soars. Remember that you are watching one of the most amazing hunters in the whole world.
