Imagine stepping outside on a freezing cold morning, with snow falling all around you and everything covered in white. You would probably run back inside to get warm. But in the mountains of Japan, there is a group of monkeys who have found a much better idea. They climb into warm, steamy pools of water and relax, just like people do in a hot bath. These amazing animals are called Japanese macaques, and most people know them by a much cuter name: snow monkeys.

Snow monkeys live in Japan, an island country in Asia. They are the monkeys that live furthest north in the whole world, which means they are used to cold weather that many other monkeys would never enjoy. Their thick, grey-brown fur keeps them warm, and their faces, hands, and bottoms are a lovely pinkish-red colour. When they sit in the snow, they look a little bit like small, furry people wearing warm winter coats.

## Life in the Snowy Mountains

The most famous snow monkeys live in a place called Jigokudani, which means Hell Valley. Do not worry, it is not a scary place at all. It got its name because steam rises from the ground there, and the rocks look steep and wild. In winter, the valley is covered in deep snow, and the trees wear thick white hats. It can be very cold, and the monkeys have to be tough to live there.

Luckily, the valley also has natural hot springs. A hot spring is a pool of water that is warmed by heat from deep inside the Earth. The water stays warm all year round, even when snow is falling. Long ago, the monkeys discovered these warm pools, and they have been enjoying them ever since. On a cold day, you might see a whole group of monkeys sitting in the water with only their heads poking out, looking calm, sleepy, and very happy.

The monkeys do not just jump in whenever they feel like it. They are clever about it. Young monkeys often play in the snow first, rolling around and chasing each other, and then they climb into the warm water to warm up again. Older monkeys sit quietly in the pool, sometimes closing their eyes as if they are having a lovely rest. When they get out, the steam rises from their wet fur, and they shake themselves dry before finding a sunny rock to sit on.

## A Big, Busy Family

Snow monkeys do not live alone. They live in large groups called troops, and a troop can have many monkeys in it. Living together helps them stay safe and warm. At night, they huddle close together to share body heat, a bit like a big family cuddle. During the day, they move around together, looking for food and playing in the snow.

Every troop has its own way of doing things. The monkeys know each other well, and they spend a lot of time grooming each other. Grooming means carefully picking through another monkey's fur to clean it. It may sound like a funny job, but it is very important. Grooming helps keep the monkeys clean, and it also helps them stay friends. A monkey that grooms another monkey is saying, in monkey language, that they care about them.

Mother monkeys are very loving. A baby snow monkey stays close to its mother for a long time. The mother carries the baby on her tummy when it is tiny, and later the baby rides on her back, holding on tight. Baby monkeys love to play. They climb trees, chase each other through the snow, and sometimes even make little snowballs with their hands. Watching them play is one of the sweetest sights in the animal world.

## What Do Snow Monkeys Eat?

Snow monkeys are not fussy eaters. In the warmer months, they eat leaves, fruits, seeds, nuts, and insects. They also enjoy eating bark and buds from trees. In winter, when the ground is covered in snow and food is harder to find, they eat whatever they can. They might dig through the snow to find roots and shoots, or nibble on tree bark. Sometimes, people who look after the monkeys leave out extra food for them, such as sweet potatoes and grains, to help them through the coldest days.

Finding food in winter takes a lot of work, and that is another reason the hot springs are so helpful. After a long morning of searching for food in the cold, a warm soak helps the monkeys relax their tired bodies. It is a bit like coming home after a long day and having a warm bath before bed.

Snow monkeys are also very clever, and mothers teach their babies new skills, which shows that they are smart animals with good memories.

## Why the Hot Springs Matter

The hot springs are not just a fun place to relax. They help the monkeys survive the winter. Cold weather can be hard on animals, and staying warm uses a lot of energy. By soaking in the warm water, the monkeys can warm up without using as much energy. That leaves them with more energy for finding food, playing, and looking after their babies.

The monkeys also use the pools at different times of day. In the morning, they might sit in the water to warm up before they start looking for food. In the afternoon, after a busy day, they return for another soak. Some monkeys even fall asleep in the water, with their heads resting on the edge of the pool. They look so peaceful that it is easy to forget they are wild animals.

People from all over the world come to see the snow monkeys. They watch quietly from a safe distance so that the monkeys are not frightened.

## Fun Things to Remember

Snow monkeys are the most northern-living monkeys in the world. They have thick fur, pink faces, and a love of warm water. They live in big family groups, groom each other to stay close, and raise their babies with lots of care. They play in the snow, eat what the forest gives them, and take warm baths in natural hot springs when the weather turns cold.

Next time you have a warm bath on a cold day, think of the snow monkeys in Japan. Somewhere far away, in a snowy mountain valley, a group of furry friends is doing just the same thing, soaking in a steamy pool with snowflakes falling gently on their heads.
