If you look closely at a fish tank, you might spot something tiny, colourful and very busy. It has long, waving feelers, lots of little legs, and it is always nibbling away at something. That is an aquarium shrimp! These tiny creatures are the cleaners of the fish world, and they are one of the most fun and helpful pets you can keep in a tank.

## Meet the Aquarium Shrimp

Aquarium shrimp are small crustaceans, which means they are cousins of crabs and lobsters. But unlike their big cousins, pet shrimp are tiny — most are only a couple of centimetres long, about the size of your little fingernail. They come in many beautiful colours: bright red cherry shrimp, sunny yellow shrimp, deep blue shrimp, and even striped and spotted ones.

Two of the most popular pet shrimp are cherry shrimp and amano shrimp. Cherry shrimp are small, bright red and very pretty, and they love to live in groups. Amano shrimp are bigger, with a see-through body and a dotted pattern, and they are famous for eating algae, the green stuff that grows on tank walls. Ghost shrimp are almost clear, so you can see right through them — you can even see their tiny tummy working!

Shrimp have ten legs, long antennae that they wave around to feel their way, and a tail that they flick to swim backwards in a sudden dart. They also have tiny claws that they use to pick up food. Watching a shrimp use its little front legs to stuff food into its mouth is one of the funniest sights in a fish tank.

## The Tiny Cleaners

Aquarium shrimp get their nickname, "the tiny cleaners of the tank," because they are always eating. They munch on algae, the green fuzz that grows on glass, plants and decorations. They nibble leftover fish food that falls to the bottom. They clean up dead plant leaves and other tiny bits of waste. In this way, they help keep the tank clean and healthy for the fish.

A single shrimp can only eat a little bit, but a group of shrimp working together can make a real difference. That is why shrimp are usually kept in groups of six or more. A team of shrimp will spread out across the tank, each one cleaning its own little patch, like a tiny cleaning crew.

Shrimp are also very good at finding food in places other animals cannot reach. They can climb onto plants, squeeze into tiny gaps between stones, and even hang upside down from a leaf while they nibble. Their little legs are perfect for picking and scraping.

## A Colourful Underwater World

Shrimp do more than clean — they also make a tank beautiful. A group of bright red cherry shrimp moving through green plants looks like a scattering of rubies. Blue shrimp glow against dark stones. A tank full of shrimp is like a living jewel box.

## What Do Shrimp Eat?

Shrimp eat almost anything they can find, which is why they are such good cleaners. Their main foods are algae and biofilm, the thin, slippery layer of tiny living things that grows on surfaces in the tank. They also eat leftover fish food, dead plant leaves and special shrimp food that comes in tiny pellets or wafers.

Grown-ups can also give shrimp a treat of blanched vegetables — vegetables that have been quickly boiled and cooled — such as courgette, spinach or carrot. The shrimp will gather around the food and nibble it happily. Any uneaten food should be removed after a day or so, so it does not make the water dirty. ## A Shrimp's Home and Care

Shrimp need a tank with clean water, gentle filtration and plenty of hiding places. Plants are very important — shrimp love to climb on them, hide under them and graze on the biofilm that grows on their leaves. Moss, in particular, is a favourite shrimp hangout. Stones, driftwood and small caves also make great shrimp shelters.

The tank should have a gentle filter, because shrimp are small and can be sucked into a strong one. A sponge filter is a good choice, because it is gentle and the shrimp can even graze on it. Shrimp are sensitive to changes in their water, so the tank needs to be set up and running well before the shrimp move in. An adult should check the water with a test kit to make sure it is safe. Shrimp like stable, clean water, and they do not like sudden changes.

Shrimp are quiet, peaceful pets that are mostly watched, not handled. They should not be taken out of the water, because they breathe through gills and need to stay in the tank.

## Growing a New Shell

Like many small animals with hard shells, shrimp moult. A shrimp's body is covered by a hard shell that does not grow, so as the shrimp grows, it sheds its old shell and grows a new, bigger one. After moulting, the shrimp is soft and hides away until its new shell hardens. You might find an empty shell in the tank that looks like a ghost shrimp — do not worry, that is normal, and the shrimp will often eat its old shell to get back the minerals in it.

## Fun Facts About Aquarium Shrimp

- Cherry shrimp get their bright red colour from the food they eat and the water they live in — happy shrimp are brighter shrimp.
- Amano shrimp are named after Takashi Amano, a famous Japanese aquarium designer who loved using them to keep tanks clean.
- A female shrimp carries her eggs under her tail, fanning them gently with her little legs to keep them clean.
- Baby shrimp are called shrimplets, and they are so tiny that they are hard to see without looking closely.

Aquarium shrimp may be small, but they are mighty helpers. They clean, they sparkle, they explore and they turn an ordinary fish tank into a tiny, busy underwater city. Next time you see a fish tank, look closely — you might just spot a tiny cleaner hard at work!
