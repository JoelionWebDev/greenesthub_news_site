Octopuses are some of the cleverest animals in the sea, and they are full of surprises. An octopus has a soft, squishy body, eight long arms covered in suckers, and no bones at all. It can change colour in the blink of an eye, squeeze through a gap as small as a coin, and squirt a cloud of dark ink to escape from danger. Octopuses live in oceans all around the world, from warm coral reefs to cold, deep waters. They are excellent hunters and amazing escape artists, and scientists have watched them solve puzzles, open jars, and slip out of tanks. With three hearts and blue blood, octopuses are truly some of the strangest and most wonderful animals on Earth.

## What is an octopus?

An octopus is a sea animal and a cousin of the squid and the snail. Its body is soft and has no bones, which is why it can squeeze into such tiny spaces. An octopus has a round head with two big eyes, and eight arms that grow out from its head. The arms are covered in suckers, which are like tiny cups that grip onto rocks, shells, and food. Octopuses have three hearts, and their blood is blue, which is very unusual. An octopus also has a beak, a bit like a parrot's beak, hidden where its arms meet, which it uses to bite its food. Its skin is covered in tiny sacs of colour that let it change its skin in an instant.

## The masters of disguise

One of the most amazing things about octopuses is how they change colour. An octopus can turn red, brown, white, blue, or almost any colour in a flash. It can even change the texture of its skin, making bumpy spots to look like a rock or smooth skin to look like sand. This helps the octopus hide from hungry animals like sharks and eels, and it also helps it sneak up on its dinner. When an octopus is scared, it can squirt a cloud of dark ink into the water. The ink makes a dark, cloudy puff that hides the octopus while it swims away fast. Changing colour is also a way octopuses talk to each other, showing whether they are calm, angry, or excited.

## The great escape artists

Because an octopus has no bones, it can squeeze through the tiniest gaps. The only hard part of its body is its beak, so if its beak can fit through a hole, the whole octopus can follow. Octopuses have escaped from tanks through small pipes and cracks, and they can open lids and unscrew jars to get to food inside. An octopus uses its arms to feel and explore, tasting with its suckers as it touches things. If an octopus loses an arm, a new one can grow back in time. 

## Where do octopuses live?

Octopuses live in oceans all over the world, from warm, shallow coral reefs to the cold, dark deep sea. They like to live in rocky places, coral reefs, and sandy sea floors where there are lots of cracks and holes to hide in. An octopus makes its home in a den, which might be a hole in a rock, an empty shell, or a gap under a stone. It often decorates the entrance of its den with shells and pebbles. Octopuses are shy animals and they spend much of the day hiding in their dens, coming out to hunt at night or when the water is quiet. Wherever they live, octopuses need clean, salty sea water and plenty of places to hide.

## What do octopuses eat?

Octopuses are hunters, and they eat crabs, shrimps, snails, clams, and small fish. They hunt mostly at night, creeping along the sea floor on their long arms and feeling into cracks and holes with their suckers. When an octopus finds a crab, it wraps its arms around it and pulls it close. It can also use its beak to crack open a clam shell or a snail shell. Octopuses are very good at finding food that is hiding, because their arms can reach into small spaces that other animals cannot. After a meal, an octopus often leaves the empty shells outside its den, like a tidy pile of dishes. A hungry octopus is a patient hunter, and it will wait quietly until just the right moment to pounce.

## Baby octopuses

A mother octopus lays many tiny eggs, sometimes thousands of them, in her den. She hangs the eggs in long strings from the roof of her den, like bunches of tiny grapes. Then she stays with them, guarding them and blowing clean water over them to keep them safe. She does not leave to eat while she guards her eggs, and she keeps watch until they hatch. When the baby octopuses hatch, they are very tiny, about the size of a grain of rice, but they look like perfect little octopuses with eight arms. The babies float up into the sea and drift with the currents, eating tiny animals as they grow. Octopuses do not live for very long, usually only a few years, but in that short time they live a clever and busy life.

## Fun facts about octopuses

- Octopuses have three hearts and blue blood.
- An octopus can squeeze through any gap its beak can fit through, because it has no bones.
- Octopuses can change colour and skin texture in the blink of an eye to hide or to talk.
- An octopus can squirt a cloud of dark ink to hide while it escapes from danger.
- If an octopus loses an arm, a new one can grow back.

Octopuses are clever, colourful, and full of surprises. With eight arms, three hearts, and a brain that loves to solve puzzles, they are the greatest escape artists of the sea. Next time you look at the ocean, imagine an octopus hiding in the rocks below, changing colour to match the coral.
