Imagine a frog so clear you can see its tiny heart beating inside its body. That is the glass frog, a real little wonder of the rainforest. These small frogs live in the rainforests of Central and South America, and they look like they were made from green glass. When one sits on a leaf, you can see right through its belly to the organs inside. It is one of the strangest and most beautiful sights in the animal world.

## A Frog Made of Glass

Glass frogs are small, about the size of a coin or a little bigger. Their backs are a bright leafy green, which helps them hide among the leaves during the day. But their bellies are clear, like a window. If you look at a glass frog from below, you can see its heart, its liver and even its bones. It looks like a tiny living jewel.

Scientists think the see-through belly helps the frog hide. When light shines through the leaves, a solid frog would cast a shadow and be easy to spot. But a glass frog lets the light pass through, so it blends in with the leaf it is sitting on. It is a clever trick that keeps it safe from hungry birds and snakes.

There are many kinds of glass frogs, and they come in different shades of green. Some have tiny white or yellow spots on their backs. Some have green bones, which makes them look even more magical. They are all small, gentle creatures that would fit easily in the palm of your hand.

## Life in the Rainforest

Glass frogs live near streams and rivers in the rainforest, where the air is warm and damp. During the day they sleep on the undersides of leaves, hiding from the sun and from animals that might eat them. At night they wake up and start to sing. The males make a soft, high whistle to call to the females, and the forest fills with their tiny songs.

They eat small insects such as ants, flies and tiny beetles. They catch their food with their long, sticky tongues, which shoot out quickly and pull the insect into their mouths. Glass frogs are very good hunters for their size, and they help keep the insect numbers in the forest in balance.

Glass frogs need clean water and healthy forests to survive. They breathe partly through their skin, which must stay wet, so they live close to streams and in places where it rains a lot. If the forest is cut down or the stream is polluted, the frogs cannot live there any more.

## Singing in the Night

When night falls in the rainforest, the glass frogs begin to sing. Each male finds a good leaf near the stream and starts to whistle, a soft, high sound that carries through the damp air. The females listen carefully, because each male has his own song, and they choose the male whose song they like best. It is like a singing contest in the dark, with the prize being a family of little glass frogs.

The males are very brave about their singing. They sit out in the open on a leaf, where a hungry snake or bird could spot them, but they keep singing anyway. Their bright green backs help them blend in with the leaf, and they stay very still between songs, hoping that no one notices them.

If another male comes too close, the two frogs may have a little argument. They face each other, puff themselves up and make louder calls, trying to scare each other away. Usually one of them gives up and hops off to find a different leaf, and the winner keeps his singing spot.

## The Bravest Frog Dads

One of the most wonderful things about glass frogs is how the fathers look after the eggs. The female lays her eggs on the underside of a leaf that hangs over a stream. Then she hops away, and the father stays behind to guard them.

The father frog sits beside the eggs for days, keeping them safe from hungry insects and other animals. He keeps the eggs wet by sitting on them, and if an egg starts to dry out, he will press his body against it to give it moisture. He is a very devoted dad.

When the eggs hatch, the tiny tadpoles drop down from the leaf into the stream below. Splash! They swim away into the water, where they will grow and change. Over time they grow legs, lose their tails and become little frogs, ready to climb up into the leaves themselves.

## Why Glass Frogs Matter

Glass frogs are important because they tell us how healthy the rainforest is. Because they breathe through their skin, they are very sensitive to changes in the air and water. If glass frogs are doing well, it usually means the forest is healthy. If they disappear, it is a warning that something is wrong.

Sadly, glass frogs are in danger in many places. Their forests are being cut down, and the streams they need are drying up or becoming dirty. People are working to protect the rainforests of Central and South America, so that glass frogs and all the other amazing animals that live there can survive.

## A Tiny Window Into Nature

The glass frog shows us that nature is full of wonders we could never imagine. A frog you can see through sounds like something from a fairy tale, but it is real, and it is living right now in the rainforests of the Americas. It reminds us that even the smallest animals can be extraordinary.

So next time you look at a leaf, imagine a tiny green frog sitting on the other side, its heart beating softly inside its see-through body. The world is full of hidden magic like that, and the glass frog is one of its brightest little jewels.
