Imagine an animal so small it could sit in the palm of your hand, with eyes so big they are almost as wide as its whole head. That animal is real, and it is called a tarsier. Tarsiers are some of the smallest primates in the world, and they are also some of the strangest and most wonderful. Let's travel to the forests of Southeast Asia and meet these tiny, big-eyed creatures.

## Meet the Tarsier

Tarsiers are primates, which means they are related to monkeys, apes, and even humans. But tarsiers are very different from other primates. They are tiny, they are nocturnal, and they have the largest eyes of any mammal compared to the size of their body. Each of a tarsier's eyes is bigger than its brain. That is a truly amazing fact.

A tarsier's body is only about the size of a small squirrel. It has soft, grey or brown fur, a long thin tail, and very long fingers and toes. Its ears are large and pointed, and they can move independently, like little satellite dishes picking up sounds from all directions. Its face is round, with a small nose and a tiny mouth.

Tarsiers live in the forests of Southeast Asia, on islands like Borneo, Sumatra, Sulawesi, and the Philippines. They live in trees and bushes, where they can hide during the day and hunt at night. They like forests with plenty of low branches and thick undergrowth, where they can leap from branch to branch in search of food.

There are several different kinds of tarsiers, and they live on different islands. Each kind has adapted to its own home, but they all share the same big eyes, long fingers, and amazing jumping ability. Scientists study tarsiers to learn more about how primates evolved and how they live in the wild.

## Eyes That See in the Dark

A tarsier's eyes are its most famous feature. They are huge, round, and forward-facing, just like our eyes. But a tarsier's eyes are so big that they cannot move them from side to side. Instead, the tarsier turns its whole head to look around. It can turn its head almost all the way around, like an owl. This helps it spot insects and other small animals in the dark.

Tarsiers are nocturnal, which means they are active at night and sleep during the day. Their huge eyes help them see in the dark, gathering as much light as possible. At night, when the forest is lit only by the moon and stars, a tarsier can spot a tiny insect moving on a leaf from far away. Its eyes are like night-vision goggles, built right into its head.

During the day, tarsiers sleep in a safe hiding place. They might curl up in a hollow tree, hide in a tangle of vines, or press themselves flat against a tree trunk, where their fur blends in with the bark. They sleep with their eyes closed, of course, and they are very hard to spot. If you walk through a tarsier forest during the day, you might pass right by a sleeping tarsier and never know it was there.

A tarsier's ears are also amazing. They are large and can move independently, which means one ear can point one way while the other ear points another way. This helps the tarsier listen for the tiny sounds of insects moving in the dark. Its hearing is so sharp that it can hear sounds too high for humans to hear.

## The Super Jumper

Tarsiers are incredible jumpers. Their back legs are very long and strong, and they can leap many times their own body length in a single jump. Imagine if you could jump over a whole bus in one leap. That is what a tarsier can do, in its own small scale.

Tarsiers use their jumping ability to move through the forest and to catch food. They leap from branch to branch, using their long fingers and toes to grip the wood. Their fingers have round, sticky pads on the tips, which help them hold on to branches and leaves. Their long tail helps them balance when they jump and land.

When a tarsier spots an insect, it leaps towards it with amazing speed and accuracy. It snatches the insect out of the air or off a leaf with its hands, then eats it quickly. Tarsiers are very good at this, and they can catch insects that are flying, crawling, or hiding in the leaves.

Tarsiers are the only primates that eat only meat. Most other primates eat fruit, leaves, and other plant foods, but tarsiers are strict carnivores. They eat insects like crickets, grasshoppers, and beetles. They also eat spiders, small lizards, and even small birds if they can catch them. They do not eat plants at all.

## Long Fingers for a Tiny Hunter

A tarsier's fingers are very long and thin, almost like the fingers of a tiny person. These long fingers help the tarsier grip branches and catch insects. The tips of its fingers have round pads that work a bit like suction cups, helping it hold on tightly. This is very useful when the tarsier is leaping through the trees at night.

Tarsiers also have long toes on their feet, which help them grip branches when they land from a jump. Their feet are specially designed for climbing and leaping. The tarsier's ankle bones are very long, which gives its legs extra spring. This is why tarsiers are such good jumpers. The name "tarsier" comes from these long ankle bones, which scientists call tarsals.

When a tarsier is resting, it often sits upright on a branch, holding on with its hands and feet. It looks like a tiny person sitting on a tree limb, watching the world go by. Its long tail hangs down behind it, helping it balance. This upright posture is unusual for a primate, and it makes tarsiers look very cute and very funny.

The tarsier is a tiny animal with huge eyes and an even huger personality. It leaps through the dark forests of Southeast Asia, hunting insects and living a life that is all its own. The next time you look up at the moon, think of the tarsier, wide awake in the trees, its enormous eyes shining in the dark.
