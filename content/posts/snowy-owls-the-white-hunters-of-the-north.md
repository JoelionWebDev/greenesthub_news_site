Imagine a big white bird sitting quietly on a snowy rock, watching the world with bright yellow eyes. That is a snowy owl. Snowy owls are some of the most beautiful birds in the world. Their feathers are white, soft, and thick, and they help the owl blend in with the snow. In the far north, where snow covers the ground for much of the year, a snowy owl can sit still and almost disappear. You might walk right past one without even knowing it was there.

Snowy owls live in the cold lands of the far north, in a place called the Arctic. The Arctic is a land of snow, ice, wind, and long winter nights. It is one of the coldest places on Earth, but the snowy owl is made for it. Its thick feathers keep it warm, its strong wings help it fly through cold winds, and its sharp eyes help it find food even when the land looks empty and white. Snowy owls are strong, clever, and patient hunters.

## A Coat as White as Snow

The first thing you notice about a snowy owl is its colour. Most snowy owls are white, and many have dark spots and bars on their feathers. Male snowy owls are often whiter than females, and young owls often have more dark marks. No two snowy owls look exactly the same, just as no two snowflakes are exactly the same.

The white feathers are not just pretty. They are a clever disguise. When a snowy owl sits on snow-covered ground, its white feathers help it hide from other animals. This is called camouflage, and it helps the owl stay safe and helps it hunt without being seen too soon. A snowy owl can sit very still for a long time, watching and waiting, until the right moment comes.

Snowy owls also have thick feathers on their legs and feet. Most owls have bare legs, but a snowy owl wears a warm, feathery coat right down to its toes. Those feathery feet work a little like warm boots and help the owl walk on snow and ice without getting too cold. Its sharp claws, called talons, are hidden under the feathers, ready to grab food when the owl needs them.

## Eyes That See in the Snow

Snowy owls have large, round, bright yellow eyes. Their eyes are so big and beautiful that they look like two golden suns in a white face. Those big eyes help the owl see very well, even when the light is low. In the far north, the sun can stay low in the sky for a long time, and in winter the days can be very short. A snowy owl needs sharp eyes to find food in that dim light.

Unlike many owls, snowy owls are often awake during the day. Most owls hunt at night, but snowy owls hunt in daylight too. In the Arctic summer, the sun can stay up for a very long time, and there is hardly any night at all. A snowy owl that only hunted at night would go hungry, so it has learned to hunt whenever it can see its food.

## A Silent Hunter

Snowy owls are hunters, and they are very good at it. They eat small animals that live in the Arctic, such as lemmings and other small rodents. Lemmings are small, furry animals that look a little like mice, and they are the snowy owl's favourite food. A snowy owl may also eat other small animals and birds when it can catch them.

A snowy owl hunts by sitting high up and watching. It may sit on a rock, a mound of snow, or a low hill, and it will watch the ground below with great care. When it sees a small animal moving in the snow, it swoops down fast and quietly. Its wings are big and soft, and they make almost no sound as they beat the air. That silent flight helps the owl surprise its food.

## Life in the Cold North

Snowy owls live in open, treeless lands called tundra. The tundra is a wide, flat land with low plants, rocks, and snow. There are very few trees, so a snowy owl can see a long way in every direction. It is a hard place to live, with strong winds and very cold weather, but the snowy owl is built for it. Its thick feathers, warm feet, and strong body help it stay warm even when the wind blows hard.

In spring and summer, snowy owls build nests on the ground. The mother owl lays her eggs in a shallow hollow in the ground, often on a small hill where she can see all around. She sits on the eggs to keep them warm while the father owl brings her food. When the baby owls hatch, they are covered in soft, grey-white down, and they look like little fluffy clouds.

Baby snowy owls grow fast. Their parents bring them food every day, and the young owls learn to fly and hunt as they grow bigger.

## A Traveller of the North

Snowy owls do not always stay in one place. When food is hard to find, they fly south to new lands where there is more to eat. Some snowy owls travel a long way, and people in places far from the Arctic are sometimes lucky enough to see one sitting on a fence, a roof, or a beach. Bird watchers get very excited when one appears.

Snowy owls are wild birds, and they need wild, open spaces to live and hunt. They need clean air, quiet places, and plenty of small animals to eat. People can help snowy owls by looking after wild places and by watching owls from a safe distance. A snowy owl that feels safe will stay and hunt, and it may even come back to the same place again.

If you ever see a snowy owl, take a quiet moment to watch it. Look at its white feathers, its golden eyes, and its calm, proud way of sitting. It is a hunter, a traveller, and a true bird of the north. The snowy owl reminds us that even in the coldest, snowiest places on Earth, life is strong, beautiful, and full of wonder.
