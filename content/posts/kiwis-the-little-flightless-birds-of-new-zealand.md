# Kiwis: The Little Flightless Birds of New Zealand

Imagine a small, round bird, about the size of a chicken, covered in soft, shaggy brown feathers that look like fur. It has a long, thin beak, tiny wings hidden under its feathers, and it cannot fly at all. It comes out at night, sniffing the ground with its beak, looking for worms and insects. This is a kiwi, one of the most unusual birds in the world, and it lives only in New Zealand.



## A Bird That Cannot Fly

Kiwis are flightless birds, which means they cannot fly. Their wings are very small and hidden under their feathers, so small that you can hardly see them. Long ago, New Zealand had no land mammals like foxes or cats to hunt birds, so kiwis did not need to fly to escape danger. Instead, they became good at walking and running on the ground.

Kiwis are about the size of a chicken, and they are round and plump. Their feathers are soft and shaggy, more like fur than feathers, and they are brown or grey, which helps them hide in the forest at night. They have strong legs and sharp claws, which they use to run and to dig in the soil.

Kiwis are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. During the day, they hide in burrows or under logs and bushes. At night, they come out to look for food, shuffling through the forest floor in the dark.

## A Beak With a Super Sense of Smell

The kiwi's beak is long, thin, and a little bit curved. It is one of the most special beaks in the bird world, because it has nostrils at the tip. Most birds have nostrils at the base of their beak, near their face, but the kiwi's nostrils are right at the end. This helps the kiwi smell its food underground.

Kiwis have a wonderful sense of smell, which is very unusual for a bird. Most birds rely on their eyes to find food, but kiwis rely on their noses. They poke their beaks into the soft soil and sniff out worms, insects, and other small creatures hiding underground. They also have whiskers around the base of their beak, like a cat's whiskers, which help them feel their way in the dark.

When a kiwi finds a worm, it pulls it out of the ground and eats it. Kiwis also eat berries, seeds, and fallen fruit. They are not fussy eaters, and they will eat whatever they can find on the forest floor.

## The Giant Egg

One of the most amazing things about kiwis is their egg. A female kiwi lays an egg that is huge compared to her body. It is one of the biggest eggs of any bird, compared to the size of the mother. The egg is so big that it takes up a lot of space inside the kiwi, and the mother has to eat a lot of food to get ready to lay it.

After the egg is laid, the father kiwi does most of the sitting on it. He sits on the egg in the burrow, keeping it warm, while the mother goes off to feed. The egg takes a long time to hatch, and when the chick comes out, it is already quite big and well-developed. The chick has feathers, and it can walk and feed itself soon after hatching, which is different from many other baby birds that need their parents to feed them for weeks.

Kiwi chicks grow up quickly, but life is not easy for them. They have to be careful, because they are small and the forest can be dangerous. Young kiwis stay close to their burrows at first, and then they wander off to find their own territories.


## The National Bird of New Zealand

The kiwi is the national bird of New Zealand, and it is very important to the people there. New Zealanders are often called Kiwis, after the bird, and the kiwi is on coins, stamps, and logos all over the country. The kiwi is a symbol of New Zealand, and people are very proud of it.

The kiwi's name comes from the sound it makes. Its call sounds like "kee-wee, kee-wee," and the Maori people, who are the first people of New Zealand, named the bird after its call. The kiwi is a special part of Maori culture, and its feathers were once used to make beautiful cloaks for important people.

## Protecting the Kiwis

Kiwis are in danger. Long ago, New Zealand had no land mammals, so kiwis had no predators to worry about. But when people came to New Zealand, they brought animals like stoats, rats, cats, and dogs, which hunt kiwis and their eggs. These animals are a big problem for kiwis, especially for young chicks, which are small and cannot defend themselves.

People in New Zealand are working hard to protect kiwis. There are special programmes that trap and remove predators from kiwi forests, and there are safe places called sanctuaries where kiwis can live without danger. Some kiwi chicks are raised in safe places until they are big enough to look after themselves, and then they are released into the wild. Scientists study kiwis to learn more about them, and people are taught to keep their dogs on leads in kiwi areas, so the dogs do not hurt the birds.

## Fun Facts About Kiwis

- Kiwis are the only birds with nostrils at the tip of their beaks.
- They have a super sense of smell, which is rare for a bird.
- Kiwis cannot fly, and their wings are tiny and hidden under their feathers.
- A kiwi egg is huge compared to the mother's body, one of the biggest eggs for a bird's size.
- The father kiwi does most of the sitting on the egg.
- Kiwi chicks can walk and feed themselves soon after they hatch.
- Kiwis are nocturnal, so they are active at night and sleep during the day.

Kiwis are small, strange, and wonderful birds. With their long beaks, their super sense of smell, and their giant eggs, they are unlike any other bird in the world. Next time you hear the word "kiwi," remember the little flightless bird of New Zealand, shuffling through the forest at night, sniffing out its dinner in the dark.
