Have you ever met a lizard that waves hello? Bearded dragons do just that! These gentle reptiles are some of the friendliest lizards in the world, and lots of families keep them as pets. They come from the warm, dry lands of Australia, where they spend their days basking in the sunshine on rocks and branches. With their calm nature, funny faces, and curious eyes, bearded dragons have become one of the most loved pet lizards anywhere.

What makes one so special? It has a beard — a flap of skin under its chin that can puff out and turn dark when the dragon feels excited or wants to look big and brave.

## Where Do Bearded Dragons Come From?

Bearded dragons come from Australia, a country famous for sunshine, deserts, and amazing animals. In the wild, they live in dry places with lots of rocks, sandy ground, and scrubby bushes. They love warm weather because they are cold-blooded animals, which means their bodies need the sun to warm them up. In the morning, a wild bearded dragon will climb onto a sunny rock and stretch out flat to soak up the heat.

Pet bearded dragons come in sandy tan, golden yellow, and soft orange shades. Bearded dragons are daytime animals, which means they are awake during the day and sleep at night, just like us. That makes them wonderful pets, because you can watch them explore, eat, and bask while you are awake too.

## What Does a Bearded Dragon Look Like?

A bearded dragon is a medium-sized lizard with a wide, flat head, a sturdy body, and a long tail. Its skin is covered in tiny scales, and it has spiky little scales along the sides of its body and around its head. Do not worry — those spikes look prickly, but they are soft to touch and will not hurt you.

Under its chin is the famous beard. Boy bearded dragons puff their beards more often than girls, especially when they see another dragon or something new. Sometimes the beard turns dark grey or black when it puffs up, which makes the dragon look very serious — even though it is really quite sweet.

Bearded dragons have strong legs with little claws that help them climb rocks and branches. They often tilt their heads to look at you, as if they are listening.

## The Famous Wave Hello

One of the cutest things bearded dragons do is wave. Yes, really wave! A bearded dragon will lift one of its front legs and move it slowly in a circle, like it is saying hello. Scientists think this wave is a friendly signal that means "I am calm, and I am not looking for trouble." In the wild, a smaller dragon might wave to a bigger dragon to show it is friendly.

Bearded dragons also do a funny little head bob. They move their heads up and down quickly, like they are nodding yes, yes, yes! Head bobbing can mean different things. Sometimes it means "Hello, I am here!" and sometimes it means "This is my space." It is one of the ways dragons talk to each other without making a sound.

When a bearded dragon is happy and relaxed, it might close its eyes while you gently stroke its back, or sit calmly on your hand.

## What Do Bearded Dragons Eat?

Bearded dragons are not fussy eaters. When they are young, they eat mostly insects, because they need lots of protein to grow strong. As they grow older, they start to eat more vegetables and leafy greens, and fewer insects. Grown-up bearded dragons eat a mix of both, which makes them omnivores — animals that eat both plants and animals.

Their favourite insects include crickets, which they love to chase and catch. Watching a bearded dragon hunt a cricket is very fun. It watches carefully, then — snap! — its quick tongue flicks out and catches the insect. Their vegetable meals include dark leafy greens, squash, carrots, and other colourful vegetables, chopped into small pieces so the dragon can nibble them easily. They need fresh water to drink every day.

## A Happy Home for a Bearded Dragon

A pet bearded dragon needs a home that feels like its sunny homeland in Australia. Pet owners keep them in a special tank called a vivarium, a big glass home with warm lights that act like the sun. One side of the tank is warm and bright for basking, and the other side is cooler, so the dragon can choose where it wants to be.

Inside the tank, there are rocks and branches to climb on, and a cosy hiding spot where the dragon can rest when it wants some quiet time. Bearded dragons also need time outside their tank, with gentle handling. They like to explore a safe room or sit on a warm lap, and children should always have a grown-up nearby when they hold one.

## Are Bearded Dragons Good Pets?

Bearded dragons make wonderful pets for families who are ready to look after them properly. They are calm, they do not bite, and they are awake during the day, so you can enjoy watching them. They do not need walks like a dog, and they do not make loud noises, so they are quiet housemates too.

But like all pets, they need care every single day — fresh food and water, a warm and clean home, and gentle attention — and a visit to a vet if they ever feel poorly. If you are thinking about one as a pet, learn all about them first, and talk to a grown-up.

## Fun Facts

- A bearded dragon can change the colour of its beard to dark grey or black when it puffs it out.
- Bearded dragons wave one front leg in a slow circle as a friendly greeting.
- Bearded dragons close their eyes when they feel calm and content.
- A bearded dragon's tongue is quick and sticky, perfect for catching insects.
- Wild bearded dragons live in Australia, where they bask on rocks in the warm sunshine.
- Young bearded dragons eat mostly insects, while grown-ups eat lots of vegetables too.

Bearded dragons are gentle, funny, and full of character. If you ever meet one, watch for that slow, friendly wave — it might just be saying hello to you!
