Walk into a farmyard in Africa, and you may hear a loud, rattling call before you see anything at all. Look up, and you might see a grey bird with white spots all over its feathers, a little helmet on its head, and bright red skin around its eyes. That is a guinea fowl, one of the loudest and most useful birds on any farm. With spotty feathers, loud voices and a fearless heart, the guinea fowl is the farm bird that watches over the yard and gobbles up ticks and insects.

## A Bird from Africa

Guinea fowl come from Africa, where their wild cousins still live on the grasslands and in the bush. Long ago, people in Africa began keeping them on farms because they were so useful, and later the birds travelled to farms all over the world. Today, you can find guinea fowl on farms in many countries, from Nigeria to France to the United States.

The most common kind is the helmeted guinea fowl. It gets its name from the hard, bony bump on top of its head, which looks like a little helmet. Its feathers are dark grey with neat rows of white spots, like a starry night sky. Its neck is bare, with skin that can be blue, red or white, and it has a small beard of feathers under its chin. Guinea fowl are not as pretty as peacocks, but they have a style all of their own.

Guinea fowl are strong fliers for a farm bird, and they love to roost in trees at night, where they are safe from animals on the ground. If they are kept on a farm, they need a safe place to sleep, such as a hen house or a high perch.

## The Loudest Alarm in the Farmyard

Guinea fowl are famous for their loud voices. They make a harsh, rattling call that sounds a bit like a machine gun, and they call out whenever they see something strange. A stranger walking into the yard, a dog passing by, or even a snake in the grass will set them off, and the whole flock will call out together at the top of their lungs.

Farmers love this noisy habit, because guinea fowl act like living alarm bells. They warn the farmer and the other animals when danger is near, and their loud calls can scare away snakes, rats and other unwanted visitors. Some farmers keep guinea fowl just for this reason, to guard their chickens and other farm animals.

Guinea fowl are also brave. They will chase away small animals that come too close, and they have even been known to attack snakes. They do not back down easily, and their loud voices and flapping wings can be very frightening to a sneaky visitor.

## The Best Bug Hunters

Guinea fowl are wonderful bug hunters. They love to eat insects, and they spend most of the day walking around the farmyard, pecking at the ground in search of a tasty meal. They eat ticks, grasshoppers, beetles and many other insects that can bother people and animals. In fact, guinea fowl are so good at eating ticks that some farmers keep them just to help control ticks on their land.

Unlike chickens, guinea fowl do not scratch up the garden very much, and they do not eat many plants. They prefer to hunt for insects in the grass, which makes them very welcome in gardens and farms. They also eat seeds and small grains, and they enjoy a treat of leafy greens now and then.

Guinea fowl are social birds, and they love to be in a flock. They move around together, calling to each other as they go, and they are happiest when they have plenty of friends around them. A single guinea fowl on its own can become lonely and sad, so farmers usually keep a whole flock together.

## Eggs and Babies

Female guinea fowl lay eggs in the warm months of the year. They often lay their eggs in a hidden nest in the grass, where the eggs are hard to find. The eggs are smaller than chicken eggs, with very hard shells and a rich, tasty yolk. Farmers collect the eggs to eat, or they let the mother sit on them until they hatch.

Baby guinea fowl are called keets, and they are tiny, fluffy little birds with striped feathers. Keets are very delicate when they are young, and they need to be kept warm and dry. They grow quickly, and within a few weeks they are running around the farmyard with their mothers, learning how to find insects and stay safe.

Guinea fowl mothers are very protective, and they call their keets with soft, gentle sounds. The keets follow their mother everywhere, peeping loudly if they get lost. It is a sweet sight to see a mother guinea fowl leading a long line of little keets across the grass.

## Why Farmers Love Guinea Fowl

Guinea fowl are hardy birds that do not need much looking after. They can find much of their own food, they are good at avoiding danger, and they do not get sick as easily as some other farm birds. They are also very entertaining to watch, with their funny calls, their spotty feathers and their brave little attitudes.

Some people keep guinea fowl as pets, and they make wonderful companions for people who have a big garden. They are not cuddly pets like cats or dogs, but they are full of character, and they reward their owners with fresh eggs, fewer insects and lots of laughter.

## A Spotty Little Hero

The guinea fowl shows us that the loudest animal is not always the most annoying, and that a small bird can be a big help on the farm. With its spotty feathers, its loud alarm calls and its love of eating ticks, the guinea fowl is a true friend to farmers and a guardian of the farmyard.

Next time you visit a farm, listen for the loud, rattling call of the guinea fowl. It is the sound of a brave little bird doing its job, watching over the yard and keeping the insects away, one tick at a time.
