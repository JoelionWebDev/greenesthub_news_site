On the island of Tasmania, off the southern coast of Australia, lives a small black animal with a very big voice. It is called the Tasmanian devil, and it is one of the noisiest animals in the whole animal kingdom. When it is excited, angry or fighting over food, it lets out loud screeches, growls and coughs that can be heard from far away in the dark forest. On the island of Tasmania lives a small black animal with a very loud voice, powerful jaws, and a job that keeps the forests clean and healthy.

## A Small Animal with a Big Attitude

The Tasmanian devil is about the size of a small dog, with a stocky body, short legs and a thick, bushy tail. Its fur is mostly black, with white patches on its chest and rump, and each devil has its own pattern of white marks, like a fingerprint. It has a big head, a short nose and ears that turn pink when it is excited.

Long ago, the first European settlers in Tasmania heard the devil's loud, scary screams in the night and thought the forest was haunted. That is how the animal got its name. But the Tasmanian devil is not really a devil at all. It is a shy animal that usually runs away from people, and it only makes those loud noises when it is fighting over food or feeling threatened.

Tasmanian devils are marsupials, which means the mothers carry their babies in a pouch, just like kangaroos and koalas. They are the largest meat-eating marsupials left in the world, which makes them very special.

## The Strongest Bite for Its Size

Tasmanian devils have one of the strongest bites of any animal for their size. Their jaws are so powerful that they can crunch through bones, which helps them eat every part of a dead animal, right down to the bones and fur. This is a very useful skill, because devils are scavengers, which means they eat animals that are already dead.

When a devil finds a dead animal, it eats quickly and noisily, growling and screeching to warn other devils to stay away. Sometimes several devils gather around one meal, and they argue loudly over who gets the best pieces. The noise can be so loud that people living nearby can hear the dinner party from inside their houses.

Devils also hunt live animals, such as small birds, frogs and insects, and they are good at catching animals that are sick or slow. Their strong sense of smell helps them find food in the dark, and their whiskers help them feel their way around at night.

## Babies in the Pouch

A mother Tasmanian devil can have many babies, called joeys, at once, but she only has four teats in her pouch, so only four joeys can survive. The tiny joeys are born very small, about the size of a grain of rice, and they crawl into their mother's pouch, where they stay safe and warm while they drink milk and grow.

After a few months, the joeys are big enough to come out of the pouch, and they stay in a nest made of leaves and bark while their mother goes out to find food. The mother comes back to feed them and teach them how to find food for themselves. When the young devils are about a year old, they leave their mother and go off to find their own place in the forest.

Tasmanian devils are mostly active at night. During the day they sleep in burrows, hollow logs or thick bushes, and at night they come out to search for food. They can travel many miles in a single night, padding through the forest on their short legs, sniffing the air for the smell of a meal.

## The Cleaners of the Forest

Tasmanian devils do a very important job in the forest. By eating dead animals, they help keep the forest clean and stop sickness from spreading. Without devils, dead animals would lie on the forest floor for much longer, and germs would spread to other animals. The devils are like the forest's clean-up crew, and the whole forest depends on them.

Devils also help farmers, because they eat animals that can damage crops. They are part of the great chain of life in Tasmania, and when they are healthy, the forest is healthy too. That is why people in Tasmania are so proud of their devils, even though they are noisy and a little bit grumpy.

## A Fight to Save the Devil

Tasmanian devils are in trouble. A sickness called devil facial tumour disease has spread through the wild devils, and many of them have died. The sickness makes lumps grow on the devil's face, which makes it hard for them to eat. Scientists have been working very hard to find a way to stop the disease and save the devils.

To help, people have set up special safe places where healthy devils can live and have babies, away from the sickness. Some of these devils live on small islands off the coast of Tasmania, where there is no sickness at all. Others live in zoos around the world, where people can see them and learn about them. Scientists are also studying the devils to understand how some of them fight off the sickness on their own.

There is good news too. The number of healthy devils is growing in some places, and scientists have found that some devils are naturally able to resist the disease. People in Tasmania are working together to protect the devils, and there is hope that one day the wild devils will be strong and healthy again.

## A Little Fighter Worth Saving

The Tasmanian devil reminds us that even small, noisy animals can be very important. With its powerful jaws, its loud voice and its job as the forest's cleaner, the devil is a true hero of Tasmania. It may not be cute and cuddly, but it is brave, tough and full of character, and the forests of Tasmania would not be the same without it.

Next time you hear a loud screech in the night, do not be scared. It might just be a Tasmanian devil, the noisy little fighter of Tasmania, out in the forest doing its very important job.
