Imagine walking along a quiet beach at night, under a sky full of stars, listening to the soft sound of waves. Suddenly, you see a huge sea turtle crawling slowly up the sand. She has come ashore to lay her eggs, just as sea turtles have done for a very long time.

Sea turtles are ancient animals. They have swum in the oceans for a very long time, long before people lived on Earth. Today, many sea turtles need help, because their beaches and oceans face many dangers.

## A Turtle's Long Journey Home

Female sea turtles return to the same beach where they were born to lay their eggs. This is an amazing journey. A turtle may swim across a whole ocean, travelling for a long time, before she finds her way back to the beach of her birth. No one knows exactly how she finds her way, but scientists think she uses the Earth's magnetic field, the sun, and the smell of the sea to guide her.

When she reaches the beach at night, the mother turtle crawls up the sand and digs a hole with her back flippers. She lays her eggs in the hole, covers them with sand, and then crawls back to the sea. She does not stay to look after her eggs. The warm sand keeps the eggs safe and helps them grow.

The eggs rest in the sand for many weeks, warmed by the sun. Inside each egg, a tiny baby turtle is growing. When the time is right, the baby turtles hatch together and dig their way up through the sand. Then they must make a dash to the sea, and that dash is the most dangerous part of their lives.

## The Brave Baby Turtles

Baby sea turtles are tiny, about the size of a child's hand, with soft shells and flippers that are made for swimming. When they hatch at night, they crawl towards the sea, guided by the light of the moon on the water. It is a race against time, because many dangers wait on the beach. Birds, crabs, and other animals may try to catch them. Bright lights from buildings can confuse them, and they may crawl the wrong way.

Only a small number of baby turtles survive to become grown-ups. That is why every baby turtle that reaches the sea matters. Once they are in the water, they swim with all their might, using their flippers to push through the waves. They will spend their lives in the ocean, eating seaweed, jellyfish, and other sea creatures, and growing bigger and stronger each year.

Baby turtles face dangers in the sea too, from bigger fish and from rubbish in the water. But the sea is their home, and they are made for it. Their shells protect them, their flippers help them swim fast, and their sharp eyes help them find food.

## How Turtle Patrols Help

Turtle patrols are groups of people who watch over beaches where turtles lay their eggs. They walk the beach at night, looking for turtle tracks in the sand. A turtle's tracks look like two lines made by her flippers, with a smooth path in the middle where her body dragged along. When patrols find fresh tracks, they follow them to find the nest.

Once they find a nest, patrols mark it gently, so that no one steps on it by accident. Sometimes, they move the eggs to a safer place, if the nest is too close to the water or in danger from people. They do this very carefully, wearing gloves and keeping the eggs in the same position, so the baby turtles inside stay safe. Moving a nest is a job for trained people, and it is done with great care.

Patrols also help baby turtles on the night they hatch. They watch the nest and guide the babies towards the sea, making sure they do not get lost or stuck. They keep bright lights away from the beach, because lights can confuse the babies.

## Protecting the Ocean Home

Turtle patrols do more than watch the beaches. They also help to protect the ocean where turtles live. They pick up litter on the beach, because plastic bags and other rubbish can harm turtles. A turtle may mistake a plastic bag for a jellyfish, which is one of its favourite foods, and eating plastic can make it very sick. Keeping beaches clean helps turtles stay healthy.

Patrols also teach people about turtles. They talk to visitors, children, and local families, and they explain why turtles need our help. They show people how to watch turtles without disturbing them, how to keep lights low at night, and how to keep beaches clean.

Some patrols also help sick or injured turtles. If a turtle is hurt, tangled in fishing nets, or washed up on a beach, patrol members call for help and take the turtle to a rescue centre. There, vets and carers look after the turtle until it is strong enough to return to the sea.

## How You Can Help Sea Turtles

You can help sea turtles too, even if you live far from the sea. The most important thing is to keep rubbish out of the ocean. Always put your litter in a bin, and never leave plastic bags or bottles on a beach. If you see litter on a beach, pick it up carefully and put it in a bin, with a grown-up's help. Every piece of litter you remove could save a turtle's life.

You can also learn about sea turtles and share what you learn with your friends and family. Tell them how mother turtles return to the same beach, how baby turtles race to the sea, and how patrols help them. The more people know about turtles, the more people will want to protect them.

If you ever visit a beach where turtles nest, remember to be quiet and gentle. Stay away from marked nests, keep lights low at night, and never touch a turtle or its eggs. Watch from a safe distance, and let the patrols do their important work.

Sea turtles have swum in our oceans for a very long time, and with the help of turtle patrols and caring people like you, they can keep swimming for many years to come. The next time you hear the sound of waves, think of the tiny baby turtles, racing towards the sea under the moonlight, and the kind people who walk beside them, keeping them safe.
