If you ever feel like you are moving too slowly, meet the sloth. Sloths are the slowest animals in the trees, and they are proud of it! They hang upside down from branches, move at a gentle pace, and sleep for a very long time each day. Their slow, sleepy life is not lazy at all. It is a clever way to stay safe in the rainforest. Let us meet these smiley, sleepy animals.

## What Is a Sloth?

A sloth is a mammal that lives in trees in Central and South America. It has long, curved claws that work like hooks, helping it to hang from branches. Sloths spend most of their time hanging upside down, and they are so good at it that they can even sleep that way. Their fur is shaggy and brownish, and it often has a green tint because tiny green plants called algae grow in it. The algae helps the sloth to hide among the green leaves.

Sloths have a round head, a flat face, and a gentle smile that makes them look friendly. They have small ears and dark eyes. Some sloths have two toes on their front feet, and some have three toes. That is how scientists tell them apart. Sloths move slowly because their bodies use very little energy. They have a slow tummy, too, and it takes them a long time to digest their food. Moving slowly helps them to save energy and to stay hidden from animals that might want to eat them.

## Where Do Sloths Live?

Sloths live in the warm rainforests of Central and South America. They spend almost all of their lives high up in the trees, in the canopy. They rarely come down to the ground, and when they do, they move very slowly and awkwardly. On the ground, they have to drag themselves along, because their legs are made for hanging, not for walking.

The rainforest canopy is a perfect home for a sloth. It is warm, green, and full of leaves to eat. Sloths move from tree to tree by reaching out with their long arms and pulling themselves along branches. They are strong, even though they are slow. Their claws grip the branches tightly, so they do not fall. Sloths also like to live near rivers and streams, where the trees are tall and the leaves are fresh. At night, they curl up in the branches and sleep, safe and hidden among the leaves.

## What Do Sloths Eat?

Sloths are herbivores, which means they eat plants. Their favourite food is leaves, and they eat leaves from many different trees. They also eat buds, flowers, and sometimes fruit. Sloths are not fussy eaters, but they are very slow eaters. They chew their food slowly and carefully, and it can take a long time for a meal to travel through their tummy.

Because leaves do not give a lot of energy, sloths need to save energy in other ways. That is why they move slowly and sleep a lot. Sleeping helps them to rest and to digest their food. Sloths also drink water from rain that collects on leaves and in tree hollows. They do not need to drink very often, because they get some water from the leaves they eat. A sloth's slow life is perfectly suited to its leafy diet, and it has lived this way for a very long time.

## Baby Sloths

Baby sloths are called pups, and they are very cute. When a baby sloth is born, it clings tightly to its mother's fur, holding on as she moves slowly through the trees. The mother carries her baby everywhere, keeping it safe and warm. Baby sloths drink their mother's milk, and as they grow, they start to nibble on leaves too.

The mother teaches her baby which leaves are safe to eat and how to hang from branches. Baby sloths learn by watching their mother and copying what she does. They practise hanging, climbing, and moving slowly. Play is important for baby sloths, too. They wriggle, stretch, and explore the branches near their mother. Baby sloths stay with their mothers for a long time, learning all the skills they need to live on their own in the trees. When they are old enough, they leave and find their own tree to call home.

## Fun Facts About Sloths

Sloths have many fun secrets. One of the best is that they are very good swimmers! Even though they move slowly on land, they can swim well in rivers. They use their long arms to paddle through the water, and they can hold their breath for a long time. Swimming helps them to cross rivers and find new trees.

Another fun fact is that sloths are so slow that algae grows in their fur, turning it green. This green tint helps them to blend in with the leaves, like a natural camouflage. Moths and other tiny insects also live in a sloth's fur, and they help to feed the algae. Sloths also have extra bones in their neck, which lets them turn their head in a wide circle to look around without moving their body. That is very handy when you are hanging upside down and want to see what is happening around you.

## How We Can Help Sloths

Sloths need our help, because their rainforest homes are being cut down. When trees are cleared for farms and towns, sloths lose their homes and their food. They also face danger from power lines and roads, because they move so slowly that they cannot get out of the way quickly. Some sloths are also taken from the wild to be kept as pets, which is not kind and is not good for them.

Many people are working to protect sloths. Rescue centres care for injured and orphaned sloths and help them to return to the wild. Conservationists protect rainforest areas and plant new trees. They also build rope bridges across roads, so sloths can cross safely from one tree to another. You can help sloths by learning about them and by supporting wildlife charities that protect rainforests. You can also remember that wild animals belong in the wild, not as pets. When we protect the rainforest, we protect sloths and all the other animals that live there.
