Gobble, gobble! Can you hear that funny sound coming from the farmyard? That is a turkey! Turkeys are big, proud birds with colourful feathers, long necks, and a very loud voice. They may look a little bit silly, but they are clever, curious, and full of surprises. Let us step into the farmyard and meet them.

## Where Do Turkeys Come From?

Turkeys come from North America, where wild turkeys have lived in forests and open fields for a very long time. Long ago, the native peoples of Mexico began to keep turkeys as farm birds, and later, people in Europe and all over the world started keeping them too. Today, you can find turkeys on farms in many countries, and wild turkeys still roam the forests of the United States, Canada, and Mexico.

Wild turkeys are strong flyers for short distances, and they can run fast too. They spend their days scratching the ground for food and their nights sleeping safely up in trees.

## What Does a Turkey Look Like?

A turkey is a big bird. A male turkey, called a tom, can be as tall as a small child, and he is much heavier than a chicken. Turkeys have strong legs, wide wings, and a long neck with a small head. Their feathers are usually brown, black, and grey, with a shiny, bronze glow in the sunlight. Male turkeys have very showy feathers, and when they want to impress, they spread their tails into a huge, beautiful fan.

The turkey's face is one of the funniest faces in the animal world. It has a long, wobbly piece of skin hanging from its beak called a snood, and a red, wrinkly piece of skin under its chin called a wattle. The skin on a turkey's head and neck can change colour! It can turn red, white, or blue depending on how the turkey is feeling.

## The Famous Gobble

The most famous thing about a turkey is its gobble. Only male turkeys gobble, and they do it to show off and to call to the females. A gobble is a loud, rattling sound that starts low and ends with a funny gurgle. It can be heard from far away across the farmyard.

Female turkeys, called hens, do not gobble. Instead, they make softer sounds like clucks, purrs, and yelps. Turkeys use many different sounds to talk to each other. They cluck when they are happy, they purr when they are calm, and they make a sharp alarm call when they spot danger.

## What Do Turkeys Eat?

Turkeys are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and small animals. Their favourite foods are seeds, grains, nuts, berries, leaves, and insects. Wild turkeys spend much of their day scratching the ground with their strong feet, looking for acorns, seeds, and tasty bugs. They also eat grass, flowers, and fruit that has fallen from trees.

On a farm, turkeys eat grain and special feed that helps them grow strong and healthy. Farmers also give them fresh water and sometimes let them roam free in fields, where they can peck at grass and hunt for insects.

## The Turkey's Big Fan Dance

One of the most amazing things a male turkey does is his big fan dance. When a tom wants to impress a female turkey, he puffs up his feathers, spreads his tail into a wide fan, drags his wings on the ground, and struts around in a slow, proud circle. He gobbles loudly and turns around so the hen can see his beautiful feathers from every side.

This dance is called strutting, and it is a sight to see. The tom's feathers shine in the sun, his head turns bright blue and red, and he looks like the king of the farmyard. The hens watch carefully, and they choose the tom with the best feathers and the best dance.

## Baby Turkeys Are Called Poults

A baby turkey is called a poult. Poults are small, soft, and fluffy, with speckled brown feathers that help them hide in the grass. A mother turkey, called a hen, lays her eggs in a nest on the ground, hidden among grass and leaves. She sits on the eggs to keep them warm until they hatch.

When the poults hatch, they can walk and peck for food almost right away, but they still need their mother to keep them warm and safe. The hen talks to her poults with soft clucks, and the little birds follow her everywhere. At night, the poults snuggle under their mother's wings to stay warm.

## Turkeys and People

People have kept turkeys on farms for hundreds of years. Turkeys give us eggs and meat, and their feathers have been used to make decorations and warm bedding. But turkeys are more than just farm animals. They are curious, social birds that like to be with other turkeys, and they can even learn to recognise the people who feed them.

Turkeys are famous around the world because of Thanksgiving, a holiday in the United States and Canada when families gather for a big meal, and a roast turkey is often the star of the table.

## Fun Facts About Turkeys

Here are some fun facts about turkeys. A turkey can run fast, and a wild turkey can fly short distances at surprising speed. Turkeys have excellent eyesight and hearing, and they can spot danger from far away. A turkey's wattle and snood can change colour with its mood.

## Why We Love Turkeys

Turkeys remind us that farm animals can be funny, proud, and full of character. They gobble, they strut, they fan their tails, and they make the farmyard a louder, livelier place.

The next time you hear a turkey gobble, stop and listen. That loud, rattling sound is a turkey saying hello to the world — and maybe, just maybe, it is saying hello to you.
