High in the rocky mountains of Asia lives one of the world's most amazing climbers: the markhor. This wild goat has long, spiral horns that twist like a corkscrew, a shaggy beard, and feet that can grip steep cliffs as if they were flat ground. Watching a markhor climb is like watching a mountain champion at work.

Markhors live in some of the steepest, rockiest places on Earth. They leap from rock to rock, balance on narrow ledges, and climb slopes that would make most animals dizzy. Let us head up into the mountains and meet this wonderful wild goat.

## What Do They Look Like?

The markhor is a large wild goat with a thick, shaggy coat. In the summer, its coat is a light brownish-grey colour, and in the winter it grows longer and thicker to keep the animal warm in the cold mountain air. Both male and female markhors have a beard under their chin, but the male's beard is longer and more impressive.

The most famous thing about the markhor is its horns. The horns are long and twist in a spiral, a bit like a corkscrew or a twisted rope. Male markhors have especially big horns, which can grow very long and twist in a grand spiral. Female markhors have smaller, shorter horns. The horns are used for display, and males sometimes use them when they are showing off to each other.

Markhors have strong, sturdy legs and hooves with a hard outer edge and a soft, grippy centre. This helps them to hold on to rocky surfaces and not slip. Their eyes are set on the sides of their head, which gives them a wide view of the mountains around them, so they can spot danger early.

## Where Do They Live?

Markhors live in the mountains of Central and South Asia, in countries like Pakistan, Afghanistan, India, and nearby lands. They make their homes on steep, rocky mountain slopes, where there are cliffs, ledges, and scrubby bushes to hide among.

They like places that are high up and hard to reach. This keeps them safe from many animals that might want to catch them, because few hunters can follow a markhor up a near-vertical cliff! In the summer, they move higher up the mountains to find fresh grass and leaves. In the winter, when snow covers the high peaks, they come down to lower slopes where they can still find food.

Markhors are very good at living in rocky places. They can leap from one narrow ledge to another, climb up steep rock faces, and balance on the tiniest of footholds. Their strong legs and clever hooves make them some of the best climbers in the animal kingdom.

## What Do They Eat?

Markhors are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. In the mountains, they munch on grasses, leaves, and the shoots of shrubs and small trees. They are very fond of the leaves of oak trees and other mountain plants, and they will stretch up on their back legs to reach tasty leaves on low branches.

In the winter, when fresh green plants are hard to find, markhors eat dry grass, twigs, and bark. They are not fussy eaters, and they make the most of whatever the mountains have to offer. They spend much of the day grazing and browsing, moving slowly from one patch of food to the next.

Like many goats, markhors are good at finding food in places that look bare and empty to us. A rocky slope that seems to have nothing on it may be full of tasty little plants to a hungry markhor. They use their lips and tongues to pick out the best bits, and their strong teeth help them chew tough mountain plants.

## Family Life on the Cliffs

Markhors live in small groups. The females, called does, usually stay together with their young ones in groups, while the adult males, called bucks, often live alone or in small groups of their own. The groups come together at certain times of the year, when the males join the females.

A baby markhor is called a kid. When a kid is born, it is small and wobbly at first, but it soon learns to stand and walk. It does not take long before the young markhor is practising its climbing skills, following its mother up and down the rocks. Kids love to play, leaping about on the ledges and chasing each other around the cliffs.

Mothers look after their kids very carefully, keeping them close and showing them which plants are good to eat. The kids stay with their mothers for a long time, learning the ways of the mountains before they are ready to live on their own.


## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about markhors:

- The markhor is the national animal of Pakistan.
- Its name is thought to come from words meaning snake eater, because of an old story that markhors eat snakes — but they really eat only plants!
- Male markhors have huge spiral horns that can grow very long.
- Markhors can climb steep cliffs and leap between narrow rock ledges with ease.
- Their hooves have a hard edge and a soft, grippy centre, perfect for climbing.
- In winter, their coat grows long and thick to keep them warm in the cold mountains.
- Markhors are most active in the early morning and late afternoon, resting during the middle of the day.
