Have you ever seen a fish that looks like a jewel swimming in water? With long, flowing fins in red, blue, purple, or green, it glides through the water like a tiny underwater dancer. Meet the betta fish — small in size, but big in colour and spirit.

## What Is a Betta Fish?

A betta fish is a small tropical fish that comes from Southeast Asia — from countries like Thailand, Cambodia, and Vietnam. In the wild, bettas live in shallow, warm waters like rice paddies, slow-moving streams, and ponds.

Bettas are also known as Siamese fighting fish. "Siam" was the old name for Thailand, where these fish come from. They got the name "fighting fish" because male bettas will fight each other if they are put in the same tank. That is why male bettas must always live alone.

A betta is a small fish, about as long as your thumb when it is fully grown. But what it lacks in size, it makes up for in colour. Wild bettas are usually a dull green or brown colour, which helps them hide from bigger fish. But pet bettas have been bred by people for hundreds of years to have amazing colours — bright red, royal blue, deep purple, sunny yellow, and even rainbow patterns. Some bettas have fins so long and flowing that they look like silk scarves trailing in the water.

## The Betta's Special Trick

Bettas have a special trick that most other fish do not have. They can breathe air from the surface of the water!

Most fish can only breathe by taking oxygen from the water through their gills. Bettas can do that too, but they also have a special organ called a labyrinth organ. This organ lets them take in air from above the water, a bit like taking a breath.

This trick helps bettas survive in their home waters. Rice paddies and ponds can get very warm, and warm water does not hold much oxygen. When the water has little oxygen, a betta simply swims to the surface and takes a gulp of air. That is why you will often see a betta swimming up to the top of its tank for a breath.

## Why Do Bettas Flare Their Fins?

One of the most exciting things about a betta is when it flares its fins. Flaring is when a betta spreads its fins wide and opens its gill covers, making itself look much bigger and more colourful. It is a bit like a peacock spreading its tail feathers.

Bettas flare for a few reasons. A male betta flares when it sees another male betta, to say "this is my place — stay away!" It is a warning. In the wild, two males will flare at each other, and usually one will swim away before a fight starts.

A betta may also flare when it sees its own reflection in the glass, because it thinks another betta is looking back at it. And sometimes a betta flares when it is excited or showing off.

## Bettas Build Bubble Nests

Here is another wonderful thing about bettas: male bettas build nests out of bubbles!

When a male betta is ready to have babies, he blows tiny bubbles at the surface of the water. The bubbles stick together with a bit of sticky saliva, forming a floating raft of foam. This is called a bubble nest.

The nest is a safe place for the betta's eggs. After the female lays her eggs, the male carefully picks them up in his mouth and places them one by one into the bubble nest. Then he guards the nest day and night, fixing any bubbles that pop and chasing away anything that comes near.

## Caring for a Betta Fish

Bettas are popular pets because they are beautiful and do not need a huge tank. But that does not mean they are easy pets. A betta needs proper care to be happy and healthy.

First, a betta needs a tank that is big enough. Even though you often see bettas in tiny bowls or cups in pet shops, that is not a good home for them. A betta needs a tank with enough space to swim around and clean, warm water. Bettas are tropical fish, so their water should be warm, like a warm bath.

The water in a betta's tank must be kept clean. A gentle filter helps, and the water should be changed regularly. Dirty water can make a betta sick. The tank also needs plants or decorations where the betta can hide and rest. Bettas like to rest on leaves near the surface, and they enjoy exploring their tank.

Bettas eat small amounts of food, once or twice a day. They like special betta pellets, and they also enjoy treats like tiny worms. But be careful not to give them too much food. A betta's stomach is about the size of its eye, so a little food goes a long way. Food that is left uneaten will sink and dirty the water.

One of the most important rules of keeping bettas is this: never put two male bettas in the same tank. They will fight, and they can hurt each other badly. Each male betta needs its own home.

## Fun Facts

- Bettas can breathe air from the surface of the water using a special organ called a labyrinth organ.
- Male bettas build nests out of bubbles to keep their eggs safe.
- A male betta will flare its fins to look bigger and scare away rivals.
- Bettas can recognise the person who feeds them and may swim over to say hello.
- Wild bettas are dull green or brown, but pet bettas can be red, blue, purple, yellow, or even multicoloured.

## Why We Love Bettas

Bettas are small, but they have huge personalities. They are colourful, clever, and full of spirit. Watching a betta glide through the water with its fins flowing behind it is like watching a tiny piece of art come alive.

If you ever keep a betta, remember that it is a living creature that depends on you. Give it a good home, clean warm water, the right amount of food, and a little attention every day. In return, your little fighter will brighten your room with its colours and its character for years to come.
