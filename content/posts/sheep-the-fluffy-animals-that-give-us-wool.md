## What is a sheep?

A sheep is a gentle farm animal covered in a thick, soft coat called wool. Sheep have four legs, a short tail and a friendly face. Most sheep are white, but some are black, brown or grey, and some have spots. Their wool grows all over their bodies like a warm, fluffy blanket.

A female sheep is called a ewe, a male sheep is called a ram, and a baby sheep is called a lamb. Lambs are some of the sweetest baby animals on the farm. They have tiny legs, soft wool and a little voice that sounds like "baa".

Rams often have big curved horns that curl around the sides of their heads. Ewes usually have smaller horns or no horns at all. Sheep have rectangular pupils, which are the dark parts in the middle of their eyes. This funny shape helps them see all around them, so they can spot danger coming from the sides.

## Where do sheep live?

Sheep live on farms all over the world. They are happiest in open fields with lots of grass to eat and space to walk around. In some places, sheep live on green hills and in valleys. In other places, they live on dry, rocky land where only tough little plants grow. Sheep are strong animals, and they can live in many kinds of weather.

On a farm, sheep sleep in a barn or a sheltered pen at night. The shelter keeps them dry when it rains and safe from wild animals. During the day, they go out to the fields to graze.

Some sheep live in very cold places, high up in the mountains. Their thick wool keeps them warm, even when snow covers the ground. Other sheep live in hot, dry places, where their wool protects them from the hot sun too.

## What do sheep eat?

Sheep are herbivores, which means they eat plants. Their favourite food is grass. They spend most of the day grazing, which means walking slowly across a field and nibbling grass. They also eat leaves, clover, hay and grains. Hay is dried grass that farmers store to feed animals when fresh grass is hard to find.

Sheep have a special way of eating. They chew their food, swallow it, and then bring it back up to chew it again. This is called chewing the cud, and it helps them get all the goodness out of tough grass and leaves.

Sheep need clean water every day. They also need salt, which farmers sometimes give them in a hard block called a salt lick.

## Sheep families

Sheep are very social animals. They like to stay close to their friends and family in the flock. Sheep can remember the faces of other sheep, and they can even remember the faces of people who are kind to them.

A ewe usually gives birth to one or two lambs at a time. When a lamb is born, the mother licks it clean and stays close to it. The lamb soon learns its mother's voice and smell, and it follows her everywhere.

Lambs drink their mother's milk when they are very young. As they grow, they start to nibble grass, just like the grown-up sheep.

Sheep talk to each other with sounds. The most famous sheep sound is "baa". A ewe baas to call her lamb, and the lamb baas back. Each sheep's baa sounds a little different, so mothers and lambs can find each other in a big flock just by listening.

## Wool: the sheep's special gift

The most famous thing about sheep is their wool. Wool is the soft, thick coat that grows on a sheep's body. It keeps the sheep warm in winter and cool in summer. People have used wool for thousands of years to make clothes, blankets, carpets and many other useful things.

Once a year, usually in spring, a farmer shears the sheep. Shearing means carefully cutting off the wool with special clippers, a bit like getting a haircut. It does not hurt the sheep, and it helps the sheep stay cool in the warm weather. The wool grows back again, ready for the next year.

After shearing, the wool is washed, cleaned and spun into yarn. Yarn is a long thread that can be knitted or woven into jumpers, scarves, hats and blankets. Wool clothes are warm, soft and cosy, which is why so many people love wearing them in cold weather.

Some sheep have very fine, soft wool, and some have thicker, rougher wool. The finest wool comes from a breed of sheep called the Merino. Merino wool is so soft that it is used to make clothes that feel gentle against the skin.

## Sheep and people

People and sheep have been friends for a very long time. Long ago, people kept sheep for their wool, their milk and their meat. Sheep milk can be used to make cheese and yoghurt.

Sheep also help farmers in another way. As they graze, they keep the grass short and tidy. Their droppings help the soil stay healthy, which helps new plants grow. In some places, sheep are even used to keep grass short in parks and gardens.

Sheepdogs are clever dogs that help farmers look after sheep. A sheepdog runs around the flock, guiding the sheep in the right direction, while the farmer gives the dog signals, sometimes with whistles.

Sheep appear in many stories, songs and nursery rhymes. You may know the rhyme "Baa, Baa, Black Sheep" or the story of a little lamb that followed a girl to school.

## Fun facts about sheep

- Sheep have very good memories and can remember other sheep and people for a long time.
- A sheep's wool never stops growing, which is why sheep need to be sheared every year.
- Sheep can see almost all the way around themselves without turning their heads.
- Lambs can stand up and walk within minutes of being born.
- Some sheep have wool that is black, brown or grey, not just white.
- Sheep are very good at finding their way home, even across large fields.
- A group of sheep is called a flock, and a group of lambs is sometimes called a fold.
- Sheep have a great sense of smell and can tell different plants apart by sniffing them.
- Counting sheep is a famous trick people use to help them fall asleep.
- The softest wool in the world comes from Merino sheep.
