Imagine an animal with the body of a deer, the stripes of a zebra on its legs, and a head that looks like a giraffe. It sounds like something from a storybook, but it is real. It is called the okapi, and it lives deep in the rainforests of the Congo in central Africa. For a long time, people outside Africa did not believe the okapi was real at all. Even scientists were surprised when they finally saw one. That is why the okapi is sometimes called the forest giraffe — and it is one of the shyest animals on Earth.

## A Cousin of the Giraffe

The okapi is the closest living relative of the giraffe. Look closely and you can see the family likeness. Okapis have long necks, big ears, and long dark tongues that can reach up into the trees. Male okapis even have short little horns on their heads, just like giraffes do. But okapis are much smaller than giraffes, and they live in a very different place.

A giraffe lives out on the open savanna, where everyone can see it. An okapi lives in the thick, dark rainforest, where the trees grow so close together that sunlight hardly reaches the ground. Its chestnut-brown coat helps it blend into the shadows of the forest. Its striped legs look like sunlight shining through leaves, which helps it hide from animals that might want to hunt it. When an okapi stands still in the forest, it can be very hard to spot.

## A Very Shy Animal

Okapis are shy and quiet animals. They live mostly alone, wandering through the forest on their own. They do not make loud noises. They move softly and carefully between the trees, and they are very good at staying out of sight. This is one of the reasons they stayed hidden from the rest of the world for so long.

People who live near the forests of the Congo have known about okapis for a very long time. They gave the animal its name and told stories about it. But scientists from other parts of the world only learned about the okapi in the early 1900s, when they finally saw one for themselves. Before that, many people thought the stories were just tales. The okapi reminds us that the world still holds wonderful surprises.

## What Do Okapis Eat?

Okapis are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They munch on leaves, buds, fruits and fungi that grow in the forest. Their favourite foods are the young leaves of trees and shrubs. With their long tongues, they can reach leaves that other animals cannot get to. Their tongues are so long that an okapi can lick its own ears and even its own eyes! Imagine being able to do that.

Okapis also eat clay from the ground sometimes. This might sound strange, but the clay gives them minerals that they need to stay healthy. It is a clever trick that many forest animals use.

Because okapis live alone and eat quietly, they do not fight over food. Each okapi has its own area of forest where it wanders and eats. They mark their area by rubbing their necks on trees and leaving their scent behind, so other okapis know someone lives there.

## A Mother and Her Baby

Baby okapis are called calves. When a mother okapi is ready to give birth, she finds a quiet, safe spot deep in the forest. The calf is born with a soft brown coat, and for the first few days of its life, it stays hidden in a nest of leaves on the forest floor while its mother goes off to eat.

This might sound worrying, but it is a clever plan. A baby okapi that stays still and quiet is much harder for hunters to find than one that follows its mother around. The mother comes back often to feed her calf and check that it is safe. After a while, the calf is strong enough to follow her through the forest.

Young okapis grow up slowly. They stay with their mothers for many months, learning which leaves are good to eat and how to move quietly through the trees. When they are old enough, they wander off to find their own part of the forest.

## The Forest Home of the Okapi

Okapis only live in one place in the whole world: the Ituri rainforest in the Democratic Republic of Congo. They do not live anywhere else. This makes them very special, but it also means they need their forest home to stay safe.

The rainforest is a busy place, full of trees, plants and other animals. Okapis share their home with forest elephants, monkeys, birds and many other creatures. They help the forest by spreading seeds. When an okapi eats fruit and later drops the seeds in another part of the forest, new plants can grow there. In this way, okapis are gardeners of the forest, helping it stay healthy.

## Protecting the Forest Giraffe

Okapis need our help. Their forest home is shrinking as trees are cut down, and some people hunt them. Because okapis are shy and hard to find, it is difficult to know exactly how many are left. But scientists believe their numbers are falling, and that is worrying.

There are special protected areas in the Congo where okapis are safe. Rangers work hard to guard the forest and stop hunting. Some okapis also live in zoos around the world, where people can see them and learn about them. Zoos help scientists learn more about how okapis live and how to protect them.

You can help too, just by learning about okapis and telling others about them. The more people who know about this shy, striped, long-tongued forest animal, the more people will want to protect it.

## Why We Love Okapis

Okapis are a wonderful reminder that nature is full of surprises. An animal that looks like three animals mixed together, that hid from scientists for years, that can lick its own ears and lives only in one forest in the whole world — that is truly special. At Greennest Hub, we love the okapi because it shows us that even in a world full of cameras and explorers, there are still shy, quiet creatures living their lives in the deep green forest, waiting to be discovered and protected.
