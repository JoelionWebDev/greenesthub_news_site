Have you ever wondered who looks after the animals in a zoo? The answer is zookeepers! Zookeepers are the people who feed, clean, and care for zoo animals every day. They work with lions, elephants, monkeys, penguins, and many other animals. They are kind, hardworking, and love animals very much. Let us find out what a zookeeper does.

## What Is a Zookeeper?

A zookeeper is a person who looks after animals in a zoo, wildlife park, or aquarium. Zookeepers learn about animals, their food, their homes, and their behaviour. Behaviour is the way an animal acts. Zookeepers study hard to learn how to care for different animals, because each animal has its own needs. A lion needs different food and care from a penguin, and a monkey needs different care from an elephant.

Zookeepers are not just feeders. They are also carers, cleaners, trainers, and friends to the animals. They watch the animals carefully every day, looking for signs that they are happy and healthy. If an animal seems unwell, the zookeeper will tell the vet. Zookeepers also help to keep records about each animal, such as what it eats, how much it weighs, and how it behaves. This helps the zoo to give the best care possible to every animal.

## Where Do Zookeepers Work?

Zookeepers work in zoos, wildlife parks, safari parks, and aquariums all around the world. A zoo is a place where animals live in enclosures, which are safe, fenced areas that look like their natural homes. An aquarium is a place where water animals, such as fish, sharks, and penguins, live in large tanks. Zookeepers work outdoors in all kinds of weather, in rain, sun, and cold, because animals need care every day.

A zookeeper's workplace is busy and varied. They might work in the big cat house, the elephant house, the reptile house, or the bird aviary. Some zookeepers work with just one kind of animal, such as primates, which are monkeys and apes, while others work with many different animals. Zookeepers also work with visitors, answering questions and teaching people about animals. They help visitors to learn why animals are special and how we can protect them.

## What Does a Zookeeper Do Every Day?

A zookeeper's day starts early. In the morning, zookeepers check on all the animals, making sure they are safe and well. They clean the enclosures, removing old food and waste, and putting in fresh bedding, such as straw or leaves. They also prepare food for the animals, chopping fruit, vegetables, and meat, and measuring out the right amounts for each animal.

During the day, zookeepers feed the animals, give them fresh water, and provide enrichment. Enrichment is a fun activity or toy that helps animals to stay active and happy. For example, a zookeeper might hide food in a puzzle feeder for a monkey, or give a ball to an elephant to play with. Zookeepers also train animals to do simple things, such as coming to a certain spot for a health check. This training is done gently, with treats and praise, and it helps the vet to examine the animal safely.

## How Zookeepers Care for Baby Animals

Zookeepers care for baby animals with extra love and attention. When a baby animal is born in a zoo, zookeepers watch it closely to make sure it is healthy and that its mother is looking after it well. They check that the baby is feeding, growing, and moving properly. If a baby animal needs extra help, zookeepers may feed it with a bottle, keep it warm, and care for it around the clock.

Zookeepers also help to prepare baby animals for life in the zoo. They teach them where to find food and water, and they help them to get used to people and to their enclosure. As baby animals grow, zookeepers watch them play, learn, and explore. Watching a baby animal grow up is one of the happiest parts of a zookeeper's job. Zookeepers also help with breeding programmes, which are plans to help rare animals to have babies, so that their species can survive.

## Fun Facts About Zookeepers

Being a zookeeper is a wonderful job, and it has many fun facts. Zookeepers get to know each animal as an individual, with its own personality, likes, and dislikes. They know which foods each animal loves, which toys it likes to play with, and which spots it likes to sleep in. Zookeepers form strong bonds with the animals they care for, and the animals often recognise them and greet them.

Another fun fact is that zookeepers need to be strong and fit, because their work is physical. They lift heavy buckets of food, push wheelbarrows, and clean large enclosures. They also need to be patient and calm, because animals can sense when a person is nervous. Zookeepers wear special uniforms and boots, and they use tools such as brooms, hoses, and buckets. They are always learning, and they share what they learn with visitors, so that everyone can understand animals better.

## How Zookeepers Help Animals and People

Zookeepers help animals by giving them good food, clean homes, medical care, and enrichment. They help animals to stay healthy and happy, even though they live in a zoo and not in the wild. Zookeepers also help to protect endangered animals, which are animals that are rare and in danger of disappearing. By caring for these animals and helping them to have babies, zoos help to save species for the future.

Zookeepers also help people by teaching them about animals. When you visit a zoo, a zookeeper can tell you amazing facts about the animals, why they are special, and how we can protect them in the wild. This learning helps people to care about animals and nature. If you love animals and want to work with them when you grow up, you could become a zookeeper. It takes hard work, learning, and a big love for animals, but it is a very rewarding job.
