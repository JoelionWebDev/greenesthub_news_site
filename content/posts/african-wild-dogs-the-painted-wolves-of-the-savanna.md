Imagine a dog wearing a coat that looks like it was painted by an artist. Splashes of black, brown, white and gold cover its fur in a pattern that is different for every single dog. That is the African wild dog, also called the painted wolf. These beautiful animals live on the grassy plains of Africa, and they are some of the cleverest and kindest team players in the whole animal kingdom.

## A Coat Like No Other

The first thing you notice about an African wild dog is its amazing coat. Each dog has a patchwork of colours — black, white, brown, tan and gold — splashed across its body in swirls and spots. No two wild dogs look exactly the same. It is a bit like a human fingerprint. Scientists and animal watchers can tell one dog from another just by looking at its coat.

Their coats are not just pretty. The patchy colours help the dogs blend in with the dappled light and shade of the African bush. African wild dogs are about the size of a large pet dog, but they are slimmer and lighter, with long legs built for running. Their ears are big and round, and they can move in different directions to catch the smallest sounds. These big ears also help the dogs stay cool in the hot African sun, because heat can escape from the thin skin of the ears.

## Life in a Pack

African wild dogs live together in groups called packs. A pack is like a big family, and it usually has several grown-up dogs and their pups. The dogs in a pack care for each other in ways that surprise many people. They are loyal, gentle with each other, and they share everything.

Wild dogs greet each other in a very sweet way. When pack members meet after being apart, they wag their tails, lick each other's faces, and make happy, twittering sounds. It is like a big family hug. They also sneeze to vote! When the pack is deciding whether to go hunting, the dogs sneeze, and if enough of them sneeze, the pack sets off together. Is that not the funniest way to make a decision?

## Hunting as a Team

African wild dogs are brilliant hunters, and their secret is teamwork. They do not hunt alone. Instead, the whole pack works together like a football team, with each dog knowing its job. They hunt mostly in the early morning and in the late afternoon, when the sun is not too hot.

When a pack hunts, the dogs chase their prey together, taking turns to run at the front. The dogs at the front do the hard running, and when they get tired, other dogs take their place. In this way, the pack can keep chasing for a very long time. They can run fast for a long distance, and they hardly ever give up.

Their prey is usually animals like antelopes, which are fast runners too. The wild dogs use clever plans. Some dogs chase from behind while others run around the sides to cut off the escape. When the pack catches food, they share it. The pups eat first, and dogs that are old, sick or hurt are given food too. Even dogs that stayed behind to look after the pups get a share when the hunters return. Some dogs even bring food back in their tummies for the pups and the babysitters.

## Pups: The Heart of the Pack

Baby African wild dogs are called pups, and they are some of the cutest babies in Africa. They are born in a den, which is a safe, hidden place, often an old burrow dug by another animal. A mother can have a large litter, with many pups at once. When they are born, the pups are dark and fluffy, and their eyes are closed. They stay in the den for the first few weeks of their lives.

The whole pack helps to raise the pups. While the mother stays with the babies, other dogs go hunting and bring food back for her. When the pups are old enough to come out of the den, the grown-up dogs play with them, teach them, and guard them. Pups love to play. They wrestle, chase each other, and practise the skills they will need when they grow up.

## Home on the Savanna

African wild dogs live in the savannas and open woodlands of Africa, south of the great Sahara desert. Their home is a land of wide grassy plains, scattered trees, and rivers. The savanna is home to many animals — zebras, giraffes, elephants, lions and antelopes all live there too.

## Fun Facts About African Wild Dogs

- Each wild dog's coat pattern is unique — no two dogs look the same!
- Wild dogs "vote" by sneezing before the pack decides to go hunting.
- A pack shares its food, and the pups always eat first.
- Wild dogs have only four toes on each front foot, while most dogs have five.
- Their big round ears help them hear very well and stay cool in the heat.
- A wild dog pack can run and chase its prey for a very long time without giving up.
- The white tip on a wild dog's tail helps pups follow their parents through tall grass.

## Why Painted Wolves Need Our Help

Sadly, African wild dogs are in danger. There are not many of them left in the wild, and their numbers have fallen over the years. Their homes are shrinking as land is used for farms and towns, and sometimes they get into trouble with people who keep farm animals. Roads can be dangerous for them too, and diseases spread by pet dogs can make them sick.

But there is hope. Many people in Africa and around the world are working hard to protect wild dogs. National parks and reserves keep their homes safe, and wildlife rangers watch over the packs. Scientists study the dogs to learn how to help them, and special programmes teach people how to live happily alongside wild animals.

You can help too. Learning about painted wolves and telling your friends and family about them is a wonderful start. The more people know about these colourful, caring animals, the more people will want to protect them. African wild dogs show us that teamwork, kindness and family matter — and that a painted coat can be the most beautiful coat of all.
