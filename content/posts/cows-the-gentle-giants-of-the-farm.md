Cows are the gentle giants of the farm. They are big, calm and kind, with soft eyes, big ears and a slow, peaceful way of moving. You will often see cows standing in green fields, munching grass in the sunshine. Cows give us fresh milk, which we drink and use to make cheese, butter and yoghurt.

## What is a cow?

A cow is a large farm animal with four legs, a big body and a long tail. A female cow is called a cow, a male cow is called a bull, and a baby cow is called a calf. Bulls are usually bigger and stronger than cows, and they often have thicker necks. Calves are sweet and curious, and they love to run and play in the field.

Cows come in many colours. Some are black and white, some are brown, some are white, and some are a soft golden colour. Some cows have spots, and some have patches of colour. Cows have big, soft eyes with long lashes, and large ears that they can move to shoo away flies.

## Munching grass all day

Cows spend much of their day eating grass. They use their long, rough tongues to pull grass into their mouths, and their strong teeth to tear it off. A cow's tongue is like a big, pink scoop. It wraps around the grass and pulls it in. Cows eat a lot of grass every day, because grass is their main food.

Cows are ruminants, which means they have a special stomach that lets them chew their food twice. First, a cow swallows its food quickly, without chewing it very much. Later, when the cow is resting, it brings the food back up and chews it slowly and carefully. This second chewing is called chewing the cud. You will often see a cow lying in the field, chewing slowly with a calm and happy look on its face.

## Fresh milk for everyone

One of the most important things about cows is their milk. A mother cow gives milk after she has had a calf. The calf drinks its mother's milk when it is very young, and the milk helps it grow strong. People have kept cows for milk for thousands of years. We drink cow milk, and we use it to make butter, cheese, yoghurt and ice cream.

Farmers milk their cows every day, usually in the morning and in the evening. The milk is kept cool and clean, and it is taken to shops and markets for people to buy. A healthy cow that is well fed and well looked after can give a lot of milk. Milk is full of goodness that helps our bones and teeth grow strong.

## A mother's love

A mother cow loves her calf very much. When a calf is born, its mother licks it all over to clean it and to help it dry. The licking also helps the mother and the calf learn each other's smell, so they can always find each other in the herd. A new calf is wobbly on its legs at first, but it soon learns to stand and walk.

The calf stays close to its mother for many months. It drinks her milk and follows her around the field, learning where to find the best grass. The mother cow protects her calf, standing between it and anything she thinks might be dangerous. She calls to her calf with a low moo, and the calf answers back. It is a lovely sound to hear in a quiet field.

## Life in the herd

Cows are social animals, which means they like to live together in a group, called a herd. In a herd, cows graze together, rest together and look after each other. Cows have friends in the herd. They often choose one or two other cows to spend time with, and they lick and groom each other. Grooming helps cows feel calm and happy.

Cows talk to each other with moos. A cow has different moos for different feelings. A mother calls her calf with a soft moo, and a cow that is hungry or wants attention makes a louder one. Cows also show their feelings with their bodies. A cow that is relaxed holds its head low and chews slowly, while a cow that is worried holds its head up and looks around.

## The cow's big, strong body

Cows are big animals, and their bodies are made for a life of grazing. They have four stomachs that work together to break down tough grass. They have strong legs that carry their heavy bodies across the field, and hard hooves that protect their feet. A cow's tail is long, with a tuft of hair at the end, and it swishes from side to side to keep flies away.

Cows have a very good sense of smell, and they can smell food from far away. They can also hear well, and their big ears turn to listen for sounds. Even though cows are big and strong, they are gentle animals, and they rarely hurt anyone on purpose.

## Looking after cows kindly

Cows need kind care every day. They need fresh, clean water, plenty of grass or hay to eat, and a dry, shady place to rest. In hot weather, cows need shade and lots of water, because they can get very hot in the sun. In the rainy season, they need a dry shelter so they do not have to stand in the mud.

If you visit cows, move slowly and speak softly. Do not run at them or shout, because that can frighten them. Let a cow come to you if it wants to, and stroke it gently on its neck if the farmer says it is safe. A cow that is treated kindly is calm and friendly. Cows give us so much, from milk to cheese to butter, so the least we can do is give them clean food, fresh water, shelter and gentle hands.

## Fun facts about cows

- A cow's tongue is long and rough, and it uses it like a scoop to pull grass into its mouth.
- Cows chew their food twice — they swallow quickly, then bring it back up to chew again.
- A mother cow licks her new calf all over to clean it and to learn its smell.
- A group of cows living together is called a herd.
- Cows groom their friends by licking them, which helps them feel calm and happy.
