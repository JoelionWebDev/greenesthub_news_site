Kunekune pigs are small, hairy pigs from New Zealand with a sweet nature and a love of company. They are happy to eat grass all day long. The word kunekune means "fat and round" in the Maori language, and it suits these pigs perfectly. They are round, hairy and friendly, and they have become some of the most loved pet pigs in the world.

## Pigs from New Zealand

Kunekune pigs come from New Zealand, a country of green hills and farms in the Pacific Ocean. Long ago, the Maori people, who are the first people of New Zealand, kept kunekune pigs around their villages. The pigs were valued for their meat, and they were also kept as pets and friends.

Kunekune pigs are different from most other pigs because they can live happily on grass alone. Most pigs need a lot of grain and other food, but kunekunes are content to graze in a field like sheep or cows. This made them very useful for farmers in New Zealand, who could keep them in grassy fields without spending a lot on feed.

For a while, kunekune pigs almost disappeared. By the late 1970s, there were only a few dozen left in the whole world. Kind people worked hard to save them, breeding them carefully and sending them to new homes in other countries. Today, kunekune pigs live in many places, including Britain, America and Australia, and their numbers are growing again.

## Round, Hairy and Happy

Kunekune pigs are small for pigs, but they are still sturdy animals. They have short legs, round bodies and long, shaggy hair that can be black, brown, white, ginger or a mix of colours. Their hair grows longer in winter to keep them warm, and they shed some of it in summer.

One of their most charming features is the little tassels that hang from their chins. These are called wattles, and they look like two small earrings under the pig's jaw. Not every kunekune has them, but most do, and they make the pigs look very cute.

Kunekune pigs have short, upturned snouts, which means they are not good at digging up the ground like other pigs. This is good news for farmers, because kunekunes will not ruin a field by rooting it up. They prefer to nibble grass from the surface, which keeps the field looking neat.

## The Friendliest Pigs

Kunekune pigs are famous for their gentle nature. They are calm, friendly and they love people. They enjoy being stroked, they will follow their owners around, and some will even roll over for a belly rub, just like a dog. They are very social animals and they love company, both from other pigs and from people.

They are also clever. Kunekune pigs can learn their names, they can learn to come when called, and they can learn simple tricks. They are curious animals, and they enjoy exploring their field, sniffing new things and meeting new friends.

Kunekune pigs live in groups, and they form strong friendships with each other. They like to sleep close together in a pile, keeping each other warm. A kunekune that is kept alone can become lonely, so they should always have at least one pig friend.

## What Kunekune Pigs Eat

Kunekune pigs are grazers, which means they eat grass for most of the day. They wander around their field, munching grass, clover and other green plants. They also enjoy vegetables, fruit and hay, especially in winter when the grass is short.

Because they eat mostly grass, kunekune pigs are easy and cheap to keep compared with other pigs. But they still need fresh water every day, a dry shelter to sleep in, and some extra food in winter when there is less grass to eat. Like all pigs, they should not be given too many treats, or they can become too fat, which is bad for their health.

## Baby Kunekunes

Baby kunekune pigs are called piglets, and they are very cute. They are born with soft hair and they stay close to their mothers, drinking milk and learning to eat grass. Mother kunekunes are very caring, and they build soft nests of straw and hay for their piglets to sleep in.

Piglets grow quickly, and within a few weeks they are running around the field, playing with each other and exploring. They love to play, and they will chase each other, roll in the grass and pretend to fight. Play helps them learn and grow strong.

## Looking After a Kunekune

Kunekune pigs need a field or a large garden with a strong fence, a dry shelter, and fresh water. They need a vet to check them from time to time, and their feet and teeth need care. They also need love and attention, because they are social animals that enjoy being with people.

They are wonderful pets for families who have enough space and time. They are gentle with children, they are fun to watch, and they are very loyal friends. But they are still pigs, and they need proper care for many years, so families should think carefully before bringing one home.

## A Pig Worth Saving

The kunekune pig is a wonderful story of a breed that was almost lost and then saved by people who cared. From just a few dozen pigs, there are now many thousands around the world, all because people saw how special these friendly little pigs are.

If you ever meet a kunekune, look for its wattles, its shaggy coat and its happy, round face. Then give it a gentle pat. You will have made a friend — a pig that loves grass, loves people and loves life.
