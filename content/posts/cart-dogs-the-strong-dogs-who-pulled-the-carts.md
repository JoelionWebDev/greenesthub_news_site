Long before there were cars, vans and delivery trucks, the streets of many European towns were filled with a very different kind of delivery driver: dogs. Strong, hard-working dogs pulled little carts loaded with milk, bread, vegetables and other goods, trotting through the streets to deliver them to people's doors. Long before cars and vans, strong dogs pulled little carts through the streets of Europe, carrying milk, bread and vegetables to people's doors.

## The Dogs That Delivered Your Breakfast

In the 1800s and early 1900s, many people in countries such as Belgium, the Netherlands, France and Germany used dogs to pull carts. The carts were small, with two wheels, and they were loaded with goods to sell. A milkman might use a dog cart to carry his milk churns from house to house. A baker might use one to deliver fresh bread. A farmer might use one to take vegetables to the market.

The dogs were strong and sturdy, and they were trained to pull the carts calmly through busy streets. They wore special harnesses, like little horse harnesses, that spread the weight across their chests and shoulders. The owner would walk beside the cart, guiding the dog with a lead and a few kind words. It was a familiar sight in many towns, and people loved their cart dogs.

For poor families, a dog cart was a wonderful thing. A horse was expensive to buy and feed, but a dog was much cheaper, and it could do the same job on a smaller scale. A dog could pull a cart full of goods that would have been too heavy for a person to carry, and it could do it day after day, in all kinds of weather.

## The Breeds That Pulled Best

Not every dog could pull a cart. The best cart dogs were big, strong breeds with calm, steady personalities. In Belgium, a breed called the Bouvier was often used. In the Netherlands, farmers used large, sturdy farm dogs. In other places, people used mastiffs, Bernese mountain dogs, and other strong breeds that were built for hard work.

These dogs were not just strong. They were also clever and loyal, and they learned the routes they had to follow. A good cart dog knew the way to the market, the way to each customer's house, and the way home again. Some dogs were so well trained that they could find their way even if their owner was not holding the lead.

The dogs were also good guards. While the owner was busy selling goods or talking to customers, the dog would stay with the cart and watch over it. If a stranger came too close, the dog would bark a warning. This made the cart dog a very useful partner, both as a worker and as a protector.

## A Hard Life That Changed

Life as a cart dog was not always easy. The carts could be heavy, and the dogs worked long hours, in hot summers and cold winters. Some owners were kind and looked after their dogs well, giving them good food, rest and care. But others were not so kind, and they made their dogs pull loads that were too heavy, or worked them for too long without rest.

People began to notice that some cart dogs were suffering, and they started to speak up for them. Animal lovers and charities campaigned for laws to protect working dogs, and in some places, new rules were made about how much weight a dog could pull, and how long it could work. In Britain, dog carts were banned in London in the 1800s, and in other countries, rules were brought in to make sure the dogs were treated fairly.

As cars and vans became more common, dog carts slowly disappeared from the streets. By the middle of the 1900s, they were mostly a thing of the past, and the dogs that once pulled carts became pets and companions instead of workers.

## Cart Dogs Today

Today, you will not see dog carts delivering milk in most towns, but the tradition has not completely disappeared. In some places, people still use dogs to pull carts for fun, in a sport called carting or draft work. The dogs wear special harnesses and pull small carts in competitions, where they are judged on how well they follow commands and how happily they work.

Some large breeds, such as Bernese mountain dogs and Newfoundlands, still love to pull carts, and their owners train them for fun and exercise. It is a way of keeping the old tradition alive, and of giving these strong, clever dogs a job to do. The dogs seem to enjoy it, and they wag their tails as they pull their little carts along.

Cart dogs are also remembered in museums and in old photographs, where you can see them trotting through the streets with their carts, doing their daily work. They are a part of history, and they remind us of a time when animals and people worked together in a very close and practical way.

## A Dog That Helped People Every Day

The cart dog shows us that dogs have been helpers to people for a very long time, not just as pets, but as workers too. They pulled carts, guarded goods, and helped families earn a living. They were strong, loyal and hard-working, and they did their jobs with a wagging tail and a happy heart.

Next time you see a delivery van driving down the street, think of the cart dogs, the strong dogs who pulled the carts, trotting through the town with milk and bread, helping people in their daily lives, one little cart at a time.
