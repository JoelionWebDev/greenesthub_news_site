Have you ever seen a tiny animal with stripes on its back and cheeks puffed out like two little balloons? That is a chipmunk. Chipmunks are small, quick, and very cheeky animals that live in forests and gardens. They scurry along the ground, dart up trees, and pop in and out of holes in the earth. They are always busy, always looking for food, and always ready to run.

Chipmunks are members of the squirrel family, but they are much smaller than most squirrels. They live on the ground more than they live in trees, and they love places with lots of trees, bushes, and fallen leaves. If you sit quietly in a forest, you might hear a chipmunk before you see it. It makes a quick, chirping sound, then dashes away in a flash of stripes and fur.

## Stripes, Cheeks, and a Busy Tail

A chipmunk is easy to spot once you know what to look for. It has brown fur with light and dark stripes running down its back and along its sides. Those stripes help it hide among leaves, twigs, and shadows on the forest floor. When a chipmunk sits still, it can be hard to see, even if it is right in front of you.

The most famous part of a chipmunk is its cheeks. A chipmunk has special pouches inside its cheeks that can stretch out like little pockets. When it finds food, it stuffs the food into its cheek pouches and carries it home. A chipmunk with full cheeks looks very funny, as if it has eaten far too much, but it is just carrying lunch home for later.

Chipmunks have a short, fluffy tail that they hold up as they run. They also have bright, dark eyes and small, round ears. Their front paws are tiny and quick, and they use them to hold nuts and seeds while they eat. A chipmunk eating a nut is one of the cutest sights in the forest.

## The Great Food Hoarders

Chipmunks love food, and they love to save food for later. They eat nuts, seeds, berries, and fruit, and they also eat buds, mushrooms, and sometimes insects. They are always searching for something tasty. In the autumn, when trees drop their nuts and seeds, chipmunks work very hard. They gather as much food as they can and carry it back to their home.

A chipmunk does not eat all its food straight away. Much of it goes into a store room in its burrow, where it keeps the food safe for the cold months. This is called hoarding, and chipmunks are some of the best hoarders in the animal world. A chipmunk may fill its store room with many nuts and seeds, so it has plenty to eat when food is hard to find outside.

## A Cosy Home Underground

A chipmunk's home is called a burrow. It is a tunnel dug into the ground, often under a tree root, a rock, or a pile of leaves. The burrow has rooms for sleeping, rooms for storing food, and tunnels that lead in and out. A chipmunk digs its burrow with its strong front claws, and it carries the loose earth away so that the entrance is hard to find.

The burrow is a safe, warm place. In the cold months, when snow covers the ground and food is scarce, a chipmunk spends a lot of time in its burrow. It sleeps for long stretches, wakes up to eat some of the food it stored, and then goes back to sleep. This long, sleepy time is called hibernation, and it helps the chipmunk save energy until spring comes.

In spring, the chipmunk wakes up and comes out to enjoy the warm sun. It cleans out its burrow, stretches its legs, and starts searching for fresh food. The forest is full of new buds, berries, and seeds, and the chipmunk is ready for another busy season.

## Quick, Clever, and Careful

Chipmunks are very quick. They can run fast, climb trees, and jump from branch to branch. They need to be quick because many other animals would like to catch them. Foxes, hawks, snakes, and cats all hunt chipmunks, so a chipmunk must always be ready to run and hide.

A chipmunk is also very careful. It stops often to sit up, look around, and listen. Its bright eyes watch for danger, and its ears listen for strange sounds. If it sees or hears something that scares it, it gives a sharp chirp to warn other chipmunks, then dashes into its burrow or up a tree. That chirp is a little alarm bell for the forest.

Chipmunks talk to each other with different sounds. They chirp, chatter, and make quick trills. Some sounds mean danger, and some sounds are just chipmunks chatting as they go about their day. If you listen in a quiet forest, you may hear a chipmunk's cheerful chirp coming from the bushes.

## Baby Chipmunks and Family Life

In spring, a mother chipmunk has babies in her burrow. The babies are born tiny, blind, and without fur, and they stay safe in the warm nest while their mother looks after them. She feeds them, keeps them warm, and guards the burrow until they are old enough to come out.

As the babies grow, they open their eyes and grow soft fur with stripes just like their mother. They begin to explore the burrow, and soon they follow their mother outside to learn how to find food. They watch her dig, sniff, and gather seeds, and they copy what she does. Learning from their mother is how young chipmunks learn to live on their own.

When the young chipmunks are big enough, they leave home and dig burrows of their own. They find their own patch of forest, gather their own food, and start their own busy lives. Each chipmunk has its own home and its own store of food, and each one plays a part in the life of the forest.

Chipmunks may be small, but they are full of life and energy. They work hard, they play, they chatter, and they help the forest grow by planting seeds. The next time you walk through a forest or a garden, look for a flash of stripes and a pair of puffy cheeks. If you are quiet and patient, you might just spot a chipmunk, the tiny, cheeky hoarder of the forest.
