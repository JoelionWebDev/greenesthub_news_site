Have you ever watched a tiny animal that never seems to sit still? Meet the degu! Degus are small, busy rodents from Chile, a long, thin country in South America. They love to dig, climb, run, chew and chatter to each other all day long. With their big eyes, round ears and long tails, degus look a bit like a mix between a mouse, a squirrel and a tiny guinea pig — and they are full of energy.

In the wild, degus live in the dry, hilly parts of Chile, where they dig long tunnels under the ground. Their burrows are like little underground houses with many rooms and entrances. Whole families of degus live together in these burrows, working as a team to dig, watch for danger and raise their babies. Pet degus come from breeders, not from the wild, and they need owners who understand how busy and social they are.

## What Does a Degu Look Like?

A degu is about the size of a hamster, but with a longer body and a longer tail. Its fur is soft and brown-grey, with a paler tummy. Its tail is long and thin, with a small tuft of dark fur at the end, a bit like a paintbrush. Degus have big, dark eyes, round ears and strong back legs for jumping and running.

One of the cutest things about a degu is its little hands. Degus use their front paws like hands, holding food while they nibble. They sit up on their back legs, hold a piece of hay or a seed in their paws, and munch away happily. Watching a degu eat is one of the best parts of having one as a pet.

## Chatty Little Friends

Degus are very social animals. In the wild, they live in big family groups, and they talk to each other all the time. They make lots of different sounds: chirps, squeaks, chatters and even a funny little "squeaking" noise when they are excited. Each sound means something different, like "come here," "watch out," or "I am happy."

Because degus are so social, they should never live alone. A lonely degu can become very sad. Pet degus need at least one degu friend to live with — two or three is even better. They groom each other, sleep in a pile to keep warm, and play together. Watching degus cuddle up together is enough to make anyone smile.

## Busy Builders and Diggers

If there is one word for degus, it is busy! They are always doing something: digging, climbing, chewing, running in a wheel or rearranging their home. A bored degu is an unhappy degu, so their home needs to be full of fun things to do.

In the wild, degus dig tunnels with their strong front paws and their teeth. Pet degus love deep bedding they can dig in, tunnels to run through, and wooden houses to hide in. They also love to climb, so branches and shelves give them places to explore. A running wheel made for degus helps them use up their energy — but it must be a solid wheel, not one with bars, so their tails and feet stay safe.

Degus love to chew. Their teeth never stop growing, so they must chew every day to keep their teeth the right length. Wooden toys, branches from safe trees and cardboard tubes are perfect for chewing. If a degu has nothing to chew, its teeth can grow too long and hurt — so chew toys are not just fun, they are important for health.

## What Do Degus Eat?

Degus are plant eaters. In the wild, they munch on grasses, leaves, seeds and bark. Pet degus need lots of good-quality hay every day, which is the most important part of their food. Hay keeps their tummies working well and helps wear down their teeth as they chew.

They also enjoy small amounts of special degu food, fresh vegetables and the odd treat. But here is something important: degus cannot handle sugar. Their bodies are not made to eat sweet foods, and sugary treats can make them very sick. That means no fruit, no honey sticks and no sweet treats from the kitchen — hay and healthy food only. An adult should always check which foods are safe for a degu before offering anything new.

Fresh, clean water must always be available, usually in a water bottle fixed to the side of their home. Degus also need a dust bath — a shallow bowl of special dust they roll in to keep their fur clean and soft. It looks silly and fun, and the degus love it!

## Caring for Degus Kindly

Degus need a big home. Because they are so active and love company, a small cage is not enough. A large cage with several levels, deep bedding, hiding houses, a wheel and chew toys gives them room to run, dig and play. Their home must be cleaned often, so it stays fresh and healthy.

Like all pets, degus need a vet who knows about small animals if they ever get sick. They can live for many years — often five to eight years — so bringing degus home is a promise to care for them for a long time. Families should think carefully before getting degus, and make sure they have time every day to feed them, clean their home and spend time with them.

Degus also need gentle, quiet handling. They are small and quick, and loud noises or sudden grabs can frighten them. Never pick a degu up by its tail — their tails are delicate and can be hurt. The best way to hold a degu is to scoop it gently with both hands and keep it close to your body.

## Why Degus Matter

Degus teach us that friendship matters, even for tiny animals. They live together, talk to each other, share food and look after each other's babies. They remind us that being part of a group helps everyone feel safe and happy.

They also teach us that being busy is good. Digging tunnels, building nests and chewing wood are not just games — they are how degus stay healthy and strong.

So next time you see a degu digging, chirping or nibbling hay with its little hands, remember this small, busy friend from Chile — a chatty, clever little animal that shows us how much fun teamwork and a busy day can be.
