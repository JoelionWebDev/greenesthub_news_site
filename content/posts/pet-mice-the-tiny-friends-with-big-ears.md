Hello, little friend! Can you see those big round ears and that tiny twitchy nose? That is a pet mouse! Mice are some of the smallest pets you can keep, but they have big personalities. They are curious, clever, and full of energy, and they love to explore. Let us peek into the world of these tiny friends.

## Where Do Pet Mice Come From?

Pet mice come from wild mice that have lived all over the world for a very long time. The mice we keep as pets today come from the house mouse, a small brown mouse that has lived near people for thousands of years. Long ago, people in Asia began to keep mice as pets, and they bred them in many pretty colours. Later, pet mice became popular in Europe and all over the world.

Wild mice are small, brown, and very quick. Pet mice are much tamer than wild mice. They are used to people, and they come in many colours, including white, black, brown, grey, and spotted.

## What Does a Pet Mouse Look Like?

A pet mouse is tiny. It is about as long as your thumb, not counting its tail, and it weighs about as much as a few coins. It has a small, pointed face, a pink nose that never stops twitching, and two big, round ears that move to catch every sound. Its eyes are bright and shiny, like tiny black beads.

A mouse's tail is long, thin, and pink, and it helps the mouse balance when it climbs. Mice have four little legs with tiny toes, and they use their front paws like hands to hold food while they eat. Their whiskers are long and sensitive, and they use them to feel their way around in the dark. Whiskers help a mouse know if a space is big enough to squeeze through.

Pet mice come in many colours and patterns. Some are all white, some are all black, and some have patches and spots. No matter what colour they are, all pet mice are cute.

## How Do Mice Talk to Each Other?

Mice are social animals, which means they like being with other mice. In the wild, mice live in groups, and pet mice are happiest when they live with other mice too.

Mice talk to each other with sounds and smells. They squeak when they are excited, scared, or talking to their friends. Some of their squeaks are so high that people cannot hear them at all! Mice also use their sense of smell to recognise each other. They leave tiny scent marks to tell other mice, "This is my home," or "I was here."

Mice also talk with their bodies. They wiggle their noses, twitch their whiskers, and flick their ears. When a mouse is happy and relaxed, it might groom its friends, licking their fur clean.

## What Do Pet Mice Eat?

Mice are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and small bits of other food. In the wild, mice eat seeds, grains, nuts, fruit, and insects. Pet mice eat a special mouse food made of seeds and grains, and they love fresh treats like carrots, broccoli, apples, and peas.

Mice have strong teeth that never stop growing, so they need to gnaw on things to keep their teeth short and healthy. They love to chew on wooden toys, cardboard, and hard treats.

## The Mouse's Busy Home

A pet mouse lives in a cage or a tank that is its home. Inside, it needs soft bedding to sleep in, a little house to hide in, a wheel to run on, and tunnels and toys to explore. Mice love to climb, dig, and build nests. They use soft paper, hay, and shredded bedding to make a cosy nest where they can sleep.

Mice are very active, especially at night. They are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and sleep during the day. While you are asleep, your pet mouse might be running on its wheel, climbing its toys, digging in its bedding, and exploring every corner of its home.

## Baby Mice Are Called Pups

A baby mouse is called a pup, or sometimes a pinkie, because newborn pups are tiny, pink, and have no fur at all. A mother mouse builds a soft nest for her babies, and she keeps them warm and feeds them with her milk. The pups grow very fast. Within a couple of weeks, they open their eyes, grow soft fur, and start to explore the world.

Mother mice are very caring. They carry their pups gently in their mouths to move them to a safer spot, and they lick them clean. As the pups grow up, they learn from their mother and their brothers and sisters how to find food, how to climb, and how to stay safe.

## How to Care for a Pet Mouse

Pet mice need gentle, kind care. They need fresh food and clean water every day, and their home needs to be cleaned regularly so it stays fresh and healthy. Mice also need safe toys and things to chew on, and they need a quiet place to sleep during the day.

If you want to hold a pet mouse, you need to be very gentle and patient. Mice are small and quick, and they can be nervous at first. With time and patience, a pet mouse can learn to trust you. It might climb onto your hand, sniff your fingers, and even sit on your shoulder. Always wash your hands before and after handling a mouse, and never squeeze it or grab it by its tail.

## Fun Facts About Mice

Here are some fun facts about mice. A mouse's whiskers can feel the tiniest movements in the air. Mice have excellent hearing and can hear sounds that people cannot hear. A mouse's heart beats very fast, much faster than a person's heart. And mice are very clean animals — they spend a lot of time grooming themselves and each other.

## Why We Love Pet Mice

Pet mice remind us that small does not mean unimportant. These tiny friends have big ears, brave hearts, and curious minds, and they fill their little world with energy and fun.

The next time you see a pet mouse twitching its nose and wiggling its whiskers, stop and watch. That tiny mouse is exploring its world, talking to its friends, and living its life to the full — and it is doing it all in a body smaller than your hand.
