Pygmy goats are small, sturdy little goats with big hearts and a love of climbing, jumping and playing. They are some of the friendliest farm animals. If you have ever seen a tiny goat standing on top of a hay bale, or bouncing around a field like a spring, it was probably a pygmy goat. These little goats bring joy wherever they go.

## Little Goats from West Africa

Pygmy goats come from West Africa, where they have lived for hundreds of years. In their home countries, such as Cameroon, they were kept by families for milk and meat, and they learned to survive in hot weather with simple food. They are tough little animals, used to living outdoors and finding food in all sorts of places.

Travellers brought pygmy goats to Europe and America, where people quickly fell in love with them. Today, pygmy goats live on farms and in gardens all over the world, and they are often kept as pets. They are popular at children's farms and petting zoos, where children can meet them up close.

Pygmy goats are much smaller than ordinary farm goats. An adult pygmy goat stands about as high as a young child's knee, and it is short, sturdy and strong. They come in many colours, including black, brown, white and grey, and many have pretty patterns on their coats.

## Born to Climb and Jump

Pygmy goats are wonderful climbers. They love to climb on rocks, logs, fences and anything else they can find. If you give a pygmy goat a wooden platform or a pile of hay bales, it will climb straight to the top and look around proudly, like a king on a mountain.

They also love to jump. Young pygmy goats, called kids, bounce around the field with great leaps, twisting in the air with joy. This jumping is not just for fun — it helps them grow strong and learn to move quickly. Watching a group of pygmy goat kids playing is one of the happiest sights on a farm.

Pygmy goats have cloven hooves, which means each hoof is split into two parts. This helps them grip rocks and climb steep places. They also have small horns, which both males and females grow. The horns are used for scratching, for pushing other goats gently, and for showing who is in charge in the herd.

## What Pygmy Goats Eat

Pygmy goats are not fussy eaters. They love hay, grass and leaves, and they enjoy munching on weeds and shrubs. They will eat almost any green plant they find, and they are very good at clearing overgrown gardens and fields. Farmers sometimes call them "living lawnmowers" because they eat so much grass.

They also enjoy treats such as carrots, apples and leafy greens. But they should not eat too many treats, or they can become overweight. Like all animals, pygmy goats need a balanced diet, fresh water and plenty of exercise to stay healthy.

Pygmy goats have four stomachs, just like other goats and cows. This helps them digest tough plants such as hay and grass. They chew their food, swallow it, and then bring it back up to chew it again. This is called chewing the cud, and it is why goats always seem to be chewing, even when they are resting.

## Friendly and Clever

Pygmy goats are very friendly animals. They love people, and they enjoy being stroked and petted. Many pygmy goats will follow their owners around the farm, hoping for a treat or a scratch behind the ears. They are curious too, and they will investigate anything new in their field, from a bucket to a ball.

They are also clever. Pygmy goats can learn their names, they can learn to come when called, and some can even learn simple tricks. They remember people and places, and they can tell the difference between a kind stranger and someone they do not know.

Pygmy goats live in herds, which means groups of goats that live together. They are social animals, and they do not like to be alone. A pygmy goat that lives by itself can become sad and lonely, so they should always have at least one goat friend to live with.

## Baby Pygmy Goats

Baby pygmy goats are called kids, and they are some of the cutest babies on the farm. They are born small and wobbly, but within a few hours they are up on their feet and trying to jump. They drink their mother's milk for the first few weeks, and they start nibbling grass and hay when they are just a few days old.

Mother goats are very caring. They lick their babies clean, keep them warm, and call to them with soft bleats. The kids stay close to their mothers for the first few months, learning how to eat, climb and play. By the time they are a few months old, they are brave little goats, ready to explore the world.

## Looking After Pygmy Goats

Pygmy goats need a safe field or garden with a strong fence, because they are good at escaping through small gaps. They need a dry shelter to sleep in at night, fresh water every day, and hay to eat. Their hooves need trimming from time to time, and they need visits from the vet to stay healthy.

They also need love and attention. A pygmy goat that is stroked and talked to every day becomes a happy, trusting pet. They are wonderful animals for families who have space and time to care for them properly.

## Small Goats, Big Joy

Pygmy goats show us that happiness often comes in small packages. These tiny goats climb, jump, play and make friends with everyone they meet. They are hard workers in the garden, loyal friends on the farm and joyful companions for children.

If you ever visit a farm and see a little goat standing on top of a hay bale, looking very pleased with itself, wave hello. It is probably a pygmy goat, and it will be very happy to see you.
