Imagine a little bird that can whistle your favourite tune. That is a cockatiel! Cockatiels are small, cheerful parrots with a spiky crest on their heads and bright orange cheeks. They love company, they love to sing, and many of them learn to whistle songs that their owners teach them. If you want a pet that greets you with music every morning, a cockatiel might be the perfect friend for you.

Cockatiels are small cousins of the big cockatoos. In the wild, they live in big, noisy flocks, and pet cockatiels still love being part of a flock — with you in it!

## Where Do Cockatiels Come From?

Cockatiels come from Australia, the same sunny country as kangaroos and koalas. In the wild, they live in the dry, open lands of the Australian outback, where they fly in large flocks looking for seeds and water. They are strong fliers, and a wild flock can travel a long way in a day to find food.

Wild cockatiels are mostly grey, with a yellow face and orange cheek patches. Pet cockatiels come in many colours too, including soft yellow, white, and pretty patterns of grey and white. No matter what colour they are, they all have the same cheeky crest and the same happy nature.

Pet cockatiels still need a fresh bowl of clean water every day, and they love a gentle spray or a shallow bath.

## What Does a Cockatiel Look Like?

A cockatiel is a small parrot, about the size of a grown-up's hand from beak to tail. The most eye-catching part of a cockatiel is its crest — a tuft of feathers on top of its head that goes up and down like a little crown.

The crest tells you how a cockatiel is feeling. When it stands tall, the bird is curious or excited. When it lies flat, the bird feels calm and safe. If it is very flat and the bird looks puffed up, it might be frightened, so give it some quiet space.

Cockatiels have bright orange cheek patches, like two little circles of sunshine on their faces. Boy cockatiels often have brighter yellow faces than girls, and boys are usually the best whistlers. Their strong, curved beaks are perfect for cracking seeds, and their feet have two toes pointing forward and two pointing back, which helps them grip branches.

## The Whistle and the Talk

Cockatiels are famous for their whistling. They can learn to whistle whole tunes, and some learn to copy sounds from around the house, like the ring of a telephone or the beep of a microwave. Many owners teach their cockatiels a simple song, and the bird will whistle it back again and again, sometimes adding its own little twist.

Boy cockatiels are usually the best singers, and a happy male might whistle all day long to his favourite person. Girl cockatiels whistle too, but they are often quieter.

Some cockatiels even learn to say a few words, like "hello" or their own name, in a squeaky little voice. The best way to teach one is to repeat a word or tune calmly, again and again, and to praise the bird when it tries.

Cockatiels also talk with their bodies, bobbing their heads when they are happy and grinding their beaks softly when they are sleepy.

## What Do Cockatiels Eat?

In the wild, cockatiels eat mostly seeds from grasses and plants. Pet cockatiels need a mix of foods to stay healthy. Their main food is a good seed mix or small pellets made for parrots, and they also enjoy fresh vegetables, leafy greens, and small pieces of fruit.

Cockatiels love foods like carrots, broccoli, sweetcorn, and apples. Owners wash the fruit and vegetables well and chop them into small pieces. A cockatiel often holds food in its foot while it nibbles.

Some foods are not safe for birds, so owners always check before offering something new. Food and water bowls should be cleaned every day.

## A Happy Home for a Cockatiel

A pet cockatiel needs a big cage with room to stretch its wings, climb, and play. Inside the cage, there should be perches at different heights, a swing, and toys to chew and explore. Cockatiels are clever birds, and they get bored if they have nothing to do, so owners change the toys often to keep things interesting.

Cockatiels also need time outside their cage every day, in a safe room with the windows closed. They love to sit on a shoulder and explore, and this is when they learn to trust their owners.

A cockatiel's cage should sit in a bright part of the house, where the bird can see the family. Cockatiels do not like being alone for a long time — they need company and play every day.

## Are Cockatiels Good Pets?

Cockatiels make wonderful pets for families who have time for them. They are gentle, they rarely bite, and they love to be with people. They are awake during the day, so you can watch them play and listen to them sing while you are awake too. They are also smaller and quieter than many bigger parrots, which makes them good pets for homes with children.

But cockatiels need attention every single day — fresh food and water, a clean cage, toys, and time with their family. If they are left alone too much, they can become sad and lonely. A cockatiel is a friend for many years, so the whole family should help look after it.

If you are thinking about a cockatiel, learn all about them first, and talk to a grown-up.

## Fun Facts

- A cockatiel's crest goes up when it is curious and lies flat when it is calm.
- Boy cockatiels are usually better whistlers than girls.
- Cockatiels can learn to whistle whole songs, and some learn a few words too.
- Wild cockatiels live in Australia, where they fly in big flocks across the outback.
- Cockatiels grind their beaks softly when they are sleepy and content.
- A cockatiel's long tail makes up about half of its whole length.

Cockatiels are cheerful, musical, and full of love. If you ever hear a little bird whistling a tune, it might just be a cockatiel saying hello!
