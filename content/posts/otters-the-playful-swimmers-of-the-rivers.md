Splash! Slide! Dive! Otters are some of the most playful animals in the world. They love to swim, slide down muddy banks, and play with each other in the water. But otters are not just playful. They are also clever hunters, caring parents, and excellent swimmers. Let us dive in and meet these happy river animals.

## What Is an Otter?

An otter is a mammal that lives in and near water. It has a long, sleek body, a thick tail, and short legs with webbed feet. Webbed feet are feet with skin between the toes, a bit like a duck's feet, and they help the otter to swim fast. Otters also have thick, waterproof fur that keeps them warm and dry in cold water. Their fur is brown, and their belly is often lighter in colour.

Otters have small ears, a short nose, and whiskers that help them to feel their way in the water. Their whiskers can sense tiny movements, which helps them to find fish even in murky water. Otters are very flexible and can twist and turn in the water with ease. There are different kinds of otters that live in rivers, lakes, and even in the sea. River otters live in fresh water, while sea otters live in the ocean. All otters are good swimmers, and they love to play.

## Where Do Otters Live?

Otters live in many parts of the world, wherever there is clean water and plenty of food. River otters live along rivers, lakes, and streams, where they can swim, hunt, and play. Sea otters live in coastal waters, where they float on their backs and dive for food. Otters need clean water to live, because dirty water can harm their fur and their food.

An otter's home is called a holt, or a den. It is often a burrow in a riverbank, or a sheltered spot among rocks and roots. Otters line their den with leaves and grass to make it soft and cosy. They use their den to sleep, to rest, and to raise their babies. Otters are often active during the day, but some otters are more active at night. They like to live in places with plenty of hiding spots, such as reeds, bushes, and rocks, where they can stay safe from danger.

## What Do Otters Eat?

Otters are carnivores, which means they eat meat. Their favourite food is fish, and they are excellent fish hunters. They use their sharp teeth, strong jaws, and quick swimming to catch fish. They also eat crabs, shellfish, frogs, and other small water animals. Sea otters love to eat shellfish, and they are clever enough to use tools. A sea otter will float on its back, place a shell on its belly, and crack it open with a stone. That is a very smart trick!

Otters need to eat a lot of food, because swimming and playing in cold water uses up a lot of energy. They hunt every day, and they are very good at it. They can hold their breath underwater for a while as they chase fish. After a meal, otters often groom their fur, rolling and rubbing it to keep it clean and waterproof. Clean fur is very important for otters, because it keeps them warm in the water. Baby otters drink their mother's milk, and as they grow, their mother teaches them how to hunt and swim.

## Baby Otters

Baby otters are called pups, and they are very cute. When pups are born, they are tiny, blind, and covered in soft fur. They stay in the den with their mother, who feeds them with milk and keeps them warm. The mother is very caring, and she spends a lot of time grooming and cuddling her pups.

As pups grow, they start to explore the world outside the den. Their mother teaches them how to swim, how to dive, and how to catch fish. At first, pups are not very good swimmers, and their mother may carry them on her belly in the water. Play is very important for otter pups. They wrestle, chase each other, and slide down banks, which helps them to learn how to move and hunt. Pups stay with their mothers for a long time, learning all the skills they need. When they are old enough, they leave to find their own home by the water.

## Fun Facts About Otters

Otters have many fun habits. One of the best is that they love to slide! Otters will slide down muddy or snowy banks on their bellies, just for fun. They do it again and again, like children on a slide. This sliding is not only fun, it also helps them to move quickly down to the water.

Another fun fact is that sea otters often hold hands while they sleep! They float on their backs in the water, holding paws with each other so they do not drift apart. This is called rafting, and it is a very sweet sight. Otters also have a special pocket of skin under their arm, where they can store their favourite stone for cracking shells. They keep the same stone for a long time, and they use it again and again. Otters are also very clean animals, and they spend a lot of time grooming their fur to keep it waterproof.

## How We Can Help Otters

Otters need clean water to live, and that is why we need to look after our rivers, lakes, and seas. When water is polluted with rubbish and chemicals, otters and their food can be harmed. Otters also need safe places to live, with plenty of fish and shelter. In some places, otters have become rare because their homes have been lost or their water has become dirty.

Many people are working to protect otters. Conservationists clean up rivers, protect wetlands, and help to create safe habitats for otters. They also study otters to learn more about them and to find the best ways to help them. You can help otters by keeping water clean. Do not drop litter in rivers or lakes, and always put rubbish in a bin. You can also learn about otters and tell your friends why clean water is important. When we look after our water, we help otters and many other animals that live in and near it.
