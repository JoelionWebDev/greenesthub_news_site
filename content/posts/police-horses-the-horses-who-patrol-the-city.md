Clip-clop, clip-clop. Have you ever heard that sound on a city street and looked up to see a tall, beautiful horse with a police officer riding on its back? Police horses are real working animals, and they have been helping officers keep cities safe for a very long time, long before police cars existed. Even today, with cars, motorbikes and radios, horses are still an important part of the police team. Let us find out why.

## Why Do Police Still Use Horses?

You might wonder why the police need horses when they have cars. The answer is that horses can do some jobs better than any machine. A police officer on a horse sits high up, so they can see over crowds and across busy streets. That helps them spot trouble early and keep an eye on large groups of people at events like parades, sports matches and festivals.

Horses can also go places cars cannot. They can walk down narrow paths, through parks and along beaches. They are calm and steady in crowds, and people often feel safe and happy when they see a friendly police horse. Children love to wave at them, and a police horse can help officers chat with people in the community and build trust. A horse is a wonderful way to say hello!

## Choosing a Police Horse

Not every horse can be a police horse. The police look for horses that are calm, brave and strong. Big, sturdy horses are often chosen because they need to carry an officer and all their equipment. The horse must not be scared easily. It needs to stay calm around loud noises, flashing lights, traffic and big crowds.

Many police horses are a special kind of horse known for being gentle and steady. Before a horse joins the police, it goes through training to see how it behaves in different situations. Trainers check whether the horse is comfortable with busy streets, strange sounds and lots of people. Only the calmest and bravest horses make the team.

## Training Together

A police horse and its rider train together for a long time before they go out on patrol. The horse learns to walk calmly through crowds, to stand still when asked, and to move in different directions with just a small signal from the rider. It learns to be comfortable around cars, buses, music and even fireworks.

The rider learns to understand the horse. A good rider can tell when their horse is nervous, tired or happy, just by the way it moves its ears or holds its head. The horse and rider become a team, and they trust each other completely. That trust is very important, because when they are out on the streets, they need to work together like best friends.

## A Day on Patrol

A police horse's day starts early. First, the horse is groomed. That means its coat is brushed until it shines, its mane is combed, and its hooves are cleaned and checked. Then it is fed a healthy breakfast of hay and grain, and given fresh water. The horse's saddle and bridle are checked to make sure everything is clean and comfortable.

Then it is time for patrol. The officer rides the horse through the city streets, parks and busy squares. They keep watch, help people who are lost, and make sure everyone is safe. Sometimes they help at big events, where crowds of people gather. The horse's height helps the officer see everything, and the horse's calm presence helps keep the crowd relaxed.

After patrol, the horse goes back to the stables for a rest. It gets more food, fresh water and a comfortable bed of straw. Horses also get days off, just like people, and they spend time in fields where they can run, roll in the grass and play with other horses.

## Looking After a Police Horse

Police horses are looked after very well. They have their own stables, which are like cosy bedrooms, and they are cared for by grooms who feed them, brush them and check their health every day. A vet visits regularly to make sure they are healthy and strong. Their hooves are trimmed and fitted with horseshoes by a farrier, a special person who looks after horses' feet.

When a police horse gets older and it is time to retire, it does not just stop working one day. It goes to live in a peaceful home, often with a kind family or on a farm, where it can enjoy the rest of its life. Some retired police horses are adopted by their own riders, who have become their best friends over many years.

## Saying Hello to a Police Horse

If you ever see a police horse, it is fine to wave and smile. But remember, the horse is working, so always ask the officer before you touch it. Some horses love a gentle pat on the neck, while others are busy and need to stay focused. Never shout, run up behind a horse, or feed it without asking, because that can startle it. A calm, quiet hello is the best way to greet a police horse.

Police horses remind us that animals and people can work together as a team. They are strong, gentle and brave, and they help keep our cities safe. So next time you hear clip-clop on the street, look up and wave. You might just see one of the hardest-working animals in the city, doing its job with a swish of its tail and a proud, steady step.
