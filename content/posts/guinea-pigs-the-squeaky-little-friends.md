If you have ever heard a tiny "wheek, wheek, wheek!" coming from a cage, you have met a guinea pig. These small, round, furry animals are some of the friendliest pets in the world. They squeak when they are happy, purr when they are cuddled, and do a funny little jump called "popcorning" when they are excited. Guinea pigs are not pigs at all — and they do not come from Guinea! Let us find out more about these squeaky little friends.

## What Is a Guinea Pig?

Guinea pigs are small rodents, related to animals like rats and squirrels — but much cuter and friendlier! They have plump, round bodies, short legs, and no tail at all. Their fur comes in many colours and patterns: black, white, brown, ginger, and mixtures of all of them. Some guinea pigs have short, smooth fur, while others have long, flowing hair that needs to be brushed, or curly, fluffy coats.

Guinea pigs come from South America, where wild guinea pigs still live in grasslands in countries like Peru and Bolivia. People there have kept guinea pigs as animals for thousands of years. In the 1500s, traders brought guinea pigs to Europe, where they became popular pets. Nobody knows for sure why they are called "guinea pigs." One idea is that they were sold for a coin called a guinea, and another is that people thought they came from Guinea in Africa. Whatever the reason, the name stuck!

A guinea pig is about the size of a small shoe. They have big front teeth that never stop growing — that is why they need to chew hay all the time, to keep their teeth worn down to the right length.

## The Language of Guinea Pigs

Guinea pigs are very chatty animals. They make lots of different sounds, and each sound means something different. Learning to understand these sounds is part of the fun of keeping a guinea pig.

The most famous sound is the "wheek!" — a loud, high-pitched squeak. Guinea pigs wheek when they are excited, especially when they hear the rustle of a food bag or the fridge door opening. It is their way of saying, "Food! Food! I want food!"

Guinea pigs also purr. When you gently stroke a happy guinea pig, it may make a soft, deep purring sound, a bit like a cat. This means it is relaxed and enjoying the cuddle. But a short, sharp purr can mean something different — it might mean the guinea pig is annoyed or wants to be left alone.

Another sound is a soft "chutting" noise, which guinea pigs make when they are exploring and feeling curious. And when a guinea pig is very happy, it does something called "popcorning" — it jumps straight up into the air, sometimes twisting around, over and over again. It looks exactly like a piece of popcorn popping! Popcorning is one of the happiest sights in the pet world.

## What Do Guinea Pigs Eat?

Guinea pigs are herbivores, which means they only eat plants. The most important food for a guinea pig is hay. They need to eat hay all day long, because it keeps their teeth healthy and their tummies working properly. Without enough hay, a guinea pig can become very sick.

Guinea pigs also love fresh vegetables. Their favourites include carrots, bell peppers, cucumber, lettuce and broccoli. Fruit, like apple or banana, is a nice treat, but only in small amounts, because it is sweet. And just like people, guinea pigs need vitamin C. Unlike most animals, guinea pigs cannot make vitamin C in their own bodies, so they must get it from their food. Bell peppers are a great source of vitamin C for guinea pigs!

Fresh, clean water is very important too. Guinea pigs drink from a water bottle attached to the side of their cage. Their owners must check every day that the bottle is full and working properly.

## Guinea Pig Families

In the wild, guinea pigs live in groups, and pet guinea pigs are happiest when they live with a friend. A guinea pig kept on its own can become lonely and sad. That is why pet experts say guinea pigs should almost always be kept in pairs or small groups. Two female guinea pigs usually get along well together, and two males can be friends too, as long as they have plenty of space.

Guinea pigs are gentle, shy animals. When they first meet people, they may hide and feel nervous. But if you are patient, sit quietly near them, and offer them tasty vegetables, they will soon learn to trust you. After a while, many guinea pigs love to be held gently on a lap, where they will sit happily, purring and munching a piece of carrot.

Baby guinea pigs are born with fur, open eyes, and the ability to run around almost straight away. This is very different from baby mice or rats, which are born tiny, pink and helpless. A baby guinea pig can start eating solid food within a few days, though it still drinks its mother's milk too.

## Caring for a Guinea Pig

Guinea pigs need a big home. Their cage or hutch should be large enough for them to run around, stretch, and play. They also need time outside the cage every day in a safe, enclosed area where they can explore and get some exercise. Guinea pigs love tunnels, hidey-houses, and soft bedding to snuggle into.

Their home must be kept clean and dry. Guinea pigs like to be warm and cosy, and they must be protected from draughts, rain and very hot sun. If they live outside, their hutch should be strong and safe from cats, dogs and foxes.

Guinea pigs also need their nails trimmed sometimes, and long-haired guinea pigs need to be brushed regularly so their fur does not get tangled. Gentle handling every day helps them stay friendly and tame.

## Why Guinea Pigs Make Wonderful Pets

Guinea pigs are perfect pets for children who are gentle and patient. They do not bite, they do not climb out of their cages, and they love to be talked to. Many children find that talking to a guinea pig is a lovely way to share their feelings — and guinea pigs are very good listeners!

So if you ever meet a guinea pig, listen carefully. Its wheeks, purrs and popcorn jumps are telling you a story — a story of a small, round, squeaky little friend who is very glad to be part of your family.
