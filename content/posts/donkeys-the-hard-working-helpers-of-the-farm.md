## What is a donkey?

A donkey is a strong, gentle animal that looks a little like a small horse with very long ears. Donkeys have grey or brown coats, short manes, and a dark stripe that often runs along their backs. Their long ears are their most famous feature, and they can move them in different directions to listen for sounds.

A female donkey is called a jenny, a male donkey is called a jack, and a baby donkey is called a foal. Foals are born with long, wobbly legs and big ears that look almost too large for their heads. As they grow, they grow into their ears, and they become strong and steady animals.

Donkeys are smaller than horses, but they are very strong for their size. They can carry heavy loads on their backs and pull carts along rough roads. Donkeys are also famous for being sure-footed, which means they can walk safely on rocky paths and steep hills without slipping. Their call is called a bray, and it sounds like "hee-haw".

## Where do donkeys live?

Donkeys live on farms and in villages all over the world. They are especially common in warm, dry places, where they are very good at living with little water and tough food. Wild donkeys, the ancestors of farm donkeys, come from dry lands in Africa, where they learned to survive in hot deserts and rocky hills.

On a farm, donkeys live in a stable or a sheltered yard. They need a dry place to sleep, because their coats are not waterproof like a horse's coat. Donkeys do not like rain very much, so they are happy to stand under a roof when the weather turns wet.

During the day, donkeys spend their time in fields or yards, grazing on grass and walking around. They like to have space to move, and they enjoy the company of other donkeys, horses or even goats. A donkey that lives alone can become sad, so farmers often keep two donkeys together.

## What do donkeys eat?

Donkeys are herbivores, which means they eat plants. Their favourite food is grass, but they also eat hay, leaves, straw and small branches. Donkeys are not fussy eaters. They can eat tough, dry plants that other animals would leave behind, and their strong teeth help them chew the hardest stems.

Donkeys need fresh water every day, but they do not need as much as a horse does. They are very good at saving water in their bodies, which helps them live in hot, dry places.

A donkey's stomach is small, so it is best to feed donkeys little and often, rather than giving them one big meal. Farmers give donkeys hay and grass through the day, and sometimes a little grain as a treat. Donkeys love carrots and apples too, and many children enjoy feeding a donkey a crunchy carrot.

Donkey foals drink their mother's milk when they are born. As they grow, they start to nibble grass and hay, just like the grown-up donkeys.

## Donkeys at work

Donkeys have helped people for thousands of years. They are working animals, and they are famous for carrying heavy loads. In many villages, donkeys carry water, firewood, grain and building materials on their backs. They walk along narrow paths where cars and trucks cannot go, and they can climb steep hills with their loads.

Donkeys also pull carts. A donkey pulling a cart can move heavy things from one place to another, which helps farmers take their crops to market. In some places, donkeys are used to plough fields, pulling a plough through the soil so that seeds can be planted.

Donkeys are patient workers. They walk at a steady pace, and they do not rush. They are careful on rough ground, and they think before they step. Some people say donkeys are stubborn, but really they are just careful. A donkey will stop if it thinks a path is dangerous, and that careful nature keeps both the donkey and its owner safe.

## Donkey families

Donkeys are loving and loyal animals. A jenny usually gives birth to one foal at a time. The foal is born in a warm, dry place, and the mother stays close, licking it clean and keeping it safe.

A newborn foal can stand up soon after it is born, and it quickly learns to walk beside its mother. Foals stay close to their mothers for a long time. They learn where to find the best grass, where to drink, and how to stay safe by watching their mothers.

Donkeys form strong friendships. Two donkeys that live together often become best friends. They groom each other, stand close together, and call out if they are separated.

## How do donkeys talk?

Donkeys talk with their voices and their bodies. The most famous donkey sound is the bray. A donkey brays to say hello, to call its friends, or to warn about something strange. A loud bray can be heard from a long way away.

Donkeys also make softer sounds. They snort when they are curious or unsure, and they make a gentle humming sound when they are happy and relaxed.

## Donkeys and people

People and donkeys have been friends for a very long time. Donkeys were among the first animals that people trained to help them carry things. Long ago, traders used donkeys to carry goods across deserts and over mountains, along famous old trade routes.

Today, donkeys still help people in many countries. They carry water and firewood, help farmers with their crops, and give rides to children. In some places, donkeys help people with disabilities too, because they are calm and gentle animals that are easy to be around.
