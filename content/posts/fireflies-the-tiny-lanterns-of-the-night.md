On a warm summer evening, when the sun has gone down and the sky is dark, tiny lights begin to twinkle in the garden. They blink on and off, floating slowly through the air like little stars that have come down to earth. Those tiny lights are fireflies. Fireflies are small beetles that can make their own light, and they turn a dark night into a magical show.

Fireflies are also called lightning bugs, because their light flashes like tiny lightning. They are not flies at all. They are beetles, and they belong to the same family as ladybirds and other beetles. But fireflies are special, because they carry a little lantern inside their bodies. When they fly at night, they light up the darkness with their gentle glow.

## How Fireflies Make Light

A firefly makes light in the lower part of its body, near its tail. Inside its body, special chemicals mix together and make a soft, yellow-green light. This is called bioluminescence, which means light made by a living thing. The light is cool, so it does not burn the firefly. It is a gentle, safe light, a bit like the glow of a tiny torch.

A firefly can turn its light on and off. It blinks in a pattern, flashing for a moment, then going dark, then flashing again. Each kind of firefly has its own flashing pattern, a bit like a secret code. Some flash quickly, some flash slowly, and some flash in a special rhythm. The pattern helps fireflies recognise each other in the dark.

Fireflies do not glow all the time. During the day, they rest in grass, leaves, and bushes, hiding from the sun. Their bodies are brown or black, with a little yellow or red on their heads, and they look like ordinary beetles. It is only at night, when they fly out to find a mate, that they light up and show their magic.

## Talking with Light

Fireflies use their light to talk to each other. When a male firefly flies through the night, he flashes his light in a pattern to say, "Here I am." If a female firefly on the ground likes his pattern, she flashes back with her own light to say, "I see you." In this way, fireflies find each other in the dark, using light instead of words.

This flashing is very important, because there are many kinds of fireflies, and each kind has its own pattern. A firefly must flash the right pattern to find a mate of its own kind. If it flashes the wrong pattern, the other fireflies will not answer. It is like speaking the right language so that your friends can understand you.

Fireflies also use their light to warn other animals. The light tells animals that fireflies do not taste good, so most animals leave them alone. Birds, frogs, and spiders learn that a glowing firefly is not a tasty meal, and they look for other food instead. The light keeps fireflies safe as well as helping them find friends.

## Life as a Firefly

A firefly starts its life as a tiny egg laid in damp soil or under leaves. When the egg hatches, a baby firefly, called a larva, comes out. The larva looks like a small, flat worm with legs, and it lives on the ground. It eats small snails, slugs, and worms, and it grows bigger and bigger.

Firefly larvae can glow too. Even as babies, they can make a soft light. The glow warns animals not to eat them, because they do not taste good. The larvae live on the ground for a long time, through the cold months and into the spring, growing and changing as they get ready to become adult fireflies.

When the larva is ready, it changes into an adult firefly with wings. The adult firefly flies out on warm nights to flash its light and find a mate. Adult fireflies do not live for a very long time. Their main job is to find a mate and lay eggs, so that a new generation of fireflies can grow up and light up the night again.

## Where Fireflies Live

Fireflies love warm, damp places. They live in fields, gardens, forests, and near streams and ponds, where the air is moist and there are plenty of plants to hide in. They are found in many parts of the world, especially in warm places. Some fireflies live in Africa, some in Asia, some in the Americas, and some in Europe.

Fireflies need dark nights to shine. If there are too many bright lights from houses, streets, and cars, it is hard for fireflies to see each other's flashes. That is why fireflies are easiest to see in quiet, dark places, away from bright lights. A garden with trees, grass, and a little pond is a perfect home for fireflies.

Fireflies also need clean air and clean water, and places where they can lay their eggs in damp soil. When people look after gardens, parks, and wild places, they help fireflies. Leaving a patch of long grass, planting flowers, and keeping a pond clean can give fireflies a safe home.

## Watching Fireflies

Watching fireflies is one of the joys of summer. On a warm evening, sit quietly in a garden or a field and watch the tiny lights blink on and off. Do not chase them or catch them in a jar, because fireflies are wild animals and they need to fly free. The best way to enjoy them is to watch them as they float through the dark.

If you want to see more fireflies, go out when it is just getting dark, and be very quiet. The fireflies will come out slowly, one by one, until the whole garden is twinkling. It is like watching stars appear in the sky, but much closer and much more magical.

Fireflies remind us that even the smallest animals can make something wonderful. A tiny beetle, no bigger than your fingernail, can make its own light and turn a dark night into a sparkling show. The next time you see a firefly, take a moment to watch its gentle glow. It is a tiny lantern of the night, lighting up the world one blink at a time.
