Aardvarks are strange and wonderful animals from Africa. They have long ears like a donkey, a snout like a pig, a tail like a kangaroo, and strong claws for digging, all in one animal. Aardvarks come out at night to hunt for ants and termites, and they can dig into a hard termite mound very quickly. They are shy and quiet, and they spend their days sleeping in a cool burrow underground. Even though they look a little bit odd, aardvarks are perfectly made for the life they live.

## What is an aardvark?

An aardvark is a mammal, and its name means "earth pig" in an old language from South Africa. That is a good name for it, because aardvarks love to dig in the earth and their snout looks a bit like a pig's snout. An aardvark has a long body, short legs, and skin that is greyish-brown with very little fur. Its ears are long and tube-shaped, and it can move them around to listen for sounds. Its tail is long and thick, and it helps the aardvark balance when it stands up to dig. Aardvarks have very strong legs and big, flat claws on their feet, a bit like small spades.

## Where do aardvarks live?

Aardvarks live in Africa, south of the Sahara Desert, in grasslands, savannas, and woodlands. They like open places where the soil is soft enough to dig in and where there are plenty of ants and termites to eat. Aardvarks do not like very rocky ground or thick forests, because digging is hard there. An aardvark lives alone in a burrow, which is a tunnel it digs in the ground, with a cosy chamber at the end where it sleeps during the day. When an aardvark leaves a burrow, other animals often move in and use it as a safe home, so aardvarks help many other animals too.

## What do aardvarks eat?

Aardvarks eat ants and termites, and they eat a lot of them. They use their wonderful nose to sniff out ant nests and termite mounds hidden underground. Once they find one, they dig into it with their strong claws, breaking open the hard mound in a matter of moments. Then they use their very long, sticky tongue to lick up the ants and termites. An aardvark's tongue can be very long, and it can reach deep into tunnels and cracks where the insects hide. Aardvarks do not have many teeth, and the teeth they do have are not very strong, so they swallow most of their food whole. They can eat thousands of insects in one night, and their long tongue and strong claws make them perfect ant hunters.

## The amazing diggers

Aardvarks are some of the best diggers in the animal world. Their front legs are strong and muscular, and their claws are thick and sharp, perfect for breaking through hard soil and termite mounds. An aardvark can dig a hole big enough to hide in very quickly, and if it feels scared it can dig down into the ground and disappear in a flash. Its ears can close up to keep dirt out while it digs, and its nose can close too. The aardvark's long tail acts like a brace, pushing against the ground to help it dig with all its strength. Even the ground around a termite mound, which can be as hard as concrete, is no match for an aardvark's claws. Digging is not just how aardvarks find food, it is also how they make their homes and stay safe.

## A night in an aardvark's life

Aardvarks are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. During the day, an aardvark sleeps curled up in its cool, dark burrow, safe from the hot African sun. When night falls, it comes out and starts to wander across the grassland, sniffing the air and listening with its big ears. It walks slowly at first, then stops to dig at a termite mound or an ant nest. It eats for a while, then moves on to the next mound. An aardvark can walk a long way in one night, visiting many mounds as it looks for dinner. It has a very good sense of smell, and it can smell ants and termites deep underground. Before dawn, it hurries back to its burrow to sleep again until the next night.

## Baby aardvarks

Baby aardvarks are born in the burrow, in a soft nest of grass and leaves. A mother aardvark usually has just one baby at a time. When the baby is born, it is small, pink, and has almost no hair, with wrinkled skin and big ears. The mother feeds it milk and keeps it warm and safe in the burrow. After a few weeks, the baby starts to follow its mother outside at night, learning how to find ants and termites. It practises digging with its tiny claws and licking up insects with its tongue. The mother stays with her baby for many months, teaching it everything it needs to know. When the young aardvark is old enough, it digs its own burrow and starts to live on its own, a true ant-eating digger at last.

## Fun facts about aardvarks

- The name aardvark means "earth pig" in an old language from South Africa.
- An aardvark's tongue is very long and sticky, perfect for licking up ants and termites.
- Aardvarks can dig into a hard termite mound very quickly with their strong claws.
- Aardvarks are nocturnal, which means they sleep during the day and hunt at night.
- When an aardvark leaves a burrow, other animals often move in and use it as their home.

Aardvarks may not be as famous as lions or elephants, but they are just as wonderful. They are gentle, shy, and perfectly made for their night-time life, with long ears, a clever nose, a super-long tongue, and claws that can dig through the hardest ground. Next time you think of Africa, picture an aardvark under the stars, sniffing the ground and digging for dinner, with its big ears listening to the quiet night.
