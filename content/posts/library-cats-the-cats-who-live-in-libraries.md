Imagine walking into a library. It is quiet. The shelves are full of books. Sunlight falls across a reading table. And there, curled up on a cushion between the storybooks, is a cat. It opens one eye, stretches, and goes back to sleep. This is a library cat — and some libraries are lucky enough to have one.

## Meet the Library Cat

A library cat is a cat that lives in a library. It is looked after by the librarians, and it spends its days among the books, greeting readers and keeping the building free of mice. Some library cats are famous, with their own books, calendars and fans all over the world. Others are quietly loved by the people in their town, who come to the library just to say hello.

Library cats come in all shapes and sizes. Some are big and fluffy, some are small and sleek. Some are ginger, some are black and white, and some are tabby with stripes. But they all share one thing: they love a warm, quiet place, and a library is one of the warmest, quietest places there is.

## A Very Old Job

Cats and libraries go back a long way. Long ago, when books were written by hand on paper or parchment, mice were a terrible problem. A mouse could chew through a precious book in a single night. Libraries needed a way to keep the mice away — and cats were the answer.

Even today, libraries keep cats for the same reason. A library is full of paper, and paper is warm and soft — just what a mouse likes. A cat in a library is a natural mouse guard, and it does the job without anyone having to set a trap.

## Dewey Readmore Books

One of the most famous library cats in the world was called Dewey Readmore Books. He was found as a tiny kitten in the book return box of a small library in Iowa, in America, on a freezing cold night. The librarian, Vicki Myron, took him in, warmed him up and named him Dewey, after a famous system for sorting library books.

Dewey became the library's cat, and he loved his job. He greeted people at the door, sat on their laps while they read, and played with the children at story time. He even had his own special place on the front desk. People came from far away to meet him, and a book was written about his life. The book became famous all over the world, and Dewey became one of the best-known cats ever.

## Other Famous Library Cats

Dewey is not the only famous library cat. In a library in Nevada, America, a cat called Browser became a star. Browser was a friendly tabby who loved to sit on the shelves and watch the readers. When the library tried to remove him, people all over the world wrote letters asking for him to stay. The town council listened, and Browser was allowed to stay. He even got his own badge as a library helper!

In Scotland, a library cat called Custard became well known for sleeping on the warm computers. In England, cats have lived in libraries for years, curling up on the children's reading cushions. In Russia, the famous Hermitage Museum — which is also a great library of art — has had cats for hundreds of years. They are known as the Hermitage cats, and they guard the museum's treasures from mice.

Some library cats have even appeared in books and films. Their stories remind us that cats and books belong together — and that a library is a better place with a cat in it.

## A Library Cat's Day

What does a library cat do all day? In the morning, when the librarian arrives, the cat is waiting at the door. It gets its breakfast, then settles down in its favourite spot — maybe a sunny windowsill, a soft chair or a shelf where nobody can reach it. It might sleep for a while, because cats sleep for a large part of the day.

When the library opens, the cat wakes up and starts its rounds. It walks slowly between the shelves, checking that everything is in order. It greets the regular visitors, rubbing against their legs or jumping onto their laps. Children love to read to the cat, and the cat loves to listen — or at least, to pretend to listen while it dozes.

## Why Readers Love Library Cats

People love library cats for many reasons. First, a cat makes a library feel warm and friendly. Walking into a big, quiet building can feel a little scary for a child, but a cat at the door makes it feel like home. Many children who are shy about reading feel braver when they can read to a cat, because a cat never laughs at a mistake.

Library cats also help people feel calm. Stroking a cat is relaxing, and many readers say they feel happier after spending time with the library cat. In some libraries, cats visit reading groups for children who find reading hard. The children read aloud to the cat, and slowly they become more confident.

## Fun Facts About Library Cats
- The most famous library cat, Dewey Readmore Books, was found in a book return box on a freezing night.
- The Hermitage Museum in Russia has kept cats for hundreds of years to guard its treasures from mice.
- A cat called Browser kept his job at a library in Nevada after people all over the world wrote letters to save him.
- Cats were kept in old monasteries to protect handwritten books from mice.
- Some libraries let children read aloud to cats to help them become more confident readers.

Libraries are places of stories, and a library cat is a story in itself. It is a quiet friend, a loyal worker and a warm welcome all in one furry body. Next time you visit a library, look around the shelves — you might just find a cat, fast asleep on the best book in the building.
