# Zebra Finches: The Tiny Singers of the Home

Listen carefully. Can you hear a tiny, cheerful song? It might be a Zebra Finch! Zebra Finches are very small birds with soft grey feathers, bright orange beaks, and pretty black and white stripes on their faces. They are not much bigger than your thumb, but their songs are big and joyful. Many families keep Zebra Finches as pets because they are gentle, lively, and fun to watch and listen to.

A Zebra Finch is easy to spot. Male Zebra Finches have bright orange cheeks, a black stripe across their chest like a little zebra, and small spots on their sides. Female Zebra Finches are plainer, with soft grey and white feathers and no bright orange cheeks. Both males and females have a bright red-orange beak and a long tail. When they hop around, they look busy and happy, always moving, chirping, and exploring.

## Where Zebra Finches Come From

Zebra Finches come from Australia, a country far away where the sun is hot and the land is dry. In the wild, they live in grasslands, near water, and in open bushland. They live in large groups called flocks, sometimes with many hundreds of birds flying, feeding, and singing together. They are social birds, which means they love company and do not like to be alone.

People first began keeping Zebra Finches as pets many years ago, because they are small, gentle, and easy to care for. They were brought to Europe and other parts of the world, and soon they became popular pets in homes everywhere. Today, Zebra Finches are one of the most popular pet birds in the world.

## Tiny Singers with Big Voices

Male Zebra Finches are the singers of the family. They sing a cheerful song made up of little beeps, trills, and chirps. Each male has his own song, a little bit different from other males. Young male finches learn their songs by listening to their fathers and other adult males, just like children learn songs by listening to grown-ups.

Female Zebra Finches do not sing long songs like the males, but they do make soft chirps and calls to talk to each other. Zebra Finches use their calls to say hello, to warn of danger, and to keep in touch when they are apart.

## Happy in a Flock

Zebra Finches are happiest when they live with other Zebra Finches. In the wild, they live in big flocks, and at home, they should live with at least one other finch. A single Zebra Finch kept alone can become sad and lonely. Two or more finches will preen each other, sing together, and snuggle up close when they sleep.

These little birds love to play and explore. They hop from perch to perch, flutter their wings, and peek at everything around them. They enjoy a swing in their cage and toys they can peck at. They also love to take a bath. A shallow dish of clean water is a treat for a Zebra Finch. It will hop in, splash, and shake its feathers happily.

## A Home for a Zebra Finch

A Zebra Finch needs a roomy cage where it can hop and flutter its wings. The cage should be wide rather than tall, because finches fly from side to side. It needs perches of different sizes, a food dish, a water dish, and a little nest or cosy spot where it can rest. The cage should be placed in a bright spot, away from cold draughts and away from the kitchen, where cooking smoke can harm birds.

Zebra Finches need fresh water every day, for drinking and for bathing. Their cage should be cleaned often, so it stays fresh and healthy. A clean cage, fresh water, and good food help a finch stay happy and strong.

These birds like a routine. They wake up with the sun, sing, eat, play, and rest during the day, and settle down to sleep when it gets dark. At night, they need a quiet, dark place to sleep.

## Food for Tiny Singers

Zebra Finches eat small seeds, and a good finch seed mix is their main food. They also enjoy millet sprays, which are like little branches covered in tiny seeds. Finches love to peck at millet, and it is a favourite treat. They also need fresh greens, such as lettuce or spinach, and sometimes a little fruit. These foods give them vitamins that keep them healthy.

A cuttlebone, which is a soft white bone from a sea animal called a cuttlefish, is often put in a finch cage. Finches peck at it to keep their beaks trim and to get calcium, which helps keep their bones strong. Fresh, clean water must always be available.

## Building Nests and Raising Chicks

In the wild and at home, Zebra Finches love to build nests. They use grass, soft fibres, and feathers to make a round, cosy nest. Both the male and the female help build the nest, and both help look after the eggs and the chicks. The female lays small white eggs, and both parents take turns sitting on them to keep them warm.

When the chicks hatch, they are tiny, pink, and have no feathers. Their parents feed them and keep them warm until they grow feathers and learn to fly. Watching finches build a nest and raise their chicks is a wonderful way to learn about nature.

Zebra Finches remind us that small things can bring great joy. They may be tiny, but their songs, their bright colours, and their happy hops fill a home with life. If you ever hear a cheerful little song coming from a cage, look closely. It may be a Zebra Finch, singing its heart out, just for you.
