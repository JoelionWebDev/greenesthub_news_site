Angelfish are some of the prettiest fish you can keep in a home aquarium. They have tall, flat bodies shaped a bit like a triangle, with long, flowing fins that trail behind them as they swim. They glide slowly and gracefully through the water, like dancers moving across a stage. Angelfish come in many beautiful colours, from shiny silver with dark stripes to bright gold, white, black, and marbled patterns. Watching them swim is calm and peaceful, which is why so many people love to keep them.

## What is an angelfish?

An angelfish is a freshwater fish from the cichlid family, which is a big family of fish that includes many popular aquarium fish. Its body is tall and thin from side to side, almost like a flat disc standing up in the water. Its long fins stretch out above and below its body, which makes it look even taller. Angelfish have small mouths at the front of their faces and large eyes that seem to watch everything around them. In the wild, angelfish are usually silver with dark vertical stripes that help them hide among plants and roots in the water. People who keep fish have bred many different colours over the years, so pet angelfish can be gold, black, white, blue, or a lovely mix of colours and patterns.

## Where do angelfish come from?

Angelfish come from the warm, slow-moving rivers of South America, especially the Amazon River and the rivers that flow into it. The Amazon is a huge river that winds through rainforests in countries like Brazil and Peru. In the wild, angelfish live among the roots of trees, fallen branches, and thick water plants. The water there is warm and soft, and the light that filters down through the trees is gentle and green. Angelfish hide among the plants and roots, using their tall, flat bodies and dark stripes to blend in and stay safe from bigger fish. They swim in small groups, moving slowly through the quiet water and looking for food.

## Life in the tank

Angelfish need a tall tank because their bodies are tall and their fins are long. A tank that is short and wide is not the best home for them, because they need room to swim up and down as well as from side to side. The water in the tank should be warm, just like the rivers of South America, and it should be kept clean with a filter. Angelfish love to have plants in their tank, both real plants and safe pretend ones, because plants give them places to hide and rest. Angelfish are calm fish, but they like to have their own space, and they do best when they live with other gentle fish of a similar size. Very small fish are not good tank mates, because angelfish might think they are food.

## What do angelfish eat?

Angelfish eat small bits of food that they find in the water. In the wild, they nibble at tiny insects, small water creatures, and bits of plant. In a home tank, they eat special fish food that comes in flakes or small pellets, and they enjoy frozen or live foods such as tiny brine shrimp and bloodworms as a treat. Angelfish should be fed a little bit at a time, once or twice a day, and only as much as they can finish in a few minutes. If too much food is dropped into the tank, the leftover food sinks to the bottom and makes the water dirty, which is not good for the fish. A good fish keeper watches the angelfish eat and learns how much food is just right for them.

## Angelfish and their babies

Angelfish are caring parents, and watching them look after their eggs is a real treat. When a pair of angelfish is ready to have babies, they choose a flat surface in the tank, often a broad leaf or a smooth piece of slate, and clean it carefully with their mouths. The mother angelfish lays rows of tiny, sticky eggs on the surface, and the father swims over them to fertilise them. Then both parents guard the eggs, fanning them gently with their fins to keep fresh water flowing over them and chasing away any fish that come too close. After a few days, the eggs hatch into tiny baby fish, called fry, which are so small they are hard to see at first. The parents keep looking after the fry, guiding them around the tank and protecting them until the little fish can swim and eat on their own.

## How angelfish talk to each other

Angelfish do not make sounds like we do, but they still have ways of telling each other things. They use their fins and their bodies to send messages. When an angelfish flares its fins wide and stands tall, it is telling another fish that this is its space. When two angelfish swim side by side and gently touch each other with their fins, they are being friendly and getting to know each other. Angelfish also change the way they look when they feel different things. Their colours can grow brighter when they are happy and healthy, and their stripes can darken when they are excited or a little bit stressed.

## Fun facts about angelfish

- Angelfish come from the Amazon River in South America.
- Their tall, flat bodies help them hide among plants and roots in the wild.
- Angelfish have been kept as pet fish for nearly one hundred years.
- A pet angelfish can live for many years if it is looked after well.
- Angelfish parents guard their eggs and fan them with their fins.
- Angelfish can learn to recognise the person who feeds them and swim to the front of the tank at feeding time.

Angelfish are graceful, gentle, and full of quiet charm. With their long flowing fins, bright colours, and calm, gliding swim, they turn an ordinary tank into a little piece of the Amazon River. They ask for very little: warm, clean water, good food, plants to hide among, and a kind keeper who watches over them. In return, they give hours of peaceful beauty and a glimpse of a faraway river right in your own home. Next time you see an angelfish, take a moment to watch it glide, and you will see why it is called the pretty fish of the tank.
