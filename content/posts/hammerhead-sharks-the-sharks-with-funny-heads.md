Imagine a shark with a head shaped like a hammer. Its eyes sit at the far ends of a wide, flat head, and it glides through the sea like a strange, wonderful flying machine. That is the hammerhead shark, one of the most amazing animals in the ocean. Its funny head is not just for show — it is a super tool that helps the shark hunt, see, and sense its food. Let us dive in and meet this marvellous shark.

## What Is a Hammerhead Shark?

A hammerhead shark is a kind of shark that lives in warm oceans all around the world. Its most famous feature is its head, which is wide and flat, with an eye on each end. Scientists call this special head shape a cephalofoil, which is a fancy word for "head wing." But most people just say it looks like a hammer, and the name has stuck.

There are several different kinds of hammerhead sharks. Some are small and slim, while others are big and strong. The biggest kind is called the great hammerhead, and it can grow longer than a family car. The smallest kinds are much shorter, about the length of a young child. But all of them share the same wide, flat head that makes them easy to recognise.

Like all sharks, hammerheads have smooth, grey or brown skin on top and a pale belly underneath. Their bodies are long and sleek, built for swimming fast through the water. They have a tall fin on their back, and a strong tail that pushes them forward with every swish.

## A Head Like No Other

The hammerhead's wide head is full of surprises. First of all, it gives the shark amazing eyesight. Because its eyes are set so far apart, the hammerhead can see a very wide view of the world around it, almost all the way around. It can spot food and danger from many directions at once.

But the head does even more than help the shark see. The wide, flat head is covered in tiny sensors that can feel the small electric signals made by other animals. All living things give off tiny electric pulses, and the hammerhead's head can pick them up. This means a hammerhead can find a fish or a stingray hiding under the sand, even when it cannot see it at all. It is like having a secret sixth sense.

The wide head also works a bit like the wings of an aeroplane. As the shark swims, the flat head helps it turn quickly and sharply. Hammerheads can twist and turn in the water much faster than many other sharks, which helps them catch quick, slippery prey.

## What Do Hammerhead Sharks Eat?

Hammerhead sharks are hunters, and they love to eat fish, squid, crabs, and lobsters. Their very favourite food is the stingray. Stingrays often hide under the sand on the sea floor, but a hammerhead can sense them with its special head sensors. When it finds a stingray, the hammerhead uses its wide head to pin the ray down on the sand, holding it still while it takes a bite.

Hammerheads hunt mostly at dawn and dusk, when many sea animals are active. They swim close to the sea floor, sweeping their wide heads from side to side like a metal detector, searching for hidden food. Their sharp teeth are perfect for gripping slippery fish and crunchy crabs.

Young hammerheads eat smaller food, such as tiny fish and shrimp. As they grow bigger and stronger, they hunt larger prey. A hammerhead's diet changes as it grows, just like a child's diet changes as they get older.

## Swimming in Schools

Many sharks swim alone, but hammerheads are special because they sometimes gather in huge groups called schools. During the day, dozens or even hundreds of hammerhead sharks may swim together in the open sea. They glide in big, slow circles, like a school of giant, grey birds flying underwater.

Scientists think these schools help hammerheads in many ways. Swimming together might help them stay safe from bigger sharks, and it might help young sharks learn from older ones. Some schools gather near underwater mountains or islands, where the water is rich with food.

At night, the school breaks up, and each shark swims off on its own to hunt. Then, when morning comes, they gather together again. It is one of the most amazing sights in the ocean — a whole crowd of hammer-headed sharks, swimming in perfect harmony.

## Hammerheads and People

Hammerhead sharks look a little bit scary, with their wide heads and sharp teeth, but they are not usually dangerous to people. They are shy animals that prefer to swim away from swimmers and divers. Most hammerheads are much more interested in fish and stingrays than in people.

Sadly, hammerhead sharks are in trouble in many parts of the world. People catch them for their fins, which are used to make a soup in some countries, and many hammerheads are also caught by accident in fishing nets. Because hammerheads grow slowly and have few babies, their numbers can fall quickly when too many are caught.

## Fun Facts About Hammerhead Sharks

Here are some fun things to remember about hammerhead sharks. Their eyes are set so far apart that they can see almost all the way around. Their wide heads are full of tiny sensors that can feel the electric signals of hidden prey. Hammerheads sometimes gather in schools of hundreds of sharks. They use their wide heads to pin down stingrays on the sea floor. And a hammerhead's head works a bit like a wing, helping it turn quickly in the water.

The hammerhead shark teaches us that a funny shape can hide a brilliant design. In the big blue ocean, the shark with the hammer head is a true champion.
