Far out in the warm, shallow seas of the world lives a gentle animal that spends its days slowly munching on seagrass. It is called the dugong, and people often call it a sea cow because of the way it grazes peacefully under the water, just as a cow grazes in a meadow.

Dugongs are calm, quiet animals. They do not hunt or chase other creatures. They simply swim slowly through clear, warm water, eating seagrass and looking after their babies. Let us dive into their watery world and meet these gentle giants of the sea.

## What Do They Look Like?

Dugongs have big, smooth, grey-brown bodies that are shaped a bit like a torpedo. Their skin is thick and smooth, and it often has little marks and scratches on it. They have a large, rounded head with a wide, flat snout that points downwards, which is perfect for grazing on seagrass at the bottom of the sea.

Instead of back legs, dugongs have a wide, flat tail that moves up and down, pushing them gently through the water. Their front flippers are short and paddle-shaped, and they use them to steer and to hold on to seagrass while they eat. Dugongs have small eyes and tiny ear holes, and their faces often look as if they are smiling — which makes them look very friendly indeed.

Dugongs are large animals, much bigger than a person, and they can grow to be as long as a small car. Even so, they are gentle and slow-moving, and they would much rather munch on seagrass than do anything else.

## Where Do They Live?

Dugongs live in the warm, shallow waters of the Indian Ocean and the western Pacific Ocean. You can find them in the seas around Australia, East Africa, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. They love warm water, and they stay in shallow coastal areas where seagrass grows.

Seagrass meadows are like underwater gardens, and dugongs spend most of their time swimming slowly over them, grazing as they go. These meadows are found in bays, lagoons, and sheltered places where the water is calm and clear. Dugongs do not like deep, cold water, and they rarely swim far out to sea.

Dugongs are good swimmers, but they are not fast. They move slowly and calmly, and they come up to the surface to breathe air every few minutes, just like whales and dolphins. When they surface, you might see just the top of their head and their back breaking the water before they dive down again.

## What Do They Eat?

Dugongs are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite food is seagrass, and they eat a lot of it every single day. They use their strong, bristly lips to pull up the seagrass, roots and all, and then they chew it slowly.

A dugong spends much of the day grazing. It swims slowly along the bottom of the sea, using its flippers to hold the seagrass steady while it eats. When it has finished in one spot, it moves on to another, leaving a trail of bare patches in the seagrass meadow behind it. This grazing actually helps the seagrass grow back fresh and healthy, a bit like mowing a lawn.

Dugongs need to eat a lot of seagrass to get enough energy, because seagrass is not very rich food. They are patient eaters, and they spend hours each day munching away under the water.

## Family Life Under the Sea

Dugongs are usually quiet, solitary animals, but mothers and their babies stay close together. A baby dugong is called a calf, and it is born underwater. The mother helps her calf to the surface for its first breath, and she looks after it for a long time.

Calves stay with their mothers for a year or more, learning where to find the best seagrass and how to swim safely through the shallow seas. The mother and calf often swim side by side, and the calf will rest on its mother's back or snuggle close to her as they swim. Dugong mothers are very gentle and caring, and they protect their calves from danger.

Dugongs can live for a very long time — many decades — and they grow slowly. A female dugong does not have babies very often, which is one reason why dugongs need to be protected. Every calf is precious.


## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about dugongs:

- Dugongs are sometimes called sea cows because they graze on seagrass like cows graze on grass.
- They are cousins of the manatee, another gentle sea animal.
- Dugongs can live for many decades in the wild.
- They use their strong lips to pull up seagrass, roots and all.
- A dugong's tail moves up and down, not side to side like a fish.
- Dugongs must come up to the surface to breathe air, just like whales.
- Long ago, sailors told stories of mermaids after seeing dugongs in the water — perhaps because of their gentle faces and graceful swimming!

## Why Dugongs Need Our Help

Dugongs need clean, healthy seagrass meadows to survive, and those meadows are in danger. When the sea becomes dirty or the seagrass is damaged, dugongs lose their food and their homes. People are working hard to protect seagrass meadows and to keep the seas clean, so that dugongs can go on grazing peacefully.

Dugongs are gentle, peaceful animals that ask for very little — just warm, clean water and plenty of seagrass to eat. By learning about them and caring for the oceans where they live, we can help make sure that these gentle sea cows keep swimming slowly through the warm, shallow seas for many years to come.

So next time you are near the sea, think of the dugong, grazing quietly below the waves. It is a calm and friendly giant, and it reminds us that the ocean is full of gentle wonders, if we only take the time to look.
