# Manatees: The Gentle Sea Cows

Imagine a huge, grey animal floating slowly through warm, shallow water. It has a round body, a flat paddle-shaped tail, and a face that looks a little bit like a friendly walrus. It does not hunt, it does not bite, and it does not rush anywhere. It just swims gently, eats plants, and floats in the sunshine. This is a manatee, sometimes called a sea cow, one of the gentlest animals in the whole ocean.



## What Is a Manatee?

A manatee is a large sea mammal, which means it breathes air, gives birth to live babies, and feeds its babies with milk, just like a whale or a dolphin. But unlike whales and dolphins, manatees are not hunters. They are plant eaters, and they spend most of their time grazing on sea grass and other water plants, a bit like cows grazing in a field. That is why people call them sea cows.

There are three kinds of manatees. The West Indian manatee lives around Florida, the Caribbean, and the coast of Central and South America. The Amazonian manatee lives in the Amazon River and its streams. The West African manatee lives in the rivers and coastal waters of West Africa. All three kinds look quite alike, with grey or brownish skin, a wrinkled body, and a wide, flat tail that moves slowly up and down to push them through the water.

Manatees are big animals. Grown-up manatees can be very heavy, and they are much longer than a person. But even though they are huge, they are very gentle. They have no teeth for biting or tearing meat. Instead, they have flat teeth at the back of their mouths for grinding plants, and their front teeth are replaced by rough, horny pads that help them grab sea grass.

## The Slowest Swimmers in the Sea

Manatees are famous for being slow. They usually swim at a gentle pace, drifting along in the water as they look for food. If a manatee needs to hurry, it can swim faster for a short time, but most of the time it takes life very slowly. Manatees spend about half of their time asleep under the water, coming up for air every few minutes without even waking up properly.

Manatees need to breathe air, so they come to the surface often. They have two nostrils on the top of their snout, and they close them tightly when they dive under the water. A manatee can hold its breath for a long time, and it can rest on the bottom of a river or the sea floor while it sleeps.

## What Do Manatees Eat?

Manatees eat plants that grow in the water. Their favourite food is sea grass, which grows on the sea floor in shallow, sunny water. They also eat algae, water lettuce, and other soft water plants. A manatee spends much of its day eating, munching through lots of plants. That is why they are sometimes called the lawn mowers of the sea. By eating sea grass, they help keep the sea grass beds healthy, and they help the water stay clean.

Manatees eat with their split upper lip, which works a bit like two hands. They use the two sides of their lip to grab plants and push them into their mouth. It looks funny, but it works very well.

Baby manatees drink their mother's milk for a long time. They stay close to their mothers for a year or more, learning where to find the best sea grass and the warmest water. The bond between a mother manatee and her calf is very strong, and the mother teaches her calf everything it needs to know.


## Family Life of the Sea Cow

Manatees are mostly quiet and peaceful animals. They do not live in big groups like dolphins do. Instead, they usually swim alone or in small groups, and they come together only when they need warm water or good food. Mother manatees are very caring, and they keep their calves close for a long time.

When a baby manatee is born, it is already quite big, but it stays close to its mother, nursing and learning. The calf learns to find sea grass, to come up for air, and to find warm water when the seasons change. After a year or two, the young manatee swims off on its own, ready to live its own slow, gentle life.

Manatees make soft sounds to talk to each other, like chirps, squeaks, and whistles. Mother manatees and their calves use these sounds to stay in touch, especially when the water is murky and they cannot see each other.

## Protecting the Manatees

Manatees face some serious problems. Their homes are being damaged when sea grass beds are destroyed, and when rivers and coastal waters get polluted. Boats are a big danger, and manatees are sometimes caught in fishing nets. Long ago, manatees were also hunted for their meat and oil, but hunting them is now against the law in most places.

People are working hard to protect manatees. In Florida and other places, there are special laws to protect them, and slow-speed zones for boats where manatees swim. Scientists study manatees to learn more about them, and rescue centres help injured manatees get better and return to the wild. By keeping the water clean and protecting sea grass beds, we can help manatees stay safe.

## Fun Facts About Manatees

- Manatees are sometimes called sea cows because they graze on sea grass like cows graze in a field.
- A manatee spends about half of its day asleep under the water.
- Manatees use their flippers like arms, to hold food and hug their babies.
- Their upper lip is split in two, and it works like a pair of hands to grab plants.
- Manatees are closely related to elephants, even though they live in the sea.
- Manatees have no teeth at the front of their mouths, only flat teeth at the back for grinding plants.
- A manatee can hold its breath for a long time while it rests under the water.

Manatees are some of the kindest and most peaceful animals in the sea. With their slow swimming, their love of sea grass, and their gentle nature, they remind us that not all big animals are scary. Next time you think of the ocean, picture a manatee drifting slowly through warm, clear water, munching its lunch and enjoying the sunshine.
