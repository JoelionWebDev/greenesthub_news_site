Out on the sunny savanna of Africa, you might hear a loud bark echo across the grass. It is not a dog. It is a baboon, one of the cleverest and noisiest monkeys in the world. Baboons live in big, busy groups called troops, and they are always on the move. They search for food, play games, groom each other, and keep a careful watch for danger. A baboon troop is a bit like a big, noisy family on a day out, with everyone talking at once.

Baboons have long faces that look a bit like a dog's snout, strong arms, and hands that can grip and hold things. They are smart, brave, and very good at working together. Let us spend a day with a troop and see how these clever monkeys live.

## What Is a Baboon?

A baboon is a large monkey that lives in Africa. Unlike most monkeys, baboons spend a lot of their time on the ground, walking on all fours across the grass and rocks. They have thick fur that can be grey, brown, or olive in colour, and a long, dog-like face with a strong jaw. Their hands and feet look a little like ours, with fingers that can pick up food, peel fruit, and even turn over stones to find insects hiding underneath.

Baboons have long tails, but they do not use them to hang from trees like some other monkeys. Instead, their tails stick up or curve behind them as they walk. Male baboons are bigger than females, and they have long, sharp teeth called canines, which they show when they want to warn others to keep away.

## Life in a Troop

Baboons live in troops, and a troop can be made up of many baboons, from tiny babies to wise old grandmothers. Living in a big group keeps them safe, because there are many eyes watching for lions, leopards, and eagles. When one baboon spots danger, it barks loudly, and the whole troop knows to be careful.

Each troop has a home area where it finds food and water. In the morning, the troop sets off together, walking across the savanna in a long line. The mothers carry their babies, and the young baboons play and tumble as they walk. At night, the troop climbs up into trees or onto high rocks to sleep, where it is safer from animals that hunt in the dark.

## What Do Baboons Eat?

Baboons will eat almost anything they can find. They love fruit, leaves, seeds, and grasses, and they also eat insects, bird eggs, and small animals. They are very good at finding food. A baboon will dig in the ground for roots, turn over stones to find bugs, and peel the skin off fruit with its clever fingers. It can even crack open hard nuts using its strong teeth.

Baboons also learn from each other about where to find the best food. If one baboon finds a tree full of fruit, others will soon join in. Young baboons watch their mothers closely and copy what they do. In this way, each troop learns the best places to eat in its home area, and the knowledge is passed down from one generation to the next.

## Talking With Barks and Faces

Baboons are very chatty animals. They use many different sounds to talk to each other. A loud bark can warn the troop about danger. A soft grunt might mean, "All is well, come this way." Baboons also make lip-smacking sounds when they are feeling friendly, and they scream when they are frightened. A troop of baboons can be very noisy, especially in the morning when everyone is waking up.

Faces are important too. A baboon can show its feelings with its face, just like we can. It might raise its eyebrows to show surprise, or yawn widely to show its big teeth as a warning. Baboons also use their bodies to talk. A baboon that crouches low is showing that it is not a threat, while one that stands tall and puffs out its chest is saying, "I am in charge here."

## Grooming and Friendship

One of the kindest things baboons do is grooming. Baboons sit together and pick through each other's fur, cleaning it and removing tiny bits of dirt and bugs. Grooming keeps them clean, but it is also a way of making friends. Baboons that groom each other often are usually good friends, and they look after each other when times are hard.

Baboon mothers are very loving. A baby baboon rides on its mother's belly at first, holding on tight. Later, it rides on her back, like a little passenger. The mother feeds it, keeps it warm, and teaches it how to behave. Other females in the troop often help to look after the babies too, holding them and playing with them while the mother rests.

## Brave and Clever

Baboons are brave animals. When a lion or a leopard comes near, the male baboons may stand their ground and bark loudly, showing their big teeth. They work together to protect the troop, especially the babies. A group of brave baboons can scare away an animal that is much bigger than any one of them.

Baboons are also very clever. They can remember where food and water are, even after many months. They know which plants are safe to eat and which are not. They can solve problems, such as how to reach food that is hard to get. Some baboons have even learned to open doors and containers when they live near people. Their clever brains help them survive in a world that is always changing.

## Baboons and People

Baboons and people have lived near each other for a very long time. Sometimes baboons visit farms and gardens looking for food, which can cause trouble for farmers. This is why it is important to keep wild animals wild and to give them space to live in their own homes. When baboons have enough wild land, food, and water, they are happy to stay away from towns and farms.

Baboons remind us that being clever is not just about solving puzzles. It is about living together, looking after each other, and working as a team. The next time you picture the African savanna, imagine a troop of baboons marching across the grass, barking, playing, and grooming each other in the warm sunshine. It is a busy, noisy, and wonderful sight, and it shows just how clever these monkeys of the savanna really are.
