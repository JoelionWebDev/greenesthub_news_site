When soldiers go out on a mission, they are not alone. Beside them, walking on a lead or trotting just ahead, is a brave dog. It wears a special vest, it listens carefully, and it uses its amazing nose to sniff out danger. This is a military dog — one of the bravest working dogs in the world.

## Meet the Military Dog

A military dog is a dog that is trained to work with soldiers. Its job is to help keep people safe. Military dogs can sniff out hidden dangers, track people, guard buildings and find things that people cannot see. They work in deserts, forests, mountains and cities, in hot weather and in cold.

The most common military dogs are German Shepherds and Belgian Malinois. These dogs are strong, clever and very loyal. They are also fast learners and they love to work. Other breeds, like Labrador Retrievers and Springer Spaniels, are used too, especially for sniffing work, because they have wonderful noses and gentle natures.

## A Nose Like No Other

The most amazing thing about a military dog is its nose. A dog's sense of smell is far better than a person's. While you might smell a cake baking in the kitchen, a dog could smell every single ingredient in the cake — the flour, the eggs, the sugar and the butter — all separately.

Military dogs use this superpower to find hidden dangers. They are trained to sniff out explosives, which are dangerous materials that can blow up. The dog learns the smell of many different kinds of explosives, and when it finds one, it sits down quietly to tell its handler. The dog does not bark or scratch — it sits still, because that is the safest way to say "I found something."

## Training to Be a Hero

Training is like a game. The dog learns to follow commands — sit, stay, come, heel — and to walk calmly beside its handler. Then it learns harder skills, like searching a building for hidden objects, jumping over obstacles and staying calm when there is a lot of noise. The dog is rewarded with treats, toys and praise every time it does well. Dogs love to work for rewards, and they are happiest when they have a job to do.

Training takes many months, and it never really stops. Even when a military dog is working, it practises every day with its handler. The two of them learn to understand each other so well that the handler can tell what the dog is thinking just by watching its ears and tail.

## The Handler and the Dog

The bond between a military dog and its handler is very special. The handler is the dog's trainer, its partner and its best friend. They spend almost every hour of the day together. The handler feeds the dog, brushes its coat, checks its paws and plays with it. The dog, in turn, trusts the handler completely.

On a mission, the dog walks ahead of the soldiers, sniffing the ground and the air. If the dog stops and sits, the soldiers know to stop too — there may be danger ahead. The dog's nose keeps the whole team safe. Soldiers say that they trust the dog with their lives, and the dog trusts them too.

## A Famous Hero Dog

One of the most famous military dogs in recent years was a Belgian Malinois called Conan. Conan worked with American soldiers, and he helped them find a dangerous man who was hiding in a tunnel. Conan was hurt during the mission, but he was brave and he recovered. People all over the world heard his story, and he became a hero. He was even given a special visit to meet important leaders, who thanked him for his service.

Another famous military dog was a German Shepherd called Lucca, who worked with American soldiers in Afghanistan. Lucca sniffed out many hidden explosives and kept her team safe on hundreds of missions. She was injured in an explosion and lost one of her front legs, but she survived and was given a medal for bravery. She later lived happily in retirement with her handler's family.

## Life After Service

Military dogs do not work forever. When a dog gets older, or when it is hurt, it retires from service. Retirement is a happy time. Many retired military dogs go to live with their handlers, who adopt them as family pets. After years of working together, the dog and the handler stay together for the rest of the dog's life.

Some retired dogs are adopted by other loving families. Animal groups help find them good homes, where they can sleep on a soft bed, play in a garden and enjoy a quiet life. A retired military dog still loves to work and play, but it can relax and enjoy being a pet at last.

In some countries, retired military dogs are given medals and ceremonies to thank them for their service. People stand and clap as the dog walks past, wearing its vest one last time. It is a way of saying thank you to a brave friend.

## Fun Facts About Military Dogs
- A dog's sense of smell is many thousands of times better than a person's.
- The most common military dogs are German Shepherds and Belgian Malinois.
- Military dogs are trained with treats, toys and praise — never with fear.
- Dogs carried messages across battlefields in the First World War, saving many lives.
- A military dog learns to sit quietly when it finds danger, so it does not set anything off.

Military dogs remind us that bravery comes in all shapes and sizes — sometimes with four legs, a wagging tail and a nose that can smell danger before anyone else. The next time you see a dog in a vest, working calmly beside its handler, remember: that dog is a hero, and it is proud to serve.
