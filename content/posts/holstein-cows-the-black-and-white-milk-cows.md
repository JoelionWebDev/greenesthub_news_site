When you picture a cow in your mind, what do you see? For most people, the answer is a big black-and-white cow standing in a green field. That cow is almost certainly a Holstein! Holsteins are the most famous dairy cows in the world, and they give us more milk than any other kind of cow.

Holsteins are gentle, friendly animals, and they are a very important part of farm life. They give us milk for drinking, and milk is used to make cheese, butter, yoghurt, and ice cream too. Let us visit the farm and meet these wonderful black-and-white cows.

## What Do They Look Like?

Holstein cows are easy to spot. They are large cows with a striking black-and-white coat. Each Holstein has its own special pattern of black and white patches, a bit like a fingerprint — no two Holsteins look exactly alike! Some are mostly black with white patches, and others are mostly white with black patches.

They have big, soft eyes, long eyelashes, and large ears. Their noses are wide and wet, and they have a friendly, calm look about them. Holsteins are big animals, much taller than a person at the shoulder, and they have strong, sturdy legs.

Female Holsteins, called cows, have large udders underneath them, where their milk is stored. Male Holsteins, called bulls, are even bigger and stronger. Both cows and bulls have a calm, gentle nature, and they are usually very friendly with the farmers who look after them.

## Where Do They Live?

Holsteins originally come from a place called Friesland, in the Netherlands, a country in Europe. Long ago, farmers there bred cows that gave lots of milk, and over time these cows became known as Holsteins. Today, Holstein cows live on farms all over the world, in almost every country where people keep dairy cows.

On the farm, Holsteins live in fields and barns. In the daytime, they graze in green pastures, munching on grass and enjoying the fresh air. At night, or when the weather is cold or wet, they rest in a barn, where they have dry straw to lie on and plenty of food and water.

Farmers look after their Holsteins very carefully. They make sure the cows have fresh water, good food, and a clean, comfortable place to sleep. A happy, healthy cow gives good milk, so farmers do their best to keep their cows comfortable and content.

## What Do They Eat?

Holsteins are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite food is grass, and they spend many hours each day grazing in the fields. They also eat hay, which is dried grass, and silage, which is grass that has been stored in a special way to keep it fresh.

Farmers also give Holsteins other foods, like grains and special cow feed, to help them stay healthy and produce lots of milk. They always have fresh water to drink, and a cow can drink a great deal of water every day — a whole bathtub full, or even more!

Cows have a very special way of eating. They have four stomachs, and they chew their food twice! First, they swallow their grass quickly, without chewing it very much. Then, later, when they are resting, they bring the food back up into their mouths and chew it again, slowly and carefully. This second chewing is called chewing the cud, and it helps the cow to get all the goodness out of its food. If you watch a cow lying in a field, you may see its jaw moving slowly from side to side — it is chewing its cud!

## How Do Holsteins Give Us Milk?

Female Holsteins give milk after they have had a calf. Farmers milk their cows every day, usually twice a day — once in the morning and once in the evening. The milk is collected in a clean, cool tank, and then it is taken away to be made into all the dairy foods we enjoy.

Holsteins are famous for giving a lot of milk — more than any other kind of cow. A single Holstein can give enough milk each day to fill many large bottles! This is why farmers all over the world love keeping Holsteins. They are the queens of the dairy farm.

The milk from Holsteins is used to make so many things: fresh milk for drinking, cheese, butter, yoghurt, cream, and ice cream. So next time you enjoy a glass of milk or a scoop of ice cream, you can thank a Holstein cow!

## Family Life on the Farm

A baby cow is called a calf. When a calf is born, it is small and wobbly, but it soon learns to stand up and walk. Calves drink their mother's milk, and they stay close to her for the first part of their lives. As they grow, they start to eat grass and hay, just like the grown-up cows.

Calves are playful animals. They love to run around the field, jump, and play with each other. They are curious too, and they will come up to the fence to see who is visiting. Farmers look after the calves carefully, making sure they have warm, dry places to sleep and plenty of good food.

Holsteins are social animals, and they like to be with other cows. They form friendships in the herd, and they often graze and rest together with their favourite friends. They are calm, gentle animals, and they are happiest when they are with their herd in a peaceful, green field.

## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about Holstein cows:

- No two Holsteins have the same pattern of black and white patches.
- Holsteins give more milk than any other kind of cow in the world.
- Cows have four stomachs and chew their food twice — the second time is called chewing the cud.
- A Holstein cow can drink a huge amount of water every day.
- Holsteins originally come from the Netherlands, in Europe.
- Cows are very social and have best friends in the herd.
- The milk from Holsteins is used to make cheese, butter, yoghurt, and ice cream.
