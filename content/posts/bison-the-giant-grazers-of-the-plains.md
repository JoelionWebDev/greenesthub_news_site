Bison are some of the biggest land animals in North America, and they are truly amazing to see. A bison looks like a giant, shaggy cow with a huge hump on its shoulders, a big head, and two short, curved horns. Long ago, millions of bison roamed the great plains in herds so large that people said the ground shook when they ran. Today, bison live in national parks and grasslands, where visitors can watch them graze peacefully in the sunshine. Even though they are huge and strong, bison are gentle plant-eaters that spend most of their day munching grass.

## What is a bison?

A bison is a large mammal and a close cousin of the cow. It has a massive head, a big hump of muscle over its shoulders, and a thick coat of shaggy brown fur. The fur is darker and longer on its head, neck, and shoulders, which makes the front of the animal look even bigger. Bison have short, curved horns that both males and females grow. A bison's body is very heavy, and its back legs are smaller than its front legs, which gives it a sloping shape. When a bison is born, it is called a calf, and it has reddish-orange fur, which is why people sometimes call baby bison "red dogs."

## Where do bison live?

Bison live in North America, in the wide open grasslands called the Great Plains, as well as in valleys, river plains, and mountain meadows. They like open spaces with lots of grass to eat and room to roam. Long ago, bison lived across much of North America, from Canada all the way down to Mexico. Today, most wild bison live in protected places such as Yellowstone National Park in the United States and Wood Buffalo National Park in Canada. Their thick fur is like a warm winter coat, and in spring they shed the old fur in big patches, which birds sometimes collect to line their nests.

## What do bison eat?

Bison are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite food is grass, and they spend most of the day grazing, pulling up mouthfuls of grass with their strong tongues. Bison also eat leaves, twigs, and small shrubs when grass is hard to find, especially in winter when snow covers the ground. A bison has a very big stomach with four parts, which helps it digest tough grass. Like cows, bison chew their food, swallow it, and then bring it back up to chew it again. This is called chewing the cud, and it helps the bison get all the goodness out of the grass. In winter, a bison uses its huge head like a snowplough, sweeping snow aside to reach the grass underneath. 

## Life in a herd

Bison live together in herds, and living in a group helps keep them safe. A herd can be made up of mothers and their calves, while older males often live in smaller groups or on their own. When danger comes, the bison in a herd gather close together, with the calves in the middle where they are safest. Bison can run very fast when they need to, much faster than you might expect for such a big animal, and they can also jump high and swim across rivers. In the summer, bison like to roll around in dry, dusty patches of ground. This is called a wallow, and rolling in the dust helps them scratch itchy skin, get rid of biting insects, and shed their old winter fur. 

## The giant herds of long ago

Long ago, the plains of North America were filled with bison. Travellers told stories of herds that stretched as far as the eye could see, with so many animals that it took days for the whole herd to pass by. But in the 1800s, far too many bison were hunted, and their numbers fell very low. At one point, there were only a few hundred wild bison left in the whole of North America, and people worried that they might disappear forever. Luckily, people worked hard to protect them. Parks and reserves were set up, and bison were moved to safe places where they could live and have calves. Slowly, their numbers grew again. 

## Baby bison

A baby bison is called a calf, and it is born in the spring, when the grass is fresh and green. When a calf is born, it is covered in reddish-orange fur, and it is quite small compared to its huge mother. But it grows very fast. Within an hour or two, a newborn calf can stand up and walk, and soon it is running and playing with the other calves in the herd. The mother bison feeds her calf with milk and watches over it carefully. If danger comes near, she will stand between her calf and the danger, ready to protect it. Calves love to run and play, chasing each other across the grass and practising the running and jumping they will need when they grow up. 

## Fun facts about bison

- A bison's hump is made of strong muscle that helps it push snow aside to find grass in winter.
- Baby bison are born with reddish-orange fur, which is why they are sometimes called "red dogs."
- Bison can run much faster than a person, even though they are very big and heavy.
- Bison like to roll in dusty patches called wallows to scratch itches and chase away biting insects.
- Long ago, herds of bison were so large that they stretched as far as the eye could see.

Bison are gentle giants of the grasslands, with shaggy coats, big humps, and kind, calm ways. They remind us of a time when the plains were full of wild animals, and of how people can help bring animals back from the edge of danger. Next time you picture the great plains, imagine a herd of bison grazing in the golden grass.
