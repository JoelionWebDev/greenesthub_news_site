Ostriches are amazing birds. They are the tallest and heaviest birds in the world, and they cannot fly. Instead, they run. An ostrich can run very fast on its long, strong legs, and it uses its big wings to help it balance and turn. Ostrich farmers are the people who raise these giant birds, and their days are busy, dusty, and full of fun.

Ostriches come from Africa, where they live on open grasslands called savannas. In the wild, they wander in groups, pecking at grass, seeds, and leaves, and keeping watch for danger. On a farm, ostriches live in large open fields with fences, shelters, and fresh water. Farmers try to give them plenty of space to run, just as they would have in the wild.

An ostrich farmer's day starts early. The first job is to walk around the farm and check every bird. Farmers look at the ostriches carefully. Are they eating well? Are they walking normally? Do their feathers look clean and healthy? Ostriches cannot tell us how they feel, so farmers learn to watch how they move and behave.

## Breakfast for a Giant Bird

Ostriches eat plants. They love grass, leaves, seeds, and flowers, and they also eat small stones. The stones might sound strange, but they help the ostrich grind up its food inside its stomach, because birds do not have teeth. Farmers give their ostriches fresh feed every day, along with clean water and green plants to peck at.

Farmers also scatter feed on the ground so the ostriches can peck and scratch, just as they would in the wild. Watching an ostrich eat is funny. It stretches its long neck down, picks up a leaf or a seed, and tosses its head back to swallow. Its eyes are very large, and its long eyelashes help keep dust out on a windy day.

Young ostriches are called chicks, and they need extra care. A chick is small and fluffy when it hatches, with stripes on its back that help it hide in the grass. Farmers keep chicks in a warm, safe place at first, with heat lamps and soft bedding. They feed them special chick food and fresh water, and they check on them many times a day.

As chicks grow, they get taller very quickly. Their legs grow long and strong, and their necks stretch up higher and higher. Farmers move them to bigger pens where they can run and play. Ostrich chicks love to run around together, chasing each other and flapping their little wings. It looks like a playground full of tall, feathery children.

## The Giant Egg

One of the most wonderful things about ostriches is their eggs. An ostrich egg is the biggest egg laid by any bird in the world. It is much bigger than a chicken egg, with a thick, strong shell. Farmers collect the eggs carefully every day and keep them safe.

Some eggs are kept for hatching. Farmers place them in a warm machine called an incubator, which keeps them at just the right temperature, like a mother bird sitting on her nest. The farmer turns the eggs gently and checks them often. After many weeks, a chick starts to peck its way out of the shell. It is a slow and tiring job for the chick, and farmers watch with excitement as the new baby arrives.

Other eggs may be sold as food, because one ostrich egg can feed a whole family. Farmers also use empty shells for crafts and decoration, because they are so big and strong. Nothing is wasted on a good farm.

## Keeping Ostriches Safe and Happy

Ostriches are strong, but they still need protection. Farmers build tall, strong fences to keep the birds safe inside the farm and to keep other animals out. They also build shelters where ostriches can rest in the shade on a hot day or stay dry when it rains.

Farmers check the fences and gates every day. An ostrich is a curious bird, and it may peck at a fence or try to push through a gate. A good farmer fixes any loose wire or broken post before it becomes a problem. Safety comes first, for the birds and for the people who work with them.

Ostriches can be shy, but they can also be bold. A farmer moves slowly and calmly around the birds, talking in a soft voice. Ostriches learn to trust the farmer who feeds them every day. Some farmers even give their favourite ostriches names, and they know which bird likes to run first and which one likes to sit in the shade.

If an ostrich gets sick or hurt, the farmer calls a vet, an animal doctor, who comes to help. The farmer helps keep the bird calm while the vet checks it over. Farmers also write down notes about each bird, so they can spot a problem early and keep the whole flock healthy.

## Why Ostrich Farmers Matter

Ostrich farming helps people in many ways. Ostriches give us eggs and meat, and their feathers have been used for decoration for a very long time. Their strong, soft leather is used to make shoes and bags. Farmers who raise ostriches well show that we can use what animals give us while still treating them with kindness.

Ostrich farmers also teach visitors about these wonderful birds. Many farms welcome school groups and families, who come to see the tall birds, watch them run, and learn how they live. Children love to see an ostrich egg up close and to watch a chick take its first wobbly steps.

If you ever visit an ostrich farm, remember to be calm and gentle. Do not chase the birds or shout near them. Listen to the farmer, stay behind the fence, and watch how these giant birds move, eat, and play. You will see why farmers love their work.

Ostrich farmers show us that farming is not just about cows and chickens. It can be about the biggest birds on Earth, with long necks, fast legs, and giant eggs. It takes hard work, patience, and a love of wide open spaces. Every morning, the farmer opens the gate, fills the feeders, and checks on a flock of tall, feathery friends who are ready to run into a brand new day.
