Rabbits are some of the softest animals you can ever meet. They have fluffy fur, long ears, big eyes and a tiny tail that looks like a ball of cotton wool. Some rabbits live in homes as pets, and some live in the wild, in fields and gardens. Wherever they live, rabbits are gentle, clever and full of fun. Let us hop into the world of rabbits and find out what makes them so special.

## What is a rabbit?

A rabbit is a small animal with soft fur, long ears and strong back legs. Rabbits belong to a family of animals called lagomorphs, and their close cousins are hares and pikas. A baby rabbit is called a kit, a female rabbit is called a doe, and a male rabbit is called a buck. Rabbits come in many colours. Some are white, some are black, some are brown, and some have spots or patches of different colours.

Rabbits are not rodents, even though many people think they are. They are their own special kind of animal. One easy way to tell a rabbit from a rodent is to look at its teeth. Rabbits have extra small teeth behind their big front teeth.

## Those amazing long ears

A rabbit's ears are one of the best things about it. The ears are long, soft and full of tiny blood vessels. They do much more than just help the rabbit hear. Rabbits can move each ear on its own, turning them this way and that to listen for sounds. They can hear a sound from far away, even a very quiet one, and they know which way it came from.

The ears also help a rabbit stay cool. When a rabbit gets hot, warm blood flows into its ears, and the heat escapes into the air. It is like having two built-in fans on top of its head. Some rabbits have ears that stand straight up, and some have ears that flop down beside their faces. Floppy-eared rabbits are called lops, and they look very sweet.

## Fast feet and big jumps

Rabbits may look soft and slow, but they are very fast. Their back legs are long and strong, and they can push off the ground with great power. A rabbit can run fast when it needs to escape from danger, and it can zigzag from side to side to confuse anything chasing it.

Rabbits are also wonderful jumpers. They can jump high into the air and far across the ground. When a pet rabbit is happy, it sometimes does a special jump called a binky. A binky is when a rabbit jumps up in the air and twists its body and kicks its feet, all at the same time. It is one of the happiest things you can ever see.

## What do rabbits eat?

Rabbits are herbivores, which means they eat plants and never eat meat. Their favourite food is hay. Hay is dried grass, and rabbits need to eat a lot of it every day. Hay keeps their tummies healthy and keeps their teeth worn down to the right size.

Rabbits also love fresh leafy greens, like lettuce, spinach and carrot tops. They enjoy a few vegetables too, such as carrots and broccoli, but only in small amounts. Many people think rabbits eat lots of carrots, but carrots are sweet, so they are more like a treat than a main meal. Rabbits also need clean, fresh water every day. A healthy rabbit eats all day long, little by little, and it spends a lot of time chewing.

## A rabbit's home and family

Wild rabbits live in groups and dig burrows underground. A burrow is a tunnel home with many rooms and entrances. It keeps rabbits safe from bad weather and from animals that might want to catch them. The tunnels connect to each other, so rabbits can move around underground without going outside. A group of wild rabbits that live together is called a warren.

## How rabbits talk without words

Rabbits do not bark or meow, but they still have a lot to say. They talk with their bodies. When a rabbit is happy and relaxed, it may lie down flat with its legs stretched out behind it. When it is very happy, it does a binky, that joyful twist in the air. When a rabbit is scared or feels danger, it thumps one of its back feet on the ground, hard. The thump is a warning to other rabbits that something might be wrong.

A rabbit's nose is always wiggling. The wiggle helps the rabbit smell the air and learn what is around it. When a rabbit wiggles its nose very fast, it is busy sniffing and thinking. Rabbits also grind their teeth softly when they are content, a little like a cat purring. But if a rabbit grinds its teeth loudly, it may be in pain, and that is a sign to get help from a vet.

## Looking after a rabbit kindly

Rabbits make lovely pets, but they need kind and patient care. If your family has a rabbit, or you hope to have one, here are some gentle rules. Always be calm and quiet around a rabbit, and never chase it or grab it suddenly. Pick a rabbit up carefully, with one hand under its bottom and one hand supporting its chest, and hold it close to your body so it feels safe. Never pick a rabbit up by its ears. That hurts a lot.

Give your rabbit plenty of hay every day, fresh water, and a clean home. Clean the hutch often so it stays fresh and dry. Brush a long-haired rabbit gently so its fur does not get tangled. Spend time with your rabbit every day, sitting quietly near it and letting it sniff you. Rabbits live for many years, so keeping one is a big promise. A rabbit is not a toy. It is a living friend that needs food, care and love every single day.

## Fun facts about rabbits

- A rabbit's teeth never stop growing, so it must chew hay all the time to keep them the right size.
- Rabbits can see almost all the way around themselves without turning their heads, which helps them spot danger.
- A happy rabbit does a binky — it jumps in the air and twists its body with joy.
- A group of rabbits that live together underground is called a warren.
- Baby rabbits are called kits, and they are born with their eyes closed and no fur.
