Chameleons are some of the most amazing lizards in the world. They can change the colour of their skin, move their eyes in two different directions at the same time, and shoot out a tongue that is longer than their whole body to catch insects. Chameleons move slowly and carefully through trees and bushes, gripping branches with their curly tails and special feet. They look like tiny dragons from a storybook, with bumpy skin and a gentle, curious face.

## What is a chameleon?

A chameleon is a kind of lizard, and there are many different kinds of chameleons in the world. Most chameleons are small enough to fit in your hand, but some are as long as your arm. They have skin that feels a little bumpy, and many have a crest or a horn on their head. Chameleons have long, curly tails that they use like an extra hand to hold onto branches. Their toes are joined together in groups, like little mittens, so they can grip thin branches very tightly. Chameleons are quiet, calm animals. They do not rush around. Instead, they move slowly, one careful step at a time, swaying gently like a leaf in the wind.

## Where do chameleons live?

Most chameleons live in warm places, and many of them live on the island of Madagascar, off the coast of Africa. Others live in other parts of Africa, in southern Europe, and in parts of Asia. Chameleons love trees and bushes. They spend almost all of their life up in the branches, where they can hide among the leaves. Some live in rainforests, where it is warm and wet, and others live in drier places with smaller trees and bushes. A chameleon needs a warm home because it is cold-blooded, which means its body warms up in the sun and cools down in the shade.

## How do chameleons change colour?

Chameleons are famous for changing colour, but they do not change colour just to hide. A chameleon changes colour to show how it feels, to warm up or cool down, and to talk to other chameleons. When it is calm, it may be green or brown, the colours of leaves and bark. When it is excited or wants to look big and strong, it may turn bright yellow, orange, or red. A cold chameleon may turn darker to soak up more sunshine, and a hot chameleon may turn lighter to stay cool. Under the skin, a chameleon has special colour cells that can grow bigger or smaller, and that is what makes the colours change.

## The eyes of a chameleon

A chameleon has the most wonderful eyes of any lizard. Its eyes sit in little round turrets on the sides of its head, and each eye can move on its own. That means a chameleon can look forwards with one eye and backwards with the other eye at the same time. It can watch for food in front of it while also watching out for danger behind it. When it spots a tasty insect, both eyes turn to look at it together, so it can judge exactly how far away the insect is. Chameleons have very good eyesight, and they can see small insects from far away.

## What do chameleons eat?

Chameleons eat insects, and they are very good hunters. They like to eat crickets, grasshoppers, flies, and other small insects that live in trees and bushes. A chameleon sits very still on a branch and waits. Its eyes scan the leaves until it spots an insect. Then, in a flash, it shoots out its tongue. A chameleon's tongue can be longer than its whole body, and the tip is sticky, like glue. The tongue grabs the insect and pulls it back into the chameleon's mouth in less time than it takes to blink. Chameleons do not need to chase their food. Their amazing tongue does all the hard work for them.

## A day in a chameleon's life

A chameleon's day starts with sunshine. It wakes up in the trees and basks in the warm morning light. Then it starts to move slowly through the branches, looking for food, taking one careful step at a time and gripping each branch with its feet and tail. If it feels scared, it may freeze and stay very still, or it may puff itself up to look bigger. Chameleons like to be alone, and each one has its own favourite tree or bush. At night, a chameleon sleeps curled up on a branch, holding on tight with its tail.

## Baby chameleons

Baby chameleons are called hatchlings. Most mother chameleons lay eggs in a safe, soft place, often digging a little hole in the ground. She lays her eggs there and covers them up, and then she leaves them to grow on their own. Inside each egg, a tiny chameleon grows until it is ready to hatch. When it hatches, it is a tiny copy of its parents, with the same big eyes, curly tail, and clever feet. The baby chameleon must look after itself from the very start. It climbs up into the trees, hunts tiny insects, and learns to change colour, just like the grown-ups.

## Fun facts about chameleons

- A chameleon's tongue can be longer than its whole body, and it can shoot out in the blink of an eye.
- Each of a chameleon's eyes can move on its own, so it can look in two directions at once.
- Chameleons change colour to show their feelings, to warm up or cool down, and to talk to each other.
- A chameleon's tail is curly and strong, and it uses it like an extra hand to grip branches.
- Most chameleons live in Madagascar and other warm parts of Africa, high up in trees and bushes.

Chameleons are gentle, clever, and full of surprises. They may move slowly, but their eyes, their colour-changing skin, and their super-long tongue make them some of the most wonderful lizards in the world. Next time you see a picture of a chameleon, look closely at its eyes and its curly tail, and imagine it sitting quietly in a green tree, waiting for lunch to fly by.
