Imagine a tiny creature, no bigger than your little finger, that can make one of the most beautiful and expensive materials in the world. It does not need a factory or a machine. It just uses its own body. Meet the silkworm — the tiny maker of silk.

Silk is a soft, shiny cloth that people have loved for thousands of years. Kings and queens wore silk clothes. Today, silk is still used to make scarves, dresses, ties, and beautiful fabrics. And it all starts with a little caterpillar munching on leaves.

## What Is a Silkworm?

A silkworm is not really a worm at all. It is the caterpillar of the silk moth. A caterpillar is the young form of a moth or butterfly, before it changes into its adult form.

When a silkworm first hatches from its egg, it is tiny — about the size of a small ant. It is dark in colour and covered in tiny hairs. But it does not stay small for long. A silkworm grows very fast, and it spends almost all its time eating.

Silkworms eat only one thing: the leaves of the mulberry tree. Mulberry leaves are soft and green, and silkworms love them. A silkworm can eat a huge amount of leaves every day. As it eats, it grows bigger and bigger, and its skin becomes too tight. So the silkworm sheds its old skin and grows a new, bigger one. This is called moulting, and a silkworm moults several times as it grows.

After a few weeks of eating and growing, a full-grown silkworm is about as long as your finger and as thick as a pencil. Its body is creamy white or pale yellow, smooth and soft. It is ready for the most amazing part of its life.

## The Amazing Cocoon

When a silkworm is ready to change, it stops eating and looks for a quiet, safe place. Then it begins to spin. From a tiny opening near its mouth, the silkworm squeezes out a thin thread of liquid silk. As soon as the thread touches the air, it hardens into a fine, strong fibre.

The silkworm moves its head in figure-of-eight patterns, wrapping the thread around and around itself. It spins and spins, hour after hour, day after day. Slowly, a soft oval case forms around the silkworm. This case is called a cocoon.

Inside the cocoon, the silkworm is safe and warm. The cocoon is made from one single thread of silk, and that thread can be amazingly long — hundreds of metres if it is carefully unwound. All that silk comes from one tiny caterpillar!

But the silkworm is not just hiding inside its cocoon. Something wonderful is happening in there. The caterpillar is changing its whole body. This change is called metamorphosis, and it is one of nature's greatest magic tricks.

## From Caterpillar to Moth

Inside the cocoon, the silkworm turns into a pupa. A pupa is the resting stage between caterpillar and moth. Inside the pupa, the caterpillar's body slowly changes shape. Its legs disappear. Wings begin to grow. Its whole body is rebuilt into a completely different animal.

After about two weeks, the change is complete. The pupa has become a silk moth. The moth is soft and furry, with creamy white wings and a plump body. It is a gentle, quiet creature that does not fly very far. In fact, silk moths that have been kept by people for thousands of years can barely fly at all.

The moth breaks out of the cocoon and looks for a mate. Soon after, the female moth lays tiny eggs — hundreds of them — on the leaves of a mulberry tree or in a safe place nearby. Then, after a short life of just a few days, the moth dies. But the eggs will hatch into new silkworms, and the whole cycle begins again.

## The Story of Silk

People discovered silk a very long time ago in China, thousands of years ago. According to an old story, a Chinese empress was sitting under a mulberry tree drinking tea when a cocoon fell into her cup. As she pulled it out, the cocoon began to unwind into a long, shiny thread. She realised this thread could be woven into beautiful cloth.

For a very long time, China kept the secret of silk all to itself. Nobody outside China knew that silk came from a little caterpillar. People in other countries paid a lot of money for silk cloth, and traders carried it along long roads across Asia to Europe. These roads became known as the Silk Road, and they were some of the most famous trade routes in history.

To make silk cloth, people carefully unwind the thread from the cocoon. The cocoons are placed in warm water, which softens the silk and makes it easier to unwind. Then the long thread is wound onto a reel, like winding thread onto a spool. Several threads are twisted together to make a stronger yarn, which is woven on a loom into cloth. Silk cloth is smooth, soft, and shiny. That is why people have loved it for thousands of years.

## Fun Facts

- A single silkworm cocoon is made from one unbroken thread of silk that can be hundreds of metres long.
- Silkworms only eat mulberry leaves. They will not eat anything else!
- The silk moth cannot eat. It lives for only a few days, just long enough to lay eggs.
- Silk has been made in China for thousands of years, and for a long time the secret of how to make it was kept hidden from the rest of the world.
- The roads that carried silk from China to Europe are called the Silk Road.

## Why We Love Silkworms

Silkworms remind us that even the tiniest creatures can do amazing things. A little caterpillar, munching quietly on a leaf, makes a thread so fine and beautiful that people have treasured it for thousands of years. Then it changes completely, from a crawling caterpillar into a soft, white moth.

The next time you see something made of silk — a scarf, a ribbon, or a piece of shiny cloth — think of the tiny silkworm that made it. A small caterpillar with a big talent, spinning its magic, one thread at a time.
