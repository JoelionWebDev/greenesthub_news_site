Imagine a horse so black and shiny that it looks like it has been polished. Its mane flows like silk, its tail sweeps almost to the ground, and when it moves, it lifts its legs high, as if it is dancing. That is a Friesian horse, one of the most beautiful horses in the whole world. People stop and stare whenever a Friesian walks by.

Friesian horses come from Friesland, a place in the Netherlands, a country in Europe. They have lived there for a very long time, and people in Friesland are very proud of them. The Friesian is one of the oldest horse breeds in Europe, and for hundreds of years it has been loved for its beauty, its strength, and its gentle heart.

## A Coat as Black as Night

The first thing you notice about a Friesian is its colour. Friesian horses are black, with a coat so dark and glossy that it shines in the sunlight. They have no white markings on their body, which makes them look very grand and elegant. When a Friesian stands in a field, its black coat stands out against the green grass like a picture.

Friesians also have very long, thick manes and tails. Their manes often fall to one side of the neck in beautiful waves, and their tails are so long and full that they nearly touch the ground. Owners spend a lot of time brushing and braiding the mane and tail to keep them looking their best. A Friesian with a plaited mane looks ready for a royal parade.

Another special feature of the Friesian is the long hair on its lower legs, called feathering. This soft, silky hair covers the horse's legs from the knee down to the hoof, like a pair of fluffy boots. The feathering makes the Friesian look even more splendid, especially when it is trotting along with its legs held high.

## Strong, Graceful and Proud

Friesians are big, strong horses with a powerful build. They have a long, arched neck that they hold high, a deep chest, and strong legs. When a Friesian moves, it does not just walk. It trots with high, proud steps, lifting its knees up and floating along the ground. This high-stepping trot is one of the things Friesians are famous for.

Long ago, Friesian horses were used to carry knights in armour, because they were strong enough for the job and calm enough to trust in battle. Later, farmers in Friesland used them to pull carts and ploughs, and to carry people from place to place. Friesians have always been working horses as well as beautiful ones.

Today, Friesians are still strong, but they are mostly loved for their grace and their gentle ways. They are not racehorses, and they are not built for speed. Instead, they are built for beauty, for pulling carriages, and for graceful riding displays, where horses perform elegant movements like dancers.

## Gentle Giants with Kind Hearts

Even though Friesians are big horses, they are known for being gentle and kind. They are calm, patient, and friendly with people, and they love attention. Many Friesians will lower their great head to nuzzle a child, or stand quietly while someone brushes their mane. They are often called gentle giants, and the name fits them well.

Friesians are also clever and eager to learn. They enjoy training and like to please their owners. Because they are calm and willing, they are good horses for riders who are learning, as long as an adult is there to help. A Friesian that trusts its owner will try its best to do what is asked.

Friesians form strong bonds with the people who care for them. They recognise their owners and come to greet them in the field. They enjoy being groomed, petted, and talked to. A Friesian is not just a horse; it is a friend that loves company and kindness.

## Friesians at Work and Play

Today you can see Friesians in many places. They pull carriages at weddings and parades, looking splendid with their shiny black coats and flowing manes. They perform in shows, where they trot and dance in time with music. They also appear in films, because they look like the horses from fairy tales and old stories about knights and kings.

In the Netherlands, Friesians are part of the country's history and pride. There are special clubs and shows just for Friesian horses, where breeders bring their best horses to be judged. People travel from all over the world to see them. A Friesian show is a wonderful sight, with rows of black horses gleaming in the sun.

Friesians also enjoy quiet days in the field, grazing on grass and resting in the shade. Like all horses, they love to roll in the dust, run with their friends, and munch on hay. They are herd animals, which means they like to live with other horses and feel safest when they are together.

## Looking After a Friesian

Friesian horses need a lot of care, just like all horses. They need a large field or stable, plenty of fresh grass and hay, and clean water every day. Their long manes, tails, and feathering need regular brushing to keep them clean and free of tangles. Grooming a Friesian takes time, but it is also a lovely way to spend time with the horse.

Friesians need exercise too. They enjoy being ridden, driven in a carriage, or simply led around the yard. They also need visits from the horse doctor and the farrier, the person who looks after their hooves. Good care keeps a Friesian healthy, happy, and looking its very best.

Friesian horses remind us that animals can be both strong and gentle, both grand and kind. They are horses from long ago that still steal the show today. If you ever see a Friesian horse, with its black coat shining and its mane flowing in the wind, take a moment to watch. You will be looking at one of the most beautiful horses in the world.
