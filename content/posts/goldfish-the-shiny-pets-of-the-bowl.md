Swimming in a bowl or a pond, shining like a little piece of gold, the goldfish is one of the most famous pets in the world. Children have kept goldfish for hundreds of years, and these bright orange fish are often the very first pet a child ever has. But goldfish are much more interesting than most people think. They can learn, they can remember, and with good care, they can live for many years. Let us dive in and meet the shiny pets of the bowl!

## From Grey Carp to Golden Beauty

Goldfish did not always look golden. Long, long ago, in China, people kept a dull grey-green fish called the carp in ponds. Carp were kept for food, but every now and then, a baby carp was born with a strange golden or orange colour. People thought these golden fish were beautiful, so they started keeping them as pets instead of eating them. Over hundreds of years, people carefully bred the most colourful fish together, and slowly the goldfish as we know it today appeared.

Goldfish are now found all over the world. They come in many colours — orange, white, black, red, and even calico patterns with patches of different colours. Some goldfish have long, flowing tails that look like fancy dresses. Others have round, chubby bodies. There are many different types, with names like fantail, comet, oranda, and black moor. But all of them are the same kind of fish, just bred to look different.

## A Goldfish's Body

A goldfish has a smooth, shiny body covered in scales. The scales catch the light and make the fish shimmer as it swims. Goldfish have fins that help them steer and balance in the water — fins on their back, their belly, and their tail. Their tails are especially important, because they use them like a rudder to swim and turn.

Goldfish do not have eyelids, so they cannot blink. They sleep with their eyes open, floating quietly near the bottom of the tank or pond. Goldfish breathe underwater through gills. Gills take oxygen out of the water, just like our lungs take oxygen out of the air. That is why clean, fresh water is so important for goldfish — dirty water does not have enough oxygen for them to breathe properly.

## Do Goldfish Really Forget Everything?

You may have heard that goldfish have a memory of only three seconds. That is not true at all! It is one of the biggest myths about animals. Scientists have discovered that goldfish can remember things for months. They can learn to swim through mazes, they can learn that a certain sound means dinner time, and they can even recognise the person who feeds them.

Many goldfish owners say their fish swim to the front of the tank when they see them coming, because they know food is on the way. That is real learning! Goldfish are clever little animals, and they pay attention to what happens around them.

## What Do Goldfish Eat?

Goldfish are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and small animals. In the wild, they eat tiny water plants, small insects, and little creatures that live in the water. Pet goldfish eat special goldfish flakes or pellets, which contain everything they need to stay healthy. They also enjoy treats like peas, lettuce, and tiny bits of cucumber.

One thing to remember is that goldfish will keep eating as long as there is food. They do not know when to stop! That is why their owners must be careful to give them only a small amount of food at a time. Too much food can make goldfish sick, and leftover food can make the water dirty. A good rule is to give a goldfish only as much food as it can eat in a couple of minutes, once or twice a day.

## Caring for a Goldfish

Goldfish can live for a long time — many years — if they are looked after properly. But they need more than a tiny bowl to be happy. A goldfish grows bigger as it gets older, and it needs plenty of space to swim. A large tank or a garden pond is much better than a small bowl.

Goldfish also need clean water. Their owners must change some of the water regularly and keep the tank clean. A filter helps to keep the water fresh, and plants in the tank give the goldfish places to hide and explore. Goldfish like to have a little gravel at the bottom of the tank and some decorations to swim around.

Goldfish are social animals. They enjoy the company of other goldfish, so it is nice to keep two or more together, as long as the tank is big enough. They will swim around together, explore their tank, and sometimes play by pushing little stones around with their mouths.

## Goldfish in Ponds

In many countries, people keep goldfish in garden ponds. In a pond, goldfish can grow much bigger than they do in a tank, because they have more space. They love to swim among the water plants and hide under lily pads. In winter, when the pond gets cold, goldfish slow down and rest near the bottom. They can survive under a thin layer of ice, as long as there is still some oxygen in the water.

## Why Goldfish Make Great Pets

Goldfish are wonderful first pets for children. They are quiet, they do not need to be walked, and they are beautiful to watch. Watching goldfish swim can be very calming and relaxing. Many people say that watching fish in a tank helps them feel less worried and more peaceful.

Caring for a goldfish also teaches children responsibility. A goldfish needs to be fed every day, its water needs to be kept clean, and it needs to be checked to make sure it is healthy. That is a big job for a small fish — but it is a great way to learn how to look after another living creature.

So the next time you see a goldfish gliding through the water, shining like a jewel, remember: this little fish has a history that goes back hundreds of years, a memory much better than three seconds, and a clever little brain that can recognise the person who feeds it. Goldfish may be small, but they are full of surprises — the shiny pets of the bowl!
