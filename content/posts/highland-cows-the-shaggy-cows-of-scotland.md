High in the cold, windy hills of Scotland lives one of the most amazing cows in the world. It has long, shaggy hair that covers its eyes, a pair of long, curving horns, and a thick, warm coat that keeps it cosy even in snow and rain. Meet the Highland cow, the shaggy cow of Scotland!

Highland cows look like something from a storybook. With their wild hair and gentle eyes, they are one of the most loved cattle breeds on Earth. Let us find out what makes these hairy giants so special.

## A Cow with a Hairstyle

The first thing you notice about a Highland cow is its hair. Highland cows have long, shaggy hair that falls over their forehead and covers their eyes. This hair is called a fringe, and it looks a bit like a fringe haircut!

Underneath the long outer hair, Highland cows have a thick, soft undercoat, like a warm woolly jumper. This double coat keeps them warm in cold weather and dry in the rain. Rain and snow slide right off their long outer hairs, so the cows stay dry underneath. Their coats can be many colours, from reddish-brown and golden to black, grey, and even white.

## Those Magnificent Horns

Highland cows have long horns that curve up and out from their heads. Both male and female Highland cows have horns. The horns of a male cow grow out to the sides and then point up, while the horns of a female cow are usually longer and thinner, curving gracefully upward. In the wild hills of Scotland, Highland cows use their horns to dig through snow to find grass underneath.

Even though the horns look big and strong, Highland cows are gentle animals. They do not use their horns to hurt people. Farmers who raise Highland cows find them calm and friendly, as long as they are treated with kindness.

## Hardy Cows of the Cold Hills

Highland cows come from the Scottish Highlands, a place with cold winters, strong winds, and lots of rain and snow. It is one of the toughest places in Britain for animals to live, but Highland cows are perfectly suited to it.

Their thick coats keep them warm in freezing weather. They are strong, hardy animals that can live outside all year round, even when other cattle need to be brought indoors. They are happy grazing on hillsides where the grass is thin and the wind is strong.

Because they are so hardy, Highland cows are now kept on farms in many cold countries around the world. Farmers love them because they are healthy, tough, and easy to care for, even in difficult weather.

## Gentle Giants

For such big, strong animals, Highland cows are surprisingly gentle. They have calm, kind eyes and a slow, peaceful way of moving. They enjoy the company of people who treat them gently, and many Highland cows love to be stroked and scratched behind the ears.

Mother Highland cows are very caring. They look after their calves closely, keeping them safe and teaching them how to graze on the hillside. The calves stay close to their mothers and learn everything they need to know about being a Highland cow. Highland cows also live in herds, which are groups of cows that graze and rest together.

## What Do Highland Cows Eat?

Highland cows are grazers, which means they eat grass and other plants. They spend most of their day slowly wandering across the hillside, munching on grass, heather, and leaves. They are not fussy eaters and can happily eat rough, tough plants that other cattle would leave behind.

Cows have a special way of eating. They use their long tongues to wrap around tufts of grass and pull them into their mouths. Then they chew their food slowly, over and over again. If you watch a Highland cow closely, you will see its jaw moving in a slow, steady rhythm as it chews.

They also drink plenty of fresh water from streams and ponds. A Highland cow needs water every day to stay healthy, especially in warm weather.

## Life on the Hillside

Highland cows live a quiet, peaceful life. They wake with the sun, graze on the hillsides, and rest in the shade when the day is warm. In the evening, they gather together in the herd and settle down for the night.

They do not rush anywhere. Highland cows move slowly and calmly, and they seem to enjoy simply being outside in the fresh air. Watching a herd of Highland cows on a misty Scottish morning is like looking at a picture from long ago.

## Highland Cow Calves

Baby Highland cows, called calves, are born with soft, fluffy coats. They are smaller than adult cows, of course, but they already have the beginnings of their shaggy hair and tiny horn buds on their heads.

Calves stay close to their mothers, drinking milk and learning how to graze. They are curious and playful, and they love to run and jump around the hillside with other calves. As they grow, their horns get longer and their coats get thicker and shaggier.

It takes time for a Highland calf to grow into a full-sized adult cow with long horns and a thick, flowing coat. But from the very beginning, you can see that a Highland calf is something special.

## Why Highland Cows Are Loved Around the World

Highland cows are loved not just in Scotland, but all over the world. People travel from far away just to see them and take photographs. Their shaggy hair, long horns, and gentle faces make them one of the most photographed animals in the world. Farmers love them because they are hardy, healthy, and easy to keep, even on rough land where other cattle would struggle.

## Say Hello to a Highland Cow

If you ever visit Scotland, or a farm that keeps Highland cows, look for the cow with the fringe covering its eyes and the long, curving horns. Stand quietly and watch it graze. You might see it lift its head, look at you with its calm, gentle eyes, and give its shaggy head a little shake.

Highland cows remind us that you do not need to be smooth and tidy to be beautiful. Sometimes the shaggiest, wildest-looking animals are also the kindest and gentlest. The Highland cow, with its warm coat and friendly heart, is proof of that.
