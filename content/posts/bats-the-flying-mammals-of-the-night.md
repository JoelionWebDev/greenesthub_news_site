## What Is a Bat?

When the sun goes down and most animals are getting ready for sleep, bats are just waking up. A bat is a mammal, just like a dog, a cat, or you! But bats have something no other mammal has: wings that let them truly fly. Other mammals can jump or glide, but only bats can flap their wings and fly through the air all by themselves.

A bat's wing is not like a bird's wing. If you look closely, a bat's wing is really a hand with very long fingers. Thin skin stretches between the fingers, just like the skin between your toes, but much bigger and stretchier. When a bat flies, it moves those long fingers to steer, swoop, and turn in the air. It is a bit like flying with your hands!

There are many, many kinds of bats in the world. Some are tiny, about the size of your thumb. Others are much bigger, with wings that stretch wider than your arms. Bats live almost everywhere on Earth, except in the very coldest places. They live in forests, deserts, cities, and caves. You might even have bats living near your home without knowing it.

## How Bats Fly in the Dark

Bats are mostly active at night. That is when they hunt for food. But how can they fly around in the dark without bumping into trees and walls? They use a clever trick called echolocation.

Here is how it works. A bat makes a high squeaky sound, so high that human ears usually cannot hear it. The sound travels through the air until it hits something, like a tree, a wall, or a tasty insect. Then the sound bounces back to the bat's big ears. By listening to the echo, the bat can tell how far away the thing is, how big it is, and even what shape it is.

It is a bit like shouting in a big empty hall and hearing your voice come back to you. The bat's brain is so good at this that it can catch a tiny insect in mid-air, in the dark, without ever seeing it. Some bats can even tell the difference between a moth and a beetle just by the sound that comes back.

Most small bats depend on their amazing ears and their echoes. Big fruit-eating bats, sometimes called flying foxes, have large eyes and use their eyesight to find fruit trees at night instead.

## What Do Bats Eat?

Different bats eat different foods, and that is one of the reasons they are so helpful to people and to nature.

Many bats eat insects. A single bat can eat lots of insects in one night. Think of all the mosquitoes and moths that would be buzzing around if bats were not there to eat them! Farmers like bats too, because bats eat the insects that munch on crops. In this way, bats work like tiny night-time pest controllers, and they do it for free.

Other bats eat fruit. They fly from tree to tree, munching on ripe fruit like mangoes, bananas, and figs. When they drop seeds or pass them in their droppings, new trees can grow far away from the parent tree. So fruit bats help plant forests while they have dinner!

Some bats drink nectar from flowers, just like bees and hummingbirds. As they move from flower to flower, pollen sticks to their fur and gets carried to the next flower. This helps plants make seeds and fruit. Without these bats, some plants would have a much harder time growing.

## Where Do Bats Live?

During the day, bats need a safe, dark, quiet place to rest. Many bats sleep in caves, hanging upside down from the roof. They hang by their feet, wrapped up in their wings like a little blanket. Hanging upside down might look strange to us, but for a bat it is the perfect way to rest. When it is time to fly, a bat can just let go and drop into the air.

Not all bats live in caves. Some sleep in hollow trees, under leaves, in old buildings, or in cracks in rocks.

Bats often live together in groups. A group of bats is called a colony, and some colonies are enormous, with thousands and thousands of bats all sleeping in the same cave. When evening comes, they all fly out together, and it can look like a dark river flowing across the sky.

## Why Bats Need Our Help

Some people are afraid of bats, but bats are not scary animals. Most bats are shy and gentle, and they want nothing to do with people. They are much more interested in insects, fruit, and flowers than in us.

Bats do need our help, though. In many places, the caves and forests where bats live are being damaged or cut down. When bats lose their homes, they have nowhere safe to sleep and raise their pups.

We can help bats by learning about them and sharing what we learn with others. We can also protect old trees and caves, and leave bats alone when we find them sleeping.

## Fun Facts

- Bats are the only mammals that can truly fly.
- A bat's wing is made from skin stretched between very long finger bones.
- Bats use echolocation, listening to echoes, to find their way and catch food in the dark.
- Many bats eat insects, and one bat can eat lots of insects in a single night.
- Fruit bats help new trees grow by spreading seeds.
- Some bats drink nectar from flowers and help plants make seeds, just like bees do.
- Bats sleep hanging upside down, wrapped in their wings.
- A group of bats is called a colony, and some colonies have thousands of bats.
- Baby bats are called pups, and mother bats feed them with milk.

## A Night Hunter Worth Loving

Bats are small, clever, and incredibly helpful. They keep insect numbers down, plant forests with the seeds they carry, and help flowers grow. They fly through the dark with a skill that no other mammal can match, using sound to see the world around them.

Next time you are outside in the evening, look up at the sky. If you see a small dark shape flapping quickly past, it might just be a bat, starting its night shift. Give it a little wave in your mind, and remember all the good work it is doing while the rest of us sleep.
