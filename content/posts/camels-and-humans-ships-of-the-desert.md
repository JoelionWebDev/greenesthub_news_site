In the hottest, driest places on Earth, where the sun beats down and water is hard to find, there is an animal that can travel for days without a drink. It has long legs, a shaggy coat, and one or two humps on its back. It is the camel, and people call it the ship of the desert. Just as a ship carries people across the sea, a camel carries people across the sand. For thousands of years, camels have helped people travel, trade, and live in the desert.

## What is a camel?

A camel is a large animal with long legs, a long neck, and one or two humps on its back. There are two main kinds of camel. The dromedary camel has one hump and lives in the hot deserts of North Africa and the Middle East. The Bactrian camel has two humps and lives in the cold deserts of Central Asia. Both kinds are strong, patient, and perfectly built for desert life.

The hump is the most famous part of a camel, but many people get it wrong. A camel's hump is not full of water. It is full of fat. The camel's body can turn that fat into energy and water when food and drink are hard to find. When a camel has not eaten for a long time, its hump gets smaller and floppy. A camel's body is full of clever tricks like this. Its long eyelashes keep sand out of its eyes, and it can close its nostrils to keep the sand out of its nose.

## How camels survive the desert

The desert is one of the hardest places on Earth to live. In the day, the sun is fierce and the sand is hot. At night, it can become very cold. Water is scarce, and food is hard to find. Yet camels manage very well. They can go for a long time without drinking, and when they do find water, they can drink a huge amount very quickly. They can eat dry, thorny plants that most animals would not touch, and their tough mouths are not hurt by the thorns.

Camels are also very good at saving water. They do not sweat as much as other animals, and their bodies can cope with losing water that would make another animal very ill. Their thick fur keeps the hot sun off their skin during the day and keeps them warm at night. When a sandstorm blows up, camels kneel down, close their eyes and nostrils, and wait calmly until the storm passes.

## Camels and people through history

People and camels have been friends for thousands of years. Long ago, people in the desert learned that camels could carry heavy loads across the sand, far better than horses or donkeys. Traders loaded camels with spices, silk, salt, and gold, and walked with them across the desert in long lines called caravans. A caravan could have many camels, each carrying heavy bags, and it could travel for weeks across the desert.

Camels also helped people move house, fetch water, and plough fields. In some places, people still drink camel milk, which is rich and good for them, and they use camel hair to make warm clothes and blankets. Camels are gentle animals, and they form close bonds with the people who look after them.

## A day in the life of a camel

Imagine you are a camel called Zara, living in the Sahara Desert. Your day starts at sunrise, when the air is still cool. Your owner loads soft bags onto your back, and you set off across the golden sand. Your wide feet make a soft sound as you walk, and your long legs carry you steadily along. You walk for hours, stopping now and then to nibble a dry bush or rest in the shade of a palm tree.

At midday, the sun is at its fiercest, and you rest while your owner eats and drinks. In the afternoon, you walk on again, and by evening you reach a small village, where children run out to greet you. Your owner unloads the bags, gives you a good meal of grain and hay, and brushes your coat. Then you settle down for the night, kneeling in the sand.

## The bond between people and camels

The bond between people and camels is built on trust and teamwork. In the desert, a person and their camel depend on each other. The camel carries the person and their goods, and the person gives the camel food, water, and care. Without the camel, the person could not cross the desert. Without the person, the camel would have no one to look after it. It is a partnership that has lasted for thousands of years.

Camels are also part of the stories and songs of desert peoples. They appear in tales, poems, and pictures, and they are loved as symbols of patience and strength. Children in desert lands grow up learning to look after camels, just as children in other places learn to look after dogs or horses.

## How to be kind to camels

Most of us will never meet a camel in the wild, but we can still be kind to them. If you visit a camel at a zoo or a farm, remember that it is a gentle animal that deserves respect. Do not shout at it, pull its fur, or feed it things it should not eat. Always ask the keeper before you touch or feed a camel.

You can also help camels by learning about them and telling others how amazing they are. When people understand how clever and useful camels are, they are more likely to look after them well. And if you ever get the chance to ride a camel, sit calmly, hold on gently, and enjoy the slow, swaying walk.

## Fun facts about camels

- A camel's hump is full of fat, not water, and it shrinks when the camel has not eaten.
- The dromedary camel has one hump, while the Bactrian camel has two humps.
- Camels can close their nostrils to keep sand out of their noses.
- A camel's wide, flat feet stop it from sinking into soft sand.
- Long lines of camels carrying goods across the desert are called caravans.
- Camels can eat dry, thorny plants that most other animals would not touch.
- People call the camel the ship of the desert because it carries people across the sand.
