Have you ever seen an animal that looks like a soft toy come to life? That is an alpaca! Alpacas are gentle, fluffy animals with long necks, big eyes, and coats so soft you will want to give them a hug. They have been friends with people for thousands of years. Let us meet them.

## Where Do Alpacas Live?

Alpacas come from the Andes mountains in South America, a long chain of tall mountains that runs through countries like Peru, Bolivia, and Chile. Up there, the air is thin and cold, and the land is rocky and steep. Their thick, warm fleece keeps them cosy when the wind blows cold, and their soft, padded feet help them walk gently over the rocky ground without hurting the plants.

Long ago, people in the Andes began to keep alpacas as farm animals. The animals gave people warm wool for clothes and blankets, and people gave the animals food, water, and protection. That friendship has lasted for a very long time. Today, alpacas also live on farms in many other countries around the world.

## What Does an Alpaca Look Like?

An alpaca is about the size of a very large dog, but with a much longer neck. Alpacas have small heads, short ears shaped like little spears, and big, gentle eyes with long eyelashes. Their faces look sweet and curious, and many people say alpacas always look as if they are smiling.

The most famous thing about an alpaca is its fleece. An alpaca's coat is thick, soft, and fluffy, and it comes in many beautiful colours — white, black, brown, grey, and cream, or a mix of colours. Their wool feels like a warm cloud, and it is even warmer than sheep's wool. Farmers shear alpacas once a year, in the spring, cutting off the thick fleece so the animals stay cool in summer. The fleece is then spun into yarn and made into jumpers, scarves, hats, gloves, and blankets.

There are two kinds of alpacas. Huacaya alpacas have short, fluffy fleece that makes them look like teddy bears. Suri alpacas have long, silky fleece that hangs down in beautiful locks. Both kinds give wonderful wool.

## What Do Alpacas Eat?

Alpacas are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite food is grass, and they also enjoy hay, leaves, and small plants. Alpacas have a clever way of eating. They use their split upper lip to grab grass, and then they chew it carefully. Like cows and sheep, alpacas bring their food back up to chew it again. This is called chewing the cud, and it helps them get all the goodness out of the grass.

Alpacas are gentle eaters. They nibble the tops of the plants, which means the grass can grow back, and their soft, padded feet are kind to the land. Farmers love this because it keeps their fields healthy.

## How Do Alpacas Talk to Each Other?

Alpacas are social animals, which means they like being with other alpacas. They live in groups called herds, and they feel safest when they are together. An alpaca that is kept all alone can become sad and stressed, so farmers always keep at least two or three alpacas together.

Alpacas talk to each other with a soft humming sound. A mother alpaca hums to her baby, called a cria, to comfort it. Alpacas also hum when they are happy, curious, or a little bit worried. It is a gentle, musical sound, and it is one of the sweetest sounds on a farm. When an alpaca senses danger, it can make a loud alarm call to warn the rest of the herd. Alpacas also use their body language to talk. They hold their ears up when they are relaxed, and they put their ears back when they are annoyed.

Alpacas are also known for spitting at each other when they argue over food. It is their way of saying, "Hey, that is mine!" If a person is kind and calm around alpacas, they almost never get spat at.

## Baby Alpacas Are Called Crias

A baby alpaca is called a cria, which is a Spanish word that means baby. Crias are some of the cutest baby animals in the world. They have long, wobbly legs, big eyes, and soft, fluffy coats. A cria can stand up and walk within an hour or two of being born, and it stays close to its mother for safety and milk.

Crias love to play. They run around the field, jump in the air, and chase each other in happy games. Playing helps them grow strong and learn how to be part of the herd. Mother alpacas hum softly to their crias, nudge them gently, and watch over them while they play.

## Alpacas and People Are Good Friends

People have loved alpacas for thousands of years, and it is easy to see why. Alpacas are calm, gentle, and curious. They like to come close to people, sniff their hands, and see what is happening. Many farms let visitors meet alpacas, feed them, and even take them for a walk on a lead, just like walking a dog. Alpaca walking has become a popular activity because it is so peaceful and fun.

Alpacas also help people in other ways. Their wool is used to make warm clothes, and their calm nature makes them wonderful therapy animals. Some alpacas visit schools, hospitals, and care homes, where they help people feel happy and relaxed. Just being near a gentle alpaca can make a person smile.

## Fun Facts About Alpacas

Alpacas are cousins of llamas, camels, and vicunas, and they all belong to the same animal family. Alpacas are smaller than llamas, and they were bred for their wool, while llamas were bred to carry loads. Alpacas can live for many years, often into their late teens, and they come in more natural colours than almost any other farm animal. They also have very good eyesight, which helps them spot danger from far away and keep the whole herd safe.

## Why We Love Alpacas

Alpacas remind us that gentle animals can be strong, that soft wool can keep us warm, and that friendship between people and animals can last for thousands of years. They are calm, kind, curious, and funny, and they make the world a softer, warmer place.

The next time you see a picture of an alpaca, look at its big eyes and its fluffy coat, and imagine the soft hum it makes on a cold mountain morning. You might just fall in love with alpacas too.
