## Meet the Jaguar

Deep in the rainforests of Central and South America lives a powerful big cat with a golden coat covered in dark spots. That is the jaguar! Jaguars are the largest big cats in the Americas, and they are one of the strongest cats in the whole world.

A jaguar looks a bit like a leopard, but it is bigger and stockier, with a wider head and stronger jaws. Its spots are called rosettes, and inside many of the rosettes there are one or two small black dots. That is a clever way to tell a jaguar apart from a leopard, which has rosettes without dots inside.

Jaguars live mostly in rainforests, but they can also be found in grasslands and wetlands. They love to be near water, and they are not afraid to jump into a river for a swim. In fact, jaguars are some of the best swimmers of all the big cats!

## The Strongest Bite

Jaguars have the strongest bite of any big cat, when you compare it to their size. Their jaws are so powerful that they can bite through the hard shell of a turtle, or through the thick skull of their prey. That sounds a bit scary, but it is what helps them survive in the wild.

A jaguar's body is built for power. It has short, strong legs, a big chest, and a large head with wide jaws. It does not run as fast as a cheetah, and it does not climb trees as much as a leopard, but it is a champion wrestler. When a jaguar grabs its prey, it is very hard for that animal to get away.

Jaguars hunt many animals, including deer, monkeys, birds, fish, and even caimans, which are cousins of crocodiles. They are brave hunters, and they are not afraid to take on animals that other cats would leave alone. A jaguar is a carnivore, which means it eats meat.

## A Cat That Loves Water

Most cats hate getting wet, but not the jaguar! Jaguars love water, and they are excellent swimmers. They will swim across wide rivers, play in streams, and even hunt in the water. Sometimes they catch fish by swatting them out of the water with their big paws.

Why do jaguars love water so much? The rainforest is hot and humid, so a cool swim is a great way to cool down. Water is also a great place to hunt, because many animals come to the river to drink. A jaguar can hide quietly at the edge of the water and wait for its dinner to arrive.

Baby jaguars, called cubs, learn to swim from their mothers. At first they may paddle in shallow water, but soon they are splashing around like little furry swimmers. If you ever see a jaguar in a river, remember that it is not lost or in trouble. It is right at home!

## The Quiet Hunter of the Night

Jaguars are mostly active at night and at dawn and dusk, when the rainforest is cooler and darker. Their eyes are made for seeing in the dark, and their spotted coats help them hide among the leaves and shadows. They can move through the forest without making a sound.

A jaguar hunts by stealth, which means it sneaks up quietly on its prey. It crouches low, moves slowly, and waits for the perfect moment to pounce. Then it leaps with a burst of power and grabs its prey with its strong paws and jaws.

Unlike some cats that chase their prey over long distances, a jaguar likes to get close first. It is a patient hunter, and it will wait for a long time if it needs to.

## Spots Like a Fingerprint

Every jaguar has its own special pattern of spots, just like every person has their own fingerprints. No two jaguars look exactly the same. Scientists can tell jaguars apart by looking at their spots, and they use photos to keep track of individual jaguars in the wild.

A jaguar's coat is golden yellow, with black rosettes all over its body. The spots on its head and legs are smaller and solid. This pattern helps the jaguar blend in with the dappled light that shines through the leaves of the rainforest.

## A Jaguar Family

A mother jaguar usually has one or two cubs at a time. When the cubs are born, they are small and blind, and they depend on their mother for everything. She keeps them in a safe den, hidden in thick bushes or in a hollow tree, where other animals cannot find them.

Cubs stay with their mother for about two years. She teaches them how to hunt, how to swim, and how to climb. The cubs learn by playing, wrestling, and chasing each other through the forest. Playing may look like fun, and it is, but it is also how cubs practise the skills they will need as adults.

When the cubs grow up, they leave their mother to find their own territory. Jaguars are solitary animals, which means they mostly live alone. Each jaguar has a large home range, and it marks the trees with its scent to tell other jaguars that this is its home.

## Jaguars Need Our Help

Long ago, jaguars lived in many more places, from the southern United States all the way down to Argentina. Today, they live in a smaller area, mostly in the Amazon rainforest and other parts of Central and South America. Their homes are being lost as forests are cut down for farms and towns.

Jaguars are also in danger because some people hunt them, and because their prey is becoming harder to find. But there is good news! Many people are working to protect jaguars. They are creating national parks and reserves, planting trees, and building safe passages so jaguars can travel between forests.

When we protect the rainforest, we protect jaguars and all the other animals that live there too, like monkeys, toucans, and sloths. The jaguar is a symbol of the rainforest, strong, beautiful, and wild.

Next time you think of a cat that loves to swim, remember the jaguar. With its powerful bite, its beautiful spots, and its love of water, it is one of the most amazing animals in the world. What a rainforest superstar!
