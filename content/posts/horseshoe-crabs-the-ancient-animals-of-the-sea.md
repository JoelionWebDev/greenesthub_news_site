# Horseshoe Crabs: Ancient Animals of the Sea

Have you ever seen a horseshoe crab? It looks like a little tank from the sea, with a hard, round shell, a long pointy tail, and lots of legs underneath. But here is a surprise: a horseshoe crab is not a crab at all! It is more closely related to spiders and scorpions. And it is one of the oldest animals on Earth.

## Not a Crab at All

The horseshoe crab gets its name from its shell, which is shaped like a horseshoe, the U-shaped piece of metal that horses wear on their feet. Its shell is hard and smooth, and it protects the soft body underneath.

Under the shell, the horseshoe crab has many legs. It uses these legs to walk along the sea floor and to catch food. It also has a long, pointy tail, called a telson, which helps it to steer and to flip itself over if it gets turned upside down.

Horseshoe crabs have big eyes on top of their shells, and smaller eyes on the sides. They can see light and dark, and they use their eyes to find their way in the water. They also have special eyes that can sense light from the moon, which helps them know when it is time to come to shore.

## A Very Old Animal

Horseshoe crabs have been on Earth for a very, very long time. Their family has lived in the sea for hundreds of millions of years, since long before dinosaurs walked on land. In fact, horseshoe crabs were already old when the first dinosaurs appeared!

Long ago, there were many kinds of horseshoe crabs in the sea. Over time, most of them disappeared, but the horseshoe crabs we see today have stayed much the same. That is why people call them living fossils. They are a window into the past, showing us what animals looked like a very long time ago.

Today, there are only a few kinds of horseshoe crabs left in the world. They live in the warm waters of the Atlantic Ocean and the Pacific Ocean, along the coasts of North America and Asia. They are special and precious, and we need to look after them.

## Life on the Sea Floor

Horseshoe crabs live on the sea floor, in shallow water near the shore. They like sandy and muddy places where they can dig and find food. They are slow movers, and they spend much of their time crawling along the bottom, looking for something to eat.

They are good swimmers, too, but they prefer to walk. When they swim, they turn upside down and use their legs to paddle through the water. It looks a bit funny, but it works!

Horseshoe crabs are most active at night, when the water is cool and quiet. During the day, they often bury themselves in the sand, with only their eyes and the top of their shell showing. This keeps them safe from hungry animals and from the hot sun.

## What Do Horseshoe Crabs Eat?

Horseshoe crabs eat small animals that live in the sand and mud. They eat worms, small shellfish, and other tiny creatures. They use their legs to dig in the sand and find food, and then they bring the food to their mouth.

Their mouth is in the middle of their body, under the shell. It is a strange place for a mouth, but it works well for an animal that spends its life crawling on the sea floor. Horseshoe crabs do not have teeth. Instead, they crush their food with the bases of their legs before they eat it.

Horseshoe crabs are also food for other animals. Birds, fish, and sea turtles eat them, especially when they come to shore to lay eggs. But the horseshoe crab's hard shell helps to protect it. It is tough and strong, and it keeps the soft body safe inside.

## The Great Journey to Shore

Every year, when the spring and summer come, horseshoe crabs make a special journey. They leave the deep water and come to the shore to lay their eggs. This happens at night, when the tide is high and the moon is full.

The female horseshoe crab digs a hole in the sand and lays thousands of tiny green eggs in it. Then she covers them with sand and goes back to the sea. The male horseshoe crab helps by fertilising the eggs, so they can grow into baby horseshoe crabs.

## Growing Up Slowly

Baby horseshoe crabs are tiny when they hatch. They look like little versions of their parents, but without a tail. They are called larvae, and they swim in the shallow water near the shore, eating tiny animals and growing bigger.

As they grow, they shed their shells, just like a snake sheds its skin. Each time they shed their old shell, they grow a new, bigger one. It takes many years for a horseshoe crab to grow up and become an adult. Some take a very long time, and they can live for many years.

## Fun Facts to Remember

- A horseshoe crab is not a crab. It is more closely related to spiders and scorpions.
- Its shell is shaped like a horseshoe, which is how it got its name.
- It has been on Earth for hundreds of millions of years, longer than the dinosaurs.
- Its blood is blue, and it helps doctors test medicines to make sure they are safe.
- It comes to shore at night, when the moon is full, to lay its eggs in the sand.
- Its eggs are important food for birds that fly thousands of miles.
- It has a long pointy tail that helps it to steer and to flip over if it gets stuck.

The horseshoe crab is a true survivor. It has lived through many changes on Earth, and it is still here today, crawling along the sea floor. Let us do our best to keep it safe, so that it can keep swimming in our seas for many more millions of years.
