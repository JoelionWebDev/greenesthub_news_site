Deep in the forests of Australia, there lives a bird with a truly amazing talent. It can copy the songs of other birds so well that even the birds themselves get confused. It can also copy sounds from the forest around it, from the wind in the leaves to the buzz of insects. Its name is the lyrebird, and it is one of the cleverest singers in the whole bird world.

The lyrebird gets its name from its beautiful tail. The male lyrebird has long, graceful tail feathers that spread out in the shape of an ancient musical instrument called a lyre. When he dances and sings, he lifts his tail high over his back like a fan, and it shimmers in the sunlight of the forest. It is a sight few people ever see, because lyrebirds are shy and love to hide.

## The Greatest Mimics

Lyrebirds are famous for their mimicry, which means copying sounds. A lyrebird can learn the calls of many other birds in the forest, and it can sing them all back, one after another, in its own beautiful song. It can copy the cheerful notes of small songbirds and the sweet trill of a wren, all in a single performance.

But lyrebirds do not stop at bird calls. They can also copy sounds from the world around them, like the rustle of leaves, the patter of rain, and the buzz of a bee. Some lyrebirds have even learned to copy sounds made by people, like a camera click, if they hear them often enough in the forest. Their memory for sounds is truly wonderful.

## The Dancing Tail

The male lyrebird's tail is one of the most beautiful sights in the bird world. His tail feathers are long and elegant, and when he spreads them, they form the shape of a lyre, with two curved feathers on the outside and fine, lacy feathers in the middle. The feathers are brown and grey, with soft, silvery patterns that catch the light.

When it is time to sing and dance, the male lyrebird finds a special spot on the forest floor, a little clearing he has prepared. He lifts his tail high over his head, spreads it like a fan, and begins to sing his most beautiful song. He dances as he sings, stepping from side to side and turning around, showing off his wonderful tail to any female who is watching.

The female lyrebird watches and listens carefully. She chooses a mate with the best song and the finest tail, because a good singer shows that he is strong and healthy. The dance is a very important part of lyrebird life, and the males practise their songs and dances for a long time.

## Life on the Forest Floor

Unlike many birds that live high in the trees, lyrebirds spend most of their lives on the forest floor. They walk and hop through the fallen leaves, scratching in the leaf litter with their strong feet to find food. Their brown feathers blend in perfectly with the leaves and earth, so they are very hard to spot even when they are close by.

Lyrebirds are shy birds, and they like to stay hidden. If they hear a person or an animal coming, they will freeze and stay still, or slip away quietly into the bushes. Their camouflage is so good that they can disappear right in front of your eyes.

Lyrebirds live in the forests of eastern Australia, where the trees are tall and the ground is covered in ferns and fallen leaves. They love damp, shady forests, where the leaf litter is thick and full of tasty things to eat. They are home birds, and they stay in the same patch of forest for their whole lives.

## What Lyrebirds Eat

Lyrebirds eat the small creatures that live in the leaf litter on the forest floor. They scratch through the leaves with their feet, uncovering insects, worms, spiders, and other tiny animals. Their strong beaks are perfect for picking up these little creatures and eating them one by one.

They also eat seeds and small fruits that have fallen to the ground. Lyrebirds are patient hunters, and they spend many hours each day scratching and searching through the leaves. As they scratch, they turn over the leaf litter, which helps the forest stay healthy.

## Nests and Baby Lyrebirds

Female lyrebirds build their nests on the ground, hidden among ferns, rocks, or the roots of trees. The nest is a big, dome-shaped home made of sticks, leaves, moss, and bark, with a small opening on one side. Inside, it is soft and warm, lined with feathers and fine plant material. It is a cosy little house for a baby lyrebird.

The mother lyrebird usually lays just one egg, and she sits on it to keep it warm until it hatches. She is a devoted mother, and she guards the nest carefully. When the baby hatches, it is small and helpless, with soft, dark feathers. The mother brings it food every day, and she teaches it the ways of the forest.

Baby lyrebirds grow slowly, and they stay with their mother for a long time. They learn to scratch in the leaves, to find insects, and to hide from danger. Young males begin to practise their songs and dances, copying the sounds they hear around them.

## Guardians of the Forest

Lyrebirds are very important to the forest. As they scratch through the leaf litter looking for food, they help turn over the soil and mix the old leaves into the ground. This helps new plants grow and keeps the forest floor healthy. Lyrebirds are like little gardeners, working quietly to care for their forest home.

Sadly, lyrebirds face dangers. Their forest homes are sometimes damaged by fire, and they need thick, shady forests to live in. When forests are lost, lyrebirds have nowhere to go.

You can help lyrebirds by learning about them and caring about forests. If you ever walk through an Australian forest and hear a song that sounds like many different birds all at once, stop and listen. That might be a lyrebird, the greatest mimic in the bird world, singing its beautiful song from the shadows of the forest floor.
