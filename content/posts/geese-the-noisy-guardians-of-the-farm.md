Honk! Honk! Can you hear that loud, cheerful noise? That is a goose! Geese are big, brave birds with long necks, strong wings, and very loud voices. They guard the farmyard, they love their families, and they fly across the sky in a giant V shape. Let us waddle over and meet them.

## Where Do Geese Come From?

Geese come from wild geese that have lived in Europe, Asia, and North America for a very long time. Long ago, people began to keep geese on farms because they gave eggs, meat, and soft feathers. Today, farm geese live in many countries around the world, and wild geese still fly across fields, lakes, and marshes.

Wild geese are amazing travellers. Some of them migrate, which means they fly long distances to find warmer weather when winter comes. They can fly for many hours without stopping, and they always know the way.

## What Does a Goose Look Like?

A goose is a large bird with a long neck, a strong beak, and webbed feet that are perfect for swimming. Most farm geese are white, grey, or brown, and their feathers are thick and warm. Their beaks are orange or pink, and their feet are orange too. A goose's long neck helps it reach down into the water to find food.

Geese have special feathers that keep them warm and dry. Under their outer feathers, they have a layer of soft, fluffy feathers called down. Down is so warm and light that people use it to fill pillows, duvets, and jackets.

A male goose is called a gander, and a female goose is called a goose. Ganders are often a little bigger than females, and they are very protective of their families. When a gander spreads his wings and hisses, you know he means business!

## The Famous Honk

The most famous thing about a goose is its honk. Geese honk to talk to each other, to warn of danger, and to say hello. A goose's honk is loud, and a whole flock of geese honking together can be heard from far away.

Geese are excellent guards. They have sharp eyesight and hearing, and they notice everything. If a stranger or a strange animal comes near the farm, the geese will honk loudly and flap their wings. That is why people have used geese to guard farms, houses, and even castles for hundreds of years.

## What Do Geese Eat?

Geese are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. Their favourite foods are grass, leaves, seeds, and grains. They love to graze in fields, nibbling grass just like cows and sheep. Geese also eat water plants, berries, and vegetables, and they enjoy a tasty snack of corn or wheat.

A goose's beak is strong and has tiny, tooth-like edges that help it grip grass and tear off leaves. Geese spend much of the day eating, and they need lots of fresh water to drink.

## Geese Love Their Families

Geese are very family birds. They live in groups called flocks, and they mate for life, which means a gander and a goose stay together for many years. They build their nests together, they look after their eggs together, and they raise their goslings together.

A mother goose lays her eggs in a nest made of grass and soft feathers, often near water. She sits on the eggs to keep them warm, and the gander stands guard nearby, watching for danger. When the goslings hatch, both parents look after them. The goslings follow their parents everywhere, learning where to find food and how to swim.

Goslings are some of the cutest baby birds in the world. They are small, yellow, and fluffy, with tiny orange beaks and feet. They love to follow their mother in a line, waddling along behind her. As they grow up, their fluffy down is replaced by strong feathers, and they learn to fly.

## The Amazing V Formation

One of the most amazing things about geese is how they fly together. When wild geese migrate, they fly in a shape called a V formation. One goose flies at the front, and the others follow in two lines behind it, making a giant V in the sky.

Flying in a V helps the geese save energy. Each goose flies in the wind made by the goose in front of it, which makes flying easier. The goose at the front works the hardest, so the geese take turns being the leader. When one goose gets tired, another goose flies to the front to take over. That is teamwork!

Geese also honk while they fly, to encourage each other and to keep the flock together. If you look up at the sky in autumn or spring and see a V of geese flying overhead, listen carefully — you might hear their honks drifting down to you.

## Geese and People

People have kept geese on farms for thousands of years. Geese give us eggs, meat, and feathers, and they guard the farmyard with their loud honks. In the past, people even used goose feathers to make pens for writing. These pens were called quills, and they were dipped in ink to write letters and books.

There is a famous story about geese saving the city of Rome long ago. The story says that when enemies tried to sneak into the city at night, the geese heard them and honked loudly, waking the guards and saving the city. Whether or not the story is exactly true, it shows how much people have trusted geese as guards for a very long time.

## Fun Facts About Geese

Here are some fun facts about geese. Geese can live for many years, often into their twenties. A goose has excellent eyesight and can see well in the dark. Geese are strong swimmers, and their webbed feet work like paddles. And a goose can remember people and places, so it knows who is a friend and who is a stranger.

## Why We Love Geese

Geese remind us that being loud is not always a bad thing — sometimes a loud honk is the bravest sound in the world. They are loyal to their families, they work together as a team, and they guard their homes with all their hearts.

The next time you hear a goose honk, stop and listen. That loud, cheerful sound is a goose looking after its family — and maybe, just maybe, it is looking after you too.
