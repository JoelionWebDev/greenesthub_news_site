Imagine a garden pond full of living jewels. Orange, white, black, red, and gold fish glide through the clear water, their colours shining in the sunlight. They are not just fish — they are works of art that swim. Meet the koi — the living jewels of the pond.

## What Is a Koi?

A koi is a type of carp — a large, freshwater fish that belongs to the same family as goldfish. Koi were first bred from ordinary carp, which are usually grey or brown. Over hundreds of years, people in Japan and China bred carp that had patches of colour, until they created the beautiful, brightly coloured fish we call koi today.

The word "koi" comes from the Japanese word for carp. In Japan, koi are called "nishikigoi," which means "brocaded carp." Brocade is a rich, patterned cloth, and the name is perfect — a koi's colours look like patterns woven into cloth.

A koi can grow quite big. A fully grown koi is often about as long as your arm, and some grow even bigger. They have long, flowing bodies, and two little whiskers, called barbels, on the sides of their mouths. These whiskers help the koi feel around for food at the bottom of the pond.

Koi come in many colours and patterns. Some are bright orange with white patches. Some are pure white. Some are black and gold. Some are red and white, like a flag. Each koi has its own unique pattern, a bit like a fingerprint.

## A History That Goes Back Hundreds of Years

Koi have been kept by people for a very long time. Long ago, in China, carp were kept in rice paddies and ponds as food. Some of these carp had unusual colours — a flash of red here, a patch of gold there. People noticed these colourful carp and began to keep them just because they were beautiful.

The art of breeding koi really grew in Japan, hundreds of years ago. Farmers in the mountains of Japan kept carp in ponds so they would have food in the winter. Over time, they began to breed the prettiest carp together, and the colours became brighter and more amazing with each new generation.

Koi are also important in Japanese culture. They are symbols of good luck, strength, and never giving up. There is a famous old story about a koi that swam up a waterfall and turned into a dragon. The story teaches that if you keep trying, even when things are hard, you can achieve amazing things. That is why koi are often seen as symbols of courage and determination.

## Life in the Pond

Koi live in outdoor ponds, where they have plenty of space to swim. A good koi pond is deep enough to keep the fish cool in summer and safe in winter, and it has clean water with a filter to keep it fresh. Koi ponds often have plants, rocks, and shady places where the fish can rest.

Koi are social fish. They like to live in groups, swimming together and following each other around the pond. They are not shy. In fact, koi are famously friendly. Many koi learn to recognise the person who feeds them, and they will swim to the edge of the pond when that person comes near. Some koi will even eat food right out of your hand, or let you gently touch them.

Koi are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and animals. In a pond, they eat algae, small insects, and plants. Pet koi are given special koi pellets, and they also enjoy treats like lettuce, peas, and watermelon. Koi are not fussy eaters, but they should not be overfed — food left in the pond can make the water dirty.

## How Long Do Koi Live?

Koi can live for a very long time. Many pet koi live for twenty or thirty years, and some live for much longer — fifty years or more. There are stories of koi that lived for over a hundred years, although it is hard to be sure about the oldest ones.

Because koi live so long, keeping them is a big commitment. A family that gets a young koi might still have that same fish when the children in the family are grown up with children of their own. That is one reason koi are so special — they become part of the family's story.

## Caring for Koi

Keeping koi healthy takes work, but it is worth it. The most important thing is clean water. A koi pond needs a good filter and a pump to keep the water moving and clean. The water should be tested to make sure it is safe for the fish. If the water is dirty, koi can get sick.

A koi pond also needs to be big enough. Koi grow large, and they need room to swim. A small pond is not enough for koi. They need a pond that is deep and wide, with space for several fish to live happily together.

## Why We Love Koi

Koi are more than just pretty fish. They are living works of art, and they have been treasured by people for hundreds of years. They teach us patience — it takes years for a koi to grow into its full beauty. They teach us to care for living things over a long time. And they remind us to keep trying, like the koi that swam up the waterfall.

The next time you see a garden pond full of colourful fish gliding through the water, stop and watch for a while. You are looking at living jewels — and at one of the oldest and most beautiful friendships between people and fish.
