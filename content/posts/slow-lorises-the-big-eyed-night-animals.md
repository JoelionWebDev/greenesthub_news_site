With huge round eyes and a slow, gentle way of moving, the slow loris looks like a cuddly toy. But this little night animal has a secret. The slow loris lives in the forests of Southeast Asia, and it is one of the only mammals in the world that is venomous. That means it can make a poison to protect itself. It may look sweet and sleepy, but it is a tough little survivor.

## Those Amazing Eyes

The first thing anyone notices about a slow loris is its eyes. They are enormous, round and dark, like two shiny buttons on its face. They are so big because the slow loris is a night animal, and it needs to see in the dark. Its eyes gather as much light as possible, helping it find food and move safely through the trees when the forest is dark.

The slow loris is small, about the size of a small cat, with thick, soft fur that is usually grey or brown. It has a pale face with dark patches around its eyes, which makes it look as if it is wearing a mask. Its arms and legs are strong, and its hands and feet can grip branches tightly, like little hands holding on.

It has almost no tail at all, which is unusual for an animal that lives in trees. Instead, it relies on its strong grip and slow, careful movements to stay safe high above the ground.

## Slow and Steady Wins the Race

The slow loris is one of the slowest-moving animals in the trees. It moves very carefully, one hand at a time, never rushing and never jumping. This slow movement is actually a clever trick. Many animals that hunt at night find their food by spotting movement, so by moving slowly, the slow loris is hard to notice.

It is also very quiet. It does not chatter or call loudly like some other animals of the forest. It moves through the branches in silence, like a shadow. If it senses danger, it freezes completely still, curling up into a ball and hiding its face. From a distance, it looks just like a clump of leaves.

Slow lorises eat a mix of foods. They love tree sap, which is the sticky juice inside trees, and they lick it from cracks in the bark. They also eat fruit, nectar from flowers, insects and even bird eggs. They use their long tongues to lick sap and nectar, and their strong fingers to pick fruit and catch insects.

## The Secret Poison

Here is the slow loris's amazing secret: it is venomous. It has special glands on the insides of its elbows that make a poison. When the loris feels threatened, it licks these glands and mixes the poison with its saliva. Then, if it bites, the bite can be very painful.

Mother lorises use this poison to protect their babies. Before she leaves her baby alone in a tree, she licks her elbow glands and rubs the poison into the baby's fur. This makes the baby taste terrible to any animal that might try to eat it. It is a clever way for a mother to keep her little one safe.

Because of this poison, slow lorises should never be kept as pets. Sadly, many slow lorises are caught from the wild and sold as pets, which is very cruel and also dangerous for the people who buy them. A slow loris belongs in the forest, not in a house.

## Babies in the Trees

Slow loris mothers usually have one baby at a time. The baby is born with its eyes open and clings tightly to its mother's fur. For the first few weeks, the baby rides on its mother's belly everywhere she goes, holding on with its strong little hands.

When the baby gets a little older, the mother leaves it hidden in a tree while she goes to find food. The baby stays very still and quiet, waiting for her to come back. This is why the poison in its fur is so important — it keeps the baby safe while its mother is away.

Young slow lorises stay with their mothers for many months, learning which trees have sap, which fruits are safe to eat and how to move quietly through the forest. When they are old enough, they go off to find their own home in the trees.

## Why We Must Protect the Slow Loris

Slow lorises are in danger. Their forest homes are being cut down, and many are caught to be sold as pets or used in traditional medicine. This is very sad, because the slow loris is a gentle animal that plays an important part in the forest. By eating fruit and nectar, it helps spread seeds and pollen, which helps new plants grow.

People in Southeast Asia and around the world are working to protect the slow loris. Rescue centres look after lorises that have been saved from the pet trade and try to return them to the wild. Forest reserves keep their homes safe.

## A Gentle Night Wanderer

The slow loris teaches us that slow does not mean weak. This little animal survives in a dangerous forest by being quiet, careful and clever. It moves at its own pace, sees in the dark with its huge eyes, and protects itself with a secret that scientists are still studying.

Next time you feel rushed, think of the slow loris, moving gently through the trees under the moonlight, one careful step at a time. Sometimes the slowest way is the smartest way of all.
