Imagine going fishing without a rod, a net, or a hook. Instead, your fishing partner is a bird. A bird that dives under the water, catches a fish in its beak, and brings it back to you. That may sound like a story from a fairy tale, but for cormorant fishermen, it is real life. For hundreds of years, people in parts of Asia have worked together with cormorants, clever black water birds, to catch fish in rivers at night.

Cormorant fishing is one of the oldest kinds of fishing in the world. It is a beautiful example of how people and animals can work as a team. The fisherman does not force the bird to do something strange. Instead, he uses the bird's natural talent. Cormorants are wonderful swimmers and divers. In the wild, they catch fish all by themselves. The fisherman simply guides them, helps them, and shares the catch.

## Meet the Cormorant

A cormorant is a large water bird with dark feathers, a long neck, and a sharp beak. You can often see cormorants standing on rocks or posts near water, holding their wings out wide to dry them in the sun. That is because their feathers are not as waterproof as a duck's feathers, so they need time to dry after a swim.

Cormorants are expert fish hunters. They can dive deep under the water and swim fast with their strong feet and wings. Their eyes are made for seeing under water, and their beaks are perfect for grabbing slippery fish. When a cormorant spots a fish, it darts after it in a flash. In the wild, a cormorant swallows the fish whole, head first.

Fishermen noticed this amazing skill long ago. They wondered: what if we could work with these birds instead of just watching them? That idea grew into cormorant fishing, a tradition that has been passed down through families for many generations.

## How Does Cormorant Fishing Work?

Cormorant fishing usually happens at night, on a calm river. The fisherman stands in a small wooden boat. At the front of the boat hangs a bright lantern, and a small fire burns in a basket. The light attracts fish, drawing them close to the surface where the birds can see them.

The fisherman holds several cormorants on long cords. Each bird wears a loose ring around its neck. The ring is important. It stops the bird from swallowing a large fish, but it does not hurt the bird, and it still allows the bird to swallow small fish as a reward. When the time is right, the fisherman sends the birds into the water.

The cormorants dive down and chase the fish. When a bird catches a fish, it comes back to the boat. The fisherman gently takes the fish from the bird's beak and places it in a basket. Then he sends the bird out again to catch another one. The birds and the fisherman work together like a team, moving smoothly across the river in the dark.

## A Tradition Passed Down

Cormorant fishing is most famous in Japan, where it is called ukai, and in parts of China. In Japan, the fishermen are called usho. The job is often passed from father to son, and it takes many years to learn. A young fisherman must learn how to handle the boat, how to care for the birds, and how to read the river.

The cormorants are not wild birds caught for a single night. They are raised and trained with great care. Fishermen feed them, keep them healthy, and look after them when they are old. A well-cared-for cormorant can work with a fisherman for many years. The bond between the fisherman and his birds is built on trust and daily care, not on fear.

In the past, cormorant fishing was an important way for families to catch food. Today, it is done less for food and more as a living tradition. Visitors travel from far away to watch the fishermen and their birds glide across the river in the evening light. The fishermen wear traditional clothes, and the whole scene feels like stepping back in time.

## Why People Still Practise It

You might wonder why people still fish with birds when there are modern boats and nets. The answer is that cormorant fishing is about more than catching fish. It is about keeping a beautiful tradition alive. It is about remembering how people once lived in harmony with nature, using what nature gave them in a gentle way.

It also teaches us something important. When we pay attention to animals, we can learn from them. The fishermen learned from the cormorants. They watched how the birds hunted, how they dived, and how they moved through the water. Then they found a way to work together, so both the people and the birds could benefit.

Cormorant fishing reminds us that animals are not just creatures to watch from far away. They can be partners, helpers, and friends. The fisherman trusts his birds, and the birds trust him. That trust takes time to grow, but once it is there, it is a wonderful thing to see.

## What We Can Learn From the Birds

Next time you see a cormorant drying its wings by a lake or a river, take a moment to watch it. Think about how clever it is, and how patient it must be to hunt under water. Think about the fishermen who learned to work with these birds so long ago, and who still keep the tradition alive today.

Animals have many talents that we do not have. Birds can fly, fish can swim, and cormorants can dive like champions. When people respect those talents and work with animals kindly, amazing things can happen. The story of the cormorant fishermen shows us that the best teams are not always made of people alone. Sometimes, the best team is a person and a bird, working together under the stars, on a quiet river, catching fish the old way.
