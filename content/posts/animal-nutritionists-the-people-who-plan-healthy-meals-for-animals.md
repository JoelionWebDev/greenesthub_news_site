# Animal Nutritionists: The People Who Plan Healthy Meals for Animals

Just like you need healthy food to grow strong, animals need the right food too. But what is healthy for a lion is not healthy for a rabbit, and what a baby elephant needs is very different from what an old dog needs. So who decides what each animal should eat? That is the job of an animal nutritionist. Animal nutritionists are food experts for animals. They plan meals that keep animals healthy, strong, and full of energy.

## What Is an Animal Nutritionist?

An animal nutritionist is a person who studies what animals eat and what their bodies need from food. Food gives animals energy to run, play, and grow. It also helps them stay warm, fight off sickness, and have healthy babies. If an animal eats the wrong food, it can become weak, sick, or too fat. The nutritionist makes sure that does not happen.

Animal nutritionists work in many places. Some work in zoos, planning meals for lions, monkeys, elephants, and penguins. Some work on farms, helping farmers feed cows, chickens, goats, and pigs. Some work with pet food companies, making sure the food in the bag or tin has everything a dog or cat needs. Others work with vets, helping sick animals that need a special diet to get better.

To do this job well, an animal nutritionist needs to know a lot about different animals. A cow has a very different stomach from a dog. A horse eats grass all day, while a cat eats meat.

## Why Animals Need Different Foods

Every animal is built to eat certain foods. This is called its diet. Some animals are herbivores, which means they eat plants. Cows, horses, rabbits, and elephants are herbivores. Some animals are carnivores, which means they eat meat. Lions, tigers, and cats are carnivores. Some animals are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and meat. Pigs, bears, and chickens are omnivores.

But even animals that eat the same kind of food need different meals. A baby animal needs food that helps it grow fast. An old animal needs food that is easy to chew and gentle on its stomach. A mother animal feeding her babies needs extra food to stay strong. An animal that runs a lot, like a horse, needs more energy than an animal that sleeps most of the day.

Animal nutritionists think about all of this. Then they plan a diet that gives it exactly what it needs, no more and no less.

## A Day in the Life of an Animal Nutritionist

In a zoo, an animal nutritionist starts the day by checking the animals. They talk to the zookeepers to find out how each animal is doing. Is the elephant eating well? Is the monkey leaving some of its food? Is the penguin getting enough fish? These answers help the nutritionist change the meals if needed.

Next, they plan the day's food. They might decide that the giraffes need more leaves, or that the parrots need more fruit. They weigh the food carefully so each animal gets the right amount. Too much food can make an animal unhealthy, and too little can leave it hungry and weak.

## The Building Blocks of Food

Food is made up of different parts that do different jobs in the body. Animal nutritionists learn all about these parts.

Protein helps animals grow and builds strong muscles. Meat, fish, eggs, and beans are rich in protein. Young animals need lots of protein to grow big and strong.

Carbohydrates give animals energy. Grains like corn, rice, and oats are full of carbohydrates. Animals that work hard or run a lot need plenty of energy food.

Fats also give energy and help keep animals warm. A little fat is good, but too much can make an animal unhealthy.

Vitamins and minerals are tiny but mighty. They help bones grow strong, keep eyes healthy, and help the body fight sickness. Animals get vitamins and minerals from fruits, vegetables, and special food mixes.

## Helping Sick Animals

Sometimes animals get sick and need a special diet. A dog with a sore stomach might need soft, gentle food. A cat with kidney problems might need food with less of certain things in it. A horse with a bad tooth might need soft hay instead of hard grains.

Animal nutritionists work with vets to plan these special diets. They choose foods that are easy to eat and gentle on the body, but still give the animal everything it needs to heal. It is like when you are sick and your mum or dad gives you soup and soft food to help you feel better.

## What Do Animal Nutritionists Study?

To become an animal nutritionist, a person studies animals and food for many years. They learn about animal bodies, how food is used inside the body, and what different animals need to eat. They also learn about farming, zoos, and pet care.

A good animal nutritionist loves animals and enjoys science. They like asking questions and finding answers. Why does this food help that animal? What happens if we change this part of the diet? They are always learning, because scientists keep discovering new things about animal food.

## Food for the Animals Around You

You can be a little animal nutritionist yourself. If you have a pet, learn what it should and should not eat. Dogs should not eat chocolate, and cats should not drink too much milk. Rabbits need hay every day, and birds need fresh seeds and water. Feeding your pet the right food is one of the kindest things you can do for it.

If you visit a zoo, look at what the animals are eating. You might see an elephant munching hay, a monkey peeling a banana, or a penguin gobbling fish. Behind every meal is an animal nutritionist who planned it with care.

Animals cannot cook for themselves or choose their own food. They depend on people to feed them well. Animal nutritionists make sure that every meal is just right. Thanks to them, animals all over the world — in zoos, on farms, and in our homes — get the healthy food they need to live happy lives.
