Imagine a parrot that cannot fly, loves to climb trees, comes out only at night, and smells sweet like honey. That sounds like a bird from a storybook, but it is real. It is called the kakapo, and it lives only in New Zealand. Kakapos are chubby, green, friendly birds with a sweet face and a gentle nature. They are some of the rarest parrots in the world, and people in New Zealand are working very hard to keep them safe. Let us meet this wonderful night parrot and learn why it is so special.

## What Is a Kakapo?

A kakapo is a large parrot, and it is the heaviest parrot in the world. It has soft, mossy green feathers with black speckles, which help it blend in with the forest floor. Its face is round, with a small beak and big, gentle eyes. Unlike most parrots, the kakapo cannot fly. Its wings are short, and its body is too heavy for flying. Instead, it uses its strong legs to walk and its sharp claws to climb trees. Kakapos are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and sleep during the day. They have a wonderful sense of smell, which is unusual for a bird, and they smell sweet, like honey and flowers.

## Where Kakapos Live

Kakapos live only in New Zealand, a country made up of islands in the Pacific Ocean. Long ago, they lived all over New Zealand, in forests from the mountains to the coast. Today, they live only on a few small islands that are free from predators, where rangers can keep them safe. These islands have thick forests, with plenty of trees, shrubs, and plants for the kakapos to eat and hide in. During the day, kakapos sleep in burrows or under thick bushes. At night, they come out to wander through the forest, looking for food and calling to each other with deep, booming sounds.

## A Parrot That Cannot Fly

It may seem strange for a bird not to fly, but the kakapo has good reasons. Long ago, New Zealand had no land mammals that hunted birds, so the kakapo did not need to fly away from danger. It could walk safely on the forest floor and climb trees to find food. Its wings became small over time, because it did not use them for flying. Instead, the kakapo became a wonderful climber. It can climb high into trees using its beak and claws, and then it can glide down to the ground, spreading its wings like a parachute. It is a clever way to travel without flying.

## What Kakapos Eat

Kakapos are herbivores, which means they eat only plants. They love to eat leaves, seeds, fruits, berries, and the bark of trees. Their favourite food is the fruit of the rimu tree, which only grows well in some years. When the rimu trees have lots of fruit, kakapos eat well and have more babies. Kakapos use their beaks to crush seeds and peel fruit, and they use their feet to hold food while they eat. They also grind up leaves into a soft pulp and swallow the juice, spitting out the dry fibres. This is a special way of eating that only kakapos do, and it helps them get all the goodness from tough leaves.

## The Booming Call

Male kakapos have a very special way of finding a mate. During the breeding season, they dig shallow bowls in the ground and sit in them, puffing up their chests and making a deep, booming sound that can be heard from far away. This sound is called booming, and it can travel through the forest for a long distance. Female kakapos hear the booming and walk through the forest to find the male they like best. After mating, the female goes off alone to build a nest and raise her chicks. The male stays behind and keeps booming, hoping to attract another female. It is a funny and noisy way to find a partner.

## Baby Kakapos

A mother kakapo lays her eggs in a nest hidden in a burrow or under a thick bush. She sits on the eggs to keep them warm, and when the chicks hatch, she feeds them with soft, regurgitated food from her own stomach. The chicks are fluffy and helpless at first, and they depend on their mother for everything. She keeps them warm, feeds them, and protects them from danger. Young kakapos grow slowly, and they stay with their mother for many months before they are ready to live on their own. Rangers watch the nests carefully and sometimes help by feeding chicks by hand if the mother needs extra help.

## Saving the Kakapo

Kakapos are in great danger, and there are very few of them left in the world. Long ago, people brought rats, stoats, and cats to New Zealand, and these animals hunted kakapos and ate their eggs. Kakapos could not fly away, so they were easy prey. Today, rangers and scientists in New Zealand are working hard to save them. They move kakapos to safe islands with no predators, watch their nests with cameras, and help feed chicks when needed. Every kakapo has a name and is carefully looked after. Thanks to this hard work, the number of kakapos is slowly growing. The kakapo teaches us that when people work together, we can save even the rarest animals.

So next time you think of parrots, remember the kakapo, the chubby green night parrot that walks instead of flies and smells like honey. It is a gentle, funny, and precious bird, and the forests of New Zealand would not be the same without it.
