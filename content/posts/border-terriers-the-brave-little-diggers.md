Imagine a small dog with a scruffy coat, bright eyes and a heart as big as a lion. That is the border terrier. These little dogs may not be very tall, but they are brave, clever and full of energy. They were born to work on wild, hilly farms, and they love digging, chasing and exploring. If you want a dog that is always ready for an adventure, the border terrier might be the perfect friend for you.

## From the Border Hills

Border terriers come from the border country between England and Scotland. This is a wild and beautiful place with rolling hills, stone walls and wide open fields. Hundreds of years ago, farmers in this area needed a dog that could help them look after their sheep. The problem was foxes. Foxes would sneak into the hills and try to catch lambs, and the farmers needed a dog that could chase them away.

The border terrier was the answer. It was small enough to squeeze into fox dens and rocky burrows, but tough and brave enough to face a fox. It could run for miles across the hills alongside horses and farm workers. The farmers loved these dogs because they were hard workers and loyal friends.

The breed gets its name from the border itself. Border terriers were shaped by the land they worked on: small, sturdy and built to keep going all day, whatever the weather.

## Small Body, Big Heart

A border terrier is a small dog, about the size of a school bag when it stands on all fours. It has a wiry, rough coat that feels a bit like a scrubbing brush. This coat is very useful. It keeps the rain and cold out, and it protects the dog from thorns and brambles when it pushes through bushes.

Border terriers come in several colours. Some are red, like a ginger biscuit. Some are wheaten, which is a soft golden colour. Others are blue and tan, or grizzle and tan, which means their coats have a lovely mix of grey, brown and black hairs. Whatever the colour, every border terrier has the same clever face and bright, dark eyes.

People often say border terriers have an otter-shaped head. If you look at an otter, you will see a wide, flat head with a short muzzle. The border terrier's head looks a little bit like that, and it gives the dog a sweet, friendly expression. Their ears are small and fold forward, and their tails wag with excitement whenever something interesting happens.

## Born to Dig

If there is one thing border terriers love more than anything, it is digging. Digging is in their blood. Long ago, they dug into earth and burrows to find foxes, and that digging instinct is still very strong today. Give a border terrier a garden, and it will happily dig holes all afternoon. Give it a beach, and it will dig in the sand until its nose is dusty and its paws are sandy.

This means border terrier owners need a good sense of humour and a strong fence. These dogs are clever escape artists. If there is a gap under a fence, a border terrier will find it. If there is a way over a low wall, a border terrier will try it. They are not trying to be naughty. They are just curious and adventurous, and they love to explore.

Border terriers are also excellent at games. They love fetch, tug of war, and chasing a ball across the park. They are quick learners and enjoy training, especially when there are treats involved. Many border terriers do well in dog sports like agility, where they run through tunnels, jump over hurdles and weave between poles. Watching a border terrier fly around an agility course is like watching a tiny athlete at work.

## A Friendly Family Dog

For all their toughness, border terriers are gentle and loving at home. They love people, especially children, and they are happy to curl up on the sofa after a long day of adventures. They are cheerful dogs that seem to smile, and they often make their owners laugh with their silly behaviour.

Border terriers get along well with other dogs, because they were bred to work alongside hounds and other farm dogs. They are friendly and social, and they love meeting new friends at the park. They do need to be taught not to chase smaller animals, because their hunting instinct is still strong. A squirrel or a rabbit is very exciting to a border terrier, so owners keep them on a lead in places where small animals live.

These dogs need lots of exercise every day. A quick walk around the block is not enough for a border terrier. They need long walks, runs in the park, and games that keep their clever minds busy. A bored border terrier will find its own fun, and that usually means digging up the garden or chewing something it should not. A busy border terrier is a happy border terrier.

## Looking After a Border Terrier

Border terriers are not hard to look after, but they do have some special needs. Their wiry coats need brushing a few times a week to keep them tidy. Twice a year, their old coat is stripped out by hand, which is a special kind of grooming that keeps the coat healthy and rough. It does not hurt the dog, and afterwards the coat grows back fresh and smart.

Like all dogs, border terriers need good food, fresh water, regular visits to the vet, and lots of love. They live for many years, so a family that gets a border terrier puppy is choosing a friend for a very long time.

## Why We Love Them

Border terriers remind us that you do not have to be big to be brave. These little dogs have worked on wild hills, faced foxes in dark burrows, and run for miles without giving up. Yet at home they are gentle, funny and loving. They are proof that the best friends often come in small, scruffy packages.

So if you ever meet a border terrier, give it a friendly pat. Look into its bright eyes and you will see a dog that is ready for anything: a walk, a game, a dig in the garden, or a cuddle on the sofa. That is the magic of the border terrier, the brave little digger from the border hills.
