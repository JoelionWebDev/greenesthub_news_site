Imagine a soft, glowing umbrella floating slowly through blue water. It pulses gently, opening and closing like a slow heartbeat, and long, silky threads trail behind it. That is a jellyfish. Jellyfish are some of the strangest and most beautiful animals in the sea. They have no bones, no brain, and no heart, yet they have lived in the oceans for a very long time. They drift with the currents, glowing softly in the deep blue.

Jellyfish live in oceans all over the world, from warm, sunny seas to cold, deep waters. Some live near the shore, where children can see them from a boat or a beach. Others live far out in the open sea, or deep down where the sunlight hardly reaches. Wherever they live, jellyfish move slowly and gently, carried by the water around them.

## A Body Like Soft Jelly

A jellyfish has a very simple body. Its main part is a soft, round bell, a bit like an umbrella or a bowl turned upside down. The bell is made of a jelly-like substance, and it is mostly water. That is why jellyfish feel soft and wobbly when they are washed up on a beach, and why they look almost clear in the water.

Under the bell hang long, thin parts called tentacles. Tentacles look like ribbons or threads, and they trail behind the jellyfish as it floats. Some jellyfish have short tentacles, and some have very long ones. The tentacles help the jellyfish catch food and protect itself. They are the jellyfish's most important tools.

Jellyfish have no bones to hold them up, no brain to think with, and no heart to pump blood. Instead, their simple bodies work in a different way. They do not need bones because the water holds them up, and they do not need a brain because they live by simple, gentle movements. It is amazing that an animal can live so well with such a simple body.

## How Jellyfish Move

Jellyfish do not swim like fish. They do not have fins or tails, and they cannot swim fast. Instead, they move by squeezing their bell, then letting it open again. Each squeeze pushes water out and moves the jellyfish forward a little. It is a slow, gentle, pulsing movement, a bit like a balloon filling and emptying.

Most of the time, jellyfish do not choose where they go. They drift with the ocean currents, the great rivers of water that flow through the sea. The currents carry them from place to place, sometimes for a long way. That is why jellyfish are called drifters. They go where the sea takes them.

Some jellyfish can move up and down in the water. They rise towards the surface when they want to find food, and they sink down again when they want to rest or hide. They move slowly and calmly, and they never hurry. Watching a jellyfish drift is like watching a slow, peaceful dance in the water.

## What Jellyfish Eat

Jellyfish eat tiny animals and plants that float in the sea. Their food includes small fish, tiny shrimp, and little creatures called plankton. Plankton are very small animals and plants that drift in the water, too small for us to see without a microscope. Jellyfish catch them with their tentacles as they drift by.

The tentacles of a jellyfish have tiny stinging cells. When a small animal touches a tentacle, the sting stops it from moving, and the jellyfish can then bring the food up to its mouth. The mouth is under the bell, in the middle of the jellyfish's body. It is a simple way to eat, and it works very well.

Jellyfish are food for other animals too. Sea turtles love to eat jellyfish, and some fish and seabirds eat them as well. Jellyfish are an important part of the ocean food chain. They eat tiny creatures, and bigger animals eat them, and in this way energy moves through the sea.

## Glowing in the Dark

Some jellyfish can glow. In the deep sea, where it is dark, a jellyfish may light up with soft blue, green, or pink light. This glow is called bioluminescence, and it is made by the jellyfish's own body. It is a wonderful sight, like a lantern floating in the dark water.

The glow can help a jellyfish in different ways. It may scare away animals that want to eat it, or it may help it find food. Some jellyfish glow when they are touched, lighting up for a moment like a tiny firework. In the deep sea, where there is no sunlight, glowing jellyfish are some of the most beautiful lights in the ocean.

Not all jellyfish glow, and not all of them live in the deep sea. Many jellyfish live near the surface, where the sun shines through the water. Their bells can be pink, blue, orange, or clear, and they shine in the sunlight like glass. Each kind of jellyfish has its own colour and shape.

## Staying Safe Around Jellyfish

Jellyfish are gentle drifters, but their tentacles can sting. The sting helps them catch food, but it can hurt people too. If you see a jellyfish on the beach or in the water, it is best to look but not touch. Even a jellyfish that has washed up on the sand can still sting, so it is important to keep a safe distance.

If you are swimming in the sea and you see jellyfish, tell a grown-up and swim in a different place. Lifeguards and local people often know when jellyfish are in the water, and they can tell you where it is safe to swim. Looking at jellyfish from a boat, from the shore, or in an aquarium is the best way to enjoy them safely.

Jellyfish have lived in the oceans for longer than almost any other animal. They were drifting in the sea long before people, long before many fish, and long before the first dinosaurs walked on land. They remind us that the ocean is full of strange and wonderful life. The next time you think of the sea, picture a soft, glowing jellyfish, drifting slowly and peacefully through the blue water, just as it has done for a very long time.
