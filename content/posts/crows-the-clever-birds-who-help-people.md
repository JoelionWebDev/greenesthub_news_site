Have you ever looked up at a black bird sitting on a roof or a wire and wondered what it was thinking? It may have been a crow — one of the cleverest birds in the whole world. Crows can solve puzzles, use tools, remember people's faces and even help humans keep towns clean. They are not just noisy black birds. They are smart, playful and full of surprises. Come and meet them.

## Meet the Crow

Crows belong to a bird family called the corvids. Their cousins include ravens, rooks, jackdaws and magpies, and all of them are known for being clever. A crow is about the size of a pigeon, but much bigger and stronger. It has shiny black feathers, a strong beak and bright, watchful eyes.

Crows live in many parts of the world. You can find them in towns and cities, in fields and forests, on farms and near the sea. They are very good at living close to people, and they have learned to find food in all kinds of places. In many towns, crows wake up early and go to work looking for breakfast, just like people do.

There are different kinds of crows. The carrion crow lives in Europe, the hooded crow has a grey body with a black head, and the American crow lives across North America. In Australia there is the Torresian crow. Even though they live far apart, all crows share the same big brains and curious minds.

## A Brain Like a Little Computer

For their size, crows have very large brains. Scientists who study birds say that a crow's brain works a bit like the brain of a clever mammal. Crows can count, remember things for a long time and plan ahead. They can solve puzzles that would puzzle a small child — and sometimes a grown-up too!

One famous kind of crow, the New Caledonian crow, lives on a group of islands in the Pacific Ocean. These crows make tools. They bend twigs into hooks and use them to fish insects out of holes in trees. They even trim leaves into shapes that work like little scoops. No other wild bird is known to make tools quite like this.

## Crows Never Forget a Face

Here is something amazing: crows can remember human faces. Scientists at a university in America did a clever test. They wore scary masks while catching crows for a short study, then let them go. Years later, the same crows still scolded and warned other crows when they saw those masks again. The crows even told their children and friends, who had never seen the masks, to beware of them too!

But crows remember kind faces as well as unkind ones. People who feed crows often find that the birds begin to recognise them. Some crows even bring little gifts to people who are kind to them — shiny buttons, small stones, bits of coloured glass and lost earrings. One young girl in America fed crows in her garden for years, and the crows left her a whole collection of little treasures on her bird table.

So if you are ever kind to a crow, it might just remember you. And if you are unkind, it might remember that too. That is why it is always best to be gentle with wild birds.

## Crows at Play

Crows are not always serious. They love to play! People have seen crows sliding down snowy roofs on their backs, like children on a sled. They drop sticks and chase them in the air. They tease cats and dogs by pulling their tails and flying away just in time. They even play games with each other, passing objects back and forth in the air.

Playing is not just for fun. When young animals play, they practise the skills they will need as grown-ups. A crow that plays with sticks is learning how to use tools. A crow that chases a friend in the air is learning how to fly fast and turn quickly. Play helps their brains grow, just as play helps your brain grow.

## The Crows Who Pick Up Litter

Crows do not only help themselves — they can help people too. In a town in Sweden, people trained wild crows to pick up cigarette ends from the streets. The crows dropped the cigarette ends into a special machine, and the machine gave them a little food as a reward. The crows learned very quickly, and the streets got cleaner.

In other places, people have taught crows to pick up small bits of rubbish and drop them into a bin. The crows do the job because they are rewarded, and the town gets cleaner. It is a clever idea that works because crows are so good at learning and because they like shiny things and small objects.

Of course, crows do not know they are cleaning the town. They are just playing a game that gives them food. But it shows how people and clever animals can work together to solve a problem. When we understand what an animal is good at, we can find ways to help each other.

## Fun Facts About Crows
- A group of crows is called a murder — a strange name for such clever birds!
- Crows can live for a long time. Some wild crows live for many years, and crows looked after by people have lived even longer.
- Crows can recognise themselves in a mirror, which is something only a few clever animals can do.
- The New Caledonian crow makes hooks from twigs, a skill that no other wild bird is known to share.
- Crows warn each other about danger with special alarm calls, and other birds listen and fly away too.

Next time you see a crow, stop and say hello in your mind. That clever black bird might be watching you, remembering your face, and thinking its own clever thoughts. Crows remind us that animals are smarter than we sometimes think — and that people and animals can be good neighbours, learning from each other every single day.
