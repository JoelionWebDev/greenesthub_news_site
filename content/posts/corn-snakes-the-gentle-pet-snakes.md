Some people think all snakes are scary, but corn snakes are here to change your mind. Corn snakes are calm, gentle, and beautiful. They have smooth scales in warm shades of orange, red, and yellow, with pretty patterns on their backs. They move slowly, they like to explore, and they are one of the most popular pet snakes in the world. A corn snake will curl up in a cosy hiding place, come out to explore, and flick its tongue to smell the world around it.

## What is a corn snake?

A corn snake is a kind of snake, and snakes are reptiles. That means a corn snake has scaly skin, lays eggs, and likes to feel warm. A corn snake has a long, smooth body with no legs at all. It moves by sliding its belly along the ground, using its strong muscles to push itself forward. Its scales are smooth and shiny, and they feel a bit like cool, soft leather. Corn snakes get their name because people once found them in corn fields, where they hunted mice. A corn snake has a small head, bright eyes, and a forked tongue that flicks in and out. The tongue helps the snake smell the air and find its way. Corn snakes are not venomous, which means they do not have poison, and they are gentle by nature.

## Where do corn snakes live?

Wild corn snakes live in warm places in North America. You can find them in fields, forests, and rocky hills. They like places with lots of hiding spots, like logs, rocks, and piles of leaves. Corn snakes are good climbers, and they sometimes climb trees and bushes to explore. They are most active in the evening and at night, when it is cooler. During the day, they rest in a dark, safe place. Pet corn snakes live indoors in a special home called a terrarium. Their home has a warm side and a cool side, so the snake can choose where to rest. It also has hiding places, a water dish, and things to climb on. A corn snake's home should be safe and secure, with a lid that fits tightly.

## What do corn snakes eat?

Corn snakes are hunters, and they eat small animals. In the wild, a corn snake eats mice, small rats, and sometimes small birds or lizards. It finds its food by smell, using its flicking tongue to taste the air. A corn snake does not chew its food. It swallows its food whole, and its strong body helps it digest the meal slowly. Pet corn snakes eat small mice that are bought frozen from a pet shop and warmed up by a grown-up. A grown-up should always be the one to feed a corn snake. A corn snake does not need to eat every day. It eats once every week or so, and then it rests while its food digests. Fresh water is very important too. A corn snake drinks from a water dish, and it also likes to soak in the water sometimes.

## A day in a corn snake's life

A corn snake's day is calm and quiet. During the day, it rests in a hiding place, curled up in a cosy ball. It likes dark, warm spots where it feels safe. When evening comes, the corn snake wakes up and starts to explore. It slides around its home, flicks its tongue, and sniffs the air. It may climb a branch, hide under a log, or curl up in a new spot. Corn snakes are curious animals, and they like to find out what is around them. A corn snake sheds its skin as it grows. The old skin peels off in one long piece, like taking off a sock, and fresh new skin appears underneath.

## Baby corn snakes

Baby corn snakes hatch from eggs. The mother corn snake lays her eggs in a warm, safe place, often hidden under leaves or in soft soil. The eggs are small, white, and soft, and they feel a bit leathery. The baby snakes grow inside the eggs until they are ready to hatch. When the time comes, the babies push their way out with a tiny egg tooth. Baby corn snakes are very small, but they already look like their parents, with bright patterns on their smooth scales. They can move, climb, and hunt for tiny food almost straight away. A baby corn snake grows quickly, and it sheds its skin many times as it gets bigger. Young corn snakes need a warm home, fresh water, and small meals. With good care, a baby corn snake grows into a healthy adult, and pet corn snakes can live for many years.

## Looking after a pet corn snake

Corn snakes can be lovely pets, but they need a grown-up to help look after them. A pet corn snake needs a warm, clean home with hiding places, fresh water, and the right food. Its home should be cleaned often so it stays healthy. Always move slowly and let the snake get used to you. A grown-up should help you hold a corn snake, supporting its body with both hands. Never squeeze a snake, and never grab it by its tail. Always wash your hands before and after holding a pet snake, and always ask a grown-up for help. A corn snake is not a toy, and it needs quiet time to rest.

## Fun facts about corn snakes

- Corn snakes are not venomous, which means they do not have poison.
- A corn snake smells the world with its forked tongue, flicking it in and out of its mouth.
- Corn snakes got their name because people once found them in corn fields, hunting mice.
- A corn snake sheds its skin in one long piece, like taking off a sock.
- Corn snakes are good climbers, and they sometimes climb trees and bushes.
- Pet corn snakes can live for many years when they are well looked after.

Corn snakes are gentle, beautiful, and full of quiet charm. They slide through their days, exploring, resting, and flicking their tongues to smell the world. A corn snake is not scary at all. It is a calm, curious friend.
