Imagine an animal so big that its tongue weighs as much as an elephant. Imagine a creature longer than a school bus, with a heart about the size of a small car. That animal is real, and it swims in the oceans right now. It is the blue whale — the biggest animal that has ever lived on Earth, even bigger than the largest dinosaurs that walked the land millions of years ago.

Blue whales are gentle giants. Even though they are huge, they are calm, peaceful animals that eat some of the tiniest creatures in the sea. They glide through deep blue water, singing songs that can travel for miles. Let us dive down and meet them.

## The Biggest Animal Ever

A blue whale can grow to be about as long as three school buses lined up end to end. That is a truly enormous animal. Its body is long and smooth, with a blue-grey colour that looks lighter under the water. The blue colour is what gives the blue whale its name, although up close its skin often looks more grey, with pale spots and speckles.

## A Heart as Big as a Car

Inside a blue whale is a heart so big that a small child could almost climb inside it. It is about the size of a small car, and it pumps blood through a body the size of a great hall. The whale's blood vessels are so wide that a person could almost crawl through the largest ones — although nobody would ever want to try!

A blue whale's heartbeat is slow and powerful. When the whale dives deep into the ocean, its heart slows down to save oxygen. When it comes back up to the surface, its heart beats faster again. This clever trick helps the whale stay under the water for a long time while it hunts for food.

## What Do Blue Whales Eat?

Here is a funny surprise: the biggest animal on Earth eats some of the smallest animals in the sea. Blue whales eat tiny shrimp-like creatures called krill. Krill are only about as long as your finger, but blue whales eat them by the thousands — huge mouthfuls at a time.

A blue whale has no teeth. Instead, it has rows of bristly plates in its mouth called baleen. The baleen works like a giant sieve or strainer. Here is how it works: the whale swims into a huge cloud of krill with its mouth wide open, taking in a giant gulp of seawater and krill. Then it pushes the water out through the baleen, and the krill get caught in the bristles. The whale then swallows all the krill in one big gulp.

Blue whales need to eat a lot of krill every day to keep their giant bodies going. In the summer, when krill are plentiful in the cold waters near the poles, whales eat and eat, storing up energy. In the winter, they travel to warmer waters, where they eat less and use the energy they stored.

## How Whales Talk and Sing

Blue whales are famous for their songs. They make deep, low sounds that can travel for very long distances under the water — much farther than sound travels in air. A blue whale's call is one of the loudest sounds made by any animal on Earth. Even so, people cannot usually hear it without special equipment, because much of the sound is too low for our ears.

Scientists think whales use these sounds to talk to each other across the ocean. They may call to find friends, to keep in touch with other whales, or to find a mate. Each whale's song can be a little different, and whales in different parts of the world may sing different songs.

## Travelling the Oceans

Blue whales are great travellers. They migrate, which means they travel long distances at certain times of the year. In the summer, they swim to the cold, rich waters near the North and South Poles, where krill are plentiful. In the winter, they swim to warmer tropical waters, where mothers give birth to their calves.

These journeys can be thousands of miles long. The whales swim steadily, day after day, crossing whole oceans. They are strong swimmers, and their huge tails push them through the water with slow, powerful strokes. When they are travelling, they can swim at a steady speed for hours and hours.

## Fun Facts About Blue Whales

- Blue whales are the largest animals ever known to have lived on Earth.
- A blue whale's tongue can weigh as much as an elephant.
- A blue whale's heart is about the size of a small car.
- Blue whales eat tiny creatures called krill, even though they are enormous.
- A blue whale has no teeth — it strains its food through bristly baleen plates.
- A blue whale's song is one of the loudest sounds made by any animal.
- A newborn blue whale calf is already longer than a big family car.
- When a blue whale breathes out, its spout can rise high into the air.

## Protecting the Giants of the Sea

Long ago, blue whales were hunted in great numbers, and their population fell very low. People hunted them for their oil and meat, and for a while it looked as if these gentle giants might disappear forever. But then countries around the world agreed to protect them, and hunting was stopped in most places.

Today, blue whales are still rare, but their numbers are slowly growing again. Scientists study them by listening to their songs, taking photographs of their markings, and tracking their journeys across the oceans. Each blue whale has unique speckles on its skin, so scientists can recognise individual whales from photographs, just as we recognise our friends by their faces.

Blue whales remind us how wonderful and surprising our planet is. The biggest animal on Earth is a gentle giant that sings in the deep and eats some of the smallest creatures in the sea. If we look after our oceans, these magnificent animals will keep gliding through the blue water for many years to come.
