# Crocodiles: The Ancient Hunters of the Rivers

Long, long ago, giant reptiles walked the Earth with the dinosaurs. Today, one of their closest living cousins still swims in our rivers and lakes. It is the crocodile. Crocodiles have lived on Earth for millions of years, and they have changed very little in all that time. They are strong, patient, and perfectly made for life in the water.

## Meet the Crocodile

A crocodile is a large reptile with a long body, a long tail, and four short legs. Its skin is covered in tough, bumpy scales that look like armour. These scales protect it from bumps, bites, and the hot sun. Its back is usually dark green or brown, which helps it hide in muddy water and among reeds.

A crocodile has a long snout filled with many sharp teeth. When it closes its mouth, you can still see some of its teeth sticking out. Its eyes and nostrils sit on top of its head. This is very clever. A crocodile can float in the water with only its eyes and nose above the surface, while the rest of its body stays hidden below.

Crocodiles live in warm places around the world. In Africa, the Nile crocodile lives in rivers and lakes. In Australia, the saltwater crocodile lives in rivers and along the coast. Other kinds live in Asia and the Americas. Wherever they live, they need warm water and sunny banks to rest on.

## A Body Built for Water

Crocodiles are wonderful swimmers. Their long, powerful tails push them through the water like a rudder and a motor in one. They can swim fast when they need to, and they can also float quietly, barely moving at all. Their legs tuck close to their bodies when they swim, so the water slides past them easily.

On land, crocodiles move in a different way. They can crawl on their bellies, and they can also lift their bodies up and walk on their legs. They are not built for running long distances, but they can move quickly in a short burst when they need to.

Crocodiles are cold-blooded, which means their bodies do not make their own heat. They warm up by basking in the sun. You will often see a crocodile lying still on a riverbank with its mouth open. It is not yawning. It is letting the sun warm its body, and opening its mouth helps it cool down a little.

## Patient Hunters

Crocodiles are patient hunters. They do not chase their food across the land. Instead, they wait. A crocodile may float in the water for hours, looking like a floating log. Animals that come to the river to drink may not notice it at all. Then, in a sudden splash, the crocodile moves.

Crocodiles eat fish, birds, and animals that come close to the water. Their strong jaws snap shut fast, and their teeth hold on tight. They cannot chew like we do. Instead, they tear their food into pieces that they can swallow. Their stomachs are very strong and can break down bones and tough food.

Young crocodiles eat small things like insects, frogs, and small fish. As they grow bigger, they can catch bigger food. A crocodile grows all through its life, but it grows more slowly as it gets older.

## The Death Roll

One of the most famous things about crocodiles is the death roll. When a crocodile catches something big, it may spin its whole body around and around in the water. This rolling helps it tear off a piece of food that is too big to swallow whole. It looks wild and scary, but it is a smart way for an animal without hands to break up its meal.

Crocodiles also use their tails in other ways. A strong swish of the tail can push them forward in a hurry. Mother crocodiles use their tails to guard their nests. The tail is one of the strongest parts of a crocodile's body.

## Mothers and Babies

Mother crocodiles are caring parents. The mother lays her eggs in a nest made of plants and mud near the water. She stays close to the nest and guards it from animals that might want to eat the eggs. She does not eat much while she guards, because keeping the eggs safe is more important.

When the baby crocodiles are ready to hatch, they make small chirping sounds from inside their eggs. The mother hears them and gently digs the nest open. She may even carry the tiny babies to the water in her mouth. Her big jaws are gentle enough to hold them without hurting them.

Baby crocodiles are called hatchlings. They are small, only about as long as a school ruler, and they have stripes that help them hide. They stay near their mother for a while, and she protects them from danger. Many baby crocodiles do not survive their first year, because birds, fish, and other animals hunt them. The ones that survive grow into strong adults.

## Ancient Survivors

Crocodiles have survived for a very long time because they are so good at what they do. They can go for a long time without food. They can stay under water for a while by holding their breath. They can live in rivers, lakes, swamps, and even salty sea water.

But crocodiles still need our help. They need clean rivers and safe places to lay their eggs. In some places, people hunted too many crocodiles, and their numbers fell. Now, many countries protect crocodiles, and people work hard to keep their homes safe.

Crocodiles are not pets, and we should always watch them from a safe distance. They are wild animals, and they deserve respect. From far away, we can admire their strength, their patience, and their amazing history.

The next time you see a crocodile floating in the water, remember that you are looking at a living piece of ancient history. It has been hunting in rivers since long before people walked the Earth, and with our care, it will keep swimming for many years to come.
