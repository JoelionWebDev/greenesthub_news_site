## The Pinkest Birds in the World

If you have ever seen a flamingo, you will never forget it. Flamingos are tall, elegant birds with long legs, long necks, and feathers in shades of pink, from soft blush to bright coral. They wade through shallow lakes and lagoons, looking like something from a fairy tale.

But here is a big surprise: flamingos are not born pink! Baby flamingos are grey or white when they hatch. They only turn pink as they grow up, because of the food they eat. It is one of the most amazing colour changes in the animal world, and it all starts with dinner.

Flamingos live in warm parts of the world, in Africa, Asia, the Americas, and Europe. They love salty or shallow lakes, and they gather in huge flocks, sometimes with thousands of birds standing together in the water.

## Why Are Flamingos Pink?

The secret to a flamingo's pink feathers is in its food. Flamingos eat tiny creatures that live in the water, such as small shrimp, algae, and other tiny plants and animals. These foods contain natural colouring that is orange and pink, a bit like the colour in carrots.

When a flamingo eats this food, the colour travels into its feathers, its beak, and even its legs. The more of this food a flamingo eats, the pinker it becomes. A healthy flamingo that eats well is bright pink. A flamingo that does not get enough of this food can become pale, almost white.

So a flamingo's pink colour is a sign that it is eating well and staying healthy. It is like a badge that says, "I have been eating my dinner!"

This is also why flamingo parents can lose their pink colour. When they are feeding their chicks, they give so much goodness to their babies that their own feathers can turn pale. Once the chicks grow up, their pink colour comes back.

## Eating Upside Down

Flamingos have one of the strangest ways of eating in the bird world. They eat with their heads upside down! A flamingo dips its head into the water, turns its beak upside down, and sweeps it from side to side, like a vacuum cleaner.

Their beaks are specially shaped for this. Inside the beak are tiny comb-like parts that work like a strainer. When the flamingo scoops up water, the strainer lets the water out but keeps the tiny food inside. Then the flamingo swallows its meal.

## Why Do Flamingos Stand on One Leg?

One of the most famous flamingo sights is a flamingo standing on one leg, with the other leg tucked up under its body. People have wondered about this for a long time. Why would a bird stand on one leg when it has two?

Scientists think there are a few reasons. One is that standing on one leg helps a flamingo stay warm. Flamingos spend hours standing in cold water, and tucking one leg up into their warm feathers helps them lose less body heat. It is like putting one hand in your pocket on a cold day.

Another reason is rest. Flamingos can lock one leg in place and rest the other one, a bit like we might shift our weight from one foot to the other when we are standing for a long time.

So if you see a flamingo standing on one leg, do not worry. It is not hurt. It is just keeping warm and comfortable, the flamingo way.

## Flamingo Families

Flamingos are very social birds. They live in large flocks, and they do almost everything together: feeding, resting, and even dancing! During the breeding season, flamingos gather in groups and perform dances together, marching, turning their heads from side to side, and flapping their wings.

Flamingo parents build their nests out of mud, shaped like a little volcano with a dip on top. The mother lays one egg, and both parents take turns sitting on it to keep it warm. When the chick hatches, it is grey and fluffy, with a straight beak that will bend as it grows.

Flamingo parents feed their chicks with a special food called crop milk, which they make in their own bodies. It is rich and nourishing, and it helps the chick grow quickly. Both the mother and the father can make this food, so they share the feeding work.

Young flamingos stay with their parents until they are strong enough to feed themselves. Then they join the flock, and slowly, as they eat the pink-coloured food, their grey feathers begin to turn pink.

## Flamingo Chicks and Crèches

Flamingo chicks have a clever way of staying safe. When they are a bit older, many chicks gather together in a big group called a crèche, a bit like a nursery or a kindergarten. While the chicks huddle together, the parent flamingos can go off to feed, knowing their babies are safer in a big group than alone.

The chicks in a crèche are looked after by a few adult flamingos, and the parents come back to feed their own chick. But how does a parent find its own chick among hundreds of others? By its voice! Each chick has its own special call, and its parents can pick it out from the crowd.

## Fun Facts

- Flamingos are not born pink. Baby flamingos are grey or white.
- Their pink colour comes from the food they eat, like tiny shrimp and algae.
- Flamingos eat with their heads upside down, sweeping their beaks through the water.
- Their beaks work like strainers, letting water out and keeping tiny food in.
- Flamingos often stand on one leg to stay warm and rest the other leg.
- Flamingos dance together in groups to find a partner.
- They build nests out of mud, shaped like little volcanoes.
- Parents feed their chicks with special crop milk made in their own bodies.
- Chicks gather in big groups called crèches, like a bird kindergarten.
- Flamingos can drink salty water, thanks to special glands that remove extra salt.
## A Bird Like No Other

Flamingos are proof that nature loves colour and surprises. A bird that starts life grey, turns pink because of its dinner, eats upside down, and stands on one leg is a bird worth celebrating.

Next time you see a flamingo, remember the journey it has been on: from a fluffy grey chick to one of the pinkest, most graceful birds on Earth. And remember, it is all thanks to a good dinner!
