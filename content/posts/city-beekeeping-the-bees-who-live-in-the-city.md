When you think of bees, you might picture a quiet farm or a sunny meadow full of flowers. But did you know that millions of bees live in cities too? On rooftops, in gardens, in parks, and even on top of tall buildings, bees are hard at work. City beekeeping is the practice of keeping beehives in towns and cities, and it is becoming more and more popular. The bees who live in the city are just as busy as their country cousins, and sometimes they even make more honey.

## Bees in the Big City

A city might not seem like a good home for bees. There are cars, buildings, and lots of people. But cities have a secret that bees love: flowers. Parks, gardens, window boxes, and street trees are full of flowers for much of the year. Bees fly from flower to flower, collecting nectar and pollen, and they can find food in a city almost all year round.

Bees are also very good at living high up. A hive on a rooftop is safe from many of the animals that might bother bees on the ground. The rooftop is warm and sunny, and the bees have a clear path to fly out over the city. From high above, a bee can see parks and gardens in every direction. It is like living in a tower with a view of a thousand flower shops.

## Who Keeps City Bees?

City beekeepers are people who look after beehives in towns and cities. Some are farmers who have moved their hives into the city. Others are ordinary people who learned beekeeping as a hobby. They might keep a hive on the roof of their house, in their back garden, or on top of an office building. Some hotels, schools, and museums keep bees too, and they share the honey with their guests and visitors.

A beekeeper wears a special suit to protect them from stings. The suit is usually white, with a veil that covers the face. The beekeeper also uses a smoker, a small tool that puffs gentle smoke into the hive. The smoke calms the bees and helps the beekeeper to work safely. The beekeeper opens the hive carefully, checks that the bees are healthy, and makes sure the queen is laying eggs. It is careful, gentle work, and a good beekeeper moves slowly so as not to upset the bees.

## Inside the Hive

A beehive is like a tiny city of its own. Inside, there are thousands of bees, and each one has a job to do. The queen bee is the mother of the whole hive. She lays all the eggs. The worker bees are all female, and they do almost all the work. Some workers collect nectar and pollen from flowers. Some build the wax honeycomb. Some look after the baby bees, and some guard the entrance of the hive. The male bees, called drones, do not work. Their only job is to help the hive make new queens.

Bees talk to each other in a very special way. When a worker bee finds a good patch of flowers, she flies back to the hive and does a dance. The dance tells the other bees which way to fly and how far to go. It is called the waggle dance, and it is one of the most amazing things in nature. Imagine giving directions by dancing, and your friends understanding you perfectly.

## How Bees Make Honey

Bees make honey from nectar, the sweet liquid inside flowers. A worker bee drinks the nectar and carries it back to the hive in a special stomach. Back at the hive, she passes the nectar to another bee, who passes it on again. Each bee adds something to the nectar that helps to turn it into honey. Then the bees fan their wings to dry the honey out, and they seal it into the wax honeycomb with a thin cap of wax. A jar of honey is the result of many thousands of flower visits. That is why honey is so precious.

City bees often make lots of honey because cities have so many different kinds of flowers. The honey from a city hive can taste different depending on which flowers the bees visited. Honey from a hive near a park might taste light and floral, while honey from a hive near lime trees might taste rich and golden. Every jar tells a story about the city where it was made.

## Why City Bees Matter

Bees are not just honey-makers. They are pollinators, which means they help plants to make seeds and fruit. When a bee visits a flower to collect nectar, pollen sticks to its body. When it visits the next flower, some of that pollen rubs off. This helps the plant to make fruit and seeds. Without bees, many of the fruits and vegetables we love, like apples, strawberries, and cucumbers, would be much harder to grow.

Sadly, bees are in trouble in many parts of the world. Their homes are disappearing, and they face diseases and harmful chemicals. City beekeeping helps by giving bees more safe places to live and more flowers to visit. It also helps people to learn about bees and to care for them. When people see a hive on a rooftop, they start to think about where their food comes from and how important bees are.

## What You Can Do to Help Bees

You do not need to keep a hive to help city bees. You can plant flowers that bees love, like lavender, sunflowers, and rosemary. You can leave a small dish of water with stones in it, so thirsty bees have somewhere safe to drink. And you can learn to stay calm around bees. Bees do not want to sting you. They only sting if they feel scared or trapped. If a bee flies near you, stand still and let it pass. It is just on its way to the next flower.

City beekeeping shows us that nature and cities can live side by side. High above the traffic, on a sunny rooftop, thousands of tiny bees are dancing, collecting nectar, and making golden honey. The next time you walk through a park and hear a soft buzzing sound, look up. A busy bee might be flying home, carrying a little gift of nectar for its hive.
