## Meet the Red Panda

High up in the misty mountain forests of the Himalayas lives a small, fluffy animal with rusty-red fur, a long ringed tail, and a cute, round face. That is the red panda! People sometimes call it the little fire fox, because its reddish fur looks like the colour of a flame, and its face looks a bit like a fox.

But here is a surprise: a red panda is not really a panda at all, and it is not a fox either! Giant pandas are bears, but red pandas are in their own special family. They are about the size of a house cat, with a long, bushy tail that helps them balance in the trees.

Red pandas live in the forests of Nepal, India, Bhutan, Myanmar, and China. They live high in the mountains, where the air is cool and misty, and where bamboo grows in thick green clumps. Bamboo is their favourite food, and they spend most of their day eating it.

## The Bamboo Lover

Red pandas love bamboo more than almost anything else. They eat bamboo leaves and tender shoots, and they can eat a lot of it in one day. They use their front paws like hands to hold the bamboo while they munch, which looks very cute indeed!

A red panda has a special trick for eating bamboo. On each front paw, it has an extra little bone that works a bit like a thumb. This false thumb helps it grip bamboo stems tightly. Giant pandas have the same clever trick, even though the two animals are not close cousins.

Bamboo is not very filling, so red pandas need to eat a lot of it to get enough energy. They spend most of their waking hours munching away.

## A Tail Like a Blanket

A red panda's tail is one of its most wonderful features. It is long and bushy, with rings of red and pale cream, and it is almost as long as the red panda's body. The tail helps the red panda balance as it climbs along branches, like a tightrope walker using a long pole.

When a red panda goes to sleep, it curls up into a ball and wraps its big, fluffy tail around itself like a blanket. That keeps it warm on cold mountain nights, and it also hides its face from the wind. It is one of the cutest sights in the animal world!

Red pandas are excellent climbers. Their sharp claws help them grip tree bark, and their ankles can twist so that they can climb down a tree head first, just like a squirrel. They spend most of their life up in the trees, and they feel safest there.

## A Face Full of Markings

Look closely at a red panda's face, and you will see beautiful white markings. It has white cheeks, white patches above its eyes, and a dark stripe that runs from each eye down to its mouth. Scientists think these markings might help the sun's glare stay out of its eyes, like the dark lines that football players paint under their eyes.

Red pandas also have big, pointed ears with white edges, and a small, dark nose. Their fur is thick and soft, and it keeps them warm in the cold mountain forests. The reddish colour of their fur helps them hide among the reddish-brown moss and leaves of the trees where they live.

Red pandas are mostly active at dawn and dusk, and they rest during the middle of the day. They are quiet, gentle animals, and they do not like to be disturbed.

## A Red Panda Family

A mother red panda usually has one or two cubs at a time, and sometimes three or four. The cubs are born in a nest made in a hollow tree or in a rocky cave. When they are born, they are tiny and blind, and they are covered in soft, greyish fur.

The mother takes great care of her cubs. She nurses them and keeps them warm and safe in the nest. The cubs stay in the nest for about three months, and then they start to explore the world outside. By the time they are about a year old, they are ready to live on their own.

Cubs learn how to climb and how to eat bamboo by watching their mother and by practising. They are playful little animals, and they love to wrestle and chase each other in the trees.

## Life in the Mountain Forest

Red pandas live in cool, wet forests where bamboo grows thickly under tall trees. They need these forests to survive, because without bamboo they would have nothing to eat. They also need tall trees to climb and hide in, and clean water to drink.

Red pandas are very shy animals, and they are hard to spot in the wild. They move slowly and quietly through the trees, and their reddish fur blends in with the moss and leaves. If you are lucky enough to see one, you will never forget it!

Scientists are still learning about red pandas. They used to think there was only one kind, but now they know there are two kinds: the Himalayan red panda and the Chinese red panda.

## Red Pandas Need Our Help

Sadly, red pandas are in danger. Their forests are being cut down, which means they are losing the bamboo they need to eat and the trees they need to live in. They are also in danger from dogs, from traps, and from people who want to keep them as pets, which is not good for a wild animal.

There are fewer red pandas in the wild than there used to be, and people are working hard to protect them. They are planting bamboo, protecting forests, and teaching people why red pandas are special. Zoos around the world also help by caring for red pandas and teaching visitors about them.

When we protect red pandas, we protect their whole forest home, and all the other animals that live there too. You can help by learning about red pandas, telling your friends about them, and supporting people who protect forests.

Next time you see a picture of a red panda curled up in a tree with its fluffy tail wrapped around it like a blanket, remember how special it is. Small, fluffy, and full of charm, the red panda is one of the cutest animals in the world. What a little fire fox!
