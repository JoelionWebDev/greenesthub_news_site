## Meet the Meerkat

In the hot, sandy Kalahari Desert in southern Africa lives a small, brave animal that stands up on its back legs like a tiny person. That is the meerkat! Meerkats are small members of the mongoose family, and they are famous for standing tall and keeping watch over their friends.

A meerkat is about the size of a squirrel, with light brown fur, dark stripes on its back, and dark patches around its eyes that look like a little mask. Its belly is pale, and its long tail helps it balance when it stands up tall.

Meerkats live in groups called mobs, or sometimes gangs or clans. A mob can have about twenty meerkats, and sometimes many more. They live together in burrows, which are tunnels they dig under the ground. Living together helps them stay safe, find food, and look after their babies.

## The Brave Lookout

One of the most famous things about meerkats is their lookout duty. While most of the mob is busy looking for food, one meerkat climbs up to a high spot, like the top of a bush or a rock, and stands on its back legs to keep watch. This meerkat is called a sentry, or a lookout.

The sentry scans the sky and the ground for danger, such as eagles, hawks, jackals, and snakes. Meerkats have excellent eyesight, and they can see a bird of prey flying far away in the sky. If the sentry spots danger, it makes a loud barking sound to warn the others, and everyone runs to the safety of the burrow.

The meerkats take turns being the lookout, so no one has to do it all the time. It is a team job, and every meerkat does its part to keep the group safe. Is that not a wonderful way to look after each other?

## Digging in the Desert

Meerkats are amazing diggers. They have long, curved claws on their front paws that work like little shovels. They can dig through hard, dry ground very quickly, and they use their digging skills to make burrows, to find food, and to escape from danger.

A meerkat burrow is like an underground house, with many rooms and tunnels and different entrances. It is cool inside, even when the desert sun is very hot outside. Meerkats sleep in their burrows at night, huddled together for warmth, and they hide there when danger comes.

Meerkats also dig for their food. They look for insects, spiders, worms, lizards, and even small snakes under the ground. They are not fussy eaters, and they will eat many kinds of small animals that live in the desert. Meerkats are omnivores, which means they eat both animals and plants.

## A Meerkat's Day

A meerkat's day starts early in the morning, just after the sun comes up. The meerkats come out of their burrow, sit in the sunshine to warm up, and groom each other. Grooming is when they clean each other's fur, and it helps them stay friends and stay clean.

Then it is time to look for food. The mob spreads out across the desert, digging and sniffing for insects and other tasty treats. They stay close together, and they talk to each other with little peeping sounds so that everyone knows where the group is.

At midday, when the sun is hottest, the meerkats may rest in the shade. Then they look for food again in the afternoon, before going back to their burrow for the night. It is a busy day for a little desert animal!

## Talking Meerkats

Meerkats are very chatty animals. They make many different sounds, including peeps, chirps, barks, and purrs. Each sound means something different. A soft peep might mean "I am here," a bark might mean "Danger!" and a purr might mean "I am happy."

Baby meerkats, called pups, learn these sounds from the adults. They also learn how to dig, how to find food, and how to keep watch. The grown-up meerkats are great teachers, and they even bring food to the pups so they can practise handling it.

Meerkats have dark patches around their eyes, like a mask. Scientists think these dark patches work like sunglasses, helping to block the bright desert sun so the meerkat can see better. That is a clever trick for an animal that spends its life looking up at the sky!

## A Meerkat Family

A mother meerkat usually has three or four pups at a time. When the pups are born, they are tiny and their eyes are closed. They stay in the safety of the burrow for the first few weeks of their life, while their mother and the other grown-up meerkats look after them.

The whole mob helps to look after the pups. Some meerkats babysit the pups while the mother goes out to find food, and some bring food back for them. It is a real team effort, and it shows how much meerkats care for each other.

Pups grow up quickly. They start to explore the world outside the burrow when they are about three weeks old, and they learn to dig and find food when they are a few months old. By the time they are about a year old, they are grown-up meerkats, ready to help look after the mob.

## Life in the Kalahari

The Kalahari Desert is a dry, sandy place with hot days and cold nights. It does not rain very often, and water is hard to find. Meerkats have learned to live in this tough place. They get most of the water they need from the food they eat, so they do not need to drink very often.

Meerkats share their desert home with many other animals, including lions, leopards, eagles, and snakes. They have to be brave and clever to stay safe. Their lookout system, their burrows, and their teamwork all help them survive in a place where danger can come from the sky or from the ground.

Meerkats are not in great danger as a species, but their desert home needs to be protected. When we look after wild places like the Kalahari, we help meerkats and all the other animals that live there.

Next time you see a meerkat standing tall on its back legs, remember how brave and clever it is. Small in size, but big in teamwork, the meerkat is one of the most loveable animals in the desert. What a brave little lookout!
