If you walked through a rainforest in Africa and saw a monkey with a bright blue and red face, you might think someone had painted it. But that amazing face belongs to a real animal called the mandrill. Mandrills are the most colourful monkeys in the world, and their faces look like beautiful works of art. They live in the rainforests of Central Africa, where they swing through trees, search for fruit, and live together in large, noisy families. Let us meet these wonderful, colourful monkeys and find out what makes them so special.

## What Is a Mandrill?

A mandrill is a type of monkey, and it is one of the largest monkeys in the world. Male mandrills are bigger than females, and they have the brightest colours. Their faces are a striking mix of bright blue on the cheeks and nose, with a bold red stripe down the middle. Their bottoms are also brightly coloured in blue, red, and purple. These colours are not paint. They come from the mandrill's skin, and they become brighter when a male is healthy and strong. Female mandrills have softer colours, but they are still beautiful. Mandrills have thick fur that is olive green or brown, long arms, and a short tail.

## Where Mandrills Live

Mandrills live in the rainforests of Central Africa, in countries such as Cameroon, Gabon, Equatorial Guinea, and Congo. They love the thick, green rainforest, where tall trees give them shade, food, and safe places to sleep. Mandrills spend most of their time on the forest floor, walking on all fours, but they also climb trees to find fruit and to rest. At night, they sleep high up in the trees, where they are safer from predators. The rainforest is warm and wet all year round, and it is full of the fruits, leaves, and insects that mandrills love to eat.

## A Colourful Family

Mandrills live in groups called troops, and these troops can be very large. A troop may have many females, their young, and one or two adult males. The brightest male is usually the leader of the troop. His bright colours show the other mandrills that he is strong and healthy. Mandrills are very social animals. They groom each other, which means they pick through each other's fur to remove dirt and insects. Grooming helps them stay clean, but it also helps them become friends and feel close to each other. Young mandrills play together, chasing, wrestling, and swinging from branches, just like children playing in a park.

## What Mandrills Eat

Mandrills are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and small animals. Their favourite foods are fruits, which they find in the rainforest trees. They also eat leaves, seeds, nuts, and flowers. When they can find them, they eat insects, snails, frogs, and even small lizards. Mandrills have cheek pouches, like little pockets in their cheeks, where they can store food to eat later. They are very clever foragers, and they remember where the best fruit trees are. They also help the rainforest by spreading seeds. When they eat fruit and drop the seeds in a new place, new trees can grow there.

## How Mandrills Talk

Mandrills communicate with each other in many ways. They make different sounds, such as grunts, barks, and screams, to warn each other of danger or to call the troop together. They also use their faces to show how they feel. A mandrill can show its teeth in a grin, raise its eyebrows, or make a funny face to tell others whether it is happy, scared, or angry. Their bright colours also send messages. A male with very bright colours is telling others that he is strong and in charge. Young mandrills learn these signals by watching the adults, just as children learn by watching grown-ups.

## Baby Mandrills

A mother mandrill usually gives birth to one baby at a time. The baby is tiny and clings tightly to its mother's belly for the first weeks of its life. The mother carries her baby everywhere, keeping it warm and safe. As the baby grows, it starts to ride on its mother's back and explore the world around it. Young mandrills stay close to their mothers for a long time, learning how to find food, climb trees, and get along with other mandrills. They love to play with other young mandrills, and through play they learn the skills they will need as adults. A young mandrill's face is not as bright as an adult male's, and its colours grow stronger as it grows older.

## Why Mandrills Need Our Help

Mandrills are wonderful animals, but they need our help. Their rainforest home is being cut down for farms and timber, which leaves them with less space to live and less food to eat. They are also hunted in some places, which is very sad. People who care about wildlife are working hard to protect the rainforests and to keep mandrills safe. Zoos and wildlife parks also help by caring for mandrills and teaching visitors about them. When we protect rainforests, we protect not only mandrills but also thousands of other animals and plants that live there. The mandrill, with its painted face and gentle nature, reminds us how beautiful and precious the natural world is.

So next time you see a picture of a mandrill, look closely at those amazing colours. Behind that bright face is a clever, social, and loving monkey that calls the rainforest home, and it deserves a safe forest to live in for many years to come.
