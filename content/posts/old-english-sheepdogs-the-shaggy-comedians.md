Picture a huge, fluffy dog that looks like a walking rug, with hair falling over its eyes and a waddle like a friendly bear. That is the old English sheepdog. These big, shaggy dogs are famous for being gentle, clever and very, very funny. They are herding dogs from England, which means their job was once to look after sheep, but today they are best known as lovable family pets and one of the silliest dogs you will ever meet.

## A Dog That Looks Like a Mop

Old English sheepdogs are big dogs with thick, shaggy coats that cover them from head to tail. Their coats are usually grey and white, or blue-grey and white, and the fur grows long and wavy. The hair falls over their faces, often hiding their eyes completely. When you look at an old English sheepdog, you might wonder how it can see where it is going. In fact, it can peek out from under its fringe perfectly well, and it usually knows exactly where the treats are kept.

They have a special feature that makes them easy to spot: many of them have very short tails, or almost no tail at all. That is why people sometimes call them bobtails. Long ago, working dogs with tails had to be taxed, so farmers docked the tails of their herding dogs. Today, many old English sheepdogs are simply born with a naturally short tail.

Under all that fur is a strong, sturdy dog. Old English sheepdogs are much bigger than they look, and they move with a rolling, bouncing walk that makes people smile. When they run, their whole shaggy body bounces along like a big, happy cloud.

## The Herding Days

Old English sheepdogs come from the west of England, where farmers needed strong dogs to move sheep and cattle to market. These dogs were not just pets. They were working dogs with an important job. They would walk for miles alongside the flocks, keeping the animals together and steering them along country lanes.

Because they worked in all weathers, their thick coats were very useful. The long fur kept them warm in winter and protected them from rain. Their size helped too. A big dog is hard for a flock to ignore, and sheep will follow where a sheepdog leads.

Old English sheepdogs are clever herders. They do not nip and chase like some herding dogs. Instead, they use their size and their presence, walking calmly among the sheep and guiding them gently. They are also good at thinking for themselves, which was important when they were working far from the farmer on lonely country roads.

## The Class Clown

Ask anyone who owns an old English sheepdog, and they will tell you the same thing: these dogs are comedians. They love to make people laugh. They will flop down in the middle of the room, roll onto their backs, and wave their paws in the air. They will bump into furniture because their fur covers their eyes. They will try to sit on your lap even though they are far too big, and they will look at you with a hopeful expression as if to say, "I am small, really."

They are also gentle and kind. Old English sheepdogs love children and are patient with them. They seem to know that they are big and strong, so they are careful and soft when they play with little ones. Many families say their sheepdog is like a big, furry babysitter that follows the children around the house and keeps an eye on them.

These dogs are loyal too. They love their families deeply and want to be part of everything. They will follow you from room to room, lie at your feet while you read, and rest their big shaggy head on your knee. If you leave the house, they will wait by the door until you come home, and then they will greet you like you have been away for a year.

## A Famous Face

Old English sheepdogs are so charming that they have become famous. You may have seen one in films and cartoons, or on television adverts. One of the most famous old English sheepdogs in Britain is the Dulux dog, who has appeared in paint adverts for many years. With its shaggy coat and friendly face, it is the perfect mascot for a paint company, and millions of people recognise it at once.

Their fame is no surprise. An old English sheepdog is hard to forget. Once you have seen that big, bouncing, shaggy shape, you will always remember it.

## Lots of Fur, Lots of Love

There is one thing every owner of an old English sheepdog will tell you: the coat needs a lot of work. That thick, shaggy fur tangles easily, so it must be brushed regularly to keep it soft and free of knots. Some owners keep the coat shorter and tidier, which makes brushing easier. Others let it grow long and shaggy, which looks wonderful but takes more time to care for.

Old English sheepdogs also need plenty of exercise. They are big, active dogs that love long walks, games in the park, and space to run. They enjoy learning new tricks and are clever enough to pick them up quickly, especially when there is a tasty reward waiting at the end.

Like all dogs, they need good food, fresh water, regular vet visits and a warm place to sleep. In return, they give their families years of laughter, loyalty and love.

## Why We Love Them

Old English sheepdogs remind us that being silly is a wonderful thing. They are big, strong working dogs, but they are also gentle giants with a brilliant sense of fun. They herd sheep, guard their families, and then flop onto the floor and make everyone laugh.

So if you ever meet an old English sheepdog, do not be shy. Push the fringe out of its eyes, give it a good scratch behind the ears, and get ready for a big, shaggy friend who will make you smile every single day. That is the magic of the old English sheepdog, the shaggy comedian of the dog world.
