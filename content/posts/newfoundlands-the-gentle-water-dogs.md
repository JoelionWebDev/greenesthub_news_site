Have you ever seen a dog that loves water so much that it jumps in at every chance? That is the Newfoundland. Newfoundlands are huge, gentle dogs with thick, fluffy coats and a deep love of swimming. They come from an island called Newfoundland in Canada, where fishermen once relied on them to help with work in the cold sea. Today, Newfoundlands are famous as water rescue dogs and as loving family pets.

Newfoundlands are big and strong, but they are also some of the sweetest dogs you will ever meet. They are calm, patient, and kind, and they love children very much. A Newfoundland is happy to lie beside you, to go for a swim with you, and to watch over you like a gentle guardian. If you ever meet one, you will see a dog that looks like a big, fluffy bear with a heart of gold.

## Dogs from a Cold Island

Newfoundlands come from the island of Newfoundland, off the east coast of Canada. Long ago, fishermen on the island used these big dogs to help with their work. The dogs would pull fishing nets, carry ropes, and haul heavy loads from the boats to the shore. They were strong workers, and they were not afraid of the cold, icy water.

The dogs were so good in the water that fishermen came to trust them with their lives. If someone fell into the sea, a Newfoundland would swim out and help them back to safety. Over time, people began to train Newfoundlands specially for water rescue work, and they became famous around the world as lifesaving dogs.

Newfoundlands still do water rescue work today. In some places, specially trained Newfoundlands work with lifeguards to help swimmers in trouble. Their love of water and their great strength make them wonderful rescue dogs, just as they were long ago.

## Born to Swim

Newfoundlands are made for the water. They have big, webbed paws that work a bit like the feet of a duck, helping them paddle through the water with strong, steady strokes. Their thick, double coat keeps them warm in cold water, and the outer coat turns water away so their skin stays dry underneath.

A Newfoundland is a powerful swimmer. It can swim for a long time without getting tired, and it can pull a person through the water to safety. Some people say a Newfoundland swims in a special way, moving its legs in a smooth, even rhythm that helps it glide through the water like a boat.

Even when they are not rescuing anyone, Newfoundlands love to swim. Give a Newfoundland a lake, a river, or a pool, and it will be in the water in a moment, splashing happily and paddling around. If you take a Newfoundland to the beach, be ready for a wet and happy dog, because it will want to swim again and again.

## A Gentle Giant with Children

Newfoundlands are often called gentle giants, and they are especially gentle with children. They are patient and calm, and they seem to understand that children need a soft and careful friend. A Newfoundland will lie quietly while children play nearby, and it will happily let a small child lean against its soft fur.

Long ago, people began to call the Newfoundland a nanny dog, because it seemed to watch over children so carefully. A Newfoundland would stay close to the children in its family, following them around the house and the garden, as if it were their guardian. It is a lovely name for a dog that loves children so much.

Newfoundlands are also kind to other animals. They are friendly with other dogs and often calm around cats and smaller pets. They do not like to fight or cause trouble. They would much rather make friends, and their calm nature helps other animals feel safe around them.

## A Big, Fluffy Coat

One of the first things you notice about a Newfoundland is its thick, fluffy coat. The coat is long and soft, and it comes in colours like black, brown, and grey, and sometimes white with black patches. The thick coat keeps the dog warm in cold weather and in cold water.

That wonderful coat needs care. A Newfoundland's fur should be brushed often to keep it clean and free of tangles. Brushing also helps remove loose fur, because Newfoundlands shed a lot, especially when the seasons change. A Newfoundland can leave fluffy fur all over the house, so brushing is an important job for its family.

Newfoundlands also have big, droopy jowls, which means they can drool quite a lot, especially after drinking water or when they are excited. It is part of what makes them a Newfoundland, and their families love them for it, drool and all.

## Keeping a Newfoundland Happy and Healthy

A Newfoundland needs good food, fresh water, and exercise every day. Because it is such a big dog, it needs plenty of food to stay strong and healthy. It also needs walks and play to keep fit, and swimming is one of the best kinds of exercise for a Newfoundland. A good swim keeps it happy and helps its strong muscles stay healthy.

Newfoundlands need extra care in hot weather. Their thick coats and big bodies mean they can get too warm when the sun is strong. On hot days, a Newfoundland needs shade, cool water, and a cool place to rest. They are much happier in cool weather, and a splash in cold water is their idea of a perfect day.

A Newfoundland's ears should be checked and cleaned often, because water can get into them when the dog swims. A visit to the vet helps make sure the Newfoundland is healthy, and its family can learn the best ways to care for this big, loving dog.

## Why Newfoundlands Are So Loved

Newfoundlands are loved all over the world, and it is easy to see why. They are strong enough to rescue people from the water, yet gentle enough to watch over a small child. They are loyal, patient, and full of love, and they bring calm and comfort to every home they join.

If you ever meet a Newfoundland, say hello gently and be ready for a big, happy dog to lean against you. You will meet a dog that loves the water, loves children, and loves its family with all its big, gentle heart.
