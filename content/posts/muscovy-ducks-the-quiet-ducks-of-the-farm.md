Imagine walking through a farmyard and spotting a duck that does not quack. It has a bright red face, shiny feathers, and it makes a soft hissing sound instead. You have just met a Muscovy duck, one of the most unusual ducks in the whole world!

Muscovy ducks are special for many reasons. They look different from other ducks, they sound different, and they behave a little differently too. Once you learn about them, you will see why so many farmers love keeping them around.

## A Duck with a Red Face

The first thing you will notice about a Muscovy duck is its face. Muscovy ducks have bright red skin around their eyes and beak. This red skin is bumpy and looks a bit like a mask. It makes them easy to spot among other ducks.

Their feathers can be black, white, brown, or a mix of colours. Some Muscovy ducks are mostly dark with white patches on their wings. Others are almost all white. No two Muscovy ducks look exactly alike, which makes each one special.

Muscovy ducks are also bigger than many other ducks. They have strong wings and they can fly much better than most farm ducks. In fact, Muscovy ducks love to perch up high, on fences, branches, and even rooftops!

## The Duck That Hisses Instead of Quacking

Here is the most fun fact about Muscovy ducks: they do not quack! While other ducks fill the farmyard with loud quacks, Muscovy ducks stay quiet. The males make a soft hissing sound, and the females make a gentle cooing sound.

This makes Muscovy ducks the quiet ducks of the farm. Farmers who live close to their neighbours often like Muscovy ducks because they do not make a lot of noise. You can enjoy watching them without the loud quacking early in the morning.

But do not think they are silent all the time. Muscovy ducks still talk to each other. They wag their tails when they are happy, bob their heads, and make soft sounds to greet their friends. They have their own quiet way of chatting.

## Where Do Muscovy Ducks Come From?

Muscovy ducks come from Central and South America. In the wild, they live near rivers, ponds, and lakes in warm places. They love water and are very good swimmers.

Long ago, people in Central and South America began keeping Muscovy ducks on their farms. From there, the ducks travelled to farms all over the world. Today you can find Muscovy ducks on farms in many countries, including here in Africa.

Even though they now live on farms far from their first home, Muscovy ducks still love the things their wild cousins love: water, sunshine, and plenty of space to wander.

## Water Lovers

Muscovy ducks love water more than almost anything. They splash in ponds, paddle in puddles, and dip their heads under the water to look for food. Watching a Muscovy duck enjoy a swim is one of the happiest sights on a farm.

They eat plants, seeds, insects, and small creatures they find in the water and on the ground. Muscovy ducks are also famous for eating insects like flies and mosquitoes. Farmers love this because the ducks help keep the farm free of pesky bugs!

When they are not swimming, Muscovy ducks enjoy walking around the farmyard, pecking at the ground, and exploring. They are curious birds that like to know what is going on around them.

## Muscovy Duck Families

Mother Muscovy ducks are wonderful parents. They lay their eggs in a safe, hidden nest, often in a quiet corner of the barn or under a bush. Then they sit on the eggs to keep them warm until the ducklings hatch.

When the ducklings hatch, they are soft, fluffy, and full of energy. They follow their mother everywhere, learning how to swim, find food, and stay safe. The mother duck watches over them closely and calls them with soft sounds if they wander too far.

Ducklings grow quickly. They love to swim from a very young age, and they soon learn to find their own food. Before long, they look just like their parents, with the same bright faces and shiny feathers.

## Friends on the Farm

Muscovy ducks are calm and friendly birds. They get along well with chickens, goats, and other farm animals. They are not easily scared, and they enjoy being around people who are kind and gentle with them.

Many children love Muscovy ducks because they are fun to watch. They waddle around the yard, wag their tails, and tilt their heads as if they are listening to you. If you sit quietly near them, they may come closer to say hello.

Muscovy ducks also help farmers in other ways. Their droppings make the soil rich for growing plants, and their love of eating insects helps protect crops. They are hardworking members of the farm family.

## How to Care for a Muscovy Duck

If you ever visit a farm with Muscovy ducks, remember that they need a few simple things to be happy. They need clean water to swim and drink, a safe place to sleep at night, and good food every day.

They also need space to roam. Muscovy ducks are not happy in a tiny cage. They love to walk, swim, and explore, so a farmyard with a pond is the perfect home for them.

Most of all, they need kind people around them. Muscovy ducks that are treated gently grow up to be calm and friendly. They may even learn to come when they are called!

## Why Muscovy Ducks Are So Special

Muscovy ducks remind us that being different is wonderful. They do not quack like other ducks, they have bright red faces, and they can fly and perch like birds in the trees. They do things their own way, and that makes them amazing.

The next time you visit a farm, look for the quiet duck with the red face. Watch it splash in the pond, wag its tail, and hiss a soft hello. You will be meeting one of the farm's most charming animals.

Muscovy ducks may be quiet, but they have a lot to teach us. They show us that you do not need to be loud to be noticed, and that being yourself is the best thing you can be.
