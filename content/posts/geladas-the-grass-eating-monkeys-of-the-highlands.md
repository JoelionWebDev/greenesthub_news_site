High up in the mountains of Ethiopia, in a country in East Africa, live some very special monkeys. They are called geladas, and they are unlike most other monkeys you may have heard about. For one thing, they spend most of their time on the ground, not in the trees. For another, they eat grass — lots and lots of grass — all day long!

Geladas live on cool, grassy mountain tops, where the air is fresh and the views stretch for miles. They are noisy, friendly, and very chatty animals, and they live together in big groups. Let us climb up into the highlands and meet them.

## What Do They Look Like?

Geladas are medium-sized monkeys with thick, shaggy fur. Their fur is a brownish-grey colour, and the males have a long, flowing mane around their head and shoulders, a bit like a lion. The females have shorter fur and look a little smaller.

The most striking thing about a gelada is the bright patch of bare skin on its chest. It is a vivid pinkish-red colour, shaped a bit like an hourglass, and it stands out against the monkey's fur. This patch is sometimes called a bleeding heart, because of its colour and shape, but do not worry — it is just a patch of skin, and it is perfectly normal for a gelada. The colour can change a little, becoming brighter when the monkey is excited.

Geladas have long fingers and strong hands that are perfect for picking up grass. Their faces are dark, with bright eyes that look out from under a heavy brow. When you see a group of geladas sitting on a grassy hillside, picking at the grass and chatting away, it is a sight you will not forget.

## Where Do They Live?

Geladas live only in the highlands of Ethiopia. They make their homes on high, grassy mountain plateaus, where the land is open and covered in grass. These mountains are cool, and at night it can get very cold. Geladas are the only monkeys in the world that live at such high places all the time.

At night, geladas sleep on the edges of steep cliffs. They huddle together on narrow ledges, keeping warm and safe from animals that might want to catch them. In the morning, as the sun rises, they climb up from the cliffs and spread out across the grassy tops to spend the day eating.

Geladas like open places where they can see a long way in every direction. This helps them spot danger early, so the whole group can run for safety if they need to. They are very good climbers, and they can scramble up and down steep, rocky cliffs with ease.

## What Do They Eat?

Most monkeys eat fruit, leaves, and insects. Geladas are different — they are grass eaters! Grass makes up most of what they eat, and they spend a large part of every day sitting on the ground, picking up blades of grass with their clever fingers and popping them into their mouths.

They are picky about their grass, though. They like the soft, young blades best, and they will dig with their fingers to pull up grass roots and small bulbs when the grass above the ground is dry or tough. They also eat the seeds of grasses, and sometimes flowers, leaves, and small insects if they come across them.

Eating grass all day might sound boring, but grass is quite hard to digest, so geladas need to eat a lot of it to get enough energy. They sit in a funny, hunched position as they eat, shuffling along on their bottoms as they pick grass — a movement that looks a little like a person scooting across the floor. It is quite a funny sight!

## A Very Chatty Monkey

Geladas are famous for being noisy. They make all sorts of sounds — grunts, barks, squeaks, and chattering calls. They talk to each other almost all day long! Scientists have found that geladas have many different calls, and they seem to use them to share feelings and keep in touch with their friends.

A group of geladas can be very loud, especially when they are excited or when something new happens. The males make deep, loud calls, and the whole group joins in with chattering and squealing. It is like a big, happy family dinner where everyone is talking at once.

Geladas also love to groom each other. Grooming means picking gently through each other's fur, and it is a way for monkeys to show they care and to keep their friendships strong. You will often see geladas sitting close together, grooming each other while they chatter away.

## Family Life in a Big Group

Geladas live in large groups, and these groups can be made up of many families. A family usually has one adult male, a few females, and their young ones. Several families often join together to form a bigger group, called a band, and these bands can have hundreds of monkeys in them!

Living in a big group helps keep everyone safe. With so many eyes watching, it is hard for a hungry animal to sneak up on them. If danger is spotted, the geladas sound the alarm, and the whole group rushes towards the safety of the cliffs.

Baby geladas are small and dark-furred when they are born, and they cling tightly to their mothers. As they grow, they learn how to pick grass, climb cliffs, and chatter with the rest of the group. Young geladas love to play, chasing each other, tumbling in the grass, and practising their climbing skills on the rocks.

## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about geladas:

- Geladas are sometimes called bleeding-heart monkeys because of the bright red patch on their chest.
- They are the only monkeys that eat mostly grass.
- Geladas spend most of their time on the ground, not in trees.
- They live higher up in the mountains than any other monkey in the world.
- Geladas are very chatty and make many different sounds to talk to each other.
- They sleep on cliff ledges at night and climb up to the grassy tops in the morning.
- A group of geladas can have hundreds of members, all eating grass together.
- They shuffle along on their bottoms as they pick grass — a funny way to move!
