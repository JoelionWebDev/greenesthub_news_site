If you could meet the happiest animal on Earth, who would it be? Many people would say the quokka. Quokkas are small, furry animals from Australia that always seem to be smiling. Their mouths turn up at the corners, and when they look at a camera, it looks as if they are posing for a happy selfie. It is no wonder that people call them the happiest animals on Earth.

Quokkas are related to kangaroos and wallabies, and they live on a few small islands off the coast of Western Australia, and in a small area on the mainland. Let us travel to their island home and meet these smiling little animals.

## A Small Cousin of the Kangaroo

Quokkas are marsupials, which means they are animals that carry their babies in a pouch, just like kangaroos and koalas. A quokka is about the size of a cat, with brown-grey fur, a round face, small ears, and a long tail. They have strong back legs for hopping and small front paws for holding food.

Quokkas live mostly on Rottnest Island, a small island off the coast of Perth in Western Australia. The island got its name a long time ago, when a Dutch explorer saw the quokkas and thought they were giant rats. Today, the island is a famous place where people go to see the quokkas. Quokkas also live on Bald Island and in a few places on the mainland of Western Australia, but Rottnest Island is where most of them live.

## Why Do Quokkas Look So Happy?

The quokka's famous smile is not really a smile at all. It is just the shape of their face. Their mouths naturally turn up at the corners, and their cheeks are round and chubby, which makes them look as if they are always happy. When a quokka looks at you, its dark, shiny eyes and its upturned mouth make it look friendly and cheerful.

Quokkas became famous around the world because of selfies. People visiting Rottnest Island started taking photos with quokkas, and the quokkas looked as if they were smiling for the camera. The photos spread all over the internet, and soon people from all over the world wanted to meet a quokka and take a selfie with one.

But quokkas are wild animals, and they need to be treated with respect. People are not allowed to touch quokkas or feed them human food. Visitors can take photos, but they must keep a safe distance and let the quokkas come to them if they want to. This keeps the quokkas safe and happy in their natural home.

## What Do Quokkas Eat?

Quokkas are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They munch on leaves, grasses, stems, and fruit. They love to eat the leaves of shrubs and small trees, and they are very good at finding food, even in the dry parts of the island. Quokkas can survive for a long time without drinking much water, because they get most of the water they need from the plants they eat.

## Baby Quokkas in the Pouch

Like other marsupials, quokkas carry their babies in a pouch. A baby quokka, called a joey, is born very tiny and helpless, about the size of a jellybean. It crawls into its mother's pouch, where it stays safe and warm, drinking her milk and growing bigger. The joey stays in the pouch for several months, until it is big enough to come out and hop around on its own.

Even after the joey leaves the pouch, it stays close to its mother for a while, learning how to find food and how to stay safe.

## Life on Rottnest Island

Rottnest Island is a beautiful place, with white sandy beaches, clear blue water, and bushy shrubs. It is a perfect home for quokkas, because there are no foxes or other big predators on the island that hunt them. Quokkas can hop around safely, eat the plants they like, and raise their babies without too much danger.

But life on the island is not always easy. In the summer, it can get very hot and dry, and there is not much water. Quokkas rest during the hottest part of the day and come out at night when it is cooler. They also climb trees to find leaves that are out of reach on the ground.

## Protecting the Quokkas

Quokkas are a vulnerable species, which means there are not many of them left, and they need protection. Most quokkas live on Rottnest Island, and only a small number live on the mainland. People are working hard to keep them safe.

On Rottnest Island, rangers look after the quokkas and their habitat. They make sure visitors do not feed or touch the quokkas, and they keep the island clean and safe. Scientists study quokkas to learn more about them, and they count them every year to check how many there are. On the mainland, people are working to control foxes and other animals that hunt quokkas, and to protect the bushland where quokkas live.

Visitors can help too. By not feeding quokkas, by keeping a safe distance, and by taking photos without touching them, people can enjoy meeting these happy little animals without harming them.
