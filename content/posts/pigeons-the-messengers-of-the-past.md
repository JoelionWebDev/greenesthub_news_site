Look up in a town square and you will probably see pigeons, grey birds with shiny green and purple necks, bobbing their heads as they peck at crumbs on the ground. They are so common that many people barely notice them. But pigeons have a secret past that is full of adventure. Long before telephones, radios, and emails, pigeons carried messages across the sky, flying over mountains and seas, through storms and battles, to bring news to people who were waiting far away.

## What is a pigeon?

A pigeon is a bird that lives close to people in towns and cities all over the world. The pigeons we see in parks and squares are descended from wild rock doves, birds that once lived on cliffs by the sea. People began keeping pigeons thousands of years ago, because they were useful, gentle, and easy to look after. Over time, people bred many different kinds of pigeons. Some are bred for their beautiful feathers, some for flying in races, and some for carrying messages.

A pigeon is about the size of a small chicken, with a round body, a small head, and strong wings. Its feathers are usually grey, but they can also be white, brown, or black, and many pigeons have shiny green and purple feathers on their necks that sparkle in the sun. Pigeons eat seeds, grain, and small pieces of fruit. They make a soft cooing sound, and they walk with a funny bobbing movement of the head.

## The homing instinct

What makes pigeons so special is their amazing homing instinct. A homing pigeon is a pigeon that can find its way home from a place it has never been before, even if it is very far away. Imagine being taken to a town you have never visited and then being asked to find your way home. That is what a homing pigeon can do. It flies up into the sky, looks around, and sets off in the right direction, flying home over fields, rivers, and towns.

Nobody knows exactly how pigeons do it, but scientists believe they use many clues. They may use the sun and the stars to work out which way to go, just as sailors once did. They may even sense the Earth's magnetic field, like a tiny compass inside their heads. They also remember landmarks, such as rivers, roads, and hills, that they have seen before. Young pigeons learn their way home by flying short distances first, and then going further and further away.

## Messengers in war

Because pigeons could find their way home, people began using them to carry messages. A tiny message was written on a small piece of paper, rolled up, and tied to the pigeon's leg. The pigeon was taken to a faraway place, and when it was set free, it flew home with the message. In this way, news could travel much faster than a person on horseback, and a pigeon could fly over enemy lines where no person could go.

Pigeons were used as messengers in many wars, including the First and Second World Wars. Soldiers kept pigeons in small cages near the front lines. When they needed to send a message back to their commanders, they wrote it down, tied it to a pigeon, and let the bird fly. Many pigeons flew through danger to deliver their messages, and some were hurt by enemy fire but kept flying. Some pigeons became heroes, and their stories are still told today. One famous pigeon carried a message that saved many soldiers, and it was given a medal for its bravery.

## Racing pigeons

Pigeons are not only messengers. They are also racers. Pigeon racing is a sport that people have enjoyed for a very long time. In a pigeon race, many pigeons are taken to a starting point far away from home. They are all set free at the same time, and they race home as fast as they can. The owner of the pigeon that arrives home first wins the race. It sounds simple, but it is a real test of the pigeon's speed, strength, and homing skill.

Pigeon racers look after their birds with great care. They feed them well, keep their homes clean, and train them by flying them over longer and longer distances. A racing pigeon can fly very fast, and it can keep flying for many hours. People who race pigeons love their birds, and they are very proud when one of their pigeons wins. Pigeon racing is popular in many countries, and it is a sport that brings people together.

## Pigeons and people today

Today, we do not need pigeons to carry messages, because we have phones, computers, and satellites. But pigeons are still part of our lives. They live in our towns and cities, and they remind us of the long friendship between people and animals. People still keep pigeons as pets and as racers, and scientists still study them to learn more about how they find their way home.

## How to be kind to pigeons

Pigeons are gentle birds, and there are simple ways to be kind to them. If you feed pigeons, give them seeds or grain rather than bread, because bread is not very good for them. Do not chase them or throw things at them, and do not try to catch them. Watch them quietly and enjoy their soft cooing and shiny feathers. If you see a pigeon that is hurt, tell a grown-up, who can help it.

You can also learn from pigeons. They remind us that even the most ordinary-looking animal can have an amazing talent. They teach us about loyalty, because a homing pigeon always tries to get home, no matter how far away it is. And they teach us about courage, because messenger pigeons flew through danger to help people.
