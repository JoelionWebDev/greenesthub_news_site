When people think of racing animals, they usually think of horses. But in the deserts of the Middle East, there is another racing champion: the camel. Camel racing is a thrilling sport that has been enjoyed for hundreds of years in countries like the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Oman. Thousands of people gather to watch camels thunder down the track, and the fastest camels are treated like superstars. It is a sport full of speed, tradition and excitement.

## The Racing Camel

Not every camel is a racer. Racing camels are a special kind of dromedary camel, the one-humped camel of the Arabian desert. They are bred to be tall, slim and light, with long legs built for speed. A good racing camel looks very different from a working camel that carries heavy loads. It is more like an athlete than a pack animal.

Racing camels can run surprisingly fast. When they gallop, their long legs stretch out and they seem to fly across the sand. A camel race is usually run over a distance of several kilometres, and the camels run the whole way without stopping. It takes great stamina, which is why racing camels are trained carefully for months before a big race.

The camels are fed a special diet of grains, hay and dates, and they are given plenty of rest and care. Their trainers, called handlers, look after them every day, brushing their coats, checking their health and making sure they are happy. A racing camel is a valuable animal, and it is treated like a champion.

## The Jockeys and the Robot Riders

For many years, the jockeys who rode racing camels were young boys. But that changed, because riding a racing camel at high speed is dangerous, and people wanted to protect children. Today, most camel races use robot jockeys. These are small, lightweight robots that sit on the camel's back and hold the reins. The robot is controlled by the trainer, who follows the race in a car driving alongside the track. The trainer can talk to the camel through a speaker in the robot, and can tell the robot to whip gently or to encourage the camel to run faster.

This might sound strange, but it works very well. The robots are light, so the camel can run faster. And it means that no child has to take the risk of riding at high speed. The robots wear colourful little outfits, which makes them look funny and charming as they bounce along on the camels' backs.

## A Day at the Races

Camel racing is a big event. The races often start early in the morning, when the desert air is still cool. Crowds gather at the racetrack, which is a long, straight strip of sand in the middle of the desert. The camels line up at the start, and when the signal is given, they surge forward in a cloud of dust.

The cars carrying the trainers and owners drive along a road beside the track, keeping up with the camels. The trainers shout encouragement through the robots' speakers, and the crowd cheers. The camels run and run, and the fastest one crosses the finish line first. The winning camel and its owner might win a prize of money, a car, or even a golden sword or dagger. In some big races, the prizes are very valuable indeed.

Camel racing is not just a sport — it is a festival. There is food, music and celebration, and families come together to enjoy the day. It is a proud tradition that connects people to their desert heritage.

## Training a Champion

Training a racing camel takes time and patience. Young camels start their training when they are a couple of years old. At first, they learn to wear a saddle and to walk and trot on a lead. Then they start to run short distances, building up their strength and stamina bit by bit.

Trainers use kindness and rewards to teach the camels. A camel that is treated well will try hard for its trainer. The camels learn the sound of their trainer's voice, and they respond to gentle commands. The best trainers know each of their camels personally — their moods, their strengths and their favourite foods.

Camels are clever animals. They can learn the race track, remember the route, and even get excited when they hear the sounds of a race day. Some camels are natural racers, with a competitive spirit that makes them want to run in front of the others. Trainers look for this spirit when they choose which camels to race.

## Camels and People in the Desert

Camels have been part of desert life for thousands of years. They carried people and goods across the desert, gave milk and wool, and were a sign of wealth and status. Camel racing grew out of this long relationship. In the past, people raced their camels for fun at festivals and gatherings. Over time, it became an organised sport, with proper tracks, rules and big prizes.

Today, camel racing is also a way to keep the old desert traditions alive. In a world of cars and cities, the camel reminds people of their history. The sport brings together old and new — ancient animals and modern robots, traditional music and high-tech timing systems.
