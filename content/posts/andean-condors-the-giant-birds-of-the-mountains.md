High above the Andes mountains of South America, where the air is thin and the peaks are covered in snow, a giant bird soars on wings wider than a grown person is tall. It circles slowly in the sky, barely flapping its wings, riding the wind like a glider. This is the Andean condor — one of the largest flying birds in the world, and a symbol of strength and freedom for the people of the Andes.

Andean condors are magnificent birds. They are not like the small birds we see in our gardens. They are huge, powerful and graceful, and they can live for a very long time. Let us climb up into the mountains and meet these giants of the sky.

## A Giant of the Sky

The Andean condor is one of the biggest flying birds on Earth. Its wings can stretch wider than three metres from tip to tip — wider than a tall person standing with their arms stretched out. When it spreads its wings, it looks like a great black cloak floating in the sky.

Condors are heavy birds, but they are built for flying. Their huge wings let them soar, which means they can glide on rising currents of warm air without flapping. A condor can stay in the air for hours, circling high above the mountains, hardly moving its wings at all. It is one of the most graceful flyers in the whole bird world.

## The Clean-Up Crew of the Mountains

Andean condors are scavengers, which means they eat animals that are already dead. That might sound a little yucky, but it is a very important job. Without scavengers like condors, dead animals would lie around for a long time. Condors help to clean up the mountains and keep them healthy.

Condors have an amazing sense of sight. They can spot a dead animal from high in the sky, miles away. When one condor finds food, other condors see it flying down, and they follow. Soon, a group of condors gathers around the meal. They eat quickly and neatly, and their bare heads stay clean because there are no feathers to get dirty.

Condors mostly eat large dead animals, such as deer, llamas, and farm animals. They do not hunt live animals. They are peaceful birds, and they wait patiently for their food. In this way, they are nature's clean-up crew, and the mountains would be a much messier place without them.

## Life High in the Mountains

Andean condors live in the Andes, the long chain of mountains that runs down the west side of South America. These mountains are some of the highest in the world, and the condors live at very high altitudes, where the air is thin and cold. They nest on cliff ledges and in rocky caves, high above the valleys.

## A Very Special Bird

The Andean condor is a very special bird to the people of South America. For hundreds of years, it has been a symbol of power, freedom and strength. It appears on the flags and coats of arms of several South American countries, and it is a national symbol in places like Ecuador, Colombia, Bolivia and Chile.

Condors also live for a very long time. They are among the longest-lived birds in the world, and they can live for many decades. A condor that is born today could still be soaring over the mountains when today's children are grown up with children of their own. This long life makes them even more special.

## Raising a Condor Chick

Condors are devoted parents, and they raise their chicks with great care. A mother condor lays just one egg at a time, in a safe place on a cliff ledge or in a rocky cave. The egg is precious, and both parents take turns keeping it warm.

When the chick hatches, it is covered in soft, fluffy white down, and it looks very different from its parents. The parents feed it by bringing food to the nest. The chick grows slowly, and it stays with its parents for a long time — sometimes for more than a year. This is one of the longest childhoods of any bird.

Young condors take a long time to learn to fly. They practise flapping their wings on the cliff edge, and when they are ready, they take their first flight, gliding out over the valley. Their parents stay nearby, watching over them. Because condors raise only one chick at a time and take such good care of it, every chick is very precious.

## Fun Facts About Andean Condors

- Andean condors are among the largest flying birds in the world, with wings wider than three metres.
- They can soar for hours without flapping their wings, riding on currents of warm air.
- Condors are scavengers — they clean up the mountains by eating animals that have already died.
- They have a white fluffy collar of feathers around their necks, like a scarf.
- The Andean condor is a national symbol of several South American countries.
- They are among the longest-lived birds in the world and can live for many decades.
- A mother condor lays just one egg at a time and cares for her chick for a very long time.

## Protecting the Condors

Sadly, Andean condors are in danger. Their numbers have fallen over the years, and there are not as many of them as there once were. Their mountain homes are changing, and sometimes they are hurt by eating poisoned food left out for other animals. They also face dangers from power lines and from hunting.

But many people are working hard to protect them. In several countries, special programmes raise condor chicks and release them into the wild, helping the population grow. Scientists study the condors to learn how to keep them safe, and national parks protect their mountain homes.

The Andean condor is a bird that reminds us to look up and dream big. With its great wings and its long life, it is a true giant of the sky. If we protect the mountains and the clean air the condors need, these magnificent birds will keep soaring above the Andes for many years to come, inspiring everyone who sees them.
