When people hear the word shark, many of them think of a scary monster from a film. But real sharks are very different from the ones in the movies. Most sharks are shy, graceful and careful animals that would much rather swim away from people than come close. Sharks have lived in the oceans for a very, very long time, far longer than people have been on Earth. They are important guardians of the sea, keeping the oceans healthy and balanced. Once you learn the truth about sharks, you will see them not as monsters, but as wonderful animals that deserve our respect and protection.

## Meet the shark family

There are many kinds of sharks, and they come in all shapes and sizes. Some are small enough to fit in your arms, while others are as long as a bus. The whale shark is the biggest fish in the sea, but it is gentle and eats tiny sea creatures. The great white shark is famous, but it is only one of many kinds. There are hammerhead sharks with wide, flat heads, tiger sharks with stripes, and nurse sharks that rest quietly on the sea floor. Sharks live in oceans all over the world, from warm tropical seas to cold waters. Some swim near the surface, some swim in the deep sea, and some live close to coral reefs.

## Built for life in the sea

A shark's body is made for swimming. It is smooth and shaped like a torpedo, which helps it glide through the water with ease. Its tail is strong and moves from side to side to push it forward. Its fins help it steer and balance, just like the wings and tail of a plane. A shark does not have bones like we do. Instead, its skeleton is made of a bendy material called cartilage, which is the same stuff that makes your ears bendy. This makes sharks light and flexible in the water. Sharks have several rows of teeth, and when a tooth falls out, a new one grows to take its place. This helps them keep a strong set of teeth all through their lives.

## A super sense of smell and hearing

Sharks have amazing senses that help them find food in the vast ocean. They can smell tiny traces of food in the water from far away. They can also sense small movements and vibrations in the water, which helps them find fish even when they cannot see them. Some sharks can sense tiny electric signals made by other animals, which is like having a sixth sense. Their eyes are good at seeing in dim light, which helps them hunt at dawn and dusk. These senses make sharks excellent hunters, but they are not looking for people. Sharks prefer to eat fish, seals, squid and other sea animals, and most sharks are not interested in humans at all.

## What sharks really eat

Sharks eat many different things, depending on the kind of shark they are. Some eat small fish and squid. Others eat crabs, lobsters and shellfish from the sea floor. The biggest sharks, like the whale shark, eat tiny plankton, which are so small you can hardly see them. Sharks are often called the cleaners of the sea because they eat weak and sick fish, which helps keep the other fish healthy. They also help keep the numbers of other sea animals in balance, so no one kind of animal takes over. Without sharks, the oceans would be less healthy, and many other animals would suffer. That is why scientists call sharks guardians of the sea.

## Sharks and people: the truth

It is true that sharks can bite people, but it is very rare. You are far more likely to be hurt by lightning or by a falling coconut than by a shark. Most sharks are shy and swim away when they see a person. When a shark does bite, it is usually because it made a mistake, thinking a swimmer or surfer was a seal or a fish. Sharks do not hunt people for food. People who work with sharks, such as divers and scientists, say that sharks are calm and curious, and that they often swim past without any trouble. The best way to stay safe in the sea is to swim with friends, stay close to the shore, and listen to lifeguards and local advice.

## Why sharks need our help

Sadly, sharks need our help more than we need to fear them. Many sharks are caught by fishermen, and some are caught just for their fins, which is a cruel practice. Sharks also get caught by accident in fishing nets meant for other fish. Because sharks grow slowly and have few babies, it takes a long time for their numbers to recover when many are lost. Pollution in the sea also harms sharks and the fish they eat. People can help sharks by learning the truth about them, by supporting rules that protect them, and by keeping the oceans clean. When we protect sharks, we protect the whole ocean, because healthy oceans give us food, clean air and a beautiful world to enjoy.

## Lessons from the guardians of the sea

Sharks teach us not to believe everything we see in films. They show us that an animal can look fierce and still be shy and gentle. They teach us that every animal has a job to do in nature, and that even the animals we fear are often doing important work. Sharks have survived in the oceans for millions of years by being strong, smart and well adapted to their home. If we look after the oceans and give sharks the respect they deserve, these graceful guardians will keep swimming in the seas for many years to come, keeping the oceans healthy for all the creatures that live there, including us.

## Fun facts about sharks

- A shark's skeleton is made of bendy cartilage, not bone, like the cartilage in your ears.
- When a shark loses a tooth, a new one grows in to take its place.
- The whale shark is the biggest fish in the sea, but it eats tiny sea creatures.
- Sharks can sense tiny movements and smells in the water from far away.
- Most sharks are shy of people and would rather swim away than come close.
