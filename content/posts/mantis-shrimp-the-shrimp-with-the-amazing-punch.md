If you think of a shrimp, you probably picture a small, quiet sea creature that swims around gently and minds its own business. Now forget that picture. The mantis shrimp is small, but it is one of the toughest, bravest, and most amazing animals in the whole ocean. It is brightly coloured, it has eyes that can see in ways we can only dream about, and it can throw a punch so powerful that it can smash through hard shells. It may be little, but no one messes with a mantis shrimp.

Mantis shrimps live in warm, shallow seas, in places such as the Indian and Pacific Oceans. They like to live near coral reefs and sandy sea floors, where there are plenty of places to hide. Most mantis shrimps are not very big. Many are about as long as your hand, from the tip of your wrist to the end of your fingers, and some are a little longer. But what they lack in size, they make up for in strength, speed, and cleverness.

## A Rainbow Under the Sea

One of the first things you notice about a mantis shrimp is its colour. These animals look like they have been painted by an artist. Their bodies can be bright green, blue, red, orange, and purple, all at the same time. Some have spots and stripes, and some look as if they are wearing shiny armour. Their bright colours are a warning to other animals. In nature, bright colours often mean, "Stay away, I am not an easy snack."

Mantis shrimps get their name because their front legs look a little like the folded arms of a praying mantis, the insect that sits very still and waits for its food. But a mantis shrimp is not an insect at all. It is a sea creature, and it uses those strong front legs in a very different way.

There are two main kinds of mantis shrimp, and they hunt in different ways. One kind is called a smasher. Smashers have front legs that end in hard, club-like shapes, a bit like tiny boxing gloves. The other kind is called a spearer. Spearers have sharp, spiky arms that they use to grab soft animals, such as small fish, very quickly. Both kinds are fast, and both are very good hunters.

## The Super Punch

The smasher mantis shrimp is famous for its punch, and it is easy to see why. When it spots a snail, a crab, or a clam with a hard shell, it does not try to bite through the shell. Instead, it winds up its club-like arm and strikes the shell with a huge amount of force. The punch is so fast that you can hardly see it happen. It is faster than the blink of your eye.

The force of the punch can crack the shell open, letting the mantis shrimp reach the soft, tasty animal inside. Scientists have studied this punch for many years because it is one of the fastest movements in the whole animal kingdom.

But the punch is not just for hunting. Mantis shrimps also use it to defend their homes. If another mantis shrimp tries to take over its burrow, or if a hungry fish comes too close, the mantis shrimp will warn it off with a punch. Most animals learn very quickly not to argue with a mantis shrimp.

## Eyes Like No Other Animal

If the punch was not amazing enough, the mantis shrimp also has some of the most wonderful eyes in the animal world. Each eye sits on a little stalk, and the eyes can move on their own, looking in different directions at the same time. That means a mantis shrimp can look for food in one direction while keeping watch for danger in another.

Their eyes can also see many more colours than we can. People can see three main colours of light, but mantis shrimps can see many more. Scientists think this helps them spot food, find a partner, and notice other mantis shrimps from far away.

## Home Sweet Home

Mantis shrimps are home lovers. Most of them dig a burrow, which is a tunnel or hole in the sand or in a rock, and they live inside it. The burrow is their safe place. They sleep there, hide there when danger comes near, and keep their food there.

They are not always friendly with their neighbours. Many mantis shrimps like to live alone, and they will defend their burrow fiercely. If two mantis shrimps meet and both want the same burrow, they may have a punch-up to decide who gets to stay. The loser has to swim away and find a new home.

When it is time to have babies, the mother mantis shrimp lays her eggs inside her burrow and looks after them carefully until they hatch.

## What Do Mantis Shrimps Eat?

Mantis shrimps eat a wide range of sea animals. Smashers love hard-shelled animals such as snails, crabs, and clams. Spearers prefer softer animals such as small fish and worms. They are patient hunters. A mantis shrimp will often sit quietly near the entrance of its burrow, watching and waiting. When a tasty animal swims past, it strikes in a flash. The meal is over before the other animal even knows what happened.

Even though they are fierce hunters, mantis shrimps also have to watch out for danger. Larger fish, octopuses, and other sea animals would love to eat them. Their bright colours, their speed, and their powerful punch all help to keep them safe.

## A Small Animal With Big Lessons

The mantis shrimp shows us that being small does not mean being weak. It is a tiny animal with a giant punch, rainbow colours, and eyes that can see more than ours. It builds its own home, defends it bravely, and hunts with skill and patience. It is one of the most surprising animals in the ocean, and scientists are still learning new things about it.

Next time you are at the beach or looking at pictures of coral reefs, think about the mantis shrimp hiding in its burrow under the waves. It may be small, but it is a true champion of the sea.
