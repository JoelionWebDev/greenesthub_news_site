High in the wild mountains of Wales, where the wind blows cold and the hills roll on for miles, lives one of the cleverest little horses in the world. The Welsh pony is small, strong and beautiful, with a flowing mane, bright eyes and a brain that never stops thinking. For hundreds of years, these ponies have lived on the Welsh hills, and today they are loved all over the world, especially by children learning to ride.

## Ponies of the Mountains

Wales is a country in the west of Britain, famous for its mountains, green valleys and old castles. Long ago, wild ponies roamed the Welsh mountains freely. They had to survive cold winters, find food on rocky hillsides, and stay safe from danger. Only the toughest, cleverest ponies survived, and over time they became the Welsh ponies we know today.

Because they grew up in the mountains, Welsh ponies are hardy and sure-footed. Sure-footed means they are very good at walking on rough, steep ground without slipping. Their strong legs and tough hooves help them climb hills and pick their way along narrow paths. They are also very good at finding food, even when the grass is thin and the weather is bad.

People in Wales began to tame these ponies and use them for work. They carried loads, pulled carts, and helped farmers on the hills. But they were always more than just working animals. People noticed how beautiful and intelligent they were, and Welsh ponies became treasured animals.

## Four Types, One Family

Welsh ponies come in different sizes, and they are divided into four groups called sections. Section A is the smallest, the Welsh Mountain Pony. These are tiny, pretty ponies with dainty heads and lots of spirit. Section B is a little bigger and is a popular riding pony for children. Section C is the Welsh Pony of Cob Type, which is stronger and sturdier. Section D is the biggest, the Welsh Cob, a powerful horse that can carry an adult rider and pull a cart.

Whatever the size, all Welsh ponies share the same good looks. They have pretty, intelligent heads with large, bright eyes and small, pointed ears. Their manes and tails are thick and flowing, and they often toss their heads proudly as they trot along. Their coats come in many colours, including bay, chestnut, grey, black and roan, which is a lovely mix of white and coloured hairs.

## Clever and Full of Character

Welsh ponies are famous for being clever. They learn quickly, remember things well, and sometimes work out how to open gates or untie ropes. Owners often say their Welsh pony is too clever for its own good. A Welsh pony that wants an extra carrot will find a way to get it.

They are also full of character. Welsh ponies have big personalities and they like to show them. Some are bold and cheeky, always looking for fun. Others are gentle and calm, happy to plod along quietly. But all of them are friendly and love attention. They enjoy being groomed, stroked and talked to, and they quickly learn to recognise the people who look after them.

Welsh ponies are brave too. They do not spook easily, which means they do not jump at every sudden noise. This makes them wonderful ponies for children who are learning to ride. A young rider needs a pony they can trust, and a Welsh pony is a trustworthy friend.

## Perfect Partners for Young Riders

All over the world, Welsh ponies are favourites for children learning to ride. They are just the right size for a young rider, not too big and not too small. They are strong enough to carry a child safely, but gentle enough to be kind and patient.

Welsh ponies are also very good at competitions. They shine in shows, where judges look at their beauty and movement. They are excellent jumpers, leaping over fences with grace and power. They do well in dressage, which is like dancing on horseback, and in driving, where they pull a cart or carriage. Some Welsh ponies even take part in long rides across the countryside, showing off their stamina and toughness.

Learning to ride a Welsh pony is a wonderful experience for a child. The pony teaches patience, kindness and confidence. Riders learn to groom their pony, clean its hooves, and look after it properly. In return, the pony becomes a best friend.

## Life on the Hills Today

Even today, some Welsh ponies still live wild on the Welsh mountains. These are called hill ponies, and they roam freely across the open moorland. Once a year, farmers round them up to check their health and look after them, but for most of the year they live as their ancestors did, free and wild under the open sky.

Most Welsh ponies, though, live on farms and in stables. They need grass or hay to eat, fresh water, a shelter from bad weather, and regular care from a vet and a farrier, who looks after their hooves. Their thick manes and tails need brushing to keep them free of tangles, and they love a good grooming session.

Welsh ponies love company. They are happiest with other ponies or horses nearby, and they make close friends. In a field, you will often see two ponies standing head to tail, swishing flies away from each other's faces with their tails. It is a sweet sign of friendship.

## Why We Love Them

Welsh ponies remind us that small can be mighty. These little mountain horses have survived wild winters, carried heavy loads, and won the hearts of riders all over the world. They are clever, brave, beautiful and kind, all in one small package.

So if you ever visit Wales, or see a Welsh pony at a riding school or a show, take a moment to say hello. Look into those bright eyes and you will see a clever little horse with centuries of mountain history behind it. That is the magic of the Welsh pony, the clever mountain horse that has become one of the world's favourite ponies.
