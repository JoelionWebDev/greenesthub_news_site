Imagine walking through the Australian bush at night with a torch. The trees are quiet, the stars are bright, and something small and quick moves in the shadows. It has soft fur, a long tail, and white spots all over its back, like someone sprinkled it with tiny stars. That animal is a quoll, one of Australia's most interesting hunters.

Quolls are marsupials, which means the mothers carry their tiny babies in a pouch, just like kangaroos and koalas do. They are not as famous as kangaroos, but they are just as special. Quolls have lived in Australia for a very long time, and they know the bush better than almost any other animal. They hunt at night, sleep during the day, and use their sharp senses to find food in the dark.

## What Does a Quoll Look Like?

A quoll looks a little bit like a cat and a little bit like a possum, but it is neither. It has a pointed face, a pink nose, round ears, and bright eyes that help it see in the dark. Its fur is usually brown or black, and it is covered with white spots. No two quolls have exactly the same pattern of spots, so each one is easy to tell apart if you look closely.

Quolls have strong legs and sharp claws that help them climb trees and dig in the ground. Their long tails help them balance when they climb high branches or run along fallen logs. Some quolls are small enough to fit in your school bag, while others are bigger, about the size of a small house cat. Their tails are often as long as their bodies, and they use them like a balancing pole when they move through the trees.

There are different kinds of quolls in Australia. Some live in the forests of the east, some live in the north, and some live in the west. Each kind has its own home and its own way of living, but they all share the same spotted coat and the same love of hunting at night.

## A Hunter of the Night

Quolls are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. When the sun goes down, they wake up and start to hunt. They have a very good sense of smell and good hearing, and these senses help them find food even when it is too dark to see well.

A quoll eats many different things. It hunts small animals, birds, and insects. It also eats fruit and other food it finds on the forest floor. Quolls are clever hunters. They move quietly, they listen carefully, and they pounce quickly when they find something to eat. They can climb trees to catch animals that live in branches, and they can dig to find food hidden in the ground.

Quolls are also very brave for their size. They will hunt animals that are almost as big as they are, and they do not give up easily. But they are also careful. They stay away from bigger hunters when they can, and they hide in hollow logs and rock crevices during the day to stay safe.

## Where Do Quolls Live?

Quolls live in many different parts of Australia. They like forests, woodlands, and rocky places where there are lots of trees and hiding spots. During the day, they sleep in hollow trees, in burrows, or under rocks. These safe places keep them cool and hidden from danger.

A quoll needs a large home range to find enough food. It does not stay in one tiny spot. It moves around its home area every night, checking the same paths and hunting grounds again and again. It remembers where it found food before, and it knows the best places to hide.

Sadly, quolls are not as common as they used to be. Their homes have changed over time, and they face dangers from other animals that were brought to Australia long ago. People who care about wildlife are working hard to protect quolls and the places where they live. They are learning more about quolls every year, and they are helping to keep their bush homes safe.

## Baby Quolls and Family Life

Baby quolls are born very tiny, much smaller than a baby mouse. After they are born, they crawl into their mother's pouch, where they stay safe and warm. Inside the pouch, they drink milk and grow bigger and stronger every day.

After some weeks, the babies grow too big for the pouch. Then the mother carries them on her back or leaves them in a safe nest while she goes out to hunt. She always comes back to feed them and keep them warm. The babies learn to hunt by watching their mother and by playing with each other. Play helps them learn to climb, pounce, and explore.

When the young quolls are old enough, they leave their mother and find their own homes. They must learn to hunt for themselves and to stay safe from danger. It is not easy, but quolls are smart and quick learners. Soon they are hunting on their own in the dark bush, just like their mother did.

## Why Quolls Matter

Quolls are important for the bush. They help to keep the numbers of small animals and insects in balance. When quolls hunt, they stop some animals from becoming too many, and that helps plants and other animals too. They are part of a big web of life in Australia, and every part of that web matters.

Quolls also help scientists learn about Australia's wildlife. By studying quolls, people learn how the bush works and how to protect it. Quolls remind us that even small animals can be strong, clever, and important.

If you ever visit Australia, you might not see a quoll, because they hide during the day and move quietly at night. But knowing they are out there, hunting under the stars with their spotted coats shining in the moonlight, is a wonderful thing. The quoll is a small hunter with a big heart, and it deserves a safe home in the wild bush for many years to come.
