# Beavers: The Busy Builders of the Rivers

If you walk beside a quiet river in the forest, you might see a pile of sticks and mud stretching across the water. It looks like a messy dam, but it is really a home built by one of nature's best builders. The builder is the beaver, a furry animal with strong teeth, a flat tail, and a talent for turning a river into a pond.

## Meet the Beaver

Beavers are large rodents, cousins of rats and squirrels, but much bigger and much better at building. They have thick brown fur, small round ears, and a big flat tail that looks like a paddle. Their back feet are webbed, like a duck's feet, which helps them swim. Their front paws are small and clever, a bit like hands, and they can hold sticks and branches.

A beaver's teeth are its most important tools. Its front teeth are long, strong, and orange in colour. They never stop growing, so the beaver must keep gnawing on wood to wear them down. With these teeth, a beaver can chew through a tree trunk, cutting it down piece by piece.

Beavers live in North America and in parts of Europe and Asia. They live near rivers, streams, lakes, and ponds, where there are plenty of trees and water. They are mostly active at night, when the forest is quiet and cool. During the day, they rest inside their homes.

## The Amazing Flat Tail

A beaver's tail is flat, wide, and covered in scales. It is not just for show. The beaver uses its tail like a rudder when it swims, steering through the water. On land, the tail helps the beaver balance when it stands up to chew a tree.

The tail has another clever use. If a beaver senses danger, it slaps its tail hard on the water. The loud slap warns other beavers to dive and hide. It is like ringing an alarm bell for the whole family.

In winter, beavers store fat in their tails. This helps them stay warm and gives them energy when food is hard to find. The tail is one of the reasons beavers are so good at living in cold water.

## Building a Dam

Beavers build dams to make a pond. A dam is a wall of sticks, branches, mud, and stones built across a stream. When the dam is finished, the water behind it rises and forms a pond. The pond is deep enough that it does not freeze all the way through in winter, and it is safe from animals that might want to catch a beaver.

Building a dam takes a lot of work. Beavers cut down trees and drag the branches to the stream. They push sticks into the mud and pack the gaps with mud and stones. They work together as a family, and they keep fixing the dam whenever it breaks. A beaver dam can last for many years, and it can grow very long.

The pond made by a dam helps many other animals too. Fish, frogs, ducks, and insects live in the pond. Birds come to drink and hunt. The beaver's building work creates a new home for the whole forest.

## Building a Lodge

Inside the pond, the beaver builds its home, called a lodge. A lodge is a dome made of sticks and mud, with an entrance under the water. The beaver swims in through the underwater door, then climbs up into a dry, cosy room inside. The room is above the water, warm and safe.

A lodge can be big enough for a whole beaver family. Inside, there is a place to sleep, a place to eat, and a place to keep food. The walls are thick and strong, keeping out the cold and keeping out animals that might try to get in. In winter, the beavers stay inside their lodge, eating food they stored in the pond.

Some beavers do not build a lodge. They dig a burrow into the bank of a river instead. The entrance is under the water, just like a lodge. This is another clever way to stay safe and warm.

## What Beavers Eat

Beavers are plant eaters. They love the bark, leaves, and twigs of trees, especially aspen, willow, and birch. They also eat water plants, roots, and grasses. In summer, there is plenty of food growing near the water. In autumn, beavers work hard to store food for winter.

They store food by pushing branches into the mud at the bottom of the pond. The cold water keeps the branches fresh, like a fridge. In winter, when the pond is covered with ice, the beavers swim under the ice to their food store and bring branches back to the lodge to eat.

Beavers do not hibernate in winter. They stay awake all through the cold months, living in their lodge and eating their stored food. Their thick fur and fat keep them warm, even when the water is icy.

## Beavers and Their Families

Beavers live in families. A mother and father beaver stay together for many years, and they raise their young together. Baby beavers are called kits. They are born in the lodge in spring, small and furry, with their eyes closed at first.

The kits stay in the lodge for a while, then start to swim and explore. The older brothers and sisters help look after the new kits. They teach them how to swim, how to chew wood, and how to build. A young beaver usually stays with its family for about two years, learning all the skills it needs before it goes off to build its own dam.

Beavers are hard workers, and they teach us a wonderful lesson. With patience, teamwork, and the right tools, a small animal can change a whole river. The next time you see a dam of sticks across a stream, remember the busy builder who made it. The beaver is not just chewing wood. It is building a home, a pond, and a better place for many animals to live.
