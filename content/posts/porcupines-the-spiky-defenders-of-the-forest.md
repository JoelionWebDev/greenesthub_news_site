Porcupines are big, gentle rodents with one of the most amazing coats in the animal world. Their backs are covered in long, sharp quills that are black and white, and when they feel scared they raise those quills and stamp their feet to warn other animals to stay away. Porcupines are not mean animals at all. They are quiet, slow-moving plant-eaters who love to munch on bark, leaves, and fruit, and they would much rather climb a tree or hide than pick a fight. Their quills are only for defence.

## What is a porcupine?

A porcupine is a rodent, which means it is related to rats, squirrels, and beavers, but it is much bigger than most of its cousins. Porcupines have a round, chunky body, short legs, a small head, and a tail that helps them balance. Their most famous feature is their quills. Quills are sharp hairs that have grown hard and pointy, a bit like very strong needles. Porcupines cannot shoot their quills, even though some stories say they can. The quills only come off when another animal touches them. Underneath the quills, a porcupine has soft fur and a warm, brown coat. Porcupines have strong teeth that never stop growing, and they use them to gnaw on bark and wood.

## Where do porcupines live?

Porcupines live in many parts of the world. Some live in North America and South America, and others live in Africa, Europe, and Asia. They like forests, where there are plenty of trees to climb and lots of bark and leaves to eat. Some porcupines live in rocky places and deserts too, as long as there is food and somewhere to hide. A porcupine makes its home in a hollow tree, in a rocky cave, or in a burrow in the ground. It lines its home with soft leaves and grass to make it cosy. Porcupines like to stay in one area, and they often use the same home and the same feeding trees for a long time. They are mostly active at night, so during the day they rest quietly in their den or high up in a tree.

## What do porcupines eat?

Porcupines are herbivores, which means they eat plants. They love to eat the bark of trees, especially in winter when other food is hard to find. They also eat leaves, twigs, buds, fruit, roots, and green plants. Some porcupines climb high into trees to reach the tender young leaves at the top, and they can sit on a branch for a long time, munching happily. Porcupines use their strong front teeth to peel bark from a tree, a bit like peeling a banana. They also like to gnaw on bones and antlers they find on the ground, because these give them important minerals that help their quills grow strong.

## The amazing quills

A porcupine's quills are one of nature's cleverest inventions. The quills are long, sharp, and light, and they lie flat against the porcupine's body when it is calm. When a porcupine feels scared, it raises its quills so they stick up in every direction, and it turns its back towards the danger. It may also stamp its feet, shake its body, and make a rattling sound with special hollow quills on its tail. This is a warning that says, "Stay away!" If an animal still comes too close, the quills can stick into its skin, and they are very hard to pull out. The quills grow back again, just like hair.

## A day in a porcupine's life

A porcupine's day is quiet and slow. During the day, it sleeps in its den or rests on a tree branch, curled up with its quills tucked neatly around its body. When the sun sets, it wakes up and starts to look for food. It waddles along the forest floor or climbs up a tree, using its strong claws to grip the bark. Porcupines are good climbers, even though they look big and heavy, and their long tail helps them balance on branches. They eat, rest, and eat again through the night. Porcupines are mostly solitary animals, which means they like to live alone, but a mother porcupine stays with her baby until it is old enough to look after itself. At dawn, the porcupine returns to its den to sleep away the day.

## Baby porcupines

Baby porcupines are called porcupettes, and they are born with soft quills that soon grow hard. A mother porcupine usually has just one baby at a time, and she looks after it in her den. When a porcupette is born, its quills are soft and wet, so they do not hurt its mother. Within a short time, the quills dry out and become sharp, just like the quills of a grown-up porcupine. The baby drinks its mother's milk and stays close to her as it grows. Soon it starts to nibble on leaves and bark, copying its mother as she eats. The mother teaches her baby which trees are good to climb and which plants are good to eat. After some months, the young porcupine is ready to find its own home in the forest and live on its own.

## Fun facts about porcupines

- Porcupines cannot shoot their quills, but the quills come off easily if another animal touches them.
- A porcupine's quills are sharp hairs that have grown hard, and they grow back if they fall out.
- Porcupines are excellent climbers, and they use their strong claws and long tail to balance on branches.
- Porcupines eat bark, leaves, fruit, and roots, and their strong teeth never stop growing.
- When a porcupine feels scared, it raises its quills, stamps its feet, and rattles its tail as a warning.

Porcupines may look prickly, but they are gentle, peaceful animals who just want to munch on leaves and live quietly in the forest. Their quills are not for attacking, but for keeping them safe while they enjoy their slow, calm life. Next time you think of a porcupine, picture it high in a tree at night, munching on bark under the stars, with its black-and-white quills shining in the moonlight.
