In Thailand, in South East Asia, coconut palms grow very tall, sometimes as high as a three-storey building. The coconuts grow right at the top, where people cannot easily reach them. Climbing up to pick them is hard, slow and dangerous work. But for hundreds of years, farmers in Thailand have had clever helpers: monkeys. High in the palm trees of Thailand, clever pig-tailed macaques work with their human partners, twisting ripe coconuts free and dropping them to the ground.

## The Monkey That Climbs for Coconuts

The monkeys that pick coconuts are called pig-tailed macaques. They get their name from their short, curly tails, which look a little bit like a pig's tail. They are strong, clever monkeys with thick fur, long arms and nimble hands that are perfect for climbing. They live in the forests of South East Asia, and they are excellent climbers, spending much of their lives high up in the trees.

A pig-tailed macaque can climb a tall coconut palm in just a few moments, scampering up the trunk like a child climbing a ladder. At the top, it can walk along the branches, reach out with its hands, and twist a coconut until it comes free. Then it drops the coconut to the ground, where its human partner is waiting to collect it. A good monkey can pick many coconuts in a single day, far more than a person could reach on their own.

The monkeys are trained by their owners from a young age. They learn which coconuts are ripe and ready to pick, and which ones need more time to grow. A ripe coconut makes a hollow sound when it is tapped, and the monkeys learn to listen for that sound. They also learn to pick only the coconuts their owner points to, and to leave the unripe ones alone.

## A Partnership That Is Hundreds of Years Old

The partnership between coconut farmers and monkeys is very old. People in Thailand have been working with monkeys to pick coconuts for hundreds of years, and the tradition has been passed down from one generation to the next. The monkeys are part of the family, and they live with their owners, eat with them, and work with them every day.

The monkeys and their owners understand each other very well. The owner gives the monkey signals and calls, and the monkey responds with quick, clever work. The monkey climbs the tree, picks the coconuts, and comes back down for a treat. It is a team effort, and both the monkey and the person benefit from it. The farmer gets his coconuts picked quickly and safely, and the monkey gets food, care and a safe home.

Some monkeys are so clever that they can pick coconuts from many different trees without being told which ones to climb. They learn the farmer's land, and they know which trees have ripe coconuts. They are proud of their work, and they seem to enjoy climbing and picking, just as they would in the wild.

## How the Monkeys Learn Their Job

Training a coconut monkey takes time and patience. The trainer starts with a young monkey, and teaches it step by step. First, the monkey learns to climb a short tree. Then it learns to twist a coconut. Then it learns to come back down when it is called. The trainer rewards the monkey with treats, such as fruit or rice, and praises it with kind words.

The best coconut monkeys are those that are calm, clever and eager to work. They form a strong bond with their trainer, and they trust each other completely. The trainer knows the monkey's personality, and the monkey knows the trainer's voice. It is a friendship as much as a working partnership, and it is built on trust and kindness.

Not all monkeys are suited to coconut picking, and some are better at it than others. The ones that are good at it are treasured by their owners, and they are looked after very well. They are given good food, a comfortable place to sleep, and plenty of time to rest and play.

## Monkeys in the Modern World

Today, coconut picking by monkeys is still practised in some parts of Thailand, although it is becoming less common. Many farmers now use long poles with hooks to pick coconuts, or they use machines to help them. But in some villages, the old tradition continues, and the monkeys are still an important part of farm life.

Some people worry about whether the monkeys are treated well, and they are right to ask. It is important that any animal that works with people is treated with kindness, given good food and rest, and allowed to live a happy life. In Thailand, there are now schools and training centres where monkeys are taught in a kind and gentle way, and where their welfare is looked after carefully.

The story of the coconut monkeys is a reminder that animals and people have worked together for a very long time, and that when we treat animals with kindness and respect, they can be wonderful partners. The monkeys are not just workers. They are friends, helpers and members of the family.

## A Monkey with a Special Talent

The coconut monkey shows us that animals can learn amazing skills, and that they can work together with people in clever and useful ways. With its strong arms, its quick mind and its love of climbing, the pig-tailed macaque is perfectly suited to life in the palm trees. It is a true partner of the coconut farmer, and a wonderful example of the friendship between animals and humans.

Next time you drink coconut water or eat a coconut sweet, think of the little monkey high up in the palm tree, twisting a coconut free and dropping it to the ground, working together with its human friend.
