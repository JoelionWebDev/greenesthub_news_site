On a farm, early in the morning, the sun rises over the fields and the farmer walks out to the barn. Inside, on a pile of soft hay, a cat stretches, yawns and opens its eyes. This is not a house cat. This is a barn cat — a working cat with a very important job. Its job is to guard the farm by catching mice. And it does that job very well.

## Meet the Barn Cat

A barn cat is a cat that lives on a farm, usually in the barn. It is not quite a pet and not quite a wild animal. It lives somewhere in between. Barn cats sleep in the hay, hunt in the fields and visit the farmhouse for food and a friendly pat. Some barn cats are friendly and love people. Others are shy and prefer to keep to themselves.

Barn cats come in all colours — ginger, black, white, tabby stripes and patches of many colours. They are usually strong and healthy, because they spend their lives running, jumping and hunting. They have sharp eyes that can see in the dark, long whiskers that help them feel their way in small spaces, and soft paws that let them walk without making a sound.

## Why Farms Need Cats

Why is a cat so important on a farm? The answer is mice — and rats too. A farm stores lots of grain, like wheat, maize and rice, to feed people and animals. Mice love grain. They sneak into barns at night, chew holes in sacks and eat the food. They also leave droppings that can make the grain dirty and unsafe.

A single cat can catch many mice. Just the smell of a cat can make mice stay away from a barn. Farmers say that a good barn cat is worth more than a trap, because a cat hunts day and night, in places where traps cannot reach. The cat guards the grain while the farmer sleeps.

## The Great Hunter

A cat's senses are amazing. Its ears can turn like little dishes to catch the smallest sound — the rustle of a mouse in the hay, or the squeak of a rat behind a wall. Its eyes can see in very dim light, which helps it hunt at dawn and dusk when mice are most active. Its whiskers tell it whether it can fit through a small gap, so it can follow a mouse into a tight space.

Cats also have very good balance. They can walk along a narrow beam in the barn roof, climb up a haystack and jump down without hurting themselves. Their soft paws let them move quietly, so the mouse never hears them coming. When a barn cat catches a mouse, it often brings it to the farmer — a gift, in the cat's mind, to say thank you.

## Life in the Barn

A barn cat's home is the barn itself. It sleeps in the hay, on top of sacks, or in a warm corner near the animals. In winter, the hay keeps it warm. In summer, it finds a cool spot in the shade. Some farmers make a special bed for their barn cats, with an old blanket in a wooden box. Others leave a window open so the cat can come and go as it pleases.

At feeding time, the farmer gives the barn cat food and fresh water. This is important, because even though cats hunt, they still need good food to stay healthy. A well-fed cat is a better hunter, because it has the energy to chase mice. Some farmers give their barn cats a little milk too, but cats are better off with clean water and proper cat food.

## Kittens in the Hay

Sometimes a barn cat has kittens, and that is a happy day on the farm. The mother cat finds a quiet, safe place — often in a pile of hay or behind a stack of boxes — and gives birth to her babies. Newborn kittens are tiny, with their eyes closed, and they stay close to their mother for warmth and milk.

As the kittens grow, their eyes open and they start to explore. They tumble over each other, chase their mother's tail and practise pouncing on pieces of straw. The mother cat teaches them how to hunt, bringing them a mouse to practise with. By the time they are a few months old, the kittens are ready to start catching mice on their own.

## How Farmers Look After Barn Cats

A good farmer looks after the barn cats, because a healthy cat is a happy hunter. The farmer gives the cats food and clean water every day, and takes them to the vet if they are sick or hurt. The vet may give the cat medicine to keep away fleas and worms, and will check that the cat is fit and well.

It is important to be gentle with barn cats. Some are friendly and love to be stroked, but others are shy and need time to trust people. The best way to make friends with a barn cat is to sit quietly nearby, speak softly and let the cat come to you. Never chase a cat or grab it suddenly — that will only frighten it.

## Fun Facts About Barn Cats
- A cat's whiskers are about as wide as its body, which helps it know if it can fit through a gap.
- Cats can see in light that is much dimmer than what people need — perfect for hunting at night.
- A cat's hearing is so good that it can hear a mouse squeaking from far away.
- Cats spend a large part of the day sleeping — sometimes for most of the day — so they have energy for hunting at night.
- A mother cat teaches her kittens to hunt by bringing them mice to practise with.

So next time you visit a farm, look for the barn cat. It might be asleep on a hay bale, or watching you from a window, or padding softly across the yard. Say hello quietly, and remember — that quiet little cat is one of the hardest workers on the whole farm.
