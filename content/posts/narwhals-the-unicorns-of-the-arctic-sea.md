# Narwhals: The Unicorns of the Arctic Sea

Long ago, people told stories about unicorns: magical white horses with a single, spiral horn on their forehead. Most people thought unicorns were only in fairy tales. But deep in the icy waters of the far north, there really is an animal with a long, spiral tusk. It is called a narwhal, and it is often called the unicorn of the sea.

Narwhals are a kind of whale, and they live in the cold Arctic Ocean around Canada, Greenland, and Russia. They spend most of their lives under the sea ice, in waters so cold that most animals could never survive there. Let us dive beneath the ice and meet these mysterious whales.

## The Amazing Tusk

The most famous thing about a narwhal is its tusk. It looks like a long, twisted horn sticking out of the front of its head, and it can grow very long. In fact, the tusk is not a horn at all. It is a tooth! A male narwhal has a tooth on the left side of its upper jaw that keeps growing outwards through its lip, twisting as it grows. It looks just like the horn of a unicorn from the stories.

Female narwhals do not usually have a tusk, although a few of them grow a short one. Male narwhals use their tusk to show off to other males. Sometimes two males cross their tusks together, a bit like crossing swords, to see who is stronger.

## Life in the Icy Arctic

Narwhals live in the cold waters of the Arctic, where the sea freezes in winter and melts in summer. They are medium-sized whales, growing to about the length of a small car. Their skin is pale grey or white, with dark spots and blotches that look like freckles. Baby narwhals, called calves, are dark grey all over, and they get lighter as they grow older. Very old narwhals can be almost completely white, which helps them blend in with the ice and snow.

Narwhals do not have a fin on their back like many other whales. Instead, they have a small ridge along their back. This helps them swim easily under the sea ice without bumping into it.

In the summer, narwhals swim in the bays and inlets along the Arctic coast, where the water is a little warmer and there is plenty of food. In the winter, they move out into the deep ocean, where the sea ice covers the water. They find cracks in the ice, called leads, where they can come up to breathe.

## Deep Divers

Narwhals are amazing divers. They can dive very deep into the ocean, far deeper than most people could ever swim. They hold their breath for a long time while they hunt for food in the dark, cold depths.

Under the water, narwhals use sound to find their way and to find food. They make clicks and whistles, and the sounds bounce off things in the water and come back to them, like an echo. This is called echolocation, and it works a bit like the way bats find their way in the dark. By listening to the echoes, narwhals can find fish and other food even in the darkest, deepest water, where no sunlight can reach.

Narwhals mostly eat fish, shrimp, and squid. They swim after their food and catch it with their mouth. They do not have teeth for chewing, apart from the tusk, so they swallow their food whole.

## Swimming Together

Narwhals like to swim in groups, called pods. A pod can have just a few narwhals, or it can have many hundreds of them swimming together. Sometimes, in the summer, thousands of narwhals gather in the same bay, and the water is full of their pale bodies and long tusks.

Narwhal mothers look after their calves for a long time. A baby narwhal stays close to its mother, drinking her milk and learning how to find food and how to swim under the ice.

## The Arctic's Biggest Hunters

Narwhals have a few natural hunters in the Arctic. Polar bears sometimes wait by the cracks in the ice to catch a narwhal when it comes up to breathe. Killer whales, also called orcas, hunt narwhals in the open water too. But narwhals are fast swimmers, and they can dive deep to escape danger.

People in the Arctic have hunted narwhals for a very long time. The Inuit people, who have lived in the Arctic for thousands of years, hunt narwhals for food and use every part of the animal. Today, narwhal hunting is carefully controlled, so that only a small number can be caught each year.

## Protecting the Unicorns of the Sea

Narwhals need clean, cold Arctic waters to survive, and their world is changing. As the Earth gets warmer, the sea ice in the Arctic is melting. Narwhals depend on the ice for their way of life: it protects them from hunters, and it helps them find food. If the ice disappears, narwhals will have a much harder time.

Scientists are studying narwhals to learn more about them and to help protect them. The more we learn about narwhals, the better we can protect them and their icy home.

## Fun Facts About Narwhals

- A narwhal's tusk is actually a tooth that grows out through its lip.
- The tusk can grow to about the length of a grown man.
- Narwhals have no fin on their back, just a small ridge.
- Baby narwhals are dark grey and get lighter as they grow older.
- Narwhals can dive very deep and hold their breath for a long time.
- They use echoes to find food in the dark, deep water.
- Narwhals swim in groups called pods.
- They live only in the cold waters of the Arctic.

Narwhals are not magical creatures from a storybook, but they are just as amazing as the unicorns in the tales. With their long, spiral tusks, their spotted skin, and their life under the ice, narwhals are some of the most wonderful whales in the whole world.
