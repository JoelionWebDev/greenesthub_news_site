# Hummingbirds: The Tiny Jewels of the Sky

Imagine a bird so small that it could sit on your finger. Imagine it hovering in the air like a tiny helicopter, its wings moving so fast that they are just a blur. Its feathers shine in the sun like a jewel. This is the hummingbird, one of the smallest and most amazing birds in the world.

## Meet the Hummingbird

Hummingbirds are very small birds. Many kinds are only a few centimetres long, and they weigh about as much as a coin. Even the largest hummingbirds are still small compared with most other birds. Their bodies are tiny, but they are full of energy.

Hummingbirds get their name from the humming sound their wings make. Their wings beat so fast that they make a soft hum, a little like a bee. You can often hear a hummingbird before you see it. Listen for a gentle hum near flowers, and you may spot one hovering in the air.

Their feathers are beautiful. Many hummingbirds have bright, shiny colours on their throats and heads. The colours can look green, blue, red, or purple, depending on how the light hits them. This shine is not paint. It comes from the way light bounces off tiny parts in the feathers. When the bird turns, the colour can seem to change.

## Wings That Work Like Magic

A hummingbird's wings are special. Most birds flap their wings up and down. Hummingbirds move their wings in a figure-of-eight pattern, which lets them hover in one place in the air. They can also fly backwards, sideways, and even upside down for a short moment. No other bird can fly quite like a hummingbird.

Their wings beat very fast, many times every second. You cannot count the beats with your eyes, because the wings move too quickly. This fast beating gives the hummingbird the power to stay still in the air while it drinks from a flower.

Hummingbirds are also fast fliers. When they want to travel, they can zoom through the air in a straight line. They are small, but they are strong and brave fliers, travelling long distances when they migrate to warmer places.

## Sipping Nectar from Flowers

Hummingbirds love flowers. They drink nectar, the sweet juice that flowers make. Their beaks are long and thin, perfect for reaching deep into a flower. Their tongues are long too, and they can lap up the nectar like a tiny straw.

When a hummingbird drinks from a flower, it also helps the flower. Pollen, the fine yellow dust from the flower, sticks to the bird's head and beak. When the bird flies to another flower, it carries the pollen along. This helps the flowers make seeds and grow new plants. Hummingbirds and flowers help each other, and both need each other to live.

Hummingbirds eat more than nectar. They also eat tiny insects and spiders. These give them protein, which helps them grow and stay strong. A hummingbird must eat often, because its small body uses energy very fast. It may visit hundreds of flowers in a single day.

## A Heart That Beats Fast

A hummingbird's heart beats very fast, much faster than a person's heart. Its body works hard all the time, like a tiny engine that never stops. To keep going, a hummingbird needs to eat a lot for its size. If it goes too long without food, it can get very weak.

At night, when it cannot eat, a hummingbird saves energy by going into a deep sleep called torpor. Its body slows down, its heart beats more slowly, and its body cools a little. In the morning, it wakes up and starts eating again. Torpor is like pressing a pause button to save energy until morning.

Hummingbirds live in the Americas, from Alaska in the north to the southern tip of South America. They live in forests, gardens, mountains, and deserts. Wherever there are flowers, there may be hummingbirds. Some kinds stay in one place all year. Others travel far when the seasons change, flying across land and sea to find warm weather and flowers.

## Nests Like Tiny Cups

Mother hummingbirds build tiny nests. A nest is about the size of a small cup, made from soft plant fibres, moss, and spider webs. The spider webs help hold the nest together and let it stretch as the babies grow. The outside is often covered with bits of lichen, so the nest looks like part of the tree.

The mother lays two tiny eggs, each about the size of a small bean. She sits on them to keep them warm until they hatch. The baby hummingbirds are very small and have almost no feathers at first. The mother feeds them by putting nectar and insects into their beaks.

The babies grow fast. In a few weeks, they have feathers and are ready to fly. The mother teaches them by example, and soon they are hovering at flowers on their own, learning to find food and stay safe.

## Tiny Birds, Big Journeys

Some hummingbirds make amazing journeys. One kind, the ruby-throated hummingbird, flies across the sea to reach its winter home. This is a long flight for such a tiny bird, and it must eat a lot before it sets off, to store energy for the trip. It is like filling up a tiny fuel tank before a long drive.

Hummingbirds remind us that being small does not mean being weak. These tiny jewels of the sky can hover like helicopters, fly backwards, cross seas, and help flowers grow. They are full of colour, full of energy, and full of surprises.

The next time you see flowers in a garden, stop and watch. You might hear a soft hum, see a flash of shining colour, and spot a hummingbird hovering in the air. It will be busy, beautiful, and gone in a moment, like a jewel flying past in the sunshine.
