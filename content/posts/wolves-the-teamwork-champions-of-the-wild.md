## What Is a Wolf?

A wolf is a wild animal that looks a lot like a big dog. In fact, wolves are the wild cousins of the dogs that live in our homes! Long, long ago, some wolves became friends with people, and over many years they became the pet dogs we know today. But wild wolves still live free in forests and on wide open plains.

Wolves have thick fur that keeps them warm in cold weather. They have long legs, big paws, sharp teeth, and ears that stand up straight. Wolves have yellow or golden eyes that look very clever and watchful.

Wolves are very strong animals. They can run fast and for a very long time without getting tired. Their sense of smell is amazing — a wolf can smell things from far, far away, much better than we can.

## Where Do Wolves Live?

Wolves live in many parts of the world. You can find them in North America, Europe, and Asia. They live in forests, on mountains, in cold snowy lands, and on wide grassy plains. Wolves like places where there is lots of open space to run and plenty of animals to hunt.

In some places, people are now helping wolves return to forests and mountains where they had not been seen for a long time, and that is good news for nature.

Wolves need a large home area because they travel a lot every day. A wolf pack can walk for many hours, looking for food and exploring its land. In winter, wolves grow a thicker coat of fur to stay warm in the snow, and in summer their coat becomes thinner and lighter.

## The Wolf Pack: A Family Team

Wolves do not live alone like tigers do. They live together in a family group called a pack. A pack is usually made up of a mother and father wolf, their grown-up children, and their new pups. A pack can be small, with only a few wolves, or it can be bigger, with many wolves all living and working together.

Every wolf in the pack has a job to do. The parent wolves lead the pack and make the big decisions, like where to hunt and where to rest. The older brothers and sisters help look after the young pups, bring them food, and teach them how to behave.

Wolves talk to each other in many ways. They howl, bark, whine, and growl. They also use their bodies to talk — the way they hold their ears and tail tells other wolves how they feel. Wolves greet each other by licking faces and wagging their tails!

## What Do Wolves Eat?

Wolves are meat eaters. They hunt animals such as deer, elk, and wild pigs. Sometimes they also eat smaller animals like rabbits and mice. Wolves are clever hunters, and they hunt together as a team — that is what makes them so good at it.

When a wolf pack hunts, the wolves work together like a team of players. Some wolves chase the prey, while others wait in the right place to cut it off. They take turns running so that no wolf gets too tired. By working together, wolves can hunt animals that are much bigger than one wolf alone could ever catch. Teamwork is the wolf's greatest strength!

They also eat berries and other plants sometimes, but meat is their main food. Wolves are very important in nature because they keep the numbers of other animals in balance. When wolves hunt the weak and sick animals, the healthy animals stay strong, and the forests and grasslands stay healthy too.

## Wolf Pups: Growing Up in the Pack

Baby wolves are called pups. When pups are born, they are tiny, blind, and helpless. They stay in a safe den — a cosy hole in the ground or a sheltered place under rocks or tree roots. The mother wolf stays with them, keeping them warm and feeding them, while the father wolf brings food for the whole family.

As the pups grow, they open their eyes and start to explore. They love to play! Pups wrestle with each other, chase each other around, and practise pouncing and sneaking, just like grown-up wolves do when they hunt. Playing helps them learn and grow strong. All the grown-up wolves in the pack help look after the pups. The older brothers and sisters babysit them and teach them the rules of the pack.

When the pups are bigger, they start to join the pack on short trips. They learn how to howl, how to follow scents, and how to work as part of the team. By the time they are grown up, they are ready to hunt with the pack.

## Wolves and People

For a long time, people were afraid of wolves. In many old stories and fairy tales, the wolf is the scary villain. But real wolves are not like the wolves in stories. Wild wolves are usually shy around people and try to stay far away from them. A wolf would much rather run away than meet a person.

Today, many people have learned that wolves are good for nature, and scientists study them to learn how they live and work together. Many people love to hear a wolf howl in the wild. A wolf's howl is a beautiful, wild sound that can travel a very long way across the hills and forests.

Some places have special parks and wild areas where wolves are protected, so they can live safely. People who work there watch over the wolves and make sure they have enough space and food.

- Wolves live in family groups called packs, and every wolf has a job to do.
- Wolves are the wild cousins of pet dogs.
