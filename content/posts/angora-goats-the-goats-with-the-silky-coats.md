Angora goats grow some of the softest, silkiest hair of any farm animal. Their beautiful fleece is called mohair, and people use it to make clothes. These gentle goats look like they are wearing fluffy white jumpers, and their long, silky locks fall in soft curls all the way down their bodies. They are some of the most beautiful animals on the farm.

## Goats from Far Away

Angora goats come from a place called Ankara, in the country of Turkey. Long ago, the people of Ankara kept these special goats for their wonderful fleece. The goats were so precious that for many years, they were not allowed to leave Turkey. People in other countries wanted them, but they had to wait.

Eventually, angora goats travelled to other parts of the world, including South Africa, America and Australia. Today, they live on farms in many countries, and people keep them for their mohair, which is one of the most luxurious fibres in the world.

Angora goats are medium-sized goats with long, silky hair that covers their whole body. Their hair is usually white, but it can also be brown, black or grey. They have long, floppy ears and small, curved horns. They look gentle and calm, and they usually are.

## What Is Mohair?

Mohair is the name for the fleece of the angora goat. It is soft, silky and shiny, and it feels lovely to touch. People have used mohair for hundreds of years to make warm jumpers, scarves, blankets and even carpets. It is strong, it keeps its shape well, and it takes dye beautifully, so it can be made into clothes of many colours.

Angora goats are shorn, which means their fleece is cut off, usually twice a year, in spring and autumn. Shearing does not hurt the goat — it is a bit like getting a haircut. After shearing, the goat's fleece grows back, soft and silky, ready to be cut again in a few months.

The fleece is then washed, combed and spun into yarn, which is used to knit or weave clothes. A single angora goat can produce a good amount of mohair each year, enough to make several warm jumpers.

## Gentle and Friendly Goats

Angora goats are known for their calm and gentle nature. They are friendly animals that enjoy being around people, and they are often kept as pets as well as for their mohair. They like to be stroked, and they will follow their owners around the farm, hoping for a treat.

They are also clever and curious. Angora goats like to explore their fields, nibble on leaves and shrubs, and climb on low rocks and logs. They are social animals, and they live in herds, which means groups of goats that stay together. They do not like to be alone, and they are happiest when they have goat friends to keep them company.

Angora goats are not as hardy as some other goat breeds. Their long, silky coats need care, and they can get cold and wet in bad weather. Farmers need to give them a dry shelter and keep them out of heavy rain, so their beautiful coats stay clean and healthy.

## What Angora Goats Eat

Angora goats eat hay, grass, leaves and shrubs, just like other goats. They enjoy browsing, which means eating leaves from bushes and low trees, and they are very good at clearing overgrown land. They also need fresh water every day and a mineral lick, which gives them the vitamins and minerals they need to grow strong, healthy fleece.

Because their fleece grows so fast, angora goats need good food to stay healthy. A goat that does not eat well will not grow a good coat. Farmers take special care to feed their angora goats well, so their mohair stays soft and silky.

## Baby Angora Goats

Baby angora goats are called kids, and they are born with soft, curly fleece that looks like tiny ringlets. They are very cute, and they stay close to their mothers for the first few months, drinking milk and learning to eat grass and hay.

Mother angora goats are caring and protective. They lick their babies clean, keep them warm, and call to them with soft bleats. The kids play together, jumping and bouncing around the field, and their curly fleece bounces with them. It is a delightful sight.

## Looking After Angora Goats

Angora goats need a safe field with a strong fence, a dry shelter, and fresh water. Their coats need brushing from time to time to keep them clean and free of tangles, and they need to be shorn twice a year. Their hooves need trimming, and they need visits from the vet to stay healthy.

They are wonderful animals for people who love fibre and crafts, because their mohair can be spun into yarn and made into beautiful clothes. Many people who keep angora goats also enjoy spinning and knitting, turning the soft fleece into scarves, jumpers and blankets.

## A Goat in a Silky Coat

The angora goat is a gentle, beautiful animal that gives us one of the finest fibres in the world. Its silky mohair has kept people warm for hundreds of years, and its calm, friendly nature has won it many friends on farms around the world.

If you ever see an angora goat, look at its long, silky coat, falling in soft curls. Then remember the goat from Ankara, wearing its fluffy white jumper, and the warm clothes its fleece becomes. It is a farm animal with a very special gift.
