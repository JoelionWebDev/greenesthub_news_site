Imagine you are swimming in the deep blue ocean when a huge, flat shape drifts past you. It is taller than you, round like a giant plate, and it has no tail like other fish. It looks as if someone took a normal fish and chopped off the back half. What on Earth is it? It is an ocean sunfish, one of the strangest and most wonderful fish in the whole sea.

The ocean sunfish is also called the mola mola, which means "millstone" in Latin, because its round, flat body looks a little like an old millstone used for grinding grain. It is the heaviest bony fish in the world, and it can grow to be enormous — much heavier than a grown man, and as tall as a small car is long when you measure from its top fin to its bottom fin. Yet for all its size, the ocean sunfish is gentle, slow, and quite harmless.

## A Fish That Looks Like Half a Fish

The first thing anyone notices about an ocean sunfish is its shape. Most fish are long and thin, built like torpedoes so they can zoom through the water. The sunfish is the opposite. Its body is short, flat, and round, like a huge silver pancake standing on its edge. Instead of a normal tail fin, it has a strange wavy edge at the back called a clavus, which looks like a frilly fan.

The sunfish has two very long fins, one on the top and one on the bottom. When it spreads them out, the fish can be taller than a person. It moves by flapping these big fins up and down, a bit like a bird flapping its wings. It is not a fast swimmer. It drifts and glides through the water slowly, like a lazy balloon floating through the sky. But it can dive deep, far down into the dark, cold water where the sun barely reaches, and then come back up again.

Its skin is thick and rough, with a bumpy, leathery feel. The sunfish's mouth is small and round, almost like a beak, and it cannot close it properly.

## Sunbathing in the Sea

Ocean sunfish love to float near the surface of the sea on their sides, soaking up the sunshine. This is called basking, and it is how they got the name "sunfish". Nobody knows for sure why they do it, but scientists think it helps them warm up after diving into the cold deep water, a bit like you warming up in the sun after a cold swim. It may also help them get rid of tiny parasites on their skin, because seabirds sometimes land on a basking sunfish and pick off the little pests. Imagine a fish so big and calm that birds use it as a floating restaurant table!

Sunfish live in warm and temperate oceans all around the world. They are found in the Atlantic, the Pacific, and the Indian Oceans, drifting with the currents. They travel long distances, following the warm water and the food. Young sunfish look very different from adults. Baby sunfish are tiny, spiky, and look like little stars. As they grow, they lose their spikes and become smooth and round. They grow fast, going from a tiny baby to a giant fish in a few years, which is one of the fastest growth rates of any bony fish.

## What Does a Giant Fish Eat?

For such a huge animal, the ocean sunfish eats some very small and soft food. Its favourite food is jellyfish. Jellyfish float through the water like soft, wobbly umbrellas, and the sunfish slurps them up with its small beak-like mouth. It also eats small fish, squid, and tiny sea creatures called zooplankton, which are so small you can barely see them.

Jellyfish are not very filling, so a sunfish has to eat a lot of them to stay big and strong. It spends much of its day searching for food, drifting through the water with its mouth open, sucking in whatever soft food floats by. It does not hunt like a shark or chase its food. It simply glides along and snacks as it goes.

Because jellyfish can sting, you might wonder why the sunfish does not get hurt. Its thick skin and its tough mouth help protect it, and it seems to enjoy jellyfish so much that it does not mind at all. In a way, the sunfish helps keep jellyfish numbers in balance, which is good for other sea animals too.

## Gentle Giants of the Deep

Even though ocean sunfish are enormous, they are not dangerous to people at all. They have no sharp teeth, no claws, and no venom. They are calm and slow, and they do not chase or attack anything. Divers who meet a sunfish in the water say it is a magical experience, because the giant fish often swims right up to look at them with its big round eyes, curious and unafraid.

Sunfish do have enemies, though. Sharks and sea lions sometimes attack them, especially young ones. But their huge size and thick skin help protect adults. The biggest danger to sunfish today comes from people. Sunfish often get caught by accident in fishing nets meant for other fish, and they can be hurt by plastic rubbish floating in the sea, which they sometimes mistake for jellyfish. That is why it is so important to keep our oceans clean and to protect these gentle giants.

## Why the Ocean Sunfish Is So Special

The ocean sunfish is special because it breaks all the rules of what a fish should look like. It is round instead of long, it has no real tail, it flaps its fins like wings, and it sunbathes on its side like a tourist on holiday. It is the heaviest bony fish in the sea, yet it eats tiny jellyfish and drifts along peacefully.

Scientists are still learning about sunfish. They are hard to study because they live far out in the open ocean and dive very deep. But every time we learn something new about them, they surprise us all over again.

So the next time you think of the ocean, picture a giant silver pancake drifting through the blue water, flapping its long fins, and basking in the sunshine. That is the ocean sunfish — a gentle giant, a champion jellyfish eater, and one of the strangest and most lovable fish in the whole wide sea.
