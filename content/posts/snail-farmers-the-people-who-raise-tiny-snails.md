Have you ever seen a snail slowly gliding across a leaf after the rain, leaving a shiny trail behind it? It carries its home on its back, moves at its own gentle pace, and munches on green leaves. It might look small and simple, but to a snail farmer, that tiny creature is very special.

A snail farmer is a person who raises snails on purpose, in a safe and caring place, so the snails can grow big, strong, and healthy. This kind of farming is called snail farming, and in some places it has a fancy name: heliciculture. That is a big word, but it simply means raising snails.

Snail farming is popular in many parts of the world, especially in Africa and Europe. In Nigeria, many farmers raise a kind of snail called the giant African land snail, which can grow much bigger than the tiny garden snails you might see at home. People raise snails for food, because snail meat is a tasty and healthy food in many cultures, and also for other useful things. Let us step into the slow, gentle world of snail farming.

## Meet the Snails

Snails are soft, gentle animals with a hard shell on their backs. The shell is their home and their protection. If a snail feels scared, it can pull its soft body right inside its shell and hide until the danger goes away. The shell grows as the snail grows, getting bigger and stronger over time.

A snail has a soft body, a head, and two pairs of feelers on its head. The longer pair has tiny eyes at the tips, like little periscopes that help the snail see. The shorter pair helps the snail smell and feel its way around. Snails do not have legs like we do. Instead, they have one big, flat foot that ripples and slides along the ground. That is how they move so slowly and smoothly.

Snails love damp, shady places. They come out most often after rain, or in the cool of the morning and evening, when the sun is not too hot. Hot, dry weather can make them hide away, so snail farmers must keep their homes cool and moist.

Snails eat plants. They love fresh leaves, fruits, and vegetables. They munch with a tiny tongue covered in even tinier teeth, which helps them scrape food off leaves. Some snails also need foods that help their shells grow strong, like foods rich in calcium. A healthy snail has a smooth, hard shell and a plump, happy body.

One amazing thing about snails is that they lay eggs. A single snail can lay many small, round, white eggs in soft, damp soil. Those eggs hatch into tiny baby snails, each with a tiny shell. Watching baby snails grow is one of the joys of snail farming.

## A Day on a Snail Farm

A snail farmer's day is calm and gentle, just like the snails themselves. It starts with checking on the snails. The farmer walks around the snail pens, which are the safe enclosures where the snails live, and looks for any problems. Are the snails active? Are they eating well? Is the soil damp enough? Is anyone's shell cracked?

Feeding time is very important. The farmer gives the snails fresh leaves, like pawpaw leaves, cocoyam leaves, and other green leaves that snails love. They also give them fruits like banana, pawpaw, and watermelon, and vegetables like cabbage and carrot. The food must be fresh and clean, with no chemicals that could hurt the snails.

The farmer also makes sure the snails have clean water. Snails need moisture to stay healthy, so the farmer sprinkles water on the soil and leaves, especially in hot weather. The pens are kept shady, with plants or covers to protect the snails from the hot sun.

Cleaning is another big job. The farmer removes old, rotten food, checks the soil, and makes sure the pens are tidy. A clean home means healthy snails. The farmer also checks the fences and nets around the pens. Snails are slow, but they are good at escaping through tiny gaps, and other animals like rats, birds, and ants might try to eat them. Keeping the snails safe is a top priority.

In the evening, the farmer might collect eggs from the soil, count new baby snails, and move snails to different pens as they grow. Big snails, small snails, and baby snails often live in separate pens, so the little ones do not get squashed and everyone gets the right food and space.

## From Tiny Eggs to Big, Healthy Snails

Raising snails takes patience, because snails grow slowly. A baby snail starts life as a tiny egg, smaller than a pea. When it hatches, it is very small, with a see-through shell. Over many months, it eats, grows, and its shell gets bigger and harder.

The farmer helps the snails grow by giving them good food and a safe home. Foods rich in calcium, like crushed eggshells or special snail feed, help the shells grow strong. Fresh leaves and fruits help the snails grow plump and healthy. The farmer watches them grow week by week, and it is very exciting to see a tiny baby snail become a big, strong adult.

Snail farmers must also protect their snails from sickness. If a snail gets sick, it might stop eating or hide in its shell for a long time. The farmer learns to spot the early signs of trouble, like a cracked shell, a dry body, or a snail that does not move. They keep the pens clean, give fresh food, and make sure the snails are not too crowded. Healthy snails are happy snails.

When the snails are fully grown, the farmer can sell them. People buy snails to cook and eat, and in many places snail meat is a popular and healthy food. It is rich in protein and low in fat, and many people love its taste. Snails are also used in other ways, and their shells can be used for crafts. Nothing is wasted on a good snail farm.
