If you ever walk through a forest in Africa or Asia and hear a loud, honking call from high in the trees, look up — you may have just heard a hornbill! Hornbills are big, bold birds with huge beaks and a funny helmet-like bump on top. They are noisy, colourful, and full of character.

There are many different kinds of hornbills, and they come in all sorts of sizes and colours. Some are black and white, some are brightly coloured, and some have beaks that look almost too big for their heads! Let us meet these wonderful birds and find out how they live.

## What Do They Look Like?

The first thing you notice about a hornbill is its beak. It is huge — long, curved, and often brightly coloured in shades of yellow, orange, or red. On top of the beak sits a bump called a casque, which looks a bit like a helmet. The casque can be many different shapes, depending on the kind of hornbill, and it makes the bird look very grand indeed.

Hornbills have strong bodies, long tails, and broad wings. Their feathers are often black and white, but some hornbills have bright colours too. Their eyes are big and bright, and they often have long eyelashes, which make them look quite funny and friendly.

Even though the beak looks very heavy, it is actually quite light. Inside, it is full of tiny air pockets, a bit like a sponge, so the bird can carry it around easily. The beak is strong enough to pick fruit, catch insects, and even toss food up into the air and catch it again!

## Where Do They Live?

Hornbills live in the warm parts of Africa and Asia. They make their homes in forests, woodlands, and savannas, where there are plenty of tall trees. They need big, old trees with holes in them, because that is where they build their nests.

Some hornbills live deep in thick rainforests, while others prefer open woodlands where they can fly easily between the trees. They are strong fliers, and their wings make a loud whooshing sound as they fly through the air. When a group of hornbills flies overhead, you can often hear them before you see them!

Hornbills do not migrate far. They stay in the same area all year round, moving around the forest to find food as the seasons change. They are very much at home in the trees, and they spend most of their lives up in the branches.

## What Do They Eat?

Hornbills eat many different things. Their favourite food is fruit, especially figs and other soft fruits that grow in the forest. They use their big beaks to pluck fruit from the branches, then toss it up into the air and catch it at the back of their throat. It is a clever trick, and it is fun to watch!

Hornbills also eat insects, small lizards, and even the eggs and chicks of other birds. Some kinds of hornbills spend time on the ground, hunting for insects and small creatures in the grass. They are not fussy eaters, and they will try almost anything that looks tasty.

Because hornbills eat so much fruit and fly from tree to tree, they help the forest in a very important way. When they eat fruit, the seeds pass through them and drop to the ground in a new place, where they can grow into new trees. Hornbills are gardeners of the forest, helping to plant new trees wherever they go!

## A Very Special Nest

Hornbills have one of the most amazing nesting habits of any bird in the world. When a female hornbill is ready to lay her eggs, she climbs into a hole in a tree, and then the hole is sealed up with mud, droppings, and bits of fruit — leaving only a tiny slit open. The female stays inside the sealed nest, safe and sound, while the male brings her food through the little slit.

Inside the nest, the female lays her eggs and looks after the chicks when they hatch. She stays sealed inside for a long time, while the male works hard, flying back and forth with fruit and insects for his family. When the chicks are big enough, the female breaks out of the nest, and the young birds soon follow her out into the world.

This clever nesting trick keeps the mother and her chicks safe from animals that might want to eat them. It is one of the most wonderful examples of teamwork in the bird world!

## Family Life in the Trees

Hornbills are family birds. The male and female stay together and work as a team to raise their chicks. The male is a hard-working father, bringing food to the nest many times a day. The female keeps the chicks warm and safe inside the tree hole.

Young hornbills are born without feathers, and they are completely helpless at first. They depend on their parents for everything. As they grow, their feathers come in, and they learn to eat the food their father brings. When they finally leave the nest, they stay close to their parents for a while, learning how to find fruit and fly through the forest.

Hornbills are also social birds. Some kinds live in small family groups, and you may see them flying together through the forest, calling loudly to each other as they go. Their loud calls help them keep in touch when they are far apart in the trees.

## Fun Facts

Here are some fun facts about hornbills:

- A hornbill's beak looks heavy, but it is light inside, full of tiny air pockets.
- The bump on top of the beak is called a casque, and it looks like a helmet.
- Hornbills toss fruit into the air and catch it at the back of their throat.
- The female seals herself inside the tree nest with mud, leaving only a small slit for food.
- Hornbills help plant new trees by dropping seeds as they fly through the forest.
- Their wings make a loud whooshing sound when they fly.
- Some hornbills live in Africa, and others live in Asia — but they all have big beaks!
