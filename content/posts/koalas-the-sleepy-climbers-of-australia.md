High in the gum trees of Australia lives one of the cutest animals in the world — the koala. With its big fluffy ears, round nose, and sleepy eyes, the koala looks like a soft toy come to life. But koalas are real wild animals with a very unusual way of life. They sleep for most of the day, eat only one kind of leaf, and almost never come down from their trees. Let us climb up and meet them!

## Not a Bear at All

First, an important fact: even though people sometimes call them "koala bears," koalas are not bears at all! They are marsupials, which means they belong to the same group of animals as kangaroos and wombats. Marsupial mothers carry their babies in a pouch, just like kangaroos do.

The koala's pouch is a little different from a kangaroo's pouch, though. A kangaroo's pouch opens upwards, but a koala's pouch opens backwards, towards its back legs. This might sound strange, but it makes sense: koalas climb trees, and a backwards-opening pouch stops dirt and leaves from falling in while the mother climbs.

Baby koalas are called joeys, just like baby kangaroos. When a joey is born, it is tiny — about the size of a jelly bean — with no fur and its eyes closed. It crawls all by itself from where it is born into its mother's pouch, where it stays safe, warm, and fed for many months. When it finally comes out, it rides on its mother's back or belly, holding on tightly as she climbs through the trees.

## Life in the Gum Trees

Koalas live in the eucalyptus forests of eastern Australia. Eucalyptus trees are also called gum trees, and Australia has hundreds of different kinds. Koalas are very fussy eaters — they only eat the leaves of certain kinds of eucalyptus trees. Of all the hundreds of gum tree types, koalas will only eat from a small number of them. And even then, they choose the tastiest, freshest leaves.

Koalas have strong arms and legs with sharp claws that help them grip tree branches. They have two thumbs on each front paw, which helps them hold on tightly to branches. They are excellent climbers and can move through the trees with surprising ease, even though they look slow and sleepy.

Koalas almost never come down to the ground. They eat, sleep, and raise their babies up in the trees. They only come down when they need to move to a different tree, because the trees are too far apart to jump between. On the ground, koalas are slow and clumsy, and they are in more danger from dogs and other animals — so they climb back up as quickly as they can.

## The Sleepiest Animals Around

Koalas are famous for sleeping a lot — and we mean a LOT. A koala can sleep for most of the day and night, often for around 18 to 20 hours out of every 24! Why do they sleep so much? The answer is in their food.

Eucalyptus leaves are not very nutritious. They do not give the koala much energy, and they are also tough to digest. In fact, eucalyptus leaves contain chemicals that are poisonous to most animals! But koalas have special stomachs and a very long digestive system that can slowly break down the leaves and remove the poisons. This takes a lot of time and energy, so koalas save energy by sleeping most of the day.

Koalas get most of the water they need from the leaves they eat, so they rarely need to drink. The word "koala" comes from an Aboriginal Australian word that means "no water." Is that not a wonderful name for an animal that almost never drinks?

## Koala Chatter

Koalas are mostly quiet animals, but they do make sounds. Male koalas make a deep, grunting bellow during the breeding season. It is a surprisingly loud, deep sound for such a small animal — like a snore mixed with a burp! People who hear it for the first time in the dark Australian bush can be quite startled.

Mother koalas make soft clicking and humming sounds to their joeys, and joeys make little squeaks when they want attention. Koalas also communicate by scent. They have a special scent gland on their chest, and males rub their chest against tree trunks to leave their scent behind. This tells other koalas, "This is my tree!"

## Koala Families and Homes

Koalas are mostly solitary animals, which means they like to live alone. Each koala has its own home range — a group of trees that it lives in. Male koalas have larger home ranges than females, and their ranges sometimes overlap with the females' trees.

A koala's home range needs to have plenty of the right kind of gum trees, with enough tasty leaves to eat all year round. If the trees are cut down, the koala has nowhere to live and nothing to eat. That is why protecting eucalyptus forests is so important for koalas.

## Koalas Need Our Help

Sadly, koalas are in trouble. Their forests are being cut down for houses, roads and farms. Bushfires, which happen in Australia during hot, dry summers, can destroy huge areas of forest and harm many koalas. Koalas can also get sick, and because they live in smaller and smaller patches of forest, it is harder for them to find mates and raise healthy joeys.

The good news is that many Australians love koalas and are working hard to protect them. Wildlife carers rescue injured koalas and look after them until they are well enough to return to the wild. Conservation groups plant new gum trees to replace the forests that have been lost. Scientists study koalas to learn how to help them stay healthy. In some places, special crossings and fences help koalas move safely between patches of forest without having to cross busy roads.

Koalas are a symbol of Australia, loved all over the world for their sweet faces and sleepy ways. They remind us that every animal has its own special way of living — even if that way involves sleeping for most of the day! The next time you see a picture of a koala hugging a tree trunk with its eyes half closed, remember: it is not being lazy. It is saving energy, digesting its tough leafy dinner, and living the slow, peaceful life of Australia's sleepiest climber.
