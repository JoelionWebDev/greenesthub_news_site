What if we told you there is a mammal that lays eggs, has spines like a hedgehog, a snout like a mini elephant trunk, and a tongue that can catch ants? That animal is real, and it is called the echidna. Echidnas are some of the most unusual animals on Earth, and they have been around for a very long time. Let us dig into the strange and wonderful world of the echidna.

## What Is an Echidna?

An echidna is a mammal, which means it is warm-blooded and feeds its babies with milk. But it is a very unusual mammal, because instead of giving birth to live babies, it lays eggs! Echidnas are part of a tiny group of mammals called monotremes. There are only two kinds of monotremes in the whole world: the echidna and the platypus. That makes the echidna very special indeed.

Echidnas live in Australia and on the island of New Guinea. The most common kind is the short-beaked echidna, which lives all over Australia, from hot deserts to cold mountain forests. There are also long-beaked echidnas that live in the forests of New Guinea.

Some people also call the echidna the spiny anteater, because it eats ants and has spines. But it is not really an anteater, and it is not a hedgehog or a porcupine either. It is one of a kind.

## Spines, Fur and a Super Snout

An echidna looks like a small ball of spines. Its back is covered in sharp spines, which are really thick, hollow hairs. Between the spines there is soft fur that keeps the echidna warm. The spines are usually creamy white or yellowish at the tips, with dark brown fur underneath. From a distance, an echidna looks like a prickly ball waddling through the grass.

The echidna's most amazing tool is its long, thin snout. The snout works a bit like a nose and a mouth at the same time. At the tip of the snout there are two tiny nostrils and a small mouth. Inside the mouth is a long, sticky tongue that can stretch out far beyond the snout. When an echidna finds ants or termites, it flicks its tongue in and out, catching the insects like a sticky trap. Echidnas have no teeth at all, so they crush their food against the roof of their mouth instead.

## Digging for Dinner

Ants and termites are the echidna's favourite foods. Echidnas hunt by sniffing the ground with their clever snouts. They can smell ant nests and termite mounds hidden under the soil. Once they find a nest, they dig in with their powerful claws, breaking the hard earth apart. Then they poke their long snout into the nest and lick up the insects with their sticky tongue.

An echidna can eat a huge number of ants and termites in a single day. It flicks its tongue in and out very fast, sometimes many times in a minute. Besides ants and termites, echidnas also eat beetle larvae, worms, and other small insects they find under logs and rocks.

Echidnas are not in a hurry. They waddle slowly through the bush, sniffing and digging as they go. They are most active in the early morning and late afternoon, when the air is cool. On very hot days, they shelter in burrows or under logs. In the coldest places, they can even sleep through the winter, like a bear.

## Curling into a Spiny Ball

Echidnas do not run fast, and they cannot climb trees or swim away from danger. So how do they stay safe? Their spines are their secret weapon. When an echidna feels scared, it does something very clever. It curls its body into a tight ball, tucking its head and legs underneath, so that only its sharp spines are showing. Most hungry animals take one look at that prickly ball and decide to leave it alone.

If an echidna is on soft ground, it has an even cleverer trick. It digs straight down into the soil, using its strong claws, until only its spiny back is sticking out of the ground. It is like a little fortress with sharp walls. An animal that tries to bite it will only get a mouthful of spines.

Echidnas are shy and quiet animals. They do not make much noise, and they prefer to be left alone. If you ever see one in the wild, the best thing to do is watch it from far away and let it waddle on its way.

## An Egg-Laying Mammal

Here is the most amazing thing about echidnas: they lay eggs. After mating, the mother echidna lays a single soft, leathery egg. She places the egg into a special pouch on her belly, a bit like a pocket. The egg stays warm and safe in the pouch until it hatches.

When the baby echidna hatches, it is tiny, pink, and has no spines yet. The baby is called a puggle. The puggle stays in the pouch, drinking its mother's milk. Echidna mothers do not have nipples like other mammals. Instead, the milk comes out through tiny spots on the mother's skin, and the puggle licks it up. It is a very unusual way to feed a baby, but it works perfectly.

After a few months, the puggle starts to grow spines, and the mother moves it into a burrow. She comes back to feed it every few days, while the puggle stays hidden and safe in the burrow. When the young echidna is old enough, it leaves the burrow and begins its own life, digging for ants and curling into a spiny ball when it needs to.

## Fun Facts About Echidnas

Here are some fun things to remember. An echidna's tongue can be longer than its whole snout. Echidnas have no teeth, but they can still crush their food. A baby echidna is called a puggle, and it hatches from an egg about the size of a grape. Echidnas are excellent diggers and can disappear into the ground in no time at all. And echidnas are one of only two mammals in the world that lay eggs — the other is the platypus.

The echidna shows us that nature loves surprises. A mammal that lays eggs, a spiny ball that digs for ants, a shy little animal with a super snout — the echidna is proof that the wild world is full of wonders waiting to be discovered.
