Have you ever gone on holiday and wondered who would look after your dog, your cat, or your little rabbit while you were away? That is the job of a pet sitter. A pet sitter is a kind, trusted person who comes to look after pets when their owners are away from home. They feed the pets, give them fresh water, play with them, and make sure they feel safe and loved. For many pets, staying at home with a friendly sitter is much nicer than going to a kennel, because they can sleep in their own bed and keep their own routine. Pet sitters are animal lovers who turn their love of pets into a job, and pets all over the world are very glad they do.

## Who is a pet sitter?

A pet sitter is a person who is paid to look after someone else's pets. Some pet sitters visit the pet's home once or twice a day, while others stay in the home the whole time the owner is away. Some pet sitters look after pets in their own homes. Pet sitters look after all kinds of animals: dogs and cats, of course, but also rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, birds, fish, and even reptiles. A good pet sitter is responsible, kind, and very organised. They must remember each pet's food, medicine, and routine, and they must know what to do if a pet gets sick or scared. Many pet sitters have training in animal care, and some have learned first aid for pets. Above all, a pet sitter must love animals. Pets can tell when a person is kind, and a pet sitter who truly loves animals will soon have a house full of happy, wagging tails and purring cats.

## A pet sitter's day

A pet sitter's day is full of visits and cuddles. In the morning, the sitter might visit one home to feed a cat and clean its litter tray, then walk a dog in the park, then stop at another home to feed a rabbit and a guinea pig. Each pet has its own routine. Dogs need walks, playtime, and lots of attention. Cats need fresh food and water, a clean litter tray, and a lap to sit on. Small pets like hamsters need fresh bedding and food, and fish need to be fed just the right amount. The sitter checks every pet carefully, looking to see that it is eating well, drinking water, and acting normally. In the evening, the sitter comes back for more feeding, more playtime, and more cuddles. Some sitters also water the plants, bring in the post, and turn the lights on and off, so the home looks lived in while the owners are away.

## Meeting the pets first

Before a pet sitter starts a job, they always meet the pets first. This is called a meet and greet, and it is very important. The sitter visits the home, meets the pets, and talks with the owner. The owner shows the sitter where the food is kept, how much to give, and when to feed the pets. The owner also tells the sitter about the pet's likes and dislikes. Some dogs are scared of thunder. Some cats will only eat one kind of food. Some rabbits love to be stroked, and some do not like to be picked up. The sitter writes everything down in a notebook so nothing is forgotten. The sitter also learns where the pet's vet is, and gets the vet's phone number, just in case. The meet and greet helps the pets get to know the sitter too, so when the owner goes away, the pets already have a new friend.


## Looking after cats and small pets

Cats and small pets need just as much care as dogs, even though they do not need walks. Cats are very attached to their homes and their routines. A pet sitter keeps the cat's routine the same, feeding it at the usual time and cleaning its litter tray every day. The sitter spends time playing with the cat, stroking it if it likes that, or just sitting quietly nearby if the cat is shy. Some cats hide when a stranger comes, and the sitter must be patient until the cat feels brave enough to come out. Small pets need care too. Rabbits and guinea pigs need fresh hay, vegetables, and clean water. Hamsters need fresh bedding. Birds need fresh seed and clean water, and their cages need to be kept clean. Fish need to be fed carefully, because too much food can make the water dirty. A good sitter knows that every pet, big or small, deserves the same gentle care.


## Fun facts about pet sitters

- Some pet sitters look after dozens of different pets in a single week, from dogs and cats to birds and fish.
- A pet sitter always meets the pets before the job starts, so the pets already have a friend when the owner goes away.
- Dogs can get so excited when their sitter arrives that they jump up and down with joy at the door.
- Cats love their routines, so a good sitter feeds them at the same time every day, just like their owner does.
- Some pet sitters also water the plants and bring in the post, so the home looks lived in.
- Many pet sitters take photos and videos of the pets and send them to the owners, so the owners can see their pets are happy.
- Pet sitting is a job for people who truly love animals, because pets can always tell when a person is kind.

Pet sitters do a wonderful job. They give pet owners peace of mind, knowing their beloved animals are safe, fed, and loved while they are away. And for the pets, a sitter means a new friend, extra playtime, and lots of cuddles. Next time your family goes on holiday, think of the pet sitter who will be looking after your pet. They will be there with food, water, walks, and love, making sure your pet has a happy holiday of its own, right at home.
