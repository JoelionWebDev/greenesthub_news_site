Seahorses are tiny, gentle fish that look like little horses swimming in the sea. They swim upright, with their heads held high, and they curl their tails around seaweed to hold on tight. A seahorse has a horse-like head, a curled tail, and a body covered in bony rings instead of scales. Seahorses are slow swimmers, and they drift through the water in seagrass meadows and coral reefs, looking for tiny food to slurp up. They are one of the most unusual fish in the ocean, and they have a very special family secret: it is the daddy seahorse who carries the babies. Seahorses may be small, but they are full of wonder.

## What is a seahorse?

A seahorse is a fish, even though it does not look like most fish. It has a long, tube-shaped snout, a head that looks like a horse's head, and a body covered in hard, bony rings that work like armour. Instead of scales, seahorses have these tough rings, and instead of a tail fin, they have a long, curly tail that can grip onto seaweed and coral. Seahorses have a small fin on their back that flutters very fast, like a tiny propeller, to help them move. They also have small fins behind their heads that help them steer. Most seahorses are quite small, about the size of your finger, though some kinds can grow bigger. 

## Where do seahorses live?

Seahorses live in warm, shallow seas all around the world, in places like seagrass meadows, coral reefs, and mangrove forests. They like calm, sheltered water where they can hold onto seaweed or coral with their tails and not be swept away by strong waves. Seahorses live in the Atlantic Ocean, the Pacific Ocean, and the Indian Ocean, in both tropical and temperate seas. They are often found in seagrass beds, where the long green blades give them plenty of places to hide and hold on. That is why people are working hard to protect seagrass meadows and coral reefs, so seahorses and many other sea animals can keep their homes safe.

## How do seahorses swim?

Seahorses are not strong swimmers, and they move in a very special way. Most fish swim flat, with their bodies lying sideways in the water, but seahorses swim upright, standing up in the water like tiny horses. They move by fluttering the small fin on their back very fast, and they steer with the tiny fins behind their heads. Their swimming is slow and gentle, and they drift along rather than darting fast. Because they are slow, seahorses spend much of their time holding onto seaweed or coral with their curly tails, waiting for food to float past. Their tails are strong and can grip tightly, like a monkey's tail gripping a branch. Watching a seahorse swim is like watching a tiny dancer floating slowly through the sea.

## What do seahorses eat?

Seahorses eat tiny sea animals, such as baby shrimps and other small creatures called plankton. They do not have teeth, and they cannot chew their food. Instead, they use their long, tube-shaped snouts like a straw, sucking up their food with a quick slurp. A seahorse waits quietly, holding onto seaweed, and watches for a tiny shrimp to swim past. Then, in a flash, it snaps its head forward and sucks the shrimp into its mouth. Seahorses have very good eyesight, and their eyes can move on their own, looking in different directions at the same time. This helps them spot tiny food all around them. 

## The daddies carry the babies

Seahorses have one of the most special families in the whole animal world. The mummy seahorse gives her eggs to the daddy seahorse, who carries them in a special pouch on his belly, a bit like a kangaroo's pouch. The daddy keeps the eggs safe and warm inside his pouch until they are ready to hatch. When the babies are ready, the daddy seahorse pushes them out of his pouch, and tiny baby seahorses swim out into the sea. The babies are very small, but they look like perfect little seahorses, with curly tails and horse-like heads. 

## Seahorses in danger

Seahorses are gentle animals, and they face many dangers in the sea. Because they are slow swimmers, they cannot swim away quickly from danger. Their homes in seagrass meadows and coral reefs are being damaged by pollution and by people building near the sea. Seahorses are also caught by accident in fishing nets, and some are taken to be sold as ornaments or for use in traditional medicine. Many countries now have rules to protect seahorses, and people are working to save their seagrass and coral homes. You can help seahorses by learning about them, keeping beaches and seas clean, and telling others how special these tiny horses of the sea really are.

## Fun facts about seahorses

- Seahorses swim upright, standing up in the water like tiny horses.
- The daddy seahorse carries the babies in a special pouch on his belly.
- A seahorse's eyes can move on their own, looking in different directions at the same time.
- Seahorses have no teeth and suck up their food through a long, tube-shaped snout.
- Seahorses curl their tails around seaweed to hold on tight and not be swept away.

Seahorses are small, gentle, and truly magical. With their horse-like heads, curly tails, and upright swimming, they are unlike any other fish in the sea. And with daddy seahorses carrying the babies, they remind us that families come in all shapes and sizes. Next time you see a picture of a seahorse, imagine it floating in a sunny seagrass meadow, holding onto a green blade with its tail.
