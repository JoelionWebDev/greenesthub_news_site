Turtles are some of the calmest animals you will ever meet. They do not rush, they do not shout, and they never seem to be in a hurry. Yet turtles are wonderful travellers. Some walk slowly across dry land, while others swim for great distances across the sea. A turtle carries its home with it wherever it goes, tucked safely inside a hard shell. People have admired turtles for a very long time, because they teach us that slow and steady can win the day. When you watch a turtle, you learn to slow down, look carefully, and enjoy the journey.

## A home on your back

A turtle's shell is its most famous feature. The shell is hard and strong, and it is part of the turtle's body, not something it can take off like a coat. The top of the shell is called the carapace, and the flat part underneath is called the plastron. When a turtle feels scared, it can pull its head, legs and tail inside its shell for safety. This is like hiding inside a tiny house that is always with you. The shell protects the turtle from animals that might want to hurt it. It also helps keep the turtle safe from bumps and falls. A turtle's shell grows with it as it gets bigger, just as your clothes need to be replaced when you grow.

## Turtles on land and in the sea

There are many kinds of turtles, and they live in different places. Land turtles, often called tortoises, live on dry ground. They have short, strong legs and feet made for walking. They eat plants, leaves, fruits and flowers, and they love sunny spots where they can warm up. Sea turtles live in the ocean. They have flippers instead of feet, which help them swim gracefully through the water. They eat sea grass, jellyfish and other sea plants and animals. Some turtles live in rivers and ponds, spending time both in the water and on the bank. Wherever they live, turtles are gentle animals that mind their own business and keep to themselves.

## Slow, steady and very patient

Turtles are famous for moving slowly, and there is a good reason for that. Their heavy shells and short legs are not made for speed. Instead, turtles are made for patience. They walk or swim at a calm, steady pace, and they can keep going for a long time without stopping. A sea turtle can swim for many hours, and a land turtle can walk across a field bit by bit, day after day. Their slow way of life helps them save energy and stay safe. The old story of the tortoise and the hare reminds us that going slowly and never giving up can be better than rushing.

## A turtle mother's long journey

Mother sea turtles make one of the most amazing journeys in the animal world. When it is time to lay her eggs, a mother sea turtle swims back to the very beach where she was born, even if it is far away across the sea. She comes ashore at night, digs a deep hole in the sand with her flippers, and lays her eggs inside it. Then she covers the eggs with sand and returns to the sea. She does not stay to look after her babies. The warm sand keeps the eggs safe until they are ready to hatch. When the baby turtles hatch, they dig their way out of the sand and hurry down to the sea, guided by the light of the moon on the water. It is a brave first journey for such tiny animals.

## Turtles and people, past and present

People have known about turtles for thousands of years. In many stories from around the world, the turtle is a wise and patient animal. Some stories say the world rests on the back of a great turtle. Others tell of turtles that help people or carry them across rivers. Today, people love watching turtles in the wild and in aquariums. Scientists study turtles to learn about the health of the oceans and rivers, because turtles can tell us a lot about how clean the water is. Fishermen, beach walkers and children all play a part in looking after turtles, by giving them space and keeping their beaches clean.

## Why turtles need our help

Sadly, many turtles are in danger today. Sea turtles can get tangled in fishing nets, and they can mistake plastic bags in the sea for jellyfish, which is one of their favourite foods. Eating plastic can make a turtle very sick. On land, turtles lose their homes when fields and forests are built over, and roads can be dangerous for a slow animal trying to cross. Some turtles are also taken from the wild to be kept as pets, but turtles need special care, the right food and plenty of space, and they can live for a very long time. The best way to help turtles is to keep beaches and rivers clean, to never throw plastic into the sea, and to leave wild turtles alone so they can live their lives in peace.

## Lessons from a turtle

Turtles teach us some of the best lessons in life. They teach us to be patient, to keep going even when the journey is long, and to be happy with a simple life. A turtle does not need much to be content: a sunny spot, some good food, and a safe place to rest. Turtles also teach us to protect ourselves without hurting others. When danger comes, a turtle does not fight. It simply pulls into its shell and waits until it is safe to come out again. If we are patient like turtles, and if we look after the land and sea where they live, these gentle travellers will be with us for many years to come, moving slowly and steadily on their way.

## Fun facts about turtles

- A turtle's shell is part of its body and grows with it as the turtle gets bigger.
- Mother sea turtles return to the beach where they were born to lay their own eggs.
- Baby sea turtles find their way to the ocean by following the light of the moon on the water.
- Sea turtles use their flippers to swim, while land turtles have strong legs for walking.
- Turtles can pull their head, legs and tail inside their shell when they feel scared.
