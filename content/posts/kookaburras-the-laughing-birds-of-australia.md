Imagine you are walking through the bush in Australia early in the morning, and suddenly you hear a loud, happy laugh. It sounds like a group of people laughing together, but there is no one there. That sound is a kookaburra. Kookaburras are birds that laugh, and their laugh is one of the most famous sounds in Australia. It is loud, joyful, and impossible to forget.

Kookaburras are part of the kingfisher family, but they do not live by the water and catch fish the way many kingfishers do. Instead, they live in trees in forests, woodlands, and gardens across Australia. They are big, strong birds with large heads, strong beaks, and a friendly, watchful look. People in Australia love kookaburras, and many children grow up hearing their morning laugh.

## The Famous Laugh

A kookaburra's laugh is really a call, and it sounds like "koo-koo-koo-koo-koo" followed by a burst of loud, happy laughter. It often starts with one bird, and then other kookaburras join in, until the whole group is laughing together. It is a noisy, cheerful chorus that can be heard from far away.

Kookaburras often laugh at dawn and at dusk, when the day is beginning and when it is ending. Their laugh tells other kookaburras that this is their home and their patch of trees. It is a way of saying, "We live here, and we are happy here." Families of kookaburras laugh together to stay close and to keep in touch.

The laugh also helps kookaburras find each other in the trees. If one bird flies off to look for food, its laugh can guide it back to its family. For people, the laugh is a sign that the bush is waking up or settling down for the night. Many Australians say the kookaburra's laugh is the sound of the morning.

## A Big Bird with a Big Beak

Kookaburras are easy to recognise. They have brown and white feathers, a pale head with a dark stripe through the eye, and a strong, pointed beak. Their feathers are soft and mottled, which helps them blend in with the bark and leaves of the trees where they live. When a kookaburra sits still on a branch, it can be hard to spot.

The kookaburra's beak is big and strong, and it uses it to catch food. It does not have a long, thin beak like some birds. Instead, its beak is thick and powerful, perfect for grabbing small animals on the ground. A kookaburra also has strong feet and sharp claws that help it hold on to branches and catch its food.

Kookaburras are not small birds. They are stocky and strong, with a body built for sitting, watching, and swooping. They do not fly long distances. Instead, they fly in short, quick bursts from tree to tree, always watching the ground below for something to eat.

## Hunters of the Bush

Kookaburras are hunters, and they are very patient. A kookaburra will sit on a branch for a long time, watching the ground below. It looks for small animals moving in the grass or among the leaves. When it sees something, it swoops down quickly, grabs it with its beak, and flies back up to its branch.

Kookaburras eat small lizards, snakes, mice, and insects. They also eat other small animals that live on the ground. They do not hunt in water, and they do not eat fish. Their food comes from the land, and they are very good at finding it. A kookaburra's sharp eyes help it spot even a tiny movement in the grass.

After catching its food, a kookaburra often bangs it against a branch before eating it. That sounds strange, but it helps the bird make its food safe to eat. Kookaburras are clever hunters, and they have learned the best way to catch and eat the animals they find. They hunt alone or with their family, and they share what they find.

## Family Life in the Trees

Kookaburras live in family groups. A family usually has a mother, a father, and older children from earlier years who stay to help. The older children help their parents feed the new babies, guard the nest, and look after the family home. This is called a family group, and it is a lovely way for kookaburras to live.

Kookaburras nest in holes in trees. They do not build a nest out of twigs the way many birds do. Instead, they find a hollow in a tree, or they dig a hole in a soft, rotting tree trunk. The mother lays her eggs in the hollow, and both parents take turns keeping the eggs warm until they hatch.

Baby kookaburras are born without feathers, and they need a lot of care. Their parents and older brothers and sisters bring them food every day. The babies grow quickly, and soon they have soft feathers and bright eyes. When they are old enough, they learn to fly and to hunt, and they stay with the family to help raise the next babies.

## Friends of People

Kookaburras are not afraid of people, and they often live near houses and gardens. In Australia, it is common to see a kookaburra sitting on a fence, a roof, or a washing line, watching the world go by. People enjoy seeing them, and many leave out a little food or a dish of water for them on hot days.

It is important to remember that kookaburras are wild birds. They find their own food in nature, and they do best when they hunt for themselves. People should watch them from a distance and enjoy their laugh without trying to touch them or feed them too much. A wild kookaburra that hunts for itself is a healthy, happy bird.

Kookaburras also help people by eating snakes, mice, and insects that can be a problem in gardens and farms. They are useful neighbours, and they keep the bush in balance. Farmers and gardeners are often glad to have a kookaburra family living nearby.

If you ever visit Australia, listen in the morning for a loud, happy laugh in the trees. That is the kookaburra saying good morning. It is a bird that laughs, hunts, and loves its family, and its laugh is one of the happiest sounds in the whole world.
