Every morning, when you pour milk on your cereal or drink a cool glass of milk, have you ever stopped to wonder where it came from? That milk started its journey on a farm, with a cow and a very special person: the dairy farmer. A dairy farmer is a friend to cows, and their job is to look after the cows and bring fresh milk to your home.

Dairy farming is one of the oldest jobs in the world. People have kept cows for milk for thousands of years. Long ago, farmers milked their cows by hand, sitting on a small stool beside the cow. Today, farmers still know every cow by name, but they also use clever machines to help. What has not changed is the most important part of the job: caring for the cows, every single day.

## Meet the Cows

Dairy cows are gentle, calm animals. They come in many colours, black and white, brown, or a mix of both. Some have big, soft eyes and long eyelashes. Cows are very clever too. They can remember places and people, and they know the farmer's voice. They even have best friends in the herd and like to spend time together, just like you and your friends at school.

A herd is a group of cows that live together on a farm. The farmer knows each cow in the herd. Some cows are shy, some are curious, and some love to be petted. Farmers give their cows names and learn their habits. They know which cow likes to stand in the shade, which one is always first to breakfast, and which one loves a good scratch behind the ears.

Cows spend much of their day eating grass and hay. They chew their food slowly, and they chew it twice! First they swallow the grass, then they bring it back up and chew it again. This is called chewing the cud, and it helps the cow get all the goodness out of its food. Cows also need plenty of fresh water to drink, and a comfortable place to rest.

## A Day on a Dairy Farm

A dairy farmer's day starts very early, often before the sun comes up. The farmer wakes up, puts on boots, and walks out to the barn where the cows are waiting. The cows are milked in the morning, and then again later in the day. Milking is when the farmer gently collects milk from the cow.

On many farms today, cows are milked with a special machine. The machine is soft and gentle, and it collects the milk into a clean container. The cows walk into the milking area calmly, because they know the routine. They have done it many times before, and the farmer is always patient and kind with them.

After milking, the farmer cleans everything carefully. Cleanliness is very important on a dairy farm, because milk must be fresh and safe to drink. The milk is cooled quickly to keep it fresh, and it is stored in a big, clean tank.

But milking is only part of the day. The farmer also feeds the cows, gives them fresh water, cleans their beds, and checks that every cow is healthy and happy. If a cow looks unwell, the farmer calls the vet, the animal doctor, who comes to help. Farmers watch their cows closely, because they know that a healthy cow is a happy cow.

## What Do Cows Eat?

Cows are hungry animals, and they eat a lot every day! Their main food is grass, which they eat out in the fields when the weather is good. In the colder months, or when there is no fresh grass, farmers give them hay, which is dried grass, and silage, which is grass that has been stored in a special way to keep it fresh.

Farmers also give cows special food made from grains and other plants to make sure they get all the vitamins they need. It is a bit like how you eat different foods to grow strong and healthy. The farmer works hard to give the cows the best food, because good food helps the cows stay healthy and make good milk.

Cows also love to be groomed. Farmers brush their coats to keep them clean and shiny, and cows enjoy it very much. A cow being brushed will often stand very still, close its eyes, and look very happy indeed.

## How Milk Gets to Your Home

Milk's journey is a wonderful one. It starts on the farm, in the morning milking. Then the milk truck collects it and takes it to a dairy, which is a place where milk is made safe and ready to drink. At the dairy, the milk is checked, cooled, and packed into bottles or cartons. Then it is sent to shops, where you or your family can buy it.

But milk does not just become the milk you drink. It is also used to make lots of other delicious foods. Cheese, butter, yoghurt, and ice cream all start with milk from a dairy farm. So when you eat a cheesy sandwich, spread butter on toast, or enjoy a scoop of ice cream, you can thank a dairy farmer and their cows.

## Caring for the Cows

The most important part of a dairy farmer's job is kindness. Cows are living animals with feelings, and they do their best when they feel safe and loved. Farmers speak to their cows in calm voices, move slowly around them, and never frighten them. They give them clean, dry beds of straw to sleep on, shady places to rest on hot days, and warm shelter when it rains.

Farmers also make sure their cows have room to move around and be with their friends. Cows that can walk in fields, lie in the sunshine, and spend time together are happier and healthier. Some farms have special brushes that cows can rub against whenever they like, which is a bit like having a back scratch whenever you want one!

So the next time you drink a glass of milk, think of the dairy farmer and the cows. Think of the early morning in the barn, the gentle cows in the field, the milk truck on its journey, and all the care that goes into every single drop. Milk does not just come from a shop. It comes from a farm, from a cow, and from a farmer who cares.
