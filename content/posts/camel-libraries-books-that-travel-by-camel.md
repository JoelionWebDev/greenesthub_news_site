In the dry lands of Kenya, books travel to children by camel. The camel library brings stories to villages where there are no roads or libraries. It sounds like something from a storybook, but it is real. In the far north of Kenya, where the land is dry and the villages are far apart, camels carry boxes of books to children who are hungry to read.

## A Library on Four Legs

In the dry, dusty lands of northern Kenya, many children live in small villages that are far from towns and cities. There are no libraries, no bookshops, and often no roads for cars or buses. The children go to school, but they have very few books to read. For a long time, this was a big problem.

Then, kind people had a wonderful idea. They decided to use camels to carry books to these villages. Camels are perfect for the job, because they are strong, they can walk for a long time without water, and they can travel over rough, sandy ground where cars cannot go. A camel can carry heavy boxes of books on its back, walking slowly and steadily across the desert.

The camel library works like this: a team of camels, each carrying boxes of books, sets off from a town and walks to the villages. When they arrive, the children gather round, excited to see what new books the camels have brought. The librarian opens the boxes, and the children choose books to read. It is like a library that comes to you, instead of you going to the library.

## The Camels and Their Books

Each camel in the library carries about two boxes of books, one on each side of its body. The boxes are filled with storybooks, picture books, and books about animals, science and the world. The camels walk in a line, led by their handlers, who know the way to each village.

The camels are well looked after. They are given water and food, they rest in the shade during the hottest part of the day, and they are treated with kindness by their handlers. The camels have become famous in their villages, and children look forward to their visits.

When the camel library arrives, it is a happy day. The children sit under a tree, reading their books, sharing stories with each other, and enjoying the pictures. Some children walk for a long way to meet the camels, because they love books so much.

## Why Books Matter

Books are very important for children. They help children learn to read, they teach them about the world, and they spark their imaginations. A child who reads books can dream big dreams, learn new things, and grow up to do wonderful things.

In places where there are no libraries, children can miss out on all of this. That is why the camel library is so special. It brings books to children who would otherwise have none, and it gives them the chance to learn and to dream.

The camel library also helps adults. Grown-ups in the villages borrow books too, reading stories and learning new skills. The camels bring books for everyone, young and old.

## Other Animal Libraries

The camel library is not the only animal library in the world. In some places, donkeys carry books to villages in the mountains. In other places, elephants have carried books to children in remote areas. Animals have been helping people share books for many years, in all sorts of clever ways.

In Colombia, a country in South America, a man named Luis Soriano travels with his two donkeys, carrying books to children in villages that have no libraries. The children call him the "donkey librarian", and they love his visits. Like the camel library, his donkey library brings the joy of reading to children who need it most.

These animal libraries show us that where there is a will, there is a way. If there are no roads, use a camel. If there are no cars, use a donkey. The important thing is that children get their books.

## The Camels' Journey

The camel library travels many kilometres each week, visiting different villages on different days. The camels walk slowly, but they are steady, and they always arrive. The librarians keep a record of which books have been borrowed, and they bring new books on the next visit, so the children always have something fresh to read.

The children love the camels, and they often give them treats, such as leaves and grass. The camels have become part of the community, and everyone knows their names. They are not just animals carrying books — they are friends who bring stories and joy.

## Books, Camels and Dreams

The camel library is a beautiful reminder that animals can help people in the most surprising ways. Camels are famous for crossing deserts and carrying heavy loads, but who would have thought they would also carry books to children? It is a wonderful example of how clever and kind people can find a way to solve a problem, with a little help from their animal friends.

So next time you open a book, think of the children in northern Kenya, sitting under a tree, reading a story that arrived on the back of a camel. And remember that a book, like a camel, can take you on a journey to amazing places, even if you never leave your village.
