Imagine a lizard that can open a big frill around its neck, like an umbrella or a fancy collar. When it opens the frill, it suddenly looks much bigger and scarier than it really is. That lizard is the frilled lizard, one of the most amazing reptiles in Australia and New Guinea. It is clever, fast, and full of surprises.

Frilled lizards spend most of their time in trees, hiding among the branches and leaves. They are hard to spot when their frill is closed, because their colours blend in with the bark. But when they feel threatened, they open their frill wide, hiss, and show the world that they are not to be messed with. It is a clever trick that helps them stay safe without fighting.

## What Does a Frilled Lizard Look Like?

A frilled lizard is a medium-sized lizard with a long tail, strong legs, and a special frill of skin around its neck. When the frill is closed, it lies flat against the body, like a folded cape. When the lizard opens it, the frill spreads out in a big circle around its head, and it looks like a lion's mane or a beautiful fan.

The frill can be many colours, including yellow, orange, red, and brown. The colours help the lizard blend in with the trees, but they also make it look bright and bold when the frill is open. Inside the frill there are patterns that make it look even more striking. No two frilled lizards have exactly the same colours, so each one is a little bit different.

Frilled lizards have rough, scaly skin that helps protect them. Their bodies are usually grey or brown, with patterns that look like tree bark. Their tails are very long, often longer than their bodies, and they help the lizard balance when it climbs trees or runs on the ground. Their claws are sharp, and they use them to grip bark and climb high into the branches.

## A Clever Trick to Stay Safe

Frilled lizards are not strong fighters, so they use a clever trick to stay safe. When a hunter comes too close, the frilled lizard opens its frill wide, opens its mouth, and hisses loudly. Suddenly, the small lizard looks much bigger, and the hunter may be scared away. The bright colours and the big shape make the lizard look dangerous, even though it is really just trying to protect itself.

If the trick does not work, the frilled lizard has another plan: it runs away very fast. Frilled lizards can run on their back legs, with their front legs held up in the air and their long tails held out behind them for balance. It looks funny, like a tiny dinosaur running across the ground, but it is very fast. They run to the nearest tree and climb up high, where they are safe from danger.

## What Do Frilled Lizards Eat?

Frilled lizards eat insects and other small creatures. They love to eat ants, spiders, cicadas, and other bugs they find in trees and on the ground. They sit quietly and wait for food to come close, then they dart out quickly and catch it with their mouths. Their tongues are sticky, which helps them catch insects.

Frilled lizards need water too. They drink rainwater from leaves and from small pools on the ground. In the hot Australian sun, they rest in the shade of trees during the hottest part of the day, and they come out to hunt when it is cooler in the morning and afternoon.

## Where Do Frilled Lizards Live?

Frilled lizards live in northern Australia and in parts of New Guinea. They like warm places with lots of trees, especially forests and woodlands where they can climb and hide. They are most active in the warm months, when there are many insects to eat.

During the day, frilled lizards bask in the sun to warm up their bodies. Like all reptiles, they need the sun's heat to move and hunt well. They sit on branches or rocks in the morning sun, warming up before they start their day. When they are warm, they become active and alert.

## Baby Frilled Lizards

Female frilled lizards lay eggs in the ground. They dig a small hole in the soft earth, lay their eggs inside, and cover them up with soil. The eggs stay warm in the ground, and after some weeks, tiny baby lizards hatch and dig their way out. The babies are small, but they already have tiny frills, and they can run and climb soon after they hatch.

Baby frilled lizards must learn to find food and stay safe on their own. Their mothers do not stay to look after them, so the babies must be clever from the start. They hide in trees, eat small insects, and use their frills to scare away danger, just like adult lizards do. Many baby lizards do not survive, but those that do grow up to be strong and fast.

## Why Frilled Lizards Are Special

Frilled lizards are special because they show us how clever animals can be. They do not have sharp teeth or strong claws to fight, but they have a smart trick that keeps them safe. Their frill is like a costume that makes them look big and scary, and their speed helps them escape when they need to.

They also remind us that lizards are not scary animals. They are gentle creatures that help keep insect numbers in balance, and they are an important part of the forest. By eating insects, they help plants and trees stay healthy, and they are food for other animals too.

The next time you see a picture of a frilled lizard, look at its frill and imagine it opening wide in the sunshine. It is a lizard with a big secret, a clever trick, and a very fast run. The frilled lizard proves that even a small animal can be amazing when it uses its brains and its special gifts to stay safe in the wild.
