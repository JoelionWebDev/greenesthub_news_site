Have you ever seen an animal jump straight up into the air, with all four legs stiff and its back arched, like it is bouncing on a trampoline? That amazing jump is called pronking, and the animal that does it best is the springbok. Springbok live on the grasslands of southern Africa, and they are famous for their high jumps and their speed.

Springbok are small antelopes, but they are full of energy. They live in big groups, run very fast, and leap high into the air when they are excited or when they sense danger. Watching a springbok jump is like watching a dancer in the wild. It is one of the most joyful sights in nature, and it has made the springbok one of Africa's best-loved animals.

## What Does a Springbok Look Like?

A springbok is a pretty antelope with a light brown coat on its back and a white belly. It has a dark brown stripe along each side of its body, which helps you tell it apart from other antelopes. Its face is white with a dark stripe running from each eye down to its mouth, which gives it a very friendly look.

Springbok have long, thin legs that are perfect for running and jumping. Their hooves are small and neat, and they help the springbok move quickly over grass and dry ground. Both male and female springbok have horns. The horns are short and curved, and they point back from the head. Male springbok usually have thicker horns than females.

On the back of a springbok there is a special patch of skin that can open up. When the springbok jumps, this patch opens and shows a fan of white hair. Scientists think this white flash helps warn other springbok of danger, or shows how strong and healthy the jumping springbok is. It is like a secret signal that only springbok understand.

## The Amazing Jump

The most famous thing about springbok is their jump, called pronking. When a springbok pronks, it jumps high into the air with all four feet off the ground at the same time. Its legs stay stiff and straight, its back arches, and its head goes down. It can jump again and again, bouncing across the grassland like a ball.

Why do springbok jump like this? There are a few reasons. Sometimes they jump when they are happy and full of energy, just for fun. Sometimes they jump to show other springbok that they are strong and healthy. And sometimes they jump when they spot danger, to warn the rest of the herd and to confuse a hunter. A jumping springbok is hard to catch, because it moves in a surprising way.

Young springbok love to jump and play. They practise pronking when they are small, and they get better as they grow. Watching young springbok play is a delight, because they bounce and leap with so much joy. Even adult springbok will pronk when they feel safe and happy, and the whole herd may join in together.

## Life in the Herd

Springbok live in large herds on the open grasslands. A herd can have many springbok, sometimes dozens, sometimes hundreds. Living in a big group helps keep them safe. With so many eyes watching, it is hard for a hunter to sneak up without being seen. If one springbok spots danger, it gives a warning, and the whole herd runs away together.

Springbok are very fast runners. They can run quickly across the flat grassland, turning and twisting to escape danger. Their speed and their jumping help them stay safe from lions, leopards, and other hunters. They are always alert, with their ears moving to listen for sounds and their eyes watching the grassland.

In a herd, springbok move together to find food and water. They graze on grasses and eat leaves from bushes when grass is hard to find. They can go for a long time without drinking water, because they get water from the plants they eat. When they do find water, they drink together, and then they move on to find more food.

## Food and the Grasslands

Springbok eat grasses and leaves, and they are very good at finding food in dry places. In the rainy season, when the grass is green and fresh, they eat a lot and grow strong. In the dry season, when the grass is thin, they look for leaves and other plants that still have food and water in them.

## Baby Springbok and Growing Up

A baby springbok is called a lamb. When a lamb is born, it is small and a little wobbly, but it soon learns to stand and walk. Mother springbok hide their lambs in the grass for the first few days, keeping them safe while they go out to eat. The mother comes back often to feed her lamb with milk.

After a short time, the lamb joins the herd and starts to learn how to live with other springbok. It learns to graze, to run, and to jump. Lambs play together a lot, chasing each other and practising their jumps. Play is very important, because it helps them grow strong and learn the skills they will need as adults.

## Why Springbok Are Loved

Springbok are loved by many people in Africa and around the world. They are a symbol of speed, joy, and freedom. In South Africa, the springbok is a national symbol, and it appears on sports teams and on coins. People love to watch them jump and run, and they remind us of the beauty of the wild.

Springbok also help the grassland stay healthy. By eating grass and moving around, they help new grass grow and keep the land in balance. They are food for other animals too, and they are part of a big web of life on the African plains.

The next time you see a picture of a springbok, look closely at its leap. Imagine the wind in its face and the grass below its feet. The springbok is a small antelope with a big jump, and it shows us that sometimes the best way to face the world is to leap high, run fast, and enjoy the open grassland with your friends.
