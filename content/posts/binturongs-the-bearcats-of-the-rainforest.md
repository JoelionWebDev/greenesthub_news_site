What animal has the face of a cat, the body of a small bear, a tail that can grab branches like a hand, and smells like a bag of freshly popped popcorn? It sounds like a made-up creature from a storybook, but it is real. Meet the binturong, also called the bearcat — one of the strangest and most wonderful animals in the rainforest.

Binturongs live in the warm rainforests of Southeast Asia, high up in the trees. They are shy and quiet animals, and they mostly come out at night, so people do not see them very often. But once you learn about them, you will never forget them. Let us climb up into the trees and meet these gentle, shaggy climbers.

## What Is a Binturong?

The binturong is a mammal, which means it is warm-blooded and feeds its babies with milk. It is about the size of a small dog, with a long, shaggy coat of dark brown or black fur. Its fur is coarse and thick, with pale tips that make it look a little frosty. It has a round face, small ears with white edges, and long white whiskers that help it feel its way around in the dark.

Binturongs have strong claws that help them grip tree bark, and they are excellent climbers. They move slowly and carefully from branch to branch, using all four legs. They are not in a hurry. In the rainforest, the binturong takes its time, enjoying the fruit and the fresh air high above the forest floor.

## Why Do They Smell Like Popcorn?

Here is the binturong's most famous and funniest feature: it smells like popcorn! If you ever stood near a binturong, you might think someone was making popcorn nearby. This smell comes from a scent the binturong makes in its body, and it is strongest in its pee. Scientists have found that the chemical that gives popcorn its smell is the same one found in binturong scent. Is that not amazing?

The smell is not strong or nasty. People who work with binturongs in zoos say it is a warm, buttery smell, just like popcorn at the cinema. It is one of the most surprising smells in the animal kingdom, and it makes the binturong famous.

## Life in the Trees

Binturongs are made for life in the trees. Their most amazing tool is their tail. A binturong's tail is long and strong, and it can grip branches like a hand. This is called a prehensile tail, which means it can hold on to things. The binturong uses its tail like a fifth limb, wrapping it around branches to steady itself as it climbs.

Very few animals have a tail like this. Monkeys have them, and so do some other tree-climbing animals, but the binturong is one of the few animals in Asia with a prehensile tail. It is so strong that the binturong can hang from a branch by its tail alone while it reaches for fruit with its front paws.

## What Do Binturongs Eat?

Binturongs love fruit. Their favourite food is the fig, a soft, sweet fruit that grows on trees in the rainforest. They eat figs by the dozen, and they are very important to the forest because of it. When a binturong eats a fig, the seeds pass through its body and come out in its droppings, far away from the parent tree. The seeds then grow into new fig trees. In this way, binturongs help to plant the rainforest.

Binturongs also eat other fruits, leaves, and small animals like insects, birds' eggs, and small creatures they find in the trees. They are not fussy eaters, and they will try many kinds of food. But fruit is their favourite, and they will travel through the trees to find a fig tree full of ripe fruit.

## Babies and Family

Baby binturongs are born in a nest made of leaves, high up in a tree or in a hollow in a tree trunk. A mother usually has one or two babies at a time. The babies are tiny and helpless when they are born, with their eyes closed. They stay close to their mother, drinking her milk and growing strong.

Binturong fathers are unusual among their animal family, because they help to look after the babies too. The father stays near the mother and the babies, and he may bring food and guard the nest. This is rare and sweet — a family that works together.

As the babies grow, they learn to climb. They practise on low branches, holding on with their claws and their tails. Their mother watches over them and teaches them which fruits are good to eat. Young binturongs stay with their mother for many months, learning the ways of the trees before they go off on their own.

## Fun Facts About Binturongs

- Binturongs smell like popcorn — the same chemical gives popcorn its smell!
- They have a prehensile tail that can grip branches like a hand.
- Binturongs are also called bearcats, but they are neither bears nor cats.
- They are excellent climbers and spend most of their lives in the trees.
- They help plant the rainforest by spreading fig seeds in their droppings.
- Binturong fathers help look after the babies, which is unusual.
- They are mostly active at night and sleep curled up in the trees during the day.

## Rainforest Homes Need Protection

Sadly, binturongs are in trouble. Their rainforest homes are being cut down for farms and buildings, and there are fewer trees for them to live in. They are also hunted in some places, and some are caught to be kept as pets, which is not good for a wild animal. Because of this, there are fewer binturongs in the wild than there used to be.

But there is hope. Many people are working to protect the rainforests of Southeast Asia. National parks keep the trees safe, and wildlife rangers look after the animals. Zoos around the world keep binturongs and teach visitors about them, and scientists study them to learn how to help.

You can help by learning about binturongs and telling others about them. When people know about these gentle, popcorn-smelling climbers, they care more about saving their forest homes. The binturong shows us that the rainforest is full of surprises — and that even a slow, shaggy animal high in the trees plays a big part in keeping the forest alive.
