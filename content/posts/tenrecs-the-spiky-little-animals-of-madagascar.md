Far away in the Indian Ocean lies a big island called Madagascar. It is home to animals that live nowhere else on Earth, and one of the strangest and cutest is the tenrec. Tenrecs are small, spiky, busy little animals that dig, hunt, and hide in the forests of Madagascar. Some look like hedgehogs, some look like shrews, and some even look like tiny otters. Let us meet the marvellous tenrec.

## What Is a Tenrec?

A tenrec is a small mammal that lives only on Madagascar and on a few small islands nearby. There are many different kinds of tenrecs — more than thirty! Some are as small as a mouse, while others are as big as a rabbit. They come in many shapes and sizes, but they all belong to the same family.

The most famous tenrec is the lesser hedgehog tenrec. It looks a lot like a hedgehog, with a round body covered in short, sharp spines. But it is not a hedgehog at all. Tenrecs and hedgehogs are not close cousins. They just look alike because they both use spines to stay safe. Scientists call this a wonderful example of nature finding the same clever answer in two different places.

Other tenrecs look very different. The common tenrec is bigger and has a long, pointed snout. The web-footed tenrec has feet made for swimming, and the streaked tenrecs have bold yellow and black stripes and a crown of spines on their heads. Every kind of tenrec has its own special way of living.

## Life in Madagascar

Madagascar is a very special island. It broke away from Africa a very long time ago, and the animals there grew and changed on their own, without animals from other places mixing in. That is why so many animals in Madagascar are found nowhere else in the world. The tenrec is one of them.

Tenrecs live in many different parts of Madagascar. Some live in the wet rainforests of the east, where it rains a lot and the trees are tall and green. Others live in the dry forests of the west, where the trees lose their leaves in the dry season. A few tenrecs even live near rivers and streams, where they can swim and catch food in the water.

No matter where they live, tenrecs are good at hiding. They make their homes in burrows under the ground, in hollow logs, or in piles of leaves. During the day, they rest in their hiding places. At night, they come out to hunt and explore.

## Digging, Sniffing and Hunting

Tenrecs are excellent diggers. They have strong legs and sharp claws that help them dig into the soil and leaf litter on the forest floor. They dig to find food, and they dig to make burrows where they can sleep safely. Some tenrecs can dig very fast, disappearing into the ground in the blink of an eye.

A tenrec's nose is its best hunting tool. Tenrecs have long, pointed snouts that they use to sniff out food. They can smell insects, worms, and other small creatures hiding under leaves and in the soil. Once they find something tasty, they grab it with their mouths and crunch it up. Tenrecs eat many kinds of small animals, including insects, earthworms, grubs, and snails. Some bigger tenrecs also eat small frogs and fruit.

Tenrecs hunt mostly at night. Their eyes are small and not very good at seeing in the dark, but their noses and whiskers are excellent. They shuffle through the leaf litter, sniffing and snuffling, listening for the tiny sounds of insects moving. It is a quiet, busy life, and tenrecs are very good at it.

## Spines and Other Clever Tricks

Many tenrecs are covered in spines, and those spines are their main way of staying safe. When a tenrec feels scared, it can roll into a ball or raise its spines so that they stick out in all directions. A hungry animal that tries to bite a spiky tenrec will get a sore mouth and think twice about trying again.

But spines are not the only trick tenrecs have. The streaked tenrecs have a very special talent. They have special spines on their backs that they rub together to make a high, squeaky sound. It is a bit like rubbing two sticks together, but with spines! Tenrecs use this sound to talk to each other, especially mothers and babies. It is one of the only animals in the world that makes sound this way.

## Baby Tenrecs

Baby tenrecs are born in a safe burrow or nest. A mother tenrec can have many babies at a time — sometimes a whole litter of tiny, pink tenrecs. The babies are born with their eyes closed and their spines soft. Their mother feeds them with milk and keeps them warm.

As the babies grow, their spines get harder and their eyes open. They start to explore the burrow and play with their brothers and sisters. Their mother teaches them how to sniff out food and how to dig. After some weeks, the young tenrecs are ready to leave the nest and find their own homes in the forest.

Tenrecs are in danger in some parts of Madagascar because their forest homes are being cut down. When forests are cleared for farms and towns, tenrecs lose the places where they live, dig, and hunt. Many people are working hard to protect Madagascar's forests and the animals that live in them. They save patches of forest, plant new trees, and teach others why animals like the tenrec matter. When the forests are safe, tenrecs can keep digging, sniffing, and squeaking in the dark.

## Fun Facts About Tenrecs

Here are some fun things to remember about tenrecs. There are more than thirty different kinds of tenrecs, and they all live only in Madagascar. Some tenrecs look like hedgehogs, but they are not related to hedgehogs at all. Streaked tenrecs can make sounds by rubbing their spines together. Some tenrecs can sleep for months during the dry season. And the web-footed tenrec has feet made for swimming, just like a tiny otter.

The tenrec shows us that even the smallest animals can be full of wonders. In the forests of Madagascar, under the leaves and in the burrows, the spiky little tenrec is busy living its secret, marvellous life.
