Imagine a dog that looks like a tiny Lassie, with a long, fluffy coat, a sweet face, and a very clever brain. That is the Shetland Sheepdog, often called a Sheltie for short! These little dogs come from the Shetland Islands, a group of islands off the north coast of Scotland, where they were bred to herd sheep.

Shelties are small, smart, and full of energy. They love to learn, they love to play, and they love to be with their families. Let us meet this clever little herding dog.

## What Do They Look Like?

Shetland Sheepdogs are small to medium-sized dogs with long, thick, fluffy coats. Their fur comes in many lovely colours — some are brown and white, some are black and white, some are a soft blue-grey, and some are a warm golden colour. Their coats are so thick and beautiful that they look like they are wearing a fluffy winter jacket all year round!

They have a long, narrow face with a gentle, sweet expression, and their ears tip forward at the top, which gives them a very alert, interested look. Their eyes are dark and bright, and they seem to be always watching and thinking. They have a long, feathery tail that they carry low, and their chest is covered in a thick ruff of fur, like a fluffy scarf.

Shelties are graceful, dainty dogs, and they move with a light, quick step. They are smaller than a collie, which they look a little like, but they have all the same cleverness and beauty in a smaller, cuddlier package.

## Where Do They Come From?

Shetland Sheepdogs come from the Shetland Islands, which lie to the north of Scotland. These islands are windy, cold, and rugged, with rocky hills and open moorland. Long ago, farmers on these islands needed small, hardy dogs to help them look after their sheep, and so the Sheltie was born.

The sheep on the Shetland Islands are small, and the farms are small too, so the farmers needed a small dog that was clever, quick, and gentle with the sheep. The Sheltie was perfect for the job. It could run across the hills, round up the sheep, and bring them safely home.

Today, Shelties live as beloved pets in homes all over the world. They are happy in houses with gardens, and they love to have space to run and play. They are also very popular in dog sports, where their cleverness and speed make them stars!

## What Do They Eat?

Like all dogs, Shetland Sheepdogs eat dog food, which is specially made to give them all the goodness they need. They eat both dry food, which comes as crunchy biscuits, and wet food, which comes in tins. They also enjoy healthy treats, like small pieces of carrot or apple, and special dog biscuits.

Shelties should always have fresh, clean water to drink. They are active dogs, and they need good food to keep up their energy. But they should not eat too much, because they can put on weight if they are given too many treats! A healthy Sheltie is a slim, fit dog that can run and play all day.

It is important never to give a dog chocolate, grapes, onions, or other foods that are bad for dogs. A Sheltie's owner will know exactly what foods are safe and healthy for their clever little friend.

## Clever Herding Dogs

Shelties are herding dogs, which means they were bred to help farmers look after sheep. They are very clever, and they can learn new things quickly. They are famous for being one of the smartest kinds of dogs in the world!

When a Sheltie herds sheep, it runs around the flock, gently guiding the sheep in the right direction. It uses its eyes, its body, and its bark to move the sheep along. Shelties are gentle herders — they do not chase or scare the sheep, but guide them calmly and cleverly.

Even today, when most Shelties are family pets rather than working farm dogs, they still have their herding instincts. You might see a Sheltie trying to herd children, other pets, or even its toys around the house! It is all part of their clever, busy nature. They love to have a job to do, and they are happiest when they are learning new tricks or playing games.

## A Loyal Family Friend

Shelties are wonderful family dogs. They are gentle, loyal, and very loving with their families. They often choose one favourite person, but they love the whole family too. They are happiest when they are with their people, and they do not like to be left alone for a long time.

They are gentle with children, and they love to play games like fetch and hide-and-seek. They are also very good at learning tricks, and they love to show off what they have learned. Many Shelties take part in dog agility, which is a sport where dogs run through tunnels, jump over hurdles, and weave through poles. Shelties are brilliant at agility because they are fast, clever, and love to learn!

Shelties can be a little shy with strangers, and they may bark to let their family know when someone new has arrived. They are good little watchdogs, and they take their job of looking after their family very seriously. But once they get to know someone, they are friendly and sweet.
