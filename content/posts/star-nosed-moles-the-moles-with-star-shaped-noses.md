Imagine an animal with a pink star on the end of its nose. Not a picture of a star, not a sticker — a real, living star made of tiny, wiggly feelers. That animal is the star-nosed mole, and it is one of the strangest and most wonderful creatures that lives underground. It cannot see very well, but it does not need to. Its star nose helps it "see" the world by touch, and it is so fast that it can find and eat its food in less time than it takes you to blink.

Star-nosed moles live in North America, in wet, muddy places near ponds, streams, and marshes. They spend most of their lives underground, digging tunnels and hunting for food in the dark. They are small, furry, and very busy, and their star-shaped nose makes them one of the most famous moles in the world.

## A Star You Can Touch With

The star on the mole's nose is made of twenty-two tiny pink feelers, arranged in a circle like the points of a star. These feelers are not just for show. They are packed with thousands of tiny touch sensors, more than any other animal's nose. When the mole touches something with its star, it can tell in an instant what it is — a worm, a bug, a tiny fish, or just a stone.

The mole moves its star feelers very, very quickly, touching things many times every second. It is like reading a book with your fingers, except the mole is reading the whole world around it. The feelers are so sensitive that the mole can tell the difference between food and non-food almost instantly, faster than any other mammal on Earth.

Scientists have studied the star-nosed mole's brain and found that a huge part of it is devoted to understanding signals from the star. It is a bit like how a big part of your brain is devoted to understanding what your eyes see. For the mole, the star is its eyes, its hands, and its map of the world, all rolled into one.

## The Fastest Eater in the World

The star-nosed mole is famous for being the fastest eater of any mammal. When it finds a small worm or insect, it can decide whether it is food and gobble it up in a fraction of a second. That is faster than you can blink your eyes! This amazing speed helps the mole because it needs to eat a lot. It is always hungry, and it spends much of its day searching for food.

What does it eat? Mostly small worms, insects, tiny shellfish, and little water creatures. It hunts both underground and in the water. Star-nosed moles are very good swimmers, which is unusual for a mole. They can dive into ponds and streams and use their star noses to feel for food underwater. They can even smell underwater, which sounds impossible, but they do it by blowing tiny bubbles out of their noses and then sniffing the bubbles back in. Those bubbles catch the smell of food in the water and bring it back to the mole's nose. Clever, right?

## A Champion Digger

Like all moles, the star-nosed mole is a champion digger. It has strong front feet with big claws, perfect for scooping away soil. Its body is long and round, covered in soft, dark fur that can bend in any direction, so it can move forwards and backwards in its tunnels without getting stuck. Its fur does not get muddy easily, which is handy when you spend your whole life underground.

The mole digs long tunnels under the ground, with special rooms for sleeping, eating, and raising babies. Some of its tunnels run just under the surface, and you can sometimes see the raised lines they make in soft, wet soil. Other tunnels go deeper, where the mole is safe from foxes, owls, and other animals that might want to catch it.

Star-nosed moles do not hibernate in winter. They stay active all year round, digging and hunting even under the snow. Their tunnels keep them warm and safe, and they can find food even when the ground above is frozen. They are tough little animals, built for a life that would be very hard for most creatures.

## Life in the Dark

Star-nosed moles have very small eyes, and they cannot see well at all. But they do not need good eyesight, because they live in darkness most of the time. Down in their tunnels, there is no light to see by anyway. Instead of eyes, they use their star nose, their sense of touch, and their sense of smell to find their way around.

They are mostly active during the day and night, taking short naps between hunting trips. They are not very social animals. Each mole has its own tunnels, and it likes to live alone, except when a mother is raising her babies.

Even though they live underground, star-nosed moles are important for the world above. Their digging mixes the soil and helps air and water reach plant roots. They eat insects and worms, which helps keep the soil healthy. They are small gardeners, working away in the dark, helping the land stay strong.

## Why the Star-Nosed Mole Is So Amazing

The star-nosed mole is amazing because it shows us that there are many ways to explore the world. We use our eyes, but the mole uses its star nose. It touches the world to understand it, and it does it faster than any other mammal can. Its star is one of the most sensitive touch organs in the whole animal kingdom.

It also shows us that even animals that live in the dark, underground, and out of sight can be truly wonderful. The star-nosed mole may not be as famous as a lion or an elephant, but it has a superpower that no other animal has: a star on its nose that lets it feel the world in a flash.

So the next time you walk past a muddy pond or a wet meadow, think about the little mole digging below your feet. Somewhere down there, in the dark tunnels, a tiny animal with a pink star on its nose is busy touching, tasting, and exploring its world — faster than you can blink, and happier than you can imagine.
