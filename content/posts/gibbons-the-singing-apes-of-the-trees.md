High in the rainforests of Southeast Asia, just as the sun rises, a beautiful sound floats through the trees. It is a long, clear song that rises and falls like music. That song comes from a gibbon. Gibbons are small apes with long arms, and they are the best singers in the whole animal kingdom. Their morning songs can be heard from far away, echoing through the forest like a choir.

Gibbons spend almost their whole lives in the trees. They rarely come down to the ground, because the treetops are their home, their playground, and their dining room all in one. With their long arms and quick hands, they swing from branch to branch faster than you can blink. Watching a gibbon move through the forest is like watching a dancer fly through the air.

## The Swinging Champions

Gibbons move in a special way called swinging, or brachiation. They grab a branch with one hand, swing their body forward, and grab the next branch with the other hand. They do this again and again, moving through the trees in a smooth, graceful rhythm. Their arms are very long, much longer than their legs, and that helps them reach far branches with ease.

A gibbon can leap through the air from one tree to another, catching a branch with its hands and landing safely. It can also run along the top of a branch, balancing with its arms held up high. Gibbons are so good at moving through trees that they almost never fall. Their hands are like hooks, and their fingers curl around branches with a strong grip.

Gibbons are small apes, much smaller than many of their ape cousins. They have soft fur that can be brown, black, grey, or golden, depending on the kind of gibbon. Some have white patches on their faces or hands, which makes them easy to recognise.

## Songs in the Morning

The most wonderful thing about gibbons is their singing. Every morning, gibbons sing loud, beautiful songs to tell other gibbons where they live. A pair of gibbons often sings together, and their voices blend in a lovely duet. The song starts softly, then grows louder and more exciting, with long notes that rise and fall.

Each gibbon family has its own song, a little different from every other family's song. The song tells neighbours, "This is our home, and we love it here." It helps gibbon families keep their own patch of forest and avoid arguments with other families. Singing is much nicer than fighting, and gibbons are very good at it.

Young gibbons learn to sing by listening to their parents. At first, their songs are simple and wobbly, but with practice they grow strong and clear. Some gibbons sing for a long time each morning, filling the forest with music. People who visit the rainforest say the gibbon song is one of the most beautiful sounds in nature.

## Family Life in the Treetops

Gibbons live in small family groups, usually a mother, a father, and their young ones. The parents stay together for life, and they raise their babies as a team. This is very special, because many animals do not stay together this way. A gibbon family is close and loving, and the family stays together for many years.

Baby gibbons are born tiny and helpless, and they cling tightly to their mothers. The mother carries her baby everywhere as she swings through the trees, holding it close and keeping it safe. The father helps too, playing with the baby and looking after it while the mother rests.

As baby gibbons grow, they learn to swing by watching their parents. At first, they hold on tight and practise on low branches. Bit by bit, they grow braver and stronger, until one day they can swing through the trees all by themselves. Even then, they stay with their family for a long time, helping to look after younger brothers and sisters.

## What Gibbons Eat

Gibbons love fruit, especially ripe, sweet fruit. They eat figs, berries, and many other fruits that grow in the rainforest. A gibbon will sit on a branch, pick a fruit with its hand, and eat it slowly. Fruit gives gibbons the energy they need to swing and sing all day long.

Gibbons also eat leaves, flowers, and shoots when fruit is hard to find. Sometimes they eat insects too, picking them carefully from leaves and bark. They know which trees have the best fruit, and they visit them again and again when the fruit is ripe. Gibbons are clever about food, and they remember the forest well.

Gibbons help the forest grow, too. When they eat fruit, they swallow the seeds, and later those seeds fall to the forest floor in a new place. Some of those seeds grow into brand new trees. In this way, gibbons are gardeners of the rainforest, planting trees as they travel and eat.

## Homes High in the Trees

Gibbons sleep high in the trees, curled up on a branch with their arms wrapped around their knees. They sleep close together as a family, keeping each other warm and safe through the night. Before dawn, they wake up, stretch, and begin their morning song.

Gibbons do not build nests like some animals do. Instead, they simply settle on a strong branch for the night, choosing a safe spot where they can rest. They know their home trees very well, and they return to favourite sleeping branches again and again.

Sadly, gibbons are in danger. Their rainforest homes are being cut down, and there is less and less forest for them to live in. Gibbons need tall trees to swing through and sing from, and without the forest, they cannot survive. That is why people are working hard to protect the rainforests of Southeast Asia and the gibbons who live there.

You can help gibbons by learning about them and caring about rainforests. Every tree that stays standing is a home, a playground, and a stage for a gibbon's song. If we protect the forests, the morning will always be filled with the beautiful singing of gibbons, swinging through the treetops and calling out to the rising sun.
