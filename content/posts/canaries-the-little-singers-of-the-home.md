Close your eyes and imagine a sunny morning, with a beautiful song floating through the air — a sweet, cheerful tune of trills and warbles. That song might be coming from a canary, a tiny yellow bird with one of the loveliest voices in the animal kingdom. Canaries have been filling homes with music for hundreds of years. But their story begins far away, on small islands in the middle of the ocean. Let us meet the little singers of the home!

## From the Canary Islands

Canaries come from the Canary Islands, a group of small islands in the Atlantic Ocean, near the coast of Africa. The islands are warm and sunny, with plenty of plants and seeds for small birds to eat. Wild canaries are small, greenish-yellow birds with streaky feathers that help them hide in the bushes and trees.

Sailors and traders first brought canaries to Europe hundreds of years ago. People were amazed by their sweet songs, and canaries soon became popular pets. At first, only rich people could afford them, because they were rare and expensive. But over time, more and more canaries were bred, and they became pets for everyone.

Today, pet canaries come in many colours. The most famous colour is bright yellow, but canaries can also be orange, red, white, and even a greenish colour like their wild ancestors. Some canaries are bred for their beautiful singing, while others are bred for their pretty colours or their fancy feathers.

## The Beautiful Song

The male canary is the singer of the family. He sings to attract a mate and to tell other males, "This is my home!" A canary's song is a wonderful mix of trills, warbles, and chirps, all joined together in a long, flowing melody. Some canaries sing for a long time without stopping, filling the whole house with music.

Canaries sing most in the spring, when the days get longer and the sun shines brighter. They also sing when they are happy, healthy, and feeling safe. A canary that sings is usually a happy canary. Female canaries can sing too, but their songs are usually shorter and softer than the males' songs.

Long ago, canaries had another very important job: they worked in coal mines. Miners took canaries down into the mines with them, because canaries are very sensitive to dangerous gases. If the air in the mine became dangerous, the canary would show signs of trouble before the miners did, giving the miners time to escape. Thanks to canaries, many miners' lives were saved. Today, we have machines to test the air in mines, so canaries no longer need to do this brave job — they can just stay at home and sing!

## What Do Canaries Eat?

In the wild, canaries eat seeds from grasses and plants. Pet canaries eat a special seed mix made for canaries, with small seeds like canary seed and millet. They also need fresh greens, like lettuce, spinach, and dandelion leaves. Small pieces of apple, carrot, and broccoli are healthy treats too.

Canaries need fresh, clean water every day, and they need a cuttlebone in their cage for calcium, to keep their beaks and bones strong. They also need tiny bits of grit, which helps them digest their seeds.

One thing canaries must never eat is chocolate or avocado, because these are poisonous to birds. A good canary owner always makes sure the bird's food is fresh and safe.

## Life in a Canary's Home

A canary's cage should be as large as possible, with room to fly from perch to perch. Canaries love to fly, and they need space to stretch their wings. Their cage should have perches at different heights, made of wood, and a swing to sit on. Canaries love swings!

Canaries also love to have a bath. Many canaries enjoy splashing in a shallow dish of water, or having a gentle mist sprayed over them. After a bath, a canary fluffs up its feathers and shakes itself dry, looking like a tiny yellow pom-pom!

Canaries are a little shy. They do not usually like to be held, and they are happiest when they can watch the world from inside their cage. They enjoy listening to music, hearing people talk, and watching what is going on in the room. Some canaries become tame enough to sit on their owner's finger, but most are content to sing from their perch.

Canaries need a quiet place to sleep at night. Many owners cover the cage with a cloth at night to help their canary rest. Canaries sleep with their heads tucked into their feathers, perched on one foot, looking like little yellow balls of fluff.

## Canary Families

In the spring, canaries build nests. The female canary builds a small, cup-shaped nest from soft materials like grass, feathers and soft fibres. She lays several tiny eggs, which are pale blue with little brown spots. She sits on the eggs to keep them warm until they hatch.

When the baby canaries hatch, they are tiny, pink and naked, with their eyes closed. Their parents feed them and keep them warm. The babies grow very quickly, and soon they are covered in soft feathers. After a few weeks, they leave the nest and start to explore the world. Young male canaries begin to practise their songs when they are a few months old, listening to their fathers and learning the tunes.

## Why Canaries Make Lovely Pets

Canaries are wonderful pets for people who love music and beauty. They are quiet, gentle, and easy to care for. They do not need to be walked or played with, but they do need fresh food, clean water, and a clean cage every day. Watching a canary sing, with its throat puffed out and its little beak open, is a joyful sight.

Canaries also teach us about patience and kindness. A canary that is treated gently and kept in a clean, sunny spot will sing its heart out. A canary that is frightened or unhappy may stop singing altogether. So a singing canary is a sign of a happy home.

So the next time you hear a sweet, cheerful song drifting through a house, listen carefully — it might be a canary, the little singer of the home, filling the air with music, just as its ancestors did on the sunny Canary Islands hundreds of years ago.
