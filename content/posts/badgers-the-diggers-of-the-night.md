Badgers are strong, striped animals that come out at night to dig, snuffle, and explore. They have a black-and-white striped face, a grey body, and short, powerful legs with long claws that are perfect for digging. Badgers live in amazing underground homes called setts, which they dig with their strong claws. A sett can have many tunnels and rooms, and badgers live there with their families, sleeping during the day and coming out at night to look for food. If you ever see a badger, you have met one of the hardest-working diggers in the animal world.

## What is a badger?

A badger is a mammal and a cousin of otters, weasels, and wolverines. There are several kinds of badgers in the world, and the best known is the European badger, which lives in Europe. The American badger lives in North America, and the honey badger lives in Africa and Asia. Badgers have a stocky, low body, short legs, and a bushy tail. Their most famous feature is their face, which has bold black and white stripes running from the nose to the ears. A badger's fur is grey on its back and dark underneath, and its coat is thick and coarse. Badgers have long, strong claws on their front feet, which they use like spades to dig in the earth. 

## The amazing sett

A badger's home is called a sett, and it is one of the most amazing homes in the animal world. A sett is a network of tunnels and chambers dug deep underground, with several entrances hidden among trees and bushes. Badgers dig their setts with their strong front claws, pushing the earth out behind them with their back feet. A sett can be very large, with many rooms for sleeping, and tunnels that twist and turn underground. Some setts are very old and have been used by many generations of badger families, growing bigger and bigger over the years. A sett is a safe, warm place where badgers can sleep, raise their cubs, and hide from danger.

## Where do badgers live?

Badgers live in many parts of the world, in Europe, North America, Africa, and Asia. The European badger lives in woodlands, meadows, and fields across Europe. The American badger lives in the open grasslands and deserts of North America. The honey badger lives in the savannas and forests of Africa and Asia. Badgers like places with soft soil that is easy to dig in, and with plenty of trees and bushes to hide their sett entrances. They also need a home with lots of food nearby, such as worms, insects, and fruit. Badgers live in family groups called clans, and each clan has its own territory, which it marks with scent to tell other badgers that the land is taken. A badger clan's territory can be quite large, with fields, woods, and hedges for the badgers to explore at night.

## What do badgers eat?

Badgers are omnivores, which means they eat both plants and animals. Their favourite food is earthworms, and they love to snuffle through the soil at night to find them. Badgers also eat insects, snails, mice, frogs, and birds' eggs. They eat fruits, nuts, berries, and roots too, especially in autumn when fruit falls from the trees. Badgers have a wonderful sense of smell, and they can sniff out food hidden deep in the soil. They use their strong claws to dig up worms and insects, and they eat slowly and carefully, enjoying their dinner. 

## A night in a badger's life

Badgers are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. During the day, a badger sleeps curled up in its cosy sett, safe and warm underground. When the sun sets and the sky grows dark, the badger wakes up and comes out of its sett. It sniffs the air, listens carefully, and then sets off across the fields and woods to look for food. They stop to dig for worms, to eat fallen fruit, or to explore a new smell. They also play together, chasing each other and rolling around in the grass. Before dawn, the badgers hurry back to their sett, where they curl up together in their grassy beds and sleep until the next night.

## Baby badgers

Baby badgers are called cubs, and they are born in the sett in the early spring. A mother badger usually has two or three cubs at a time. When the cubs are born, they are tiny, blind, and covered in soft, pale fur. The mother feeds them with milk and keeps them warm and safe in the cosy sleeping chamber. After a few weeks, the cubs open their eyes and start to explore their sett. They play together in the tunnels, tumbling and wrestling with each other. When they are a few months old, the cubs start to come out of the sett at night with their mother, learning how to find worms and dig in the soil. The mother teaches them everything they need to know about being a badger. 

## Fun facts about badgers

- A badger's home is called a sett, and it can have many tunnels and rooms underground.
- Badgers are nocturnal, which means they sleep during the day and come out at night.
- A badger's favourite food is earthworms, which it sniffs out with its excellent sense of smell.
- Badgers have black-and-white striped faces that make them easy to recognise.
- Some badger setts are very old and have been used by many generations of badger families.

Badgers are strong, striped, and wonderfully hard-working animals. With their powerful claws, cosy setts, and night-time adventures, they are the true diggers of the night. Next time you walk past a woodland at dusk, imagine the badgers waking up in their underground homes and digging for dinner under the stars.
