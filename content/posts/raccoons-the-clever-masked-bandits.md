Have you ever seen an animal that looks like it is wearing a mask and getting ready for a fancy dress party? That is a raccoon! Raccoons are some of the cleverest animals in the wild. They have a black mask across their eyes, a bushy ringed tail, and clever little hands that can open lids, turn taps, and untie knots. They come out mostly at night, when most of us are asleep, and they get up to all sorts of clever tricks. Let us meet these masked bandits and find out how they live.

## Meet the Masked Bandit

A raccoon is about the size of a small dog, with soft grey fur, a pointed face, and a long tail with dark rings around it. The most famous thing about a raccoon is the black mask across its eyes. It makes the raccoon look like a little robber sneaking around in the night, which is why people call it a masked bandit. But the mask is not a costume. Scientists think the dark fur helps cut down glare and helps raccoons see better in the dark, a bit like the black paint sports players wear under their eyes.

A raccoon's front paws are amazing. They look a lot like tiny human hands, with long fingers that can grab, hold, and feel things. Raccoons use their paws to pick up food, open containers, and explore everything they find. Their sense of touch is very strong, and they love to feel things with their fingers before they eat them.

## Why Do Raccoons Wash Their Food?

One of the funniest things raccoons do is dip their food in water before they eat it. People used to think raccoons were washing their food to make it clean. That is why their scientific name means "washer" in one old language. But raccoons are not really washing their dinner. They are feeling it!

When a raccoon dips its paws in water, the skin on its fingers gets softer, and it can feel things even better. It is a bit like how your fingers get wrinkly in the bath and you can feel things more clearly. Raccoons love water, and they often look for food near streams, ponds, and rivers. They dip their paws in, feel around for tasty treats, and then munch happily.

Raccoons will eat almost anything. They love fruit, nuts, berries, and seeds. They also eat insects, frogs, fish, and eggs when they can find them. In towns, they might look for food in bins, which is why people are told to keep their bin lids shut tight. Raccoons are not fussy eaters at all. If it smells good and they can reach it, they will probably try it.

## Life in the Night

Raccoons are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep during the day. Their big eyes help them see in the dark, and their whiskers help them feel their way around. When the sun goes down, raccoons wake up, stretch, and set off to look for food.

During the day, raccoons sleep in safe, cosy places. They might sleep in a hollow tree, in a burrow dug by another animal, in a pile of leaves, or even in an attic or a shed if they live near people. They like places that are dark, dry, and hard for other animals to reach.

## Clever Hands, Clever Minds

Raccoons are famous for being clever. They can remember how to solve puzzles for a long time, and they can learn from watching other raccoons. If one raccoon works out how to open a tricky bin, other raccoons nearby may soon learn the same trick.

Their clever paws help them in many ways. A raccoon can open a jar lid, lift a latch, and even turn a door handle. People who live near raccoons often have funny stories about them opening things they thought were shut tight. That is why wildlife experts say the best way to keep raccoons out of bins is to use strong lids and locks, and to keep food away at night.

## Baby Raccoons

In spring, mother raccoons give birth to babies called kits. The kits are born in a safe den, with their eyes closed and their fur soft and fluffy. They stay close to their mother for the first weeks of their lives, drinking her milk and keeping warm in the den.

After a while, the kits open their eyes and start to explore. Their mother teaches them everything they need to know: how to climb trees, how to find food, how to swim, and how to stay safe. The kits follow her everywhere, copying what she does. They play together too, wrestling and chasing each other, which helps them learn how to move and climb.

Kits stay with their mother for many months. By the time autumn comes, they are big and strong enough to look after themselves. Young raccoons often stay near their mother for a while longer, learning more tricks before they set off to find their own home.

## Raccoons and People

Raccoons and people live side by side in many towns and cities. Raccoons are wild animals, so it is best to watch them from a safe distance and never try to touch or feed them. Wild animals can bite if they feel scared, and human food is not always good for them.

## Fun Facts About Raccoons

- Raccoons have a black mask across their eyes that makes them look like little bandits.
- Their front paws work a bit like human hands, with long fingers for grabbing and feeling.
- Raccoons dip their food in water to feel it better, not really to wash it.
- A raccoon can climb down a tree head first, which is a rare trick.
- Raccoons are nocturnal, which means they are awake at night and asleep in the day.
- Baby raccoons are called kits, and they stay with their mother for many months.
- Raccoons are good swimmers and love living near water.
- A raccoon's tail has dark rings around it, like stripes on a jumper.

Next time you see a bin lid left open in the morning, or hear a little rustling in the trees at night, think of the clever masked bandit. The raccoon is out there, using its clever hands and clever mind to find its dinner, look after its babies, and live its wild life under the stars. What a clever little animal!
