Imagine a fish as wide as a small car, with wings instead of fins, gliding through the blue sea like a bird flying through the sky. That is a manta ray, one of the biggest and gentlest fish in the whole ocean. Manta rays are not scary at all. They have no sting, no sharp teeth, and no interest in hurting anyone. They are calm, curious and graceful, and people who swim with them often say it is one of the most magical moments of their lives. Gliding through warm tropical seas is a giant fish with wings, a friendly smile, and a gentle nature that has earned it the name devil fish.

## Wings That Fly Underwater

A manta ray's body is flat and wide, with two huge fins that stick out on either side like wings. When it swims, it flaps those fins slowly and smoothly, and it looks exactly like a bird flying through the air. Some manta rays are wider than a family car is long, which makes them one of the biggest rays in the world.

On the front of its head, a manta ray has two little flaps that curl up like horns. Long ago, people saw those horn-like flaps and called manta rays "devil fish", which sounds scary but is not fair at all. Those flaps are not horns. They are used to help guide food into the manta ray's mouth when it is feeding. The manta ray is one of the gentlest giants in the sea, and it has never hurt a person on purpose.

Manta rays have dark skin on their backs and pale skin on their bellies. Each manta ray has its own pattern of spots on its belly, just like a fingerprint. Scientists use those spot patterns to tell one manta ray from another, and to learn about their lives as they swim from place to place.

## Eating Tiny Food with a Giant Mouth

Even though manta rays are huge, they eat some of the tiniest food in the sea. They are filter feeders, which means they swim through the water with their mouths open and catch tiny animals called plankton. Plankton are so small that you can hardly see them, but there are millions of them floating in the sea, and a manta ray can eat a huge amount of them in a single day.

When a manta ray finds a cloud of plankton, it swims through it in big, slow loops, scooping up the tiny creatures with its wide mouth. Sometimes several manta rays feed together, turning and gliding in a beautiful underwater dance. Their mouths are huge, but they have no sharp teeth for biting. Instead, they have special plates inside that trap the plankton while the water flows out.

Manta rays also visit special places on the reef called cleaning stations. There, tiny fish swim up to the manta ray and nibble away little bits of skin and parasites. The manta ray stays very still and lets the little fish do their work, like a person sitting quietly while getting a haircut.

## Clever and Curious

Manta rays are clever animals. They have some of the biggest brains of any fish, and scientists think they are among the smartest fish in the sea. They seem to recognise themselves in mirrors, which is something very few animals can do. They also seem to remember places and other manta rays they have met before.

They are curious about people too. Divers often tell stories of manta rays swimming up to them, looking them in the eye, and gliding around them in slow circles, as if they are saying hello. Manta rays are not afraid of people, and they often come back to the same places again and again, year after year.

Manta rays travel long distances across the ocean, following the warm currents and the plankton they feed on. Some manta rays stay near one reef for most of their lives, while others swim for hundreds of miles. Scientists track them with special tags so they can learn where they go and how to protect them.

## Babies That Are Born Swimming

Female manta rays give birth to live babies, called pups, instead of laying eggs. A baby manta ray is born ready to swim, and it is already quite big when it is born. The mother looks after her pup for a short time, and then the young manta ray swims off on its own, ready to explore the ocean.

Manta rays grow slowly and live for a long time. They can live for many decades, which is a long life for a fish. But because they grow slowly and have only one baby at a time, it takes a long time for their numbers to grow back if they are harmed.

## Protecting the Gentle Giants

Manta rays face dangers in the ocean. In some places, people catch them for their gill plates, which are sold for use in old-fashioned medicines. This is sad, because the manta ray is worth far more alive, swimming in the sea, than it is in a market. Luckily, many countries have now made it against the law to catch manta rays, and more and more places are protecting them.

People are also protecting the places where manta rays feed and clean, so that these gentle giants have safe homes. Divers and snorkellers can visit manta rays in special protected areas, where they can watch them glide through the water without disturbing them. Tourism like this helps local people earn a living, and it gives them a good reason to keep the manta rays safe.

## A Giant Worth Loving

The manta ray reminds us that the biggest animals are not always the scariest. With its wide wings, its gentle nature and its graceful dance through the sea, the manta ray is one of the most beautiful creatures in the ocean. It is a true gentle giant, and the sea would be a much poorer place without it.

Next time you think of the ocean, imagine a manta ray gliding slowly through the blue water, its wings moving like a bird in flight, on its way to its next meal of tiny plankton. It is a wonderful sight, and one that reminds us how amazing the sea really is.
