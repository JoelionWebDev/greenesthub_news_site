Deep in the forest, when the sun has gone down and the moon is up, a pair of bright eyes glows in the dark. A soft, spotted shape moves quietly along a branch. Is it a cat? Not quite! It is a civet — a shy, cat-like animal that wakes up when most of the world is asleep. Let us step into the night and meet this mysterious forest friend.

## What Is a Civet?

A civet is a small mammal that looks a little bit like a cat, but it is not a cat at all. Civets belong to their own family of animals, and they are more closely related to mongooses than to house cats. They have long bodies, short legs, pointed faces, and long tails. Their fur is often grey or brown, with spots, stripes, or blotches that help them hide among the leaves and shadows.

Civets live in warm parts of the world — in the forests of Africa and Asia. Some kinds, like the African civet, live in grasslands and forests across Africa. Others, like the palm civets, live high in the trees of the rainforests of Asia. There are many different kinds of civets, and each one has its own special markings and habits, but they all share the same cat-like face and night-time ways.

## Masters of the Night

Civets are nocturnal, which means they sleep during the day and wake up at night. During the day, a civet curls up in a hollow tree, a thick bush, or a quiet burrow, hidden away from the hot sun and from animals that might want to catch it. When evening comes, it stretches, yawns, and sets off into the dark forest.

Night is the perfect time for a civet. Its big eyes are made for seeing in dim light, and its ears can hear the tiniest rustle of a mouse in the grass. Its nose is very sharp too, and it can sniff out ripe fruit hanging on a branch high above. While other animals are sleeping, the civet is busy hunting, climbing, and exploring.

## What Do Civets Eat?

Civets are not fussy eaters. They eat many different things, which helps them survive in the forest. They love fruit, especially soft, sweet fruits like figs, bananas, and mangoes. They also eat small animals such as mice, birds, frogs, and lizards, and they snack on insects, eggs, and even crabs when they can find them.

Because civets eat so much fruit, they do a very important job for the forest. When a civet eats a fruit, the seeds inside pass through its body and come out in its droppings, far away from the parent tree. Those seeds can then grow into new trees. In this way, civets help plant new forests, one fruit at a time. They are like little gardeners of the night.

Some civets have a special taste for coffee berries. In parts of Asia, palm civets eat ripe coffee berries, and the beans inside pass through their bodies. People once collected those beans to make a special kind of coffee. Today, most people agree that it is kinder to let civets eat their fruit in peace in the wild, rather than keeping them just for coffee.

## A Shy and Secret Life

Civets are shy animals. They do not like to be seen, and they are very good at staying hidden. A civet usually lives alone, and it marks its home area with a strong-smelling scent. Civets have special scent glands near their tails, and they rub their scent on trees and rocks to tell other civets, "This is my place."

That scent is called musk, and people have known about it for a very long time. In olden days, musk from civets was used to make perfumes smell stronger and last longer. Today, most perfumes are made in other ways, and civets are left to use their scent for their own secret messages.

At night, civets move quietly and carefully. They are always listening and watching. If they hear a strange sound, they freeze or slip away into the shadows. Their spotted fur is perfect camouflage, breaking up their shape among the leaves and moonlight. A civet can be right in front of you and you might never notice it.

## The Binturong: A Civet Cousin

One of the civet's most famous cousins is the binturong, which is also called the bearcat. The binturong is bigger than most civets, with shaggy black fur and a long, strong tail that can grip branches like a hand. It lives in the rainforests of Asia and spends almost all its life in the trees.

The binturong has a funny claim to fame: it smells a bit like buttered popcorn! Its scent comes from a chemical in its body, and people who visit zoos often notice the popcorn smell near the binturong's home. Like other civets, the binturong eats fruit and helps spread seeds through the forest.

Civets are in trouble in some places because their forest homes are being cut down to make room for farms and towns. That leaves civets with fewer trees to climb and fewer fruits to eat. Many people are working to protect civets and their forests. They save patches of rainforest, plant new trees, and teach others why civets matter. When forests are safe, civets can keep doing their night-time work — eating fruit, spreading seeds, and helping new trees grow.

## Fun Facts About Civets

Here are some fun things to remember about civets. A civet's tail can be as long as its body, and it uses it to balance in the trees. Civets can see very well in the dark, thanks to their big eyes. Some civets can climb down a tree head first, which is a very clever trick. Civets help forests grow by spreading seeds in their droppings. And the binturong, a civet cousin, smells like buttered popcorn!

Civets remind us that the night is full of life. While we sleep, these spotted, cat-like climbers are awake, keeping the forest alive. They are shy, secret, and very special — the quiet gardeners of the night.
