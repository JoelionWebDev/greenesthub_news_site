Dwarf hamsters are some of the smallest pets you can keep, but they have huge personalities. A dwarf hamster can fit in the palm of your hand, yet it can run for miles on its wheel every night, stuff its cheeks full of food, and dig tunnels like a tiny builder. If you want a pet that is small, fun to watch and full of surprises, a dwarf hamster might be perfect for you.

## Meet the Dwarf Hamsters

There are a few kinds of dwarf hamster that people keep as pets. The most common are the Campbell's dwarf hamster, the Winter White dwarf hamster and the Roborovski hamster. The Roborovski, often called a Robo, is the smallest of all — it is so tiny and quick that it is hard to hold. Campbell's and Winter Whites are a little bigger and calmer, so they are often better for children who want to hold their pet.

Dwarf hamsters come in lovely colours. Campbell's hamsters can be grey, brown, white or black. Winter Whites have a special trick: in winter, their fur can turn white, like snow. This happens because of the shorter days, and it helps them hide in the snow in the wild. In a warm house, they might stay grey all year, but it is still fun to know they have this secret power.

## Cheeks Like Shopping Bags

One of the funniest things about hamsters is their cheeks. A hamster has special pouches inside its cheeks that it uses to carry food. When a dwarf hamster finds a tasty seed, it stuffs it into its cheek pouch and carries it back to its nest. Its cheeks can swell up so much that its head looks twice as big!

Hamsters do this because in the wild, they need to gather food quickly and take it somewhere safe to eat. A hamster that stops to eat in the open might be spotted by a bird of prey. So it stuffs its cheeks, runs home, and then empties the food out in its nest, where it can eat in peace. It is like carrying a shopping bag in your face!

## Night-Time Adventures

Hamsters are nocturnal, which means they are most active at night. During the day, they sleep in their nests, curled up in a ball of soft bedding. But when the sun goes down, they wake up and get busy. They run on their wheels, dig in their bedding, explore their cages and munch on their food.

A dwarf hamster can run a very long way on its wheel in one night. In the wild, hamsters travel long distances to find food, so running is in their nature. That is why a hamster cage needs a good, safe wheel. If you listen at night, you might hear the gentle squeak of the wheel as your hamster has its nightly run.

Dwarf hamsters also love to dig and burrow. In the wild, they live in underground tunnels. In a cage, they need deep bedding so they can dig and make their own little tunnels. Watching a hamster pop up from a tunnel it has just dug is a real treat.

## A Home for a Dwarf Hamster

Dwarf hamsters need a home that is safe and interesting. The cage should be as big as you can manage, because these tiny animals love to explore. It should have a wheel, a small house to sleep in, tunnels to run through, and things to chew. Hamsters' teeth keep growing all their lives, so they need to gnaw on safe wooden toys and twigs to keep their teeth short and healthy.

The bottom of the cage should have deep bedding, like paper-based bedding or aspen shavings, so the hamster can dig and burrow. Never use fluffy cotton bedding, because it can tangle around a hamster's legs and hurt it.

Some dwarf hamsters like to live alone, and some like company. Roborovski hamsters often enjoy living in pairs, but Campbell's hamsters can fight if they are kept together. It is important to learn about the kind of dwarf hamster you have, and to watch them carefully if they share a cage.

## What Do Dwarf Hamsters Eat?

Dwarf hamsters eat a mix of seeds, grains and pellets made for hamsters. They also love small pieces of fresh vegetables, like cucumber, carrot and broccoli. A tiny piece of fruit, like apple or banana, is a nice treat, but only sometimes, because fruit is sweet and too much sugar is not good for them.

Fresh water is very important. Most hamster owners use a water bottle that hangs on the side of the cage. The hamster licks the little metal ball at the end of the bottle to drink. It is clever, and it keeps the water clean.

Never give a hamster chocolate, citrus fruits or sticky sweets. These can make a hamster very ill. And always wash your hands before and after handling your hamster, to keep both of you healthy.

## Making Friends with Your Hamster

Dwarf hamsters are small and quick, and they can be nervous at first. When you bring a new hamster home, give it a few days to settle in. Talk to it softly, and offer it treats from your hand. Soon, it will learn that your hand is safe, and it may climb onto your palm to explore.

Always handle a dwarf hamster gently, with cupped hands, and never squeeze it. Because they are so small, they can be hurt easily. Children should sit down when holding a hamster, so that if it wriggles, it does not fall far. With patience and kindness, a dwarf hamster can become a tame and trusting little friend.
