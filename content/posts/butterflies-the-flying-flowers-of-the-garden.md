On a sunny day in the garden, you might see a flash of orange, blue or yellow dancing in the air. Look closer, and you will see it is a butterfly, one of the prettiest insects in the world. Butterflies are often called flying flowers because their wings are so bright and soft. But butterflies are much more than pretty. They help flowers make seeds, they show us that our gardens are healthy, and their amazing change from caterpillar to butterfly is one of the greatest wonders in nature. People of all ages love butterflies, and gardens are happier places when butterflies visit them.

## From tiny egg to hungry caterpillar

A butterfly's life begins as a tiny egg, often laid on the underside of a leaf. The egg is so small that you might miss it if you are not looking carefully. After a while, a tiny caterpillar hatches out. The caterpillar has one big job: to eat and grow. It munches on leaves all day long, growing bigger and bigger. As it grows, its skin becomes too tight, so it sheds it and grows a new one. A caterpillar may do this several times. It does not look much like a butterfly yet. It is soft, wiggly and hungry, with lots of little legs. But inside that caterpillar, an amazing change is already getting ready to happen.

## The magic of the chrysalis

When the caterpillar has grown big enough, it finds a safe, quiet spot on a stem or a leaf. There it makes a hard case around itself called a chrysalis. Inside the chrysalis, the caterpillar's body changes in a wonderful way. It is not sleeping, even though it looks very still. Inside, it is slowly turning into a butterfly, growing wings, long legs and feathery antennae. This change is called metamorphosis, which is a big word that means a complete change of shape. After some time, the chrysalis opens, and a brand new butterfly crawls out. Its wings are soft and folded at first, but soon they open up, dry in the sun, and are ready to fly.

## Wings full of colour

A butterfly's wings are covered in tiny scales, so small you can hardly see them. These scales give the wings their bright colours and patterns. Some butterflies are orange and black, some are bright blue, and some have spots that look like eyes. These patterns are not just for beauty. They help the butterfly hide from hungry birds, and they can also warn other animals that the butterfly does not taste good. The colours can help butterflies find each other too. When a butterfly flies, its wings move up and down in a gentle, fluttering way. It does not fly in a straight line like a bird. It dances from flower to flower, and that dance makes gardens feel full of joy.

## How butterflies help flowers and people

Butterflies are great helpers in the garden. When a butterfly lands on a flower to drink its sweet nectar, tiny grains called pollen stick to its body. When it flies to the next flower, some of that pollen rubs off. This helps the flowers make seeds, and those seeds grow into new plants. Farmers and gardeners need butterflies and other insects to help their fruits, vegetables and flowers grow. Butterflies also help people by showing us how healthy nature is. If a garden has lots of butterflies, it is usually a healthy garden with clean air and plenty of plants. That is why many people plant special flowers to invite butterflies to visit.

## A butterfly's day in the garden

A butterfly spends its day looking for sunshine, flowers and safe places to rest. Butterflies love warm, sunny weather, because the sun warms their wings and helps them fly. They drink nectar from flowers using a long, thin tongue that works like a straw. It stays curled up when the butterfly is not using it, and unrolls when it finds a flower. Butterflies also like to rest on leaves and stones, with their wings open to catch the sun. At night, or when it rains, they hide under leaves and in sheltered spots. Some butterflies travel very far during their lives, flying over fields, towns and even seas to find warmer places and more flowers.

## People and butterflies through the years

People have loved butterflies for a very long time. Artists paint them, poets write about them, and children chase them gently in gardens all over the world. In many cultures, the butterfly is a symbol of hope and new beginnings, because it changes from a crawling caterpillar into a beautiful flying insect. Today, people can help butterflies by planting flowers that give them nectar, by leaving a wild corner in the garden, and by not using sprays that can harm them. Schools often plant butterfly gardens so children can watch caterpillars grow and see butterflies up close. When we make space for butterflies, we make our world more beautiful for everyone.

## How to help butterflies where you live

You can be a butterfly friend, even if you only have a small garden or a balcony. Plant flowers that butterflies love, such as flowers with bright colours and sweet smells. Leave some leaves and plants for caterpillars to eat, because without caterpillars there would be no butterflies. Give butterflies a sunny spot to rest, and a shallow dish of clean water with a few stones in it so they can drink safely. Never catch a butterfly by its wings, because the tiny scales can come off and the butterfly can be hurt. The best way to enjoy a butterfly is to watch it quietly and let it fly free. If you are patient, it may even land near you.

## Fun facts about butterflies

- A butterfly tastes with its feet, which helps it find the best leaves and flowers.
- A butterfly drinks nectar through a long tongue that works like a drinking straw.
- The bright colours on a butterfly's wings come from thousands of tiny scales.
- Some butterflies travel very long distances to find warm weather and flowers.
- A caterpillar grows so much that it has to shed its skin several times before it becomes a butterfly.
