Goats are some of the cleverest animals on the farm. They are curious, playful and full of character. A goat will try to climb anything, taste anything and explore everywhere. Farmers love goats because they are hardy, friendly and useful. They give us milk, and they help to keep grass and bushes tidy.

## What is a goat?

A goat is a farm animal with four legs, a short tail and a pair of horns. A female goat is called a doe or a nanny, a male goat is called a buck or a billy, and a baby goat is called a kid. Baby goats are some of the cutest animals you can see. They love to jump, play and bounce around, and they make a sweet little sound.

Goats come in many sizes and colours. Some are white, some are black, some are brown, and some have spots or patches. Some goats have long, floppy ears, and some have short ears that stick out to the sides. Many goats have a little beard under their chin.

## Clever heads and busy horns

Goats are famous for being clever. They can learn how to open gates and doors, and they remember where the tastiest food is. A goat that finds a way into the vegetable garden will remember it the next day too. Farmers often say that goats are escape artists, because they are so good at finding a way out of a pen if the fence is not strong.

A goat's horns are not just for show. Goats use them to protect themselves, and to settle who is the boss in the herd. Both male and female goats can have horns. The horns grow as the goat gets older.

## The great climbers

Goats are amazing climbers. Their hooves are hard and split into two parts, which helps them grip rocks and steep ground. Wild goats live in mountains, where they climb up rocky slopes that look almost impossible to climb. They can jump from rock to rock and stand on tiny ledges without slipping.

Farm goats love to climb too. Give a goat a pile of logs, a low wall or a wooden platform, and it will climb straight to the top. Goats love high places because they can see everything from up there. Baby goats, called kids, practise their climbing from a very young age. They jump on and off everything they can find, and they bounce around with joy. Watching kids play is one of the best things about visiting a farm.

## What do goats eat?

Goats eat plants, and they will try almost any plant they can find. They love grass, leaves, shrubs and weeds. Goats are famous for eating almost anything, and it is true that they will chew on things like old paper or cloth if they get the chance. But the truth is that goats are picky in their own way. They use their lips to pick out the best leaves and the tastiest bits, and they leave the rest.

Goats are ruminants, which means they have a special stomach that lets them chew their food twice. First they swallow their food quickly, and later they bring it back up to chew it again. This second chewing is called chewing the cud, and it helps the goat get all the goodness out of tough plants. You can often see a goat lying in the shade, chewing slowly and looking very calm and happy.

## Goats and their milk

One of the most useful things about goats is their milk. Goat milk is rich and creamy, and people use it to drink, to make cheese and to make yoghurt. In many parts of the world, goat milk is more common than cow milk. Goats are smaller than cows, so they need less food and less space, which makes them perfect for small farms and families.

A mother goat, called a doe, gives milk after she has had her kids. She looks after her kids with great care, feeding them and keeping them close. Baby goats drink their mother's milk when they are very young, and then they slowly start to eat grass and leaves. Goat milk has been used by people for thousands of years, and it is still loved today.

## Life in the herd

Goats are social animals, which means they love to be with other goats. A group of goats is called a herd. In a herd, goats play together, rest together and look after each other. They have a leader, usually an older goat, and the others follow her. Goats that live alone get sad and lonely, so they are happiest when they have friends.

Goats talk to each other with sounds. They bleat, which is a sound like "maa" or "baa", and each goat has its own voice. A mother goat can tell her kid's bleat from all the others, even in a big herd. Goats also show how they feel with their bodies. A happy goat wags its tail, and a curious goat stands with its ears forward, ready to explore.

## Looking after goats kindly

Goats are hardy animals, but they still need kind care. They need fresh water every day, good food like hay and greens, and a dry shelter to sleep in at night. Their shelter should keep out rain and cold wind. Goats also need space to walk, climb and play, because a bored goat is an unhappy goat.

If you visit goats, move slowly and let them come to you. Goats are friendly, and many will let you stroke them gently. Never pull a goat's horns or ears, and never chase a baby goat. Make sure fences are strong, because a clever goat will test every gap. With clean water, good food, shelter and kind hands, a goat will be a happy and healthy farm friend for many years.

## Fun facts about goats

- Goats have eyes with pupils shaped like sideways slits, which help them see almost all around them.
- Baby goats are called kids, and they love to jump and bounce as they play.
- Goats are great climbers and can stand on steep, rocky ledges without slipping.
- A goat chews its food twice — it swallows quickly, then brings the food back up to chew again.
- Each goat has its own voice, and a mother goat can tell her kid's bleat from all the others.
