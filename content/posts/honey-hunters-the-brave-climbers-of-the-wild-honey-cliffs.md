High in the mountains of Nepal, in the Himalayas, there is a tradition that is both brave and a little bit scary. Twice a year, men called honey hunters climb down tall cliffs on ladders made of rope and bamboo, to collect honey from the nests of the biggest honey bees in the world. The bees are angry, the cliffs are steep, and the honey is worth the risk. This is one of the oldest and most dangerous jobs in the world — and it has been done the same way for hundreds of years.

## The Giant Bees of the Himalayas

The honey hunters are after the honey of the giant Himalayan honey bee. This is the largest honey bee in the world, much bigger than the bees you see in a garden. These bees do not live in hives in trees, like other bees. They build huge, open nests that hang from the sides of cliffs, high above the valleys. A single nest can be as wide as a grown person is tall, and it can hold many kilograms of honey.

The bees make their nests on cliffs because it keeps them safe. Few animals can climb a steep cliff to reach them. The cliffs also face the sun, which keeps the bees warm in the cool mountain air. The bees collect nectar from the flowers of the mountain forests, including rhododendron flowers, and turn it into a special kind of honey. This honey is famous for its strong taste, and people believe it has special properties.

## The Climb Down the Cliff

Honey hunting is a team job, and it takes a lot of courage. The hunters work in a group, and each person has a job to do. First, they build a long ladder from ropes and bamboo poles. The ladder is lowered down the cliff face, and it sways in the wind. Then, one hunter — the bravest one — climbs down the ladder to reach the bee nests.

Before he climbs, the hunters light a fire at the top of the cliff. They burn leaves and branches to make thick smoke, and they lower burning torches down to the nests. The smoke confuses the bees and makes them calmer, so they are less likely to sting. Even so, the hunter is stung many times during the climb. He wears no special protective suit — just ordinary clothes, and sometimes a simple veil over his face. It takes a very brave person to do this job.

The hunter carries a long bamboo pole and a basket. When he reaches a nest, he uses the pole to cut the honeycomb away from the cliff, and he catches the dripping honeycomb in the basket. The basket is then pulled up to the top of the cliff by ropes, while the hunter climbs back up the ladder. It is hard, dangerous work, and the whole village depends on it.

## Why Is the Honey So Special?

The honey from the giant Himalayan bees is very special. It is a deep red colour, and it tastes strong and rich. People in Nepal have used it for hundreds of years as food and as medicine. They believe it can help with coughs, stomach problems and tiredness. In recent years, this honey has become famous around the world, and people are willing to pay a lot of money for it.

But collecting it is becoming harder. The giant bees are losing their homes as forests are cut down, and the cliffs where they nest are being disturbed. Some honey hunters now take too much honey, leaving too little for the bees to survive the winter. If the bees disappear, the honey hunters will lose their tradition, and the forests will lose their most important pollinators — the bees that help flowers and trees grow.

## A Tradition Passed Down

Honey hunting in Nepal is an old tradition, passed down from father to son for many generations. The hunters learn the skills from their elders: how to make a rope ladder, how to read the cliff, how to handle the smoke, and how to climb safely. The hunt is also a ceremony. Before the climb, the hunters make offerings and say prayers for safety and for a good harvest. They thank the bees and the cliff for the honey.

In some villages, the whole community gathers to watch the hunt. Children watch from a safe distance, learning the tradition and dreaming of the day they might climb the cliff themselves. The honey harvest is a time of celebration, when the village shares the sweet, golden honey together.

## Protecting the Bees and the Hunters

Today, people are working to make honey hunting safer and more sustainable. Sustainable means taking only what you need, so that the bees can survive and the tradition can continue. Hunters are learning to leave part of the honeycomb behind for the bees, and to take only the honey that the bees do not need. Some villages now have rules about how many hunts can happen each year.

There are also efforts to protect the forests where the bees feed, and to make sure the cliffs are not disturbed. If the bees have flowers to visit and cliffs to nest on, they will keep making honey, and the honey hunters will keep their tradition alive.

## Why We Love the Honey Hunters

At Greennest Hub, we love the story of the honey hunters because it shows how brave people can be, and how deeply people and bees are connected. The hunters risk stings and steep cliffs for a taste of golden honey, and the bees, in their turn, keep the mountain forests alive by carrying pollen from flower to flower. It is a dangerous job, but it is also a beautiful one — a tradition of courage, teamwork and respect for nature.
