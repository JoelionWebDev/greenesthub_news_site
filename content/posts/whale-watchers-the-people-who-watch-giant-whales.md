Imagine standing on a boat in the middle of the blue sea, when suddenly a giant whale leaps out of the water right in front of you. Water sparkles in the sunlight, and the whale splashes back down with a mighty crash. That is what whale watchers get to see. Whale watchers are people who sail out to sea to spot whales, watch how they behave, and learn how they live. Some whale watchers are scientists who study whales, and some are guides who take visitors out on boats to see these gentle giants. Whale watching is exciting, peaceful, and full of wonder, and it helps us care about whales and keep them safe.

## What is a whale watcher?

A whale watcher is a person who watches whales in the wild, out in the sea where the whales live. Some whale watchers do this as their job, and some do it as a hobby, but they all love whales. Whale watchers use their eyes, binoculars, and cameras to spot whales and to see what the whales are doing. They look for the spray of water that a whale blows out of the top of its head, which is called a blow. They look for a whale's tail lifting out of the water before it dives down deep. They also listen for whale songs, because many whales make beautiful sounds under the water. Whale watchers write down what they see, and scientists use those notes to learn more about whales.

## Why do people watch whales?

People watch whales for many reasons. Scientists watch whales to learn how they live, where they travel, and what they eat. Whales travel huge distances across the oceans, and watching them helps scientists understand their journeys. Guides watch whales so they can show them to visitors, who come from all over the world to see these amazing animals. Many people watch whales simply because it makes them happy. Seeing a whale leap out of the sea, or watching a mother whale swim gently beside her baby, is a moment that people never forget. Whale watching also helps whales, because when people see how wonderful whales are, they want to protect them and keep the sea clean.

## Where do whale watchers go?

Whale watchers go out to sea in boats, to the places where whales are known to swim. Whales live in oceans all over the world, from cold seas near the poles to warm tropical waters. Some places are famous for whale watching, because the whales come there every year. In some places, whales come close to the shore, and people can watch them from cliffs and beaches. In other places, whale watchers sail far out to sea, sometimes for hours, before they spot a whale. Whale watchers must be patient, because whales are wild animals and they come and go as they please. The sea is very big, and the whales are free to swim wherever they like.

## What do whale watchers see?

Whale watchers see many wonderful things. They see whales blowing water into the air, like a fountain. They see whales leaping out of the water, which is called breaching, and crashing back down with a great splash. They see a whale's huge tail, called a fluke, lifting up before the whale dives deep into the ocean. They see whales swimming slowly along the surface, and they see baby whales, called calves, staying close to their mothers. Sometimes whale watchers also see dolphins jumping beside the boat, and seabirds flying above the waves. Every trip is different, because the whales are wild animals and no one knows exactly what they will do. That is what makes whale watching so exciting.

## How do whale watchers spot whales?

Spotting a whale in the big wide sea is not easy, so whale watchers use clever tricks. First, they look for the blow, the tall spray of water that a whale breathes out when it comes to the surface. Then they use binoculars to look closer and to see what kind of whale it is. Whale watchers also listen carefully, because whales make sounds that can be heard with special machines that go under the water. They learn to tell whales apart by the shape of their tails, because every whale's tail has its own pattern, a bit like a fingerprint. They take photos of the tails, and they can spot the same whale again on another day. Whale watchers work together as a team, with everyone looking in a different direction so that no whale is missed.

## Rules for kind whale watching

Whale watchers always follow rules to keep the whales safe and happy. They never chase a whale or get too close to it, because that would frighten the animal. Boats move slowly and quietly near whales, and they keep a safe distance. If a whale swims towards the boat, the watchers let the whale come and go as it likes. Whale watchers never feed the whales, and they never try to touch them. They also take care not to throw anything into the sea. These rules are important, because whales are wild animals and they need their space. When whale watchers are gentle and patient, the whales often stay longer and show off their amazing jumps and dives.

## Fun facts about whale watchers

- Whale watchers look for the spray of water a whale blows into the air.
- A whale's tail has its own pattern, like a fingerprint, so watchers can tell whales apart.
- Whale watchers use binoculars and special machines to listen to whale songs.
- Some whales leap right out of the water, which is called breaching.
- Whale watchers help scientists learn about whales and help people want to protect them.

Whale watchers spend their days on the wide blue sea, looking for the giants of the ocean. They watch whales leap, dive, and sing, and they help us learn how these wonderful animals live. The next time you see a picture of a whale leaping out of the sea, think of the whale watchers on a small boat, waiting patiently with their binoculars. Maybe one day, you will be on a boat too, feeling the sea breeze on your face and watching a giant whale rise out of the water right before your eyes.
