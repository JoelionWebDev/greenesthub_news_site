Look for stems that are 8 to 12 months old — that is the sweet spot. They should be about as thick as your thumb, with 5 to 7 nodes, greenish-brown bark, and no black spots or dry cracks. Snap one: the inside should be white and moist, not dry or brown. Thin, woody stems from plants older than a year give weak sprouting. And yes, pay more for an improved variety like TME 419 if you can get it from a certified source — the yield difference over 3 hectares will pay for the stems many times over.
